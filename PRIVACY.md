# Privacy notice

PMx Landscape publishes, for each paper and conference abstract it reads, the type of organisation
the first author works for, and whether any author works for a drug company or a contract research
organisation. If you are an author and do not want this for your paper or abstract, write to
dpo@pharmetheus.com and we will remove it.

## Data published

PMx Landscape is an open research project on how pharmacometric modelling is done in practice. It
reads papers from PubMed and Europe PMC, abstracts of the PAGE meeting (Population Approach Group
in Europe), and FDA reviews of new drugs. For each paper and abstract it publishes:

- the ID of the paper or abstract, such as its PubMed ID, which leads to its author list;
- the type of organisation of the first author: academia, drug company, contract research
  organisation (CRO, which includes consultancies), software vendor or regulator;
- whether any author works for a drug company, and whether any author works for a CRO;
- the names of the authors' organisations, but only for regulators, software vendors, drug
  companies and CROs on a fixed list, and never for consultancies;
- what the project found about the work itself, such as which software it used.

When an affiliation matches none of the types, the item is marked "unclassified". About 3 in 100
items are. We never publish author names, contact details, the text of papers or abstracts, or any
profile of a person.

The repository also holds the text of the FDA review documents as FDA publishes them. These
documents include the names of the FDA staff who wrote or signed them. We use the documents only to
record which software the applicants used.

## Legal basis

The types of organisation let us compare practice in academia, industry and regulatory agencies,
for example whether a software tool is used more in academic papers. The legal basis is our
legitimate interest in publishing open, reproducible research on pharmacometric practice (GDPR
article 6(1)(f)).

We do not contact authors individually, because the project covers thousands of papers and
abstracts and that would take a disproportionate effort (GDPR article 14(5)(b)). We publish this
notice instead.

## Recipients

The published tables are public on GitHub, so anyone can read them.

The texts are read with the help of Claude language models from Anthropic, under terms that do
not allow Anthropic to use them to train its models. The text sent to the models is the title,
abstract and methods or main text of each item, not its author list. To check the rules that
classify affiliations, a sample of affiliation texts may be shown to a model. Anthropic is based in
the United States. As for every transfer outside the EU and EEA, Pharmetheus relies on an
adequacy decision of the European Commission (the EU-US Data Privacy Framework) or on standard
contractual clauses approved by the Commission, as its privacy policy describes.

## Retention

Each release of the project stays public, and a release is not changed after it is published. The
full records (author names, affiliations and the text of each item) are kept in a local copy that
is used to run the analysis and is never published. That copy is deleted for a source when the
source is dropped from the project, and in full when the project ends.

## Your rights

You can ask us for access to the data about your papers or abstracts, ask us to correct them, object
to their use, or ask us to erase them. Write to dpo@pharmetheus.com, name the item (its DOI, PubMed ID
or PAGE abstract number), and write from an address that appears on the item or show in another way
that you are an author. We answer within one month.

When you object or ask for erasure, all organisation data of the item are removed from every later
release. After a request for erasure, the record of the item is also deleted from our local copy. The item is then marked "unclassified", like the items whose
affiliation could not be classified, so your request cannot be seen in the data. Releases published
before your request, the history of the repository, and copies others have made, still contain the
data. Which software
the item used stays published, because it describes the work, not you. We keep a private record of
your request, seen only by the project team, so that the data stay removed when the tables are
rebuilt.

You can also complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten,
IMY), www.imy.se, or to the data protection authority of the EU or EEA country where you live or
work.
