NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation
NDA 220305
Komzifti (ziftomenib)

Efficacy: In E-R analyses for efficacy, there was no significant relationship between ziftomenib
exposure and response, suggesting that lower ziftomenib exposure in patients not receiving
CYP3A-inhibiting azoles may not adversely impact efficacy [Section 14.4.3]. However, this
assessment is limited by the limited sample size at dose levels other than 600 mg, the low CR +
CRh rate overall, and the low proportion of patients who were not on CYP3A-inhibiting azoles.

Therefore, the FDA evaluated the effect of duration of strong CYP3A-inhibiting azole use on
response in NPM/J-m R/R AML (n=112, Phase 1b EAS + Phase 2). Patients were classified
based on if they received strong CYP3A-inhibiting azoles for none (0%), some (1-75%), or the
majority (over 75%) of study time (FDA Table 24).

There was no clear relationship between extent of strong CYP3A-inhibiting azole use and
CR/CRh. Efficacy was generally comparable between patients receiving strong CYP3A-
inhibiting azoles for 0% and greater than 75% of study time. Of note, decreased efficacy
observed in the patients receiving strong CYP3A-inhibiting azoles for > 75% of study time when
comparing to 1-75% of study time may be confounded by potentially worse overall status of
patients requiring azole antifungals for the majority of study time.

This analysis further supports that lower ziftomenib exposure in patients not receiving CYP3A-
inhibiting azoles may not adversely impact efficacy, and that these patients should receive the
600 mg dose.

Safety: In E-R analyses for safety, there was no significant relationship between ziftomenib
exposure and safety, suggesting that higher ziftomenib exposure in patients receiving CYP3A-
inhibiting azoles may not adversely impact safety /Section 14.4.3]. However, this assessment is
limited by the limited sample size at dose levels other than 600 mg and the low proportion of
patients who were not on CYP3A-inhibiting azoles. Additionally, a concentration-dependent
increase in QTc was observed in n=133 R/R AML patients receiving ziftomenib 600 mg QD,
with the largest mean increase predicted to be 7.7 ms and 8% of patients with QTc over 500 ms
and increase greater than 60 ms from baseline.
