NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 218881

Inluriyo (imlunestrant)

This 64-year-old white female patient in Mexico with baseline anemia and elevated uric acid,
and metastatic disease to the bones and thyroid gland, died of upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage
on day 142, one day after the last dose of imlunestrant. She had no dose omissions or dose
reductions reported. The patient had sudden, massive hematemesis on day 142 and died on the
way to the hospital. She continued to have anemia and increased blood uric acid throughout the
study and was started on allopurinol on day 65. Thirteen days prior to starting imlunestrant the
patient’s PTT was abnormal (51.8 seconds), though her INR was 1.12. Three days prior to
starting the study drug her PTT was 38 seconds and her INR was 1.12. There were no other
known risk factors for upper gastrointestinal bleeding, however, she was taking tramadol and
paracetamol before and throughout the study for treatment of ongoing hip and back pain. She did
not have known helicobacter pylori infection, gastroduodenal ulcer disease or severe
esophagitis/gastritis/duodenitis or esophagogastric varices. Imaging done approximately 1 month
before her death did not reveal disease progression. No autopsy was performed, and no
endoscopy had been performed. The upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage is thought by the
Applicant not to be related to imlunestrant. It is difficult to assess the cause of upper
gastrointestinal hemorrhage due to confounding factors and complicated medical course and
FDA cannot rule whether imlunestrant contributed to the upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage
event.

This 69-year-old American Indian/Alaska Native female in Mexico with metastases to bone and
a history of hypertension, gout, and renal atrophy, died of an acute myocardial infarction on day
288. The last dose of study drug was on day 281. She had no dose omissions or dose reductions
reported. The patient had experienced intense chest pain on day 267 and was diagnosed with a
myocardial infarction. She underwent catheterization and percutaneous coronary intervention at
that time for single vessel 70% injury. The patient was discharged from the hospital 3 days after
these interventions. She had also received prior treatment with epirubicin and docetaxel for
metastatic breast cancer. The acute myocardial infarction is thought not to be related to
imlunestrant. The patient had known risk factors for a cardiac event. FDA agrees with the
Applicant’s assessment.
