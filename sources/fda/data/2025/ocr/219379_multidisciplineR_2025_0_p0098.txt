The Applicant's Position:

The recommended mirdametinib dose regimen of 2 mg/m? BID, with a maximum dose of 4 mg
BID, administered on a 3 weeks on/1 week off schedule for NF1 PN patients is supported by
safety, tolerability, efficacy, PopPK, and exposure-response analyses. Mirdametinib has a
concentration-dependent impact on PN tumor growth. Exposure-safety relationships were only
significant for low severity AEs, namely lab elevations that do not correlate with clinical
tolerability findings. Simulations of tumor growth and AE incidence conducted using the PopPK
and PKPDx models to predict the responses over a range of target doses of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, and
3.0 mg/m? indicated that exposures associated with the 2 mg/m? BID dose of mirdametinib
provide an optimal balance of robust efficacy for patients with NF1 PN with a tolerable
incidence of low severity exposure-related AEs.

Simulations from PopPK and PKPDx models also predict negligible changes to exposure and
response when using the same nomograms for the tablet and capsule dose forms. Thus,
mirdametinib may be dosed using a single unified nomogram, for both tablet and capsule dose
forms, using 1 mg increments for each BSA band without altering the anticipated clinical
outcomes for patients with NF1 PN. This unified nomogram compared with what was used in
Study MEK-NF-201 will prevent errors in dose calculation that may arise from having different
instructions for the two dose forms.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. The proposed unified dosing nomogram for capsules
(1, 2 mg) and tablets (1 mg) is acceptable.
