The Applicant's Position:
Based on intrinsic patient factors in the PopPK analyses, no alternative dosing regimen or

management strategy is required based on demographic factors or for patients with mild
hepatic impairment (NCI-ODWG Group B1 and B2), mild renal impairment, or moderate renal
impairment. The potential impact to mirdametinib PK from severe renal impairment or ESRD
and for moderate or severe hepatic impairment cannot be described at this time.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. A PMR will be issued to evaluate the effect of
moderate and severe hepatic impairment on mirdametinib PK.

NF1 Genotype

Enrollment in MEK-NF-201 was based on a patient having either a clinical diagnosis of NF1 using
the NIH Consensus Conference criteria or a pathogenic documented NF1 variant. In the FAS,
27/58 (47%) adult patients and 25/56 (45%) pediatric patients were enrolled based on
documented NF1 variant status (Table 28). Subject level genotype and type of NF1 variant was
not collected as part of study MEK-NF-201. In response to FDA IR dated 21 August 2024, the
Applicant provided available subject level NF1 variant data for 19/56 (34%) pediatric and 6/58
(10%) adult patients from the FAS. Given the complexity of the NF1 locus, the Applicant grouped
variants into the following categories: deletion, duplication, indel, missense, nonsense, or splice.
In pediatric patients, the distribution of NF1 variants was as follows: nonsense (N = 6, 31.6%),
splice (N = 5, 26.3%), duplications (N = 4, 21.0%), deletions (N = 3, 15.8%), and missense (N = 1,
5.3%). In the group of adult patients, 3 patients had NF1 deletions (50%), 1 patient had an indel
variant (16.7%), 1 patient had a missense variant (16.7%), and 1 patient had a nonsense variant
(16.7%). In general, the types and distribution of variants across the NF1 locus were consistent
with estimates from the literature (PMID: 34566848, PMID: 38095723, PMID: 23244495). The
ORRs in pediatric and adult patients with available NF1 variant data were similar to the ORRs in
the FAS (Table 15, Table 16). In terms of best confirmed objective responses, PRs were observed
across all NF1 variant categories with data from 21 patient per category. Of note, one adult
patient with a deletion encompassing the full coding sequence of NF1, which is associated with
larger numbers and earlier appearance of cutaneous and PNs (PMID: 34566848, PMID:
38095723), achieved a confirmed PR of the target PN. This exploratory analysis suggests that
treatment with mirdametinib induces objective responses in patients carrying different types of
pathogenic NF1 variants.
