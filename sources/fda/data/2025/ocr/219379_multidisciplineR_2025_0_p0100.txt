6.3. Comprehensive Clinical Pharmacology Review

6.3.1. General Pharmacology and Pharmacokinetic Characteristics

Data:

The PK of mirdametinib is comparable between healthy adults, adults with advanced solid
malignancies, and adults and children 2 years of age and older with NF1 PN. Therefore,
observations and conclusions from the clinical pharmacology evaluations in healthy adults and
patients without NF1 PN are of direct relevance to NF1 PN adult patients and pediatric patients.
An overview of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) properties, clinical
PK, and drug-drug interaction (DDI) characteristics of mirdametinib are provided below. For a
list of clinical studies referenced in this section, refer to Table 15.

Absorption: Mirdametinib was rapidly absorbed, with a median Tmax of approximately 1 hour
following administration of tablet or capsule formulations. Differences in Tmax were not
observed between healthy adults and patients with NF1 PN or between adult and pediatric
patients.

Human absolute bioavailability was not evaluated in clinical studies. Mirdametinib is highly
soluble and shows high permeability in in vitro assays. As such, a Biopharmaceutics
Classification System Class | compound designation request was submitted for mirdametinib.

Distribution: The mirdametinib apparent volumes of distribution estimated from a PopPK
analysis using data across clinical studies was 16 L for central compartment and 22.4 L and

54.3 L for shallow and deep peripheral compartments, respectively. Following administration of
a single oral 4 mg dose to healthy male participants in the mass balance study (Study MEK-NF-
101), the mean apparent volume of distribution (% coefficient of variation [CV]) was 255 L
(13.4%).

Mirdametinib is highly bound to protein in human plasma. The mean unbound fraction for
mirdametinib in human plasma ranged from 0.176% to 0.254% and is independent of
mirdametinib concentration.
