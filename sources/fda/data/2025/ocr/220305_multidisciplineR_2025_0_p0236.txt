Subject © Wasa 33-year-old male with de novo KMT24-r AML who had received multiple
prior therapies including induction chemotherapy, salvage therapy, allogeneic stem cell
transplant, and additional chemotherapy regimens before enrolling in a Phase 1/2 study of
ziftomenib. The patient developed DS on Day 8 of ziftomenib treatment, which progressed to
Grade 3 severity and required temporary drug interruption and treatment with dexamethasone
and hydroxyurea. His clinical course was complicated by multiple bilateral pulmonary emboli on
Day 19, followed by pneumonitis, and acute pericarditis with pericardial effusion on Day 22,
accompanied by supraventricular tachycardia and lactic acidosis. Despite ongoing management
including metoprolol for cardiac issues and continued dexamethasone tapering, the patient's
condition deteriorated with persistent AML noted on Day 27 restaging, progressive pericardial
effusion leading to cardiac tamponade, hypotension on Day 32, and ultimately cardiac arrest and
death shortly after transfer to the intensive care unit.

CDIL Comment: While the review team acknowledges multiple confounding factors, the causal
role of the documented AR of DS to the eventual patient death cannot be ruled out. As such, this
is considered a fatal AR due to DS.

Subject O° Wasa 78-year-old male with NPMJ-m AML who had an extensive medical

history including cardiovascular conditions (atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease,
hypertension), chronic kidney disease, and various hematologic abnormalities. On Day 8 of
ziftomenib treatment, the patient developed a Grade 3 device-related infection with
Streptococcus pyogenes bacteremia in the setting of Grade 4 neutropenia, presenting with fever,
fatigue, chills, and diaphoresis, which was treated with multiple antibiotics including
vancomycin, cefepime, metronidazole, and later ceftriaxone. On Day 15, the patient developed
Grade 2 DS with sudden oxygen requirement of 6L, dyspnea, and inspiratory wheezes, leading to
ziftomenib interruption and treatment with dexamethasone and hydroxyurea. The patient's
condition rapidly deteriorated on Day 16 with worsening hypoxemia requiring high-flow oxygen,
rapidly climbing white blood cell counts, and ultimately Grade 5 respiratory failure that resulted
in death on Day 16, with the patient declining escalation to intensive care.

CDIL Comment: Similarly, the prior S. pyogenes infection did not preclude DS from
contributing to the death of this study participant. The rapid onset of respiratory failure in the
context of hyperleukocytosis resulted in the attribution of this death as due to DS.
