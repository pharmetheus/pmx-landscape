6. Patient 7]

Tumor mutation status: ESR/m-not detected

This 76-year-old White female from France with metastatic breast cancer and history of
cardiomegaly, hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and ongoing alcohol use
died on study day 497 due to cardiac arrest. On day 411 the patient had cardiac decompensation
that lasted approximately 2 weeks. She received the last dose of fulvestrant on day 479 and on
day 491 was hospitalized due to ileal occlusion. She underwent surgery one day after
hospitalization, but showed signs of respiratory distress two days after the surgery. On day 497
the patient was found in cardiac arrest and the patient died that same day despite
cardiopulmonary resuscitation. The investigator did not consider the cardiac arrest related to the
study drug. FDA agrees with this assessment.

Serious Adverse Events

Data:

The incidence of TE-SAEs was similar across treatment arms (imlunestrant arm [10.4%]
compared to the SOC arm [11.4%]) (Table 8.30). TE-SAE incidence was also similar in the
ESRIm-detected safety population across the treatment arms (imlunestrant arm [11.7%]
compared to the SOC arm [14.5%]. The most common TE-SAEs occurring in more than 2
patients in each arm in the overall safety population were pleural effusion (1.2%) in the
imlunestrant arm, and fracture (1.5%) and pneumonia (0.9%) in the SOC arm. The pleural
effusions occurred in patients with either a history of pleural effusion, disease progression, or
concurrent sepsis. Six patients (1.8%) in each arm had fatal TE-SAEs, including a fatal septic
shock captured as due to study disease as per Table 8.30. Narratives for fatal TE-SAEs are
provided in the CSR.
