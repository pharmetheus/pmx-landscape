NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 218881

Inluriyo (imlunestrant)

Table 6.1: Key Clinical Pharmacology Review Issues, Recommendations, and Comments

Review Issue

Recommendations and Comments

Pivotal or
supportive
evidence of
effectiveness

The primary evidence of efficacy and safety comes from the randomized
Phase 3 Study EMBER-3. In patients with ER+, HER2-, ESR1-mutated
locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, imlunestrant 400 mg QD
resulted in an improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) as
compared to the control arm (fulvestrant or exemestane) with hazard ratio
for PFS of 0.62 and median PFS of 5.5 months in the imlunestrant arm.
compared to 3.8 months in the control arm. See Section 8 for details.

General dosing
instructions

The recommended dosage is 400 mg PO QD, on an empty stomach (at
least 2 hrs before or 1 hr after food).

Dosing in
patient
subgroups

e HI: The recommended dosage of imlunestrant for patients with
moderate (Child-Pugh Class B) or severe (Child-Pugh Class C) HI is
200 mg QD. No dosage modification is recommended in patients with
mild (Child-Pugh Class A) HI.

e Renal impairment (RI): No dosage modification is recommended for
patients with mild to moderate RI. The effect of severe renal
impairment is unknown; however, renal clearance of imlunestrant is
low.

e ESRI Mutations: No dosage modification is recommended based on
ESR1 mutations.

e Other Intrinsic Factors: No dosage modification is recommended
based on age, sex, body weight, race, ethnicity, or UGT1A1
polymorphisms (e.g., UGT1A1*1/*28 or UGT1A1*28/*28).

Drug-Drug
Interactions

e Imlunestrant as an Object: Imlunestrant is a CYP3A substrate.

o Avoid concomitant use with strong CYP3A inhibitors. If
concomitant use cannot be avoided, decrease the imlunestrant
dosage to 200 mg QD.

o Avoid concomitant use with strong CYP3A inducers. If
concomitant use cannot be avoided, increase the imlunestrant
dosage to 600 mg QD.

e Imlunestrant as a Precipitant:

o Imlunestrant inhibits P-gp and BCRP. Avoid concomitant use
unless otherwise recommended in the Prescribing Information
for P-gp or BCRP substrates where minimal concentration
changes may lead to serious adverse reactions.

Labeling

Proposed labeling language was reviewed, corrected, and updated
according to applicable FDA guidances. Overall, the proposed labeling
language in the clinical pharmacology pertinent sections is generally
acceptable upon the Applicant’s agreement to the FDA revisions.
