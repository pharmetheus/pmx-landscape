NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation
NDA 220305
Komzifti (ziftomenib)

6 Clinical Pharmacology

6.1. Executive Summary

The FDA’s Assessment:

Ziftomenib is an orally administered small molecule that inhibits the interaction between menin
and histone-lysine N-methyltransferase 2A enzyme (KMT2A) fusion proteins. The Applicant is
seeking approval of ziftomenib for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory
(RR) acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with a nucleophosmin 1 (VPM1/) mutation (VPM/-m).
The proposed ziftomenib dosage is 600 mg daily (QD).

The clinical pharmacology section of the NDA is supported by the pharmacokinetics (PK),
pharmacodynamics (PD), safety, and efficacy data from the registrational Phase 1/2 study KO-
MEN-001 and the clinical pharmacology studies KO-MEN-003 (food effect and drug-drug
interaction [DDI] study with gastric acid-reducing agent [ARA]) and KO-MEN-005 (mass-
balance and absolute bioavailability [BA]).

The clinical pharmacology review focused on the assessment of the acceptability of the proposed
ziftomenib dosage and dosing guidance with regard to (1) food, (2) concurrent use with
cytochrome P450 (CYP) 3A inhibitors and inducers, (3) coadministration with ARAs, and (4)
renal and hepatic impairment (RI and HI, respectively).

Specific recommendations/comments are summarized in FDA Table 21.

FDA Table 21. Clinical Pharmacology Review Issues, Recommendations, and Comments

Review Issues Recommendations and Comments
Evidence of The primary evidence of effectiveness comes from the Phase 1/2 pivotal
effectiveness study KO-MEN-001 /Section 6.3.2.1].

In the NPM1-m/KM724-rearrangement dose expansion phase,
ziftomenib demonstrated better activity at 600 mg versus 200 mg
(complete response [CR]/complete hematologic response [CRh] 25% vs
0%). In the NPM1-m modified intention-to-treat (mITT) dataset, CR +
CRh was 21% (n=112, Phase 1b efficacy analysis set [EAS] + Phase 2).

General dosing The recommended dosage is 600 mg QD to be administered on an empty
instructions stomach (at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal) /Section 6.3.2.2,
6.3.2.4].

Administration with a high-fat meal increases exposure ~4-fold
compared to fasted administration.
