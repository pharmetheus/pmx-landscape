Mirdametinib (GOMEKLI)

Overall Conclusions:

e Mirdametinib was predicted to have a concentration-dependent impact on tumor
growth, with an exponential decay of this effect over time. This decay was higher in
adults, possibly due to the differences in disease presentation between the pediatric
population and adults. Given this difference in decay, the half-life of effect in adults was
estimated to be 4.03 months, compared to 23.7 months in the pediatric population.

e Ofthe mirdametinib exposure metrics, AUCO-12,ss was found to be the best predictor
of BOR, with an increasing probability of BOR as AUCO-12,ss increased. No exposure-
response relationships were identified for DOR or TTP.

e Simulations suggested an increasing magnitude of %CFB and increasing response rate
with increasing dose in the full population, and in children (2-12), adolescents (12-17),
and adults. However, this trend should be taken with caution as it has not been verfieid
in the clinical trial.

19.4.3.2. ER (efficacy) Assessment Summary

The Applicant's Position:

PKPDx and exposure-response analyses established clear relationships between exposure and
both tumor growth inhibition and overall response in participants with NF1 PN. Mirdametinib
has a concentration-dependent effect on tumor growth, with increasing steady-state exposure
being predictive of greater reduction in tumor volume, increased probability of response, and
shorter time to response. The models are sufficiently robust to predict tumor response at target
dose levels below 3 mg/m?. Simulations at target dose levels of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.2, and 3.0 mg/m?
provide support for efficacy against PN in this range, with expectation of substantially improved
probability of efficacy with increasing target dose. Further evaluation of a lower target dose in
clinical studies is unlikely to alter the selection of 2 mg/m? BID as optimal for the treatment of
NF1 PN.

General Information

Goal of ER analysis PKPDx models were developed to evaluate the PKPDx and ERR
for mirdametinib in adult and pediatric participants with NF1
PN, which included the following objectives:

* Characterize the PKPDx relationships between
mirdametinib dose/exposure and change in tumor
volume;

* Characterize the relationships between mirdametinib
exposure and efficacy in NF1 PN participants;

* Conduct population and trial simulations in support of
dose justification and dose optimization.
