Mirdametinib (GOMEKLI)

For the adult cohort of Pool 1, the most frequently reported SAEs (22 participants) were COVID-
19, abdominal pain, and nephrolithiasis. In the pediatric cohort, all SAE PTs were reported by 1
participant each.

The FDA's Assessment:

FDA performed an independent analysis of the incidence of SAEs in patients included in Pool 1
(e.g., patients from the ReNeu study and Study NF-106) who received mirdametinib at 2 mg/m?
twice daily (maximum dose of 4 mg BID) for the first 21 days of each 28-day cycle. SAEs
occurred in 20 patients (15%) with the most frequently reported SAEs >1% being abdominal
pain, nephrolithiasis, musculoskeletal pain and Covid-19.

FDA’s analysis of the ReNeu study and the incidences of SAEs in the adult and pediatric patient
population were used to support Section 6 of the USPI. SAEs occurred in 17% of adult patients
who received mirdametinib. SAEs occurring in >2% of patients were COVID-19 (3.4%) and
nephrolithiasis (3.4%), and in 1 patient each: acute kidney injury, abdominal pain, ischemic
colitis, urinary tract infection, retinal vein occlusion (RVO), scoliosis, squamous cell carcinoma of
skin, cerebrovascular accident, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. SAEs occurred in
14% of pediatric patients who received mirdametinib. SAEs in >2% of pediatric patients
included viral gastrointestinal infections (3.6%), and in 1 patient each: diplopia, musculoskeletal
pain, seizure, fall, femoral neck fracture, dehydration, and hypertension. Refer to Table 52 for
FDA’s analysis of SAEs in the ReNeu Study.
