NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation
NDA 220305
Komzifti (ziftomenib)

As shown in FDA Table 68, the predicted effects of esomeprazole on ziftomenib
exposure were similar to the observed effects of rabeprazole on ziftomenib, when
ziftomenib was administered 10, 12, and 14 h following multiple-dose administration of a
PPI, indicating that staggered dosing did not help to minimize the interaction.

The effect of esomeprazole on ziftomenib exposure was predicted to be minimal when
the PPI was administered 2 h after ziftomenib administration, or when ziftomenib was
administered 22 h after PPI administration, as indicated in FDA Table 68. However, we
note that the PBPK analyses underpredicted the observed effect of rabeprazole on
ziftomenib AUC by approximately 34% following once daily dosing of 10 mg
rabeprazole for 5 days (FDA Table 68). This underprediction may result from the lower
simulated pH compared to the observed and the pKa value of ziftomenib (FDA Figure
13). Ziftomenib is a poorly soluble weak base with pK, values of 8.2 and 4.78. Its
ionization properties around pH 4.78 are expected to significantly affect its
pharmacokinetics. The dynamic pH model failed to capture the nocturnal rise of gastric
pH, and the simulated pH values were below the observed gastric pH and/or the pKa
value of ziftomenib after 12 h of PPI administration (FDA Figure 13). Therefore, the
effects of esomeprazole on ziftomenib exposure may be also underestimated if
ziftomenib is administered 14 h or 22 h after esomeprazole administration.

The ziftomenib PBPK model failed to describe the ziftomenib plasma concentration-time
profiles and did not capture its absorption phase in the absence of CYP3A inhibitors in
cancer patients (FDA Figure 12).

Taken together, the PBPK analyses do not support staggered dosing of PPIs to minimize the
effects of PPIs on ziftomenib exposure.
