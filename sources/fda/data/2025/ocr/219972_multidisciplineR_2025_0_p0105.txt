NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — NDA 219972
Sevabertinib (BAY 2927088) — Version 2.0

The Applicant provided a WOE assessment, including published literature and a NAM (human-
induced pluripotent stem cell-based assay) for reproductive risk to supplement the rat EFD study.
The referenced published literature shows that HER2 and EGFR are critically important for
reproductive and developmental processes including blastocyst implantation, placental
development, and embryofetal/postnatal survival and development. The human-induced
pluripotent stem cell-based assay showed that sevabertinib disrupted stem cell differentiation
into cardiomyocytes and hepatocytes. Fertility studies were not conducted with sevabertinib nor
are they warranted for the advanced cancer indication.

Based on findings in animals and the mechanism of action of sevabertinib, FDA recommends a
warning for embryo-fetal toxicity and advising females of reproductive potential to use effective
contraception during treatment with sevabertinib and for 1 week after the last dose, based on five
times the human half-life of sevabertinib (" hours).

5.5.4. Other Toxicology Studies

The Applicant’s Position:

The phototoxic potential of sevabertinib was assessed in a GLP-compliant in vitro 3T3 NRU
assay, with concentrations up to 50 g/mL sevabertinib in the presence or absence of UVR. The
average PIF was 1.0; thus, sevabertinib was identified as a non-phototoxic compound.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s conclusions regarding phototoxicity. In a GLP-compliant
phototoxicity study (Study No. T105051-2, Report No. PH-41909), the Applicant evaluated the
phototoxic potential of sevabertinib by measuring the relative reduction in viability of BALB/c
3T3 mouse fibroblasts exposed to sevabertinib in the presence or absence of ultraviolet radiation.
Sevabertinib did not demonstrate phototoxic potential (photoirritancy factor [PIF] = 1) compared
to the positive control chlorpromazine hydrochloride (PIF = 36.7) under the conditions of this in
vitro assay. The criterion for “phototoxic” is PIF > 5.
