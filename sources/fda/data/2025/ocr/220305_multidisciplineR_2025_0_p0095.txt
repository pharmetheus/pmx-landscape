In the previously reviewed 28-day repeat-dose toxicology study in dogs (Study Number
00275511, reviewed under IND 142028), ziftomenib was administered twice daily by oral
gavage at doses of 20, 60, or 200 mg/kg/day for 28 days. Two unscheduled deaths occurred in
high-dose males that were likely due to severe alveolar edema and hemorrhage. Additional
histologic findings were noted in the hepatobiliary organs, gastrointestinal (GI) tract, kidney, and
lymphoid tissues. It is uncertain if the lung toxicity is directly test article-related or secondary to
other drug-mediated effects of the GI tract that resulted in hypovolemic shock. Clinical
observations included abnormal feces in all dose groups. Vomitus was observed in animals
treated with > 60 mg/kg/day and dehydration was suspected in females treated with 200
mng/kg/day. Toxicity of the GI tract was associated with a dose-dependent decrease in food
consumption and an adverse decrease in body weight in animals treated with > 60 mg/kg/day.
Gross pathology was observed in the GI tract, hepatobiliary system, and lymphoid tissues, which
correlated with microscopic findings of white blood cell infiltration, vacuolation, and lymphoid
depletion. Urinalysis demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in urine protein that correlated
with microscopic findings of tubular degeneration and membranous glomerulopathy in the
kidneys. Increases in liver enzymes were observed. The thymus also had signs of toxicity that
included reduced organ size and weight with marked lymphoid depletion. Additionally, there
was an increase in the weight of the heart with no microscopic correlates, but an increase in the
mean RR interval in high-dose animals that correlated with a decreased heart rate.
