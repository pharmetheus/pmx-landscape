Additionally, there was one Black/African American subject on placebo who had a monthly
HAE attack rate of 0.18, one Native Hawaiian/Other Pacific Islander subject on garadacimab
who had a monthly HAE attack rate of 0.17, and one subject on garadacimab with race Other
who did not experience any HAE attacks during the Treatment Period.

With regard to ethnicity, there were three subjects who were Hispanic or Latino, one on
garadacimab with a monthly HAE attack rate of 0.17 and two on placebo with monthly HAE
attack rates of 0.17 and 1.27.

Given the small number of subjects who self-identified as non-white in the trial, it is difficult to
draw definitive conclusions regarding efficacy by race/ethnicity; however, the data do not raise
substantial concems regarding a differential treatment effect.

Efficacy by Geographic Location

The primary efficacy endpoint was also evaluated by geographic location (Figure 7). For subjects
at U.S. and ex-U.S. sites, there was a clinically meaningful and statistically persuasive reduction
in the mean time-normalized number of HAE attacks per month relative to placebo. While the
results for the ex-U.S. subgroup aligned more with the overall primary analysis results (Table 10)
than those for the U.S. subgroup, both trended in the same direction favoring garadacimab.
