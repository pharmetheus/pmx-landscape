5.4 Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion/Pharmacokinetics

The Applicant’s Position:

In single-dose PK and oral bioavailability studies in CD-1 mice and cynomolgus monkeys,
imlunestrant oral bioavailability ranged from approximately 8% to 44%. In all species after
single and multiple dose, imlunestrant was slowly absorbed. Following repeated dosing of
imlunestrant in rats and in monkeys, systemic exposure (C,,,, and AUC) generally increased with
increasing dose, but the increase was not consistently dose proportional. In both species,
generally no accumulation and no sex difference in exposure were observed.

Across mouse, rat, dog, and monkey, imlunestrant is highly bound (greater than 99.8%) to
plasma proteins. In mice, imlunestrant was found to cross the blood-brain barrier. Imlunestrant
was widely distributed across tissues in rats.

The metabolism of imlunestrant was primarily mediated by direct phenol glucuronidation and
sulfation of the parent, with minor contribution from oxidation. The major human metabolite
(M1, direct glucuronide conjugate) is present in rat and monkey, with appropriate steady-state
coverage.

The route of excretion of [14C]-imlunestrant-derived radioactivity was determined in rats and
indicated that the major route of elimination was in feces (greater than 50% of administered
dose).

The FDA’s Assessment:

The FDA generally agrees with Applicant’s position. See the clinical pharmacology Section 6 for
information regarding human data and drug interaction studies.
