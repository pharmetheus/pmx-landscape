Methods:
Syringe Stopper and Needle Shield
Four different extraction methods were applied to release extractable compounds from
the syringe stopper ®® and the needle shield:
1. Headspace enrichment over a heated, neat sample preparation.
2. Closed vessel extraction at 90°C for 133 hours with 0.01% polysorbate 80
(PS80) in ultrapure water (UPW).
3. Closed vessel extraction at 90°C for 133 hours with 0.01% PS80 in UPW/IPA
(75/25)
4. Closed vessel extraction at 90°C for 133 hours UPW/IPA (75/25).

The samples were analyzed by:

1. Headspace Gas Chromatography / Mass Spectrometry to determine
volatile organic compounds (VOC)

2. Gas Chromatography/Mass Spectrometry (GC/MS) to determine semi-
volatile organic compounds (SVOC)

3. High Resolution Accurate Mass (HRAM) Ultra Performance Liquid
Chromatography/Mass Spectrometry (UPLC/MS) to determine non-volatile
organic compounds (NVOC; syringe stopper only. Non-volatiles were not
analyzed for the needle shield).

Glass Syringe and Stainless-Steel Needle

The glass syringe and stainless-steel needle were analyzed for elemental impurities in
the leachable studies using accelerated degradation (40°C/75% RH). See below under
Leachable Studies.

This component was not subjected to extractables studies like the needle shield and
syringe stopper because the glass syringe and stainless-steel needle component were
widely used throughout the pharmaceutical industry and were considered by the
Sponsor to be inert for leachables, aside from potential elemental impurities. The lack of
an extractables study using harsh conditions for the glass syringe was discussed with
the CMC team, and they agreed there were no concerns with regarding the extractables
and leachables.
