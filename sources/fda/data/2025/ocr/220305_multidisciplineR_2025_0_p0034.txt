43. Devices and Companion Diagnostic Issues

For the participants in Study KO-MEN-001, local testing was used identify participants with
NPM1 gene mutations for study enrollment. Participants with NPM/ mutations identified as
Type A, B, or D caused by a 4-nucleotide insert in exon 11 that results in NPM1 cytoplasmic
localization were eligible for the study. In addition, participants with other NPM/ mutations that
were deemed reasonably likely to result in cytoplasmic localization were eligible to enroll on the
study. FDA Table 2 below shows the testing methodologies used in Phase 1b and Phase 2 of
KO-MEN-001 and the identified NPM/ mutations. The most commonly identified subtype was
Type A, and the most common testing methodology was next-generation sequencing (NGS).
However, 50% of patients in the Phase 1b portion and 28% of participants in the Phase 2 portion
of KO-MEN-001 had a mutation that was either non-ABD or not reported by the local assay. Of
note, participants were required to have a diagnostic bone marrow or peripheral blood sample
banked centrally for retrospective confirmation once a central assay had been developed; this
testing has not yet been performed.
