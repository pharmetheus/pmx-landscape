Statistical TL’s Comment: The null rate of 12% specified for the Phase 2 Cohort was based
loosely on historical data of a different endpoint (12% CR/CRi as reported in for a population of
patients who mostly had > 2 or more prior therapies). Thus, its relevance to the studied
population is unclear. However, clinical trials are evaluated based on whether they meet their
planned objectives or not. After adjudication, the Phase 2 Cohort did not meet its pre-specified
objective to rule out a CR + CRh rate of 12%. While the initial results excluded this threshold,
the impact of adjudication on the inference raises questions about the robustness of the results.

The 95% confidence interval for CR + CRh in the Pooled Efficacy Cohort excludes 12%. This
provides a more comprehensive experience and appears to confirm the initial inference by the
Applicant. This analysis does not account for the multiple-testing nature of this inference.

A more natural inference framework for such evidence synthesis might be the Bayesian
framework. While the Phase 1b and Phase 2 populations are similar, it is possible that they
differ in meaningful ways such that pooling may not reflect a homogenous population. In this
scenario, the Phase Ib results could be considered in a prior distribution with varying levels of
relevance to the Phase 2 population. The strength of evidence utilizing in a prior distribution is
often characterized by the effective sample size (ESS). FDA Table 33 considers such prior
distributions at varying levels of informativeness, with 100% ESS reflecting the scenario where
the prior distribution reflects n=20 patients worth of data.
