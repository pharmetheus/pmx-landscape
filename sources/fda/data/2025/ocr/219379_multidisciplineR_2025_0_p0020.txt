NDA/IND Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 219379/219389
Mirdametinib (GOMEKLI)

13. Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA)

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) is an autosomal dominant disorder with variable expressivity caused by germline mutations in the NF1 gene.

Plexiform neurofibromas (PNs) are benign tumors of the peripheral nerve sheath which occur in 40-50% of patients with NF1. More than 50% of
NF1-PNs are detected in early childhood, and the fastest growth rate is typically during the first decade of life. PNs may be associated with
significant morbidity including pain, disfigurement, neurologic deficits, and functional impairment (Ly and Blakely, 2019; Dombi et al, 2007). PNs
may also lead to mortality through compression of vital structures, e.g., airway compression and hemothorax (Prada et al, 2012). Malignant
transformation to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors (MPNST) can occur in 8-16% of patients with NF1-PN.

Treatment typically includes surgical resection; however, resection is often not feasible due tumor effect on adjacent tissue, nerves, and
vasculature. The only FDA-approved therapy for NF1-PN is the MEK inhibitor selumetinib, for pediatric patients 2 years of age and older with
NF1 who have symptomatic, inoperable PN. The reported ORR for selumetinib is 44% (95% Cl: 30, 59) according to Response Evaluation in
Neurofibromatosis and Schwannomatosis (REINS) criteria (i.e. PN decrease 220% compared to baseline in consecutive scans within 3-6 months)
by independent central review (Koselugo USPI). Selumetinib is currently only available in a capsule formulation and is therefore not accessible
to pediatric patients who cannot swallow capsules. There are currently no approved systemic therapies for adult patients with NF1-PN.

This NDA for mirdametinib is primarily supported by results from the ReNeu study, a multicenter, single-arm trial investigating mirdametinib in
adult and pediatric patients with NF1 and progressive or symptomatic plexiform neurofibromas not amenable to complete surgical resection.
The primary efficacy population included 114 patients, including 56 pediatric and 58 adult patients with NF1-PN. All patients in the study had
measurable disease and received mirdametinib at a dosage of 2 mg/m? orally twice daily for the first 21 days of each 28-day cycle, for up to 24
cycles in the treatment phase.

The primary endpoint to demonstrate the efficacy of mirdametinib for this population was confirmed ORR defined as PN decrease 220%
compared to baseline in consecutive scans within 2-6 months, using independent central review of MRI volumetric analysis. Because the study
calendar required end-of-study imaging at 3 months (+/- 10 days) from the second to last imaging timepoint, these modified criteria were used
to capture responses that occurred late in the study with less than 3 months between consecutive scans. DOR was a key secondary endpoint.
