NDA/IND Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 219379/219389
Mirdametinib (GOMEKLI)

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s integrated assessment of safety.

The pooled safety data from the ReNeu study and Study NF-106 (Pool 1) and the additional
safety analysis (90-day-update) allowed for an informed risk assessment of mirdametinib in the
intended NF1 PN populations. Among 133 patients who received mirdametinib, 62% were
exposed for one year or longer, 38% were exposed for 2 years or longer, and 12% were exposed
for 3 years or longer, allowing for a reasonable evaluation of risk for cumulative toxicities with
chronic dosing.

In Pool 1, serious adverse events occurred in 16% of adult patients and 14% of pediatric
patients. There was one fatal adverse event due to Covid-19. AEs leading to permanent
discontinuation occurred in 21% of adults and 9% of pediatric patients and AEs leading to dose
reduction occurred in 20% of adult and 12% of pediatric patients. The most common AEs
(>25%) in the adult population included rash, diarrhea, nausea, musculoskeletal pain, vomiting,
and fatigue. The most common AEs (>25%) in the pediatric population included rash, diarrhea,
musculoskeletal pain, abdominal pain, vomiting, fatigue, upper respiratory tract infection,
paronychia, headache, left ventricular dysfunction and nausea. Grade 3-4 AEs occurred in 33%
of adult patients and 40% of pediatric patients. Given the relatively low discontinuation and
reduction rate, it appears that AEs were tolerable and manageable with dose modifications and
supportive care in patients treated with mirdametinib.

Important risks of mirdametinib are similar to those of other MEK inhibitors including ocular,
cardiac and dermatologic toxicities. Some rare, but serious, class related safety signals (e.g.,
rhabdomyolysis) were not identified in the Pool 1 population. RVO and RPED were not observed
in the pediatric population. No new safety signals were identified for mirdametinib compared
to commercially approved MEK inhibitors. The identified toxicities can be managed through
labeling, including relevant warnings, precautions, and dose modification guidelines.

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS

8.3. Statistical Issues
The FDA’s Assessment:

The ReNeu study is an open-label, single arm, multi-center trial which served as the primary
basis for establishing the efficacy of mirdametinib for the treatment of pediatric and adult
patients with NF1-PN. The primary endpoint of the trial was confirmed ORR per BICR using
modified REINS criteria, supported by the secondary endpoint of DOR. Since the ReNeu study is
a single-arm trial, there was no control for multiplicity in any of the hypothesis tests included in
this report. Therefore, the p-values reported by the Applicant should be interpreted with
caution.
