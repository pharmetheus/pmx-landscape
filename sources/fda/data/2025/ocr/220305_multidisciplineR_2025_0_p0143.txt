Given the available PK, efficacy, and safety data, the FDA generally agrees that dose
modifications in patients receiving strong or moderate CYP3A inhibitors appears reasonable.
However, given that there may be increased risk of SAEs, dose interruption, differentiation

syndrome, and QT prolongation in patients receiving strong CYP3A inhibitors, additional safety
monitoring is warranted in these patients.

Additionally, a PMR will be issued to request the Applicant to complete the ongoing Study KO-
MEN-001 Sub-study 2 which evaluates the impact of itraconazole (strong CYP3A and P-gp
inhibitor) on ziftomenib PK (FDA Table 22).

CYP3A Inducers: The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position that concomitant use
of strong and moderate CYP3A inducers should be avoided, as coadministration is expected to
decrease ziftomenib exposure by up to ~50% to 80% /Section 14.4.4].

CYP3A Substrates: The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s assessment that ziftomenib
has weak CYP3A inhibition potential when administered alone /Section 14.4.4].
