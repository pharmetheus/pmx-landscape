Parameters Major Findings

Mortality All dams survived to scheduled necropsy on GD 18.

Clinical Signs There were no ziftomenib-related clinical observations noted at any
dose level during the dosing or postdosing phases.

Body weights Mean maternal body weight was unaffected at all dose levels
evaluated.

Reproductive and At 60 mg/kg/day: decreased mean fetal body weight values (-11.1% to

developmental -12.3% vs control), higher number of dead fetuses (0.9 fetuses/litter)

findings compared to the control group (0.0 fetuses/litter), fetal external

malformation of cleft palate (32 fetuses in 11 litters, confirmed
skeletally as split palatine in the skull [14 fetuses in 6 litters]) and
skeletal malformations of fused cervical arches (33 fetuses in 12
litters) and absent lumbar vertebrae (12 fetuses in 3 litters).
Significantly increased (vs control) mean litter proportions of skeletal
developmental variations (fused and/or misaligned costal cartilage,
short rib, supernumerary site in the suture bone, and/or fused,
unossified, and/or incompletely ossified sternebrae) were considered
secondary to the decreased fetal body weight values noted at 60
mng/kg/day.

The maternal NOAEL for ziftomenib was established at 60 mg/kg/day.
Based on decreased mean fetal body weight values and adverse fetal
external and skeletal malformations noted at 60 mg/kg/day, the
NOAEL for developmental toxicity was considered to be 20

mng/kg/day.

Toxicokinetics Systemic exposure (Cmax and AUCO-24h) to ziftomenib, KO-516, and
KO-739 increased with increasing ziftomenib dose in a generally dose-
proportional manner across the dose range on GDs 6 and 15. There
was no evidence of meaningful accumulation of any analyte in plasma
