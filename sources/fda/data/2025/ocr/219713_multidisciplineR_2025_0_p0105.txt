{IBTROZI™, Taletrectinib}

e Race and ethnicity: The patients who received taletrectinib in
the pivotal studies C203 and G208 included Asian (229/273,
84%), White (28/273, 10%) and others (16/273, 6%). Per
population PK analysis, a 30% increase in taletrectinib
exposure (AUC) was observed in Asian patients compared to
White patients, which is associated with a higher incidence of
increased liver enzymes and subsequent a higher proportion of
dose reduction in Asian patients. No clinically significant
differences in PK were observed between White and Black
patients.

e ROS] fusion partner and ROS/ secondary TKI resistance
mutations: data suggest that taletrectinib has clinical activity in
ROS1 TKI-naive and ROS1 TKI-pretreated patients with
tumors carrying ROS/ fusions with various partners. Limited
data suggest that taletrectinib has clinical activity in TKI-
pretreated patients with tumors carrying the G2032R TKI
resistance mutation in ROS/.

CYP3A is the primary CYP450 enzyme involved in taletrectinib
metabolism. Taletrectinib Cmax increased by 1.8-fold and AUC
by 3.3-fold following concomitant administration of itraconazole
(P-gp and strong CYP3A inhibitor) 200 mg daily. Taletrectinib
Cmax decreased by 42% and AUC by 86% following
concomitant administration of rifampin (P-gp and strong CYP3A
inducer) 600 mg daily.

The PBPK approach was used to simulate the effects of moderate
CYP3A4 inhibitors and inducers on the PK of taletrectinib and
DDI predicted that a moderate CYP3A inhibitor increases taletrectinib
AUC up to 2.6-fold and Cmax up to 1.5-fold, and concomitant
use with a moderate CYP3A inducer is predicted to reduce
taletrectinib AUC by 66% and its Cmax by 40%.

Taletrectinib displayed pH-dependent aqueous solubility.
Taletrectinib Cmax decreased by 65% and AUC by 40%
following concomitant administration of omeprazole (a PPI) 40
mg daily.
