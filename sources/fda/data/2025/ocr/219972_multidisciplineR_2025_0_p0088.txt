In the GLP-compliant 4-week repeat-dose toxicity study in rats (reviewed under IND 155685),
sevabertinib was orally administered at 5, 10, or 15 mg/kg once daily. All females in the 15
mg/kg group were euthanized prematurely due to significant weight loss, abnormal feces
(liquid), and poor general condition (e.g., piloerection, reduced motility, and high-stepping gait).
In animals that survived to scheduled necropsy, treatment with 15 mg/kg resulted in <14%
decreases in mean body weight and a 38% decrease in mean food consumption compared to
controls. Similar to the 13-week toxicity study, skin was a target organ with histologic findings
of up to moderate atrophy, slight erosion/ulcer, and minimal hyperplasia. Additionally, histologic
findings of up to slight lacrimal gland atrophy and minimal optic nerve hemorrhage were
observed in both sexes at doses >10 mg/kg. Sevabertinib induced extensive histologic findings in
the GI tract (small and large intestines, stomach, esophagus) of up to moderate
degeneration/atrophy and erosion/ulcer, and up to slight inflammation. Additional target organs
included the pancreas (acinar atrophy), salivary gland, thyroid gland (atrophy, colloid alteration,
hemorrhage, hypertrophy), lung (increased alveolar macrophages, hypertrophy, metaplasia),
thymus (increased tangible body macrophages), kidney (tubule atrophy, dilatation, hyperplasia,
inflammation), tongue (atrophy), liver (periportal cytoplasmic alteration, glycogen depletion,
periportal fatty change), cervix (degeneration/vacuolation), epididymis (sperm granuloma), and
mammary gland (vacuolation).
