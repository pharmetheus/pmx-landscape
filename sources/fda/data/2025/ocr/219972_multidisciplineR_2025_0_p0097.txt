NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — NDA 219972

Sevabertinib (BAY 2927088) —

Version 2.0

Methods

Dose and frequency of dosing:

0 (vehicle control), 1.5, 3, or 6 mg/kg daily from GD6 to GD17

Route of administration:

Oral gavage

Formulation/Vehicle: Ethanol/Kolliphor HS15®/Water for Injection (10/40/50 v/v/v) adjusted to pH 4
Species/Strain: Rat, Wistar

Number/Sex/Group: 20 animals/group

Age: 9 weeks

Satellite groups: -

Parameters Major findings

Mortality No sevabertinib-related mortalities.

Clinical Signs No sevabertinib-related clinical observations were noted.

Body Weight Sevabertinib -related reduced body weight gain was noted from GD 6

Feed Consumption

Gross Pathology/Cesarian section

following administration of 3 or 6 mg/kg/day, and from GD 9 following
administration of 1.5 mg/kg/day, compared with controls.

Reduced food consumption was noted throughout the dosing period following
administration of 1.5, 3, or 6 mg/kg/day, with statistically significant
reductions noted from GD 6 to 9 and from GD 9 to 12, and from GD 15
through 18, following administration of 6 mg/kg/day compared with controls.

A slight reduction in total and corrected maternal weight change was noted
following administration of 1.5, 3, or 6 mg/kg/day, compared with controls,
when corrected for the gravid uterus weight. Values did not attain statistical
significance; however, the reduced total and corrected maternal weight
change reflected the body weight changes observed inlife. Reduced mean
fetal weights (combined and individual sexes) were noted for sevabertinib-
treated groups, with statistically significant reductions noted following
administration of 6 mg/kg/day. Mean combined and mean male fetal weight
values were within the Historical Control Data (HCD) range, while mean
female fetal weight values were marginally below the HCD range. Upon
review of the individual litters, multiple litters maternally exposed to 6
mg/kg/day had combined fetal weights below the HCD range. These
changes were considered sevabertinib-related and reflected the individual
body weight changes observed in life.

No fetal malformations were observed at any dose level, and no adverse
sevabertinib-related fetal variations were observed. Increased incidences of
the variation, incomplete ossification of the mandible, were noted following
administration of 1.5, 3, or 6 mg/kg/day, compared with controls. A dose
relationship was observed, and although the percentage litter and fetal
incidence values were outside the HCD range, no adverse effects on the
developing fetus were observed. Therefore, these findings were considered
sevabertinib-related but not adverse.
