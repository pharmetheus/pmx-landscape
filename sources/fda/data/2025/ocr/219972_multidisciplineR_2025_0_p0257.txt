The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position. FDA conducted an analysis of TEAEs by
demographic subgroup in the primary and pooled safety populations. Within these populations,
there were no clear differences in the safety profile based on age, race, or sex. In the primary
safety population, however, there were very few Black or African American patients; 3.7 and
2.2% of patients in the combined Group D + E and the pooled 20 mg population were Black or
African American. Because of this, there is insufficient data to determine whether any
differences in the safety profile exist for this population. Overall, no clear AE trends were
observed among the different populations based on prior treatment, although data is limited by
small cohort numbers and differences in time of exposure to sevabertinib.

FDA also conducted an independent analysis of TEAEs by age. In the combined Group D + E
population, 40% of patients were 65 years of age and older and 11% were 75 years of age and
older. Of the patients aged 65 years and older, 44% experienced Grade 3 or 4 TEAEs and 33%
experienced SAEs. Of the patients less than 65 years of age, 41% experienced Grade 3 or 4
TEAEs and 29% experienced SAEs. Of the 268 patients in the pooled 20 mg population, 43%
were 65 years of age and older and 13% were 75 years and older. The safety results in the pooled
population subgroups by age were similar, though of note, Grade 3 diarrhea was observed in
23% of patients age >75 years and 14% of patients <75 years old. Analysis is limited by the
small number or patients 65 years of age and older; however, there is no suggestion of an overall
difference in safety by age based on the data available.
