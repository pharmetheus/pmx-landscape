Patients with NPMJ-m AML who relapse have a dismal prognosis, with demonstrable worsening
of survival outcomes with each subsequent line of therapy, as low as 3.5 months (Issa et al,
2023). Based on a retrospective analysis of 206 NPM/J-m AML patients, median RFS was as low
as 3.3 months in the R/R setting. While NPM1-m AML may be associated with a more favorable
prognosis compared with other genetic subgroups in the newly diagnosed setting, there was no
significant difference in RFS or OS among R/R patients with NPM/J-m AML compared with
NPM1 wt (Issa et al, 2023), demonstrating that any positive prognostic value associated with
NPM1-m at diagnosis is lost in the R/R setting. In the Phase 2 mITT population with a median of
2 prior therapies at baseline, the median OS of 6.6 months was longer than that of patients who
received multiagent approaches. Although KO-MEN-001 is a single arm trial, the OS outcomes
suggest a prolonged survival benefit in patients receiving ziftomenib monotherapy.

The secondary efficacy results were generally consistent between the Phase 2 mITT primary
efficacy analysis and the supportive Phase 1b EAS population, providing further confirmatory
evidence of the Phase 2 results. Ziftomenib 600 mg QD monotherapy provides temporary disease
control as a palliative treatment in the proposed indicated patient population, demonstrating
relief from disease burden.

The FDA’s Assessment:

Results of DOR are summarized in FDA Table 35 for the 24 participants from the Pooled
Efficacy Cohort who achieved a best response of CR or CRh. Due to the small number of
participants achieving a best response of CRh (i.e., 1 in Phase 1b and 4 in Phase 2), observed
DoCRh is reported in this summary table and in the USPI. Similarly, given the low CR + CRh
rate overall, the estimated Do(CR + CRh) and DoCR have large variation and should be
interpreted with caution.
