The 3 participants in the pediatric cohort with major CSR -reportable protocol deviations
excluded from the per-protocol set were not expected to impact the data integrity for the
analysis. Two additional participants in the pediatric cohort were excluded from the per-
protocol set due to not having at least 1 post-baseline disease evaluation. The ORR for the per-
protocol set supports the results from the primary analysis for the pediatric cohort (ORR from
per-protocol set: 56.9% [95% Cl: 42.2 to 70.7] vs ORR from primary analysis: 51.8% [38.0 to
65.3]). These data demonstrate that the lower limit of the 95% Cl excludes the null hypothesis
of ORR = 20%.

The 6 participants in the adult cohort with major CSR -reportable protocol deviations excluded
from the per-protocol set were not expected to impact the data integrity for the analysis. Eight
additional participants in the adult cohort were excluded from the per-protocol set due to not
having at least 1 post-baseline disease evaluation. The ORR for the per-protocol set supports
the results from the primary analysis for the adult cohorts (ORR from per-protocol set: 46.8%
[32.1 to 61.9] vs ORR from primary analysis: 41.4% [28.6 to 55.1]. These data demonstrate that
the lower limit of the 95% Cl excludes the null hypothesis of ORR = 23%.

FDA generally agrees with the description of protocol violations and deviations and that they
