6.3.2. Clinical Pharmacology Questions

6.3.2.1 Does the clinical pharmacology program provide supportive evidence of
effectiveness?

The Applicant’s Position:

Yes, the clinical pharmacology program provides supportive evidence of effectiveness for
ziftomenib, particularly at the 600 mg QD dose. The totality of clinical data and modeling and
simulation described in Section 6.2.1 supports the effectiveness of the proposed dosing regimen.
E-R analysis showed a lack of clinically significant relationship between exposure and efficacy,
thus demonstrating that exposures achieved were adequate for robust efficacy.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The 600 mg dosage is supported primarily by the efficacy data in the dose expansion phase of
KO-MEN-001 (Phase 1 Part 1b), which demonstrated improved activity at 600 mg versus 200
mg (CR + CRh 25% vs 0%). Although there was no significant E-R efficacy relationship, this is

likely an artifact of the limited sample size at dose levels other than 600 mg and low CR + CRh
overall.

Overall CR + CRh was 21% in NPMJ-m R/R AML (n=112, Phase 1b EAS + Phase 2).

D-R Analysis: In Phase 1 Part 1a (all comers), there was no clear D-R relationship for CR + CRh
(0% at 50 mg [n=1], 100% at 100 mg [n=1], 17% at 200 mg [n=6], and 0% at 400 to 1000 mg
[n=1 to 11 per cohort]).
