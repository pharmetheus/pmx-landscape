12.1.2 Assessment of Alternative Assays to Characterize the Safety of Tradipitant
Liver Models of Toxicity

Liver- and thyroid-related findings were consistently observed in nonclinical species
treated with tradipitant. These findings (i.e., increased organ weights, moderate and
reversible increases in liver enzymes, and/or minimal to mild hepatocyte and thyroid
follicular cell hypertrophy/hyperplasia) were generally considered by the Applicant to be
adaptive changes indicative of increased expression of metabolizing enzymes; the
Division considers this explanation reasonable. However, foci of inflammatory cells,
together with minimal to slight hepatocyte necrosis, were observed in 6/10 mid dose
males, 9/10 high dose males, and 3/10 high dose females in the 13-week study in Han
Wistar rats (clinical effect margins of 8.5 in males and 85 in females, respectively,
relative to the maximum clinical dose of 170 mg/day, based on HED); these findings
were considered adverse rather than adaptive. Such findings were not observed in
studies in Fisher rats of up to 6 months duration or in studies in dogs of up to 3 months
duration, and the potential explanation for the species and strain differences is
unknown.

The Applicant conducted five safety pharmacology studies using alternative in vitro
assays with the objective of further investigating and/or quantifying the in vitro
hepatotoxic potential of tradipitant and to evaluate its underlying toxicity mechanisms.
