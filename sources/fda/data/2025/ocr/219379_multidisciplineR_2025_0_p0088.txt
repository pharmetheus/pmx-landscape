NDA/IND Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 219379/219389
Mirdametinib (GOMEKLI)

In the definitive male and female rat fertility and early embryonic development study,
mirdametinib did not have any effects on male reproductive parameters, female reproductive
parameters, or embryonic development at any dose level. However, mirdametinib induced EFD
toxicities (early resorptions, post-implantation loss, dose-dependent decreased fetal weights) in
both rat and rabbit dose-range finding preliminary studies. Because of these EFD effects, no
additional developmental toxicity studies were conducted per agreement with the FDA (email,
24-Feb-2023). Of note, EFD toxicity is considered a class effect of MEK inhibitors.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of the findings of the GLP-compliant fertility study
in male and female rats and dose range finding embryo-fetal development (EFD) toxicity studies
in rats and rabbits. The FDA previously reviewed the EFD studies and concluded that
mirdametinib showed clear evidence of embryo-fetal toxicity. Based on the positive results of
the dose range finding studies and mechanism of action, the FDA agreed that further GLP-
compliant reproductive toxicology studies were not warranted.

In the EFD study in rats, actual doses administered were 0.25, 0.6, 3, and 5.0 mg/kg based on
formulation analysis for the 0.3, 1, 3 and 6 mg/kg groups. One rat at 0.6 mg/kg (1 mg/kg) was
found dead on GD21 and necropsy results indicated 15 late resorptions and no live fetuses; the
cause of death was considered due to fetal necrosis.

While we agree that mirdametinib did not affect mating performance or fertility under the
conditions tested in the dedicated fertility study, FDA notes that repeat-dose toxicology studies
with mirdametinib had reproductive organ findings that may impair female fertility. In a 3-
month repeat-dose toxicology study in rats, mirdametinib caused follicular cysts associated
with decreases in the number of corpora lutea at doses 20.3 mg/kg/day (22-times the human
exposure at the clinical dose of 2 mg/m? twice daily based on AUC) that correlated with
decreased organ weight. Male reproductive organ findings in rats at 1 mg/kg (8-times the
human exposure at the clinical dose of 2 mg/m? twice daily based on AUC) included
inflammation of the prostate in individual males and marked hypoplasia of the spermatogenic
epithelium in the testis and decreased content in the epididymis in one male. In a 1-month
repeat-dose toxicology study in mice, mirdametinib caused testicular degeneration and/or
necrosis at doses 210 mg/kg/day (223-times the human exposure at the clinical dose of 2
mg/m? twice daily based on AUC). The reversibility of effects on ovary and testes were not
assessed. Given differences in incidence and severity of male reproductive organ findings
between control and mirdametininb-treated groups and literature supporting a role for MEK in
spermatogenesis [Hasegawa (2013)], the possibility that findings in male reproductive organs in
animals were related to administration of mirdametinib could not be eliminated; however, the
clinical impact on male fertility is unclear given the low incidence of findings.
