NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation

NDA 214759
Grafapex (treosulfan)

Bioanalysis

In Study MCFludT.14/L Trial | PK Sub-study, treosulfan in human plasma and urine was
measured using an HPLC assay. Accuracy (%RE) and precision (%CV) of the quality
controls (QCs) for the runs used in measuring treosulfan plasma and urine
concentrations were <15%.

In Studies MC-FludT.16/NM and MC-FludT.17/M, treosulfan and monoepoxide in
human plasma were measured using an LC-MS/MS assay. Accuracy (RE) and
precision (%CV) of the QCs for the runs used in measuring treosulfan and
monoepoxide plasma concentrations were <15%.

Accumulation

There was no dose accumulation with once daily administration of treosulfan over 3
consecutive days.

Active Moieties

Treosulfan is converted into an active monoepoxide intermediate [(2S,3S)-1,2-
epoxybutane-3,4-diol-4-methanesulfonate] and finally to L-diepoxybutane [(2S,3S)-
1,2:3,4-diepoxybutane].

QT Prolongation

The effects of treosulfan on the QT interval in humans have not been evaluated. In
vitro, treosulfan does not significantly inhibit hERG at a concentration of 1,000 uM.

Distribution

Volume of Distribution

The volume of distribution in adult patients is 41.1 liters (CV%: 19.2%). The
treosulfan volume of distribution increases with BSA. In animal studies, treosulfan
penetration through the blood brain-barrier is limited [see Section 5.4].

Plasma Protein Binding}

Treosulfan does not bind to plasma albumin.

Elimination

Terminal Elimination
Half-Life (SD)

The median terminal half-life of treosulfan is 2.2 hours in adults and 1.5 hours in
pediatric patients.

In pediatric patients, the median terminal half-life of the active monoepoxide
intermediate is 1.6 hrs. No data are available on the PK of the monoepoxide
intermediate in adults.

Metabolism

Primary Metabolic
Pathway(s)

Treosulfan converts to mono- and di-epoxide metabolites in a temperature- and pH-
dependent manner in © human plasma. No studies have been
conducted to evaluate treosulfan or its active metabolites as substrates of major
metabolizing enzymes.

In pediatric patients (age range 4 months to 17 years), there was no relationship
between age and the monoepoxide:parent AUCin ratio, suggesting no significant
age-related differences in metabolism of treosulfan among pediatric patients. No
monoepoxide PK data were available from adult patients; additional data will be
needed to evaluate treosulfan and metabolite PK in adults.
