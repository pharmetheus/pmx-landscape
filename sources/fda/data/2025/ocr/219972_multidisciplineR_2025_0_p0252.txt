the specific details described above, that corneal changes including microcysts have been
observed in anti-EGFR or anti-HER2 directed therapy, and given the nonclinical findings in
toxicology studies of sevabertinib. In light of the potential impact of ocular toxicity on patient
quality of life, the need for provider awareness for prompt dose modification and ophthalmologic
evaluation, FDA considered a warning for ocular toxicity in the USPI for sevabertinib as
warranted.

Cardiac toxicity

As described in the dedicated QT section, FDA agrees that evidence does not suggest a large
effect of sevabertinib on QTc prolongation. FDA evaluated the adverse event dataset for terms
associated with reduced ejection fraction or heart failure, and did not find evidence for reduction
in ejection fraction. However, the FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s statement that there were
no clinically significant findings with respect to arrhythmias among the pooled safety population.
In the pooled population, 7% of patients had an arrhythmia (GT), including 1.5% Grade 3 — 4
events. Among the Grade 3-4 TEAEs, terms included QT prolonged (0.7%), arrhythmia (0.4%),
atrioventricular block (AV block) complete (0.7%), and sinus tachycardia (0.4%).

FDA reviewed narratives of events of arrhythmia. All cases were confounded by at least one
factor contributing to arrhythmia. The case of AV block occurred in a patient with prior history
of mediastinal irradiation for Hodgkin lymphoma. Other cases were confounded by concomitant
medication use (e.g., loperamide) and diarrhea with resulting electrolyte derangement. Notably
the label for loperamide carries a black box warning for Torsades de Pointes and sudden death
with higher than recommended doses of loperamide; cases of syncope and ventricular
tachycardia have also been reported in adult patients receiving the recommended dosage of
loperamide.
