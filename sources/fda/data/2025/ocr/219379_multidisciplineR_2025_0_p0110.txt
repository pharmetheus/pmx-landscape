Perpetrator DDI: No clinical DDI studies have been performed. In vitro studies suggest a low
potential to inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4 at the
highest anticipated clinical dose (4 mg BID). There is also low induction potential for CYP1A2,
CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19. Mirdametinib induced CYP3A4 mRNA expression with
apparent Emax and apparent EC50 values of “> 3.17 to “> 5.74 (fold) and “> 10uM. The
calculated R3 value of 0.98 does not indicate that mirdametinib has induction potential in vivo.
Mirdametinib also showed no detectable or limited (< 50%) inhibition of UGT1A1, UGT1A3,
UGT1A4, UGT1A6, UGT1A9, UGT2B7, and UGT2B15.

Taken together, mirdametinib is unlikely to result in CYP- or transporter-mediated DDIs at
clinically relevant exposures.

The Applicant's Position:
Based on in vitro and clinical studies, management strategies for food, gastric PH, and DDIs are

not required.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. A PMC will be issued to evaluate the effect of
concomitant a strong CYP3A4 inducer (that also co-induce UGTs, P-gp, and CES enzymes) on
mirdametinib PK.
