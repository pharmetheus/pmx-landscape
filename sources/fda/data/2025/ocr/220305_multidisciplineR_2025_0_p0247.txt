8.3.5. Analysis of Submission-Specific Safety Issues

Differentiation Syndrome

The FDA screened for potential DS cases by algorithm" in Study KO-MEN-001, including
review of the investigator-reported DS cases, and reviewed the narratives and other clinical data
as provided by the Applicant in initially supplied narratives. The algorithm flagged 98 events in
80 study participants for review. These events were categorized as unlikely DS, possible DS, or
DS; cases of possible DS were flagged for the Applicant to submit further explanation or a
narrative if one was not previously required. Based on this adjudication process, FDA identified
57 cases of DS in 43 study participants, including 29 subjects within the key safety population of
NPMI1-m AML treated at 600 mg QD. It is important to note that DS was an important safety
issue identified early in the ziftomenib development program which led to a clear focus on
identifying cases of DS as they occurred in patients so that they were appropriately managed.
This focus included continuous use of algorithmic screening of the safety database. The FDA
review only identified 3 additional cases of DS that had not been previously noted. Two new
cases of DS were noted above in the review of fatal ARs (subject © and subject °°
along with the up-grading of fatal ARs for 3 subjects (subject © subject ” and subject
© that had previously been considered lower grade events of DS.

FDA Table 50 below shows a summary tabulation of the FDA-adjudicated events by genetic
marker. There is a differential rate of DS observed when comparing participants with KM724-r
and NPMJ-m AML. This was observed early in the KO-MEN-001 study. The overall rate of DS
was 29/120 (24%) in all patients with NPMJ-m AML and 29/112 (26%) in the key safety
population. This is in comparison to the DS rates that were observed early in the KO-MEN-001
study of patients with KM724-1 AML that showed overall DS rates of 15/36 (42%). Moreover,
the severity of DS was worse in the population of patients with KM724-1 AML, who had 11/36
(31%) versus those with NPMJ-m AML with 17/120 (14%) grade 3-5 events. Notably, grade 5
events were more common in the KM724-r population (4/36 (11%)) versus in the NPMJ-m
population overall (2/120 (2%)) or in the key NPMJ-m safety population (2/112 (2%)).
