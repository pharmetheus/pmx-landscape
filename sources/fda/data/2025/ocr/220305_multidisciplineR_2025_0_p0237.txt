daily with initial clinical improvement. However, despite continued dexamethasone therapy, the
patient subsequently developed severe bone pain, rising uric acid, and DIC, prompting
ziftomenib interruption and escalation to higher dexamethasone doses (40 mg once, then 30 mg
daily). The patient's condition continued to deteriorate with worsening DIC and tumor lysis
syndrome (TLS) that was unresponsive to increased steroids, leading the investigator to conclude
this represented worsening AML rather than DS, ultimately requiring treatment with increased
hydroxyurea and IV cytarabine for progressive leukemia. However, contribution cannot be ruled
out, particularly given rapid WBC rise after dexamethasone dose reduction. Therefore,
conservative approach recommended that this case count as a DS-related death given uncertainty
and multiple organ involvement.
