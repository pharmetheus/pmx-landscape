NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — NDA 219972
Sevabertinib (BAY 2927088) — Version 2.0

Clinical data are available on the PK of sevabertinib in subjects with mild hepatic impairment.
Results from the population PK indicated no differences in exposure between participants with
norinal hepatic function and participants with mild hepatic impairment. An assessment of the
effect of moderate hepatic impairment on the PK of sevabertinib is planned.

Following oral administration to humans, sevabertinib is eliminated primarily by metabolism,
mainly by CYP3A4-mediated biotransformation. Urinary excretion of unchanged sevabertinib
was only 1.34% of the dose and is thus negligible. Clinical data are available in participants with
mild and moderate renal impairment. The population PK indicated no difference in exposure in
participants with mild or moderate renal impairment and in participants with normal renal
function. A clinical study in subjects with severe renal impairment is therefore not considered
necessary.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA acknowledges the Applicant’s plan to conduct a hepatic impairment study and a PMR will
be issued accordingly. In addition, a PMR for DDI evaluation will also be issued. See detailed
PMRs below:
e Conduct a clinical hepatic impairment trial to assess the magnitude of change in exposure
of sevabertinib and to determine appropriate dosing recommendations of sevabertinib in
patients with moderate hepatic impairment.

e Conduct a clinical drug interaction trial to assess the effect of sevabertinib on the
pharmacokinetics of MATE1/2-K substrates and to inform an appropriate drug
interaction management strategy for coadministration with sevabertinib with these
substrates.

Data:

Drug product: The to-be-marketed product is provided as a film-coated, immediate release,
round, red brown tablet of 6 mm in diameter, containing 10 mg of sevabertinib. Sevabertinib was
initially developed as a 20 mg tablet, which was used in the FiH Study 21607. This 20 mg tablet
is no longer manufactured or used. The dose strength of 10 mg was additionally included in
Study 21607 and was used for subsequent studies during clinical development. In addition, a
solution for oral use, with 5 mg sevabertinib per 1 mL (also called LSF in this document), was
used in the Dose Escalation and Backfill parts of Study 21607. An exploratory relative
bioavailability assessment in Study 21607, comparing the 20 mg tablet formulation (test) with
the LSF (reference), showed that the LSM ratio [90% CTI] for Cmax and AUC(0-24) was 2.2699
