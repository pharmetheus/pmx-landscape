NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — NDA 219972
Sevabertinib (BAY 2927088) — Version 2.0

An overview of general pharmacology and PK characteristics is provided in Section 6.3.1.

The Applicant’s Position:

The pharmacology and clinical PK of sevabertinib have been extensively studied both in vitro
and in vivo. In vitro studies using human biomaterials focused on aspects like protein binding,
blood cell/plasma partitioning, metabolism in human hepatocytes, and potential DDIs. Clinical
data comprise of PK properties from 6 clinical studies which included participants with advanced
NSCLC harboring HER? or EGFR mutations as well as healthy participants.

The clinical program started with the FiH dose escalation/expansion Study 21607 in participants
with advanced NSCLC harboring HER2 or EGFR mutations. Clinical studies conducted in
healthy participants included the mass balance Study 22249, the DDI Studies 22250 (DDI with
itraconazole and carbamazepine), 22251 (DDI with midazolam), and 22252 (DDI with
dabigatran etexilate and rosuvastatin), as well as Study 22253 which investigated the effect of
food and an acid reducing agent on sevabertinib PK.

Additional analyses were conducted to evaluate population PK, exposure-response correlations,
and the effect of sevabertinib concentration on QTcF.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position on the pharmacology and clinical PK of
sevabertinib. Refer to Sections 6.3.1, 6.3.2, and 19.4.3 for additional details.

6.2.2. General Dosing and Therapeutic Individualization
6.2.2.1. General Dosing

Data:

The proposed dose for sevabertinib is 20 mg BID, equal to a total daily dose of 40 mg.

The maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of sevabertinib was determined to be 60 mg TDD in the
FiH Study 21607, and 20 mg BID was selected as the RP2D. Data from this study demonstrated
that treatment with sevabertinib 20 mg BID resulted in rapid, deep, and durable responses in
participants with advanced NSCLC harboring HER2 activating mutations, who had received
prior systemic therapy, but are naive to HER2 ex20ins-targeted therapy, as assessed by ORR by
BICR (see Section 8.1.2). In addition, sevabertinib was generally well tolerated up to a 40 mg
TDD, with the most common drug-related TEAEs being low grade and manageable (see
Section 8.2.3). Out of 268 participants treated with 20 mg BID (Whole Study 20 mg BID), 6
(2.2%) discontinued treatment due to a TEAE, 73 (27.2%) experienced TEAEs leading to a dose
reduction, and 113 (42.2%) had TEAEs leading to dose interruption/delay.
