NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation
NDA 214759
Grafapex (treosulfan)

5.3. Pharmacology
Primary Pharmacology

In vitro pharmacology

Treosulfan exhibited antileukemic activity in pediatric tumor cells and cell lines. Munkelt et al.
(2008) employed flow cytometry to quantify the cytotoxic effect of 72-hour exposure to
treosulfan on primary pediatric leukemic cell specimens (bone marrow aspirates, pleural
effusions, and/ or peripheral blood) obtained from 20 children at the time of diagnosis, during
therapy, or at relapse. Under the conditions tested, treosulfan showed greater activity than
busulfan in all specimens except for 1 cALL relapse specimen (Table 2). Treosulfan
demonstrated the greatest activity toward stem cells (CD34+) isolated from a healthy donor
(ICso = <0.4 M).

In vivo pharmacology

Treosulfan had myeloablative properties in vivo. The Applicant assessed the ability of treosulfan
to condition mice for allogeneic bone marrow transplant (Expt. 08). Investigators treated
C57BL/6J adult male mice (5/group) with a 4-day conditioning regimen of either 4 daily doses of
25 mg/kg Busulfex or 3 daily doses of 2000 mg/kg treosulfan administered i.p., followed by
transplantation with 10x10®° bone marrow cells from B10.A donors. All Busulfex-treated mice
survived the 16-week post-transplant period with little demonstration of toxicity. In contrast,
treosulfan-treated mice experienced weight loss and diarrhea, and 1 mouse died. Donor
chimerism (anti-H-2D? staining) on myeloid and lymphoid cells showed no donor cells in
Busulfex-treated mice. Two treosulfan-treated mice exhibited 85-100% donor chimerism; mice
had grossly inflamed small intestines upon necropsy and may have developed GVHD (Figure 1).
