8.2.2. Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness

The Applicant’s Position:

Clinical efficacy was assessed using data and analysis from Study KO-MEN-001, there were no
additional trials for the assessment of clinical efficacy that were integrated. The primary efficacy
endpoint for Phase 2 was the CR/CRh rate. CR/CRh rate is an accepted binary efficacy endpoint
commonly used to demonstrate direct clinical benefit to support full approval of treatments for
AML in the palliative setting, per FDA guidance for industry (Acute Myeloid Leukemia:
Developing Drugs and Biological Products for Treatment; 2022). This approach, with
demonstration of durability and transfusion independence, was used previously with FDA-
approved targeted inhibitors in R/R acute leukemia (eg, revumenib, olutasidenib, ivosidenib,
enasidenib, gilteritinib).

The KO-MEN-001 Phase 2 cohort met the primary endpoint with statistical significance,
demonstrating a CR/CRh rate of 21.7% with a narrow CI (95% CI: 13.8, 31.6; p=0.0058). The
CR/CRh rate compared favorably to the conservative historical control of 12%. The median time
for patients to achieve CR/CRh was 2.8 months (95% CI: 1.0, 15.0) after treatment initiation,
which is consistent with the mechanism of action of a differentiating agent. Responses to
treatment were durable with a median CR/CRh duration of response of 3.7 months (95% CT: 1.0,
15.0 months). These results suggest a median period of disease control for CR/CRh exceeding 6
months. The restricted mean DOR for CR/CRh when truncated at the time when at least 10% of
patients remained at risk was 4.7 months (95% CI: 2.9, 6.5), validating the median DOR
estimate. The probability of having a CR/CRh response with a duration of 6 months was 33.5%
(95% CI: 11.2, 57.8). Furthermore, two-thirds of responders achieved CR (14.1%), which
