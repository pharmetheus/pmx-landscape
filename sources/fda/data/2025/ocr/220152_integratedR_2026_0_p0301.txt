As suggested by Eli Lilly, a WoE assessment (i.e., an integrated assessment of all
available nonclinical and clinical data) may support the conclusion that the unqualified
metabolites are expected to have similar toxicological profiles to tradipitant. Specifically,
it was suggested that the structural changes to these metabolites compared to
tradipitant are not toxicologically significant and would not result in potential differences
in toxicity profiles that have not been characterized by the completed studies, despite
the fact that the metabolites themselves were not present at adequate levels in animals.
If indeed the WoE supports the toxicological equivalence of tradipitant and its
metabolites, the available data may allow the review team to qualify the safety of the
metabolites based on total drug-related exposure in animals compared to humans (i.e.,
exposure to all measured tradipitant-related compounds) instead of on an individual
basis.

The review team notes that the WoE in 2006 was inadequate to support the conclusion
that the unqualified metabolites were expected to have toxicological profiles similar to
tradipitant. Additional studies conducted in recent years by the current Applicant are
pivotal to considering a WoE approach and included primary pharmacology (binding
and/or functional activity), secondary pharmacology, liver safety pharmacology, in vitro
distribution (protein binding), and in vitro genotoxicity studies with the metabolites, as
well as population PK modeling supporting the relevance of healthy volunteer PK
parameters for the motion sickness population and in silico analyses using various tools
and databases.

It is not expected that the unique use of this WoE approach will set a general precedent
for the qualification of metabolites, as each situation must be evaluated on its own
merits based on the totality of data and information. Although in silico analyses are
highly valuable in certain situations, they are unlikely to routinely address safety
concerns in lieu of typical in vivo toxicity studies.
