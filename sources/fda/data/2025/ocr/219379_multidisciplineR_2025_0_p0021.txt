The primary safety population for this NDA comprises a pooled population of 133 patients (58 pediatric and 75 adult) treated with
mirdametinib at the recommended dosage on the ReNeu study or Study NF-106. The most common adverse events in the ReNeu study in adult
patients (>40%) were rash, diarrhea, nausea and musculoskeletal pain, and the most common adverse events in pediatric patients (>40%) were
rash, diarrhea, and musculoskeletal pain. Serious adverse reactions in the ReNeu study occurred in 17% of adult patients; serious adverse
reactions occurring in 21% of adult patients were COVID-19 (3.4%), nephrolithiasis (3.4%), acute kidney injury (1.7%), abdominal pain (1.7%),
ischemic colitis (1.7%), urinary tract infection (1.7%), retinal vein occlusion (1.7%), scoliosis (1.7%), squamous cell carcinoma of skin (1.7%),
cerebrovascular accident (1.7%) and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (1.7%). Serious adverse reactions in the ReNeu study occurred in
14% of pediatric patients; serious adverse reactions occurring in 21% of patients included viral gastrointestinal infection (3.6%), diplopia (1.8%),
musculoskeletal pain (1.8%), seizure (1.8%), fall (1.8%), femoral neck fracture (1.8%), dehydration (1.8%), and hypertension (1.8%). One fatal
adverse reaction occurred in an adult patient (1.7%) due to COVID-19. No additional safety signals were identified in the pooled population. The
safety profile of mirdametinib is consistent with that of other MEK inhibitors, and important safety concerns include ocular toxicity, left
ventricular dysfunction, dermatologic adverse reactions, and embryo-fetal toxicity. These safety concerns will be addressed in the Warnings
and Precautions section of the USPI. The safety profile for mirdametinib is considered acceptable when assessed in the context of a serious and
life-threatening disease and with appropriate monitoring as described in the product label. Postmarketing requirements (PMRs) will be issued
for an integrated safety analysis of data to further characterize the long-term incidence, severity, and outcomes of known serious risks
including ocular toxicity, left ventricular dysfunction, and dermatologic adverse reactions as well as to further characterize the potential serious
risk of long-term adverse effects of mirdametinib on growth and development in pediatric patients. PMRs are described in further detail in
Section 13.

The results of the ReNeu study demonstrate substantial evidence of effectiveness for the treatment of adult and pediatric patients 2 years of
age or older with NF1 who have symptomatic PN not amenable to complete surgical resection. The magnitude of the observed treatment effect
on ORR and DOR is clinically meaningful and is supported by a positive trend in COA data. Given the rarity of NF1-PN and the associated
morbidity in this patient population, a randomized trial is not considered feasible.
