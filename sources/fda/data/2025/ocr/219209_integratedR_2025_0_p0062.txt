6.3.2. Handling of Intercurrent Events

Issue

The Agency identified the following potential issues regarding the handling of intercurrent
events in both Studies 104 and 105:

Imputation methods for handling intercurrent events of treatment discontinuation:

The single imputation methods (i.e., LOCF and BOCF) were used for imputing data
following treatment discontinuation. Since single imputation methods may fail to reflect
the uncertainty in data, the Agency recommended adding multiple imputation (MI) to the
proposed handling strategies.

Per Agency request as outlined above, the Applicant imputed data following treatment
discontinuation using the MI approaches. When imputing data from a normal distribution
in the MI process, the Applicant

The Agency recommended using a truncated normal
distribution with limits of 0 and 10 instead.

Regarding the Applicant’s proposed! for treatment discontinuation due to LOE
[lack of efficacy], the Agency recommended using the above-mentioned MI of BOCF
method because treatment discontinuation due to lack of efficacy is generally viewed as a
treatment failure.

the

given rescue period, or the time of the next rescue use, whichever comes first.

Past Communications

In the investigational new drug application (IND) review stage, the first issue regarding the
handling strategy for ICE of treatment discontinuation was communicated between the
Agency and the Applicant.

In the end-of-phase 2 (EOP2) meeting package, the Applicant proposed to impute missing NPRS
scores following treatment discontinuation using the last observed NPRS score prior to
discontinuation.

The Agency advised in the August 12, 2022, EOP2 meeting communication that:

Vertex proposed LOCF for subjects that discontinued the study or completed the
treatment but with missing data from certain time point to 48 hours, which is not
appropriate. For example, discontinuation due to lack of efficacy may suggest the
assessments after the discontinuation will get worse and may be different from the
observed assessments. An appropriate imputation scheme would be to assume these
subjects do not contribute any treatment benefit in the primary analysis, such as multiple
imputations of the subject’s baseline score.
