6.2.1 Pharmacology and Clinical Pharmacokinetics

Summary of Clinical Pharmacology Assessment

Treosulfan is an alkylating agent with cytotoxic activity. The PK of treosulfan was described
using a population PK model. The final population PK (PPK) model for treosulfan is a two-

compartment model with linear elimination and intercompartmental clearance. BSA is included
as a significant covariate on CL, V1, V2 and Qin the final model, supporting a BSA-based dosing
regimen.

The area under the curve (AUC; mean + SD) in adult patients was 1,157 + 193 hr-mcg/mL after
administration of 10 g/m. There was no dose accumulation.

Distribution: The volume of distribution in adult patients is about 41.1 liters (CV%: 19.2%). In
animal studies, treosulfan penetration through the blood brain-barrier is limited [see Section
5.4]. Treosulfan does not bind to plasma albumin.

Elimination: The median terminal half-life of treosulfan is 2.2 hours.

Metabolism: Treosulfan is converted into an active monoepoxide intermediate [(2S,3S) 1,2
epoxybutane 3,4 diol 4 methanesulfonate] and finally to L diepoxibutane [(2S,3S) 1,2:3,4
diepoxybutane].

Excretion: A median of 42% of the treosulfan dose is excreted unchanged with the urine within
24 hours, and 89% of this unchanged fraction is excreted within the first 8 hours after
administration.
