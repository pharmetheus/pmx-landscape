Efficacy by Age

The primary efficacy endpoint was further analyzed by age subgroups (adolescents 12 to

<18 years of age, adults 18 to <65 years of age, and geriatrics >65 years of age). For adults, there
was a clinically meaningful and statistically persuasive reduction in the mean time-normalized
number of HAE attacks per month relative to placebo (Figure 5), which aligns with the overall
primary analysis results (Table 10). Although the point estimate favored garadacimab in the
adolescent subpopulation, the CI is very wide given the small number of adolescent subjects
enrolled (N=6). The observed treatment effect in adolescents may have also been less robust
because subjects had less disease severity, as the trial eligibility criteria did not allow prior use of
prophylactic therapies for adolescent subjects.

Conclusions regarding the effectiveness of garadacimab in adolescents primarily relied on
matching PK exposure to adults and extrapolating efficacy from the adult population, as the
pathophysiology of HAE is the same across all age groups. Extrapolation of the efficacy of
garadacimab in the adolescent population was based on matching of the PK profile and the mean
garadacimab concentration-time profile to the adult population (see Section 15.2.5 for further
details). While the pivotal trial (3001) was not statistically powered to draw efficacy conclusions
in the adolescent subpopulation, it is reassuring that the primary efficacy endpoint in adolescents
trended in the same direction as adults (Figure 5).

Additionally, there were six geriatric subjects in trial 3001, all of whom were randomized to
garadacimab. For the geriatric subjects, the mean time-normalized number of HAE attacks per
month was 0.03 (95% CI=0, 0.1), which is a meaningful reduction compared to the overall

placebo group (Table 10).

Altogether, garadacimab appeared effective at reducing HAE attacks during the 6-month
Treatment Period irrespective of age.

Efficacy by Race and Ethnicity

The point estimates for the primary analysis by race subgroups that comprised more than one
subject per arm (Figure 6) were generally consistent with the overall study population

Table 10). However, due to the small number of Asian (Japanese) subjects enrolled in the trial
(N=6), substantive conclusions for this subgroup cannot be drawn.
