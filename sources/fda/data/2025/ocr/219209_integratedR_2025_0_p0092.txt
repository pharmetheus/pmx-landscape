Studies 104 and 105 were controlled phase 3 acute pain studies that evaluated SUZ for a
treatment duration of 48 hours. Study 107 was a single-arm phase 3 acute pain study that
evaluated SUZ for a treatment duration of up to 14 days or until pain resolved, whichever
occurred first. No other studies have evaluated the acute pain dosage regimen of SUZ (100 m;
first dose followed by 50 mg every 12 hours) for a longer treatment duration. mx

Assessment

Study 107 is the only source of clinical data to inform the safety of longer-term use of SUZ for
acute pain. This was a single-arm study, which has significant limitations for the evaluation of
safety or efficacy. Subjects in this study could complete study drug treatment due to reaching
pain resolution, in addition to the usual reasons for study discontinuation, such as AEs. The
median duration of treatment was 11 days as depicted in Table 50, and 105 (41.0%) subjects
completed treatment through Day 14. Only 22 subjects were exposed to SUZ for longer than
14 days. Therefore, there are limited clinical data to inform use of SUZ beyond 14 days.

The safety results from this study are discussed in Section 7.6.2. In summary, there were no
deaths, and the incidence of SAEs and AEs leading to discontinuation of the study drug was low.
The most common AEs were generally consistent with those seen in the controlled phase 3
studies. An analysis of subjects who had at least one AE that occurred within different time
windows after treatment started is depicted in Table 51 and suggests that there was no consistent
pattern between duration of treatment and incidence of AEs. There are no reliable efficacy data
to inform duration of use given the design of Study 107 and the efficacy data collected (Patient
Global Assessment of study drug).
