Only 13 patients remained at risk near the median, with 17 patients censored before reaching this
point, some likely representing informative censoring. To assess the robustness of DOR
estimates in Group D, FDA conducted three sensitivity analyses. In Sensitivity Analysis 1,
patients censored by BICR due to new anticancer therapy (ACT) were treated as having an event
at ACT initiation (n=4), resulting in a median DOR of 8.8 months (95% CI: 5.8, 12.6). In
Sensitivity Analysis 2, patients censored by BICR for ongoing response after investigator-
assessed progression were treated as having an event (n=6), resulting in a median DOR of 8.2
months (95% CI: 5.7, 11.0). In Sensitivity Analysis 3, all patients censored by BICR for ACT,
ongoing response, and progression after more than one missing assessment were treated as
having an event (n=11), resulting in a median DOR of 6.8 months (95% CI: 5.2, 9.2).

Although these sensitivity analyses may represent conservative scenarios, the results suggest that
the DOR estimate is sensitive to additional events or censoring and should be interpreted with
caution. Additional follow-up data would help confirm the durability of response and provide a
more stable estimate of median DOR.

While the Applicant presents PFS results as supportive, given the single arm nature of the trial,
the results of time-to-event endpoints in the absence of a comparator arm are challenging to
interpret and are considered descriptive.
