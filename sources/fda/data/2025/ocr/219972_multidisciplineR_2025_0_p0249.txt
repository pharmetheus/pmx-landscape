FDA reviewed LFT laboratory data from both the combined Groups D + E and the pooled 20 mg
population. In the pooled population, Grade 3 — 4 AST elevation occurred in 2.3% of patients
and ALT elevation occurred in 2.3% of patients. There were no cases of Grade 3 — 4 total
bilirubin elevation, but 12% of patients had a Grade 1-2 elevation.

Considering the three cases of Grade 3 hepatoxicity each requiring intervention, and the pattern
of LFT elevation on laboratory findings, the FDA determined a specific warning in the USPI was
warranted to advise providers of the potential for hepatotoxicity and the need for monitoring and
potential treatment modifications.

G1 toxicity

Diarrhea was the most common all grade and Grade 3-4 toxicity observed in the pooled safety
population. Among the pooled population, 86% of patients experienced any Grade TEAE of
diarrhea with 15% of patients experiencing Grade 3 — 4 diarrhea. Sixty-three percent (63%) of
the pooled 20mg population required loperamide during treatment with sevabertinib. While the
FDA agrees that there is no instance of study drug being withdrawn specifically due to diarrhea,
it appears that diarrhea contributed to two cases of drug being withdrawn for arrythmia (likely
telated to use of loperamide and electrolyte disturbances) or acute kidney injury (dehydration).
Amongst the pooled 20mg population, diarrhea led to dose interruption in 15% and to dose
reduction in 12% of patients. Median time to onset of diarrhea (as a grouped term) TEAE was
4.0 days with a wide range of 1 to 190 days. The instances of enterocolitis and colitis were very
rare although there was one Grade 3 case of enterocolitis and one Grade 1 — 2 case of colitis in
the pooled 20mg population. As was required by protocol in the SOHO-01 study, the warning
will contain instructions that antidiarrheal treatment should be initiated at the first sign of
diarrhea or increased bowel movement frequency.
