In conclusion, nonclinical safety data from rodents, rabbits, and dogs enabled the hazard
identification and risk characterization relevant for the clinical use of dordaviprone in treating
H3 K27M-mutant diffuse glioma in adult and pediatric patients. The nonclinical adverse effects,
linked to species with higher plasma concentrations than those in humans, are associated with
monitorable endpoints that are included in the assessment of clinical safety throughout clinical
development.
