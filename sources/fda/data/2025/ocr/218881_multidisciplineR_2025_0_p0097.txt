FDA conducted exploratory analyses assessing the impact of ESR/ mutations on imlunestrant
efficacy in the ESR/ mutation-detected (ESR/m-detected) population. Hazard ratios for PFS of
imlunestrant vs control treatment were estimated from Cox proportional hazards model.
Participants carrying single ESR/ mutations demonstrated greater treatment effects for
imlunestrant compared to control than those carrying multiple SR/ mutations. Participants
carrying the D538G and Y537N mutations showed the largest treatment effects [(D538G:
HR=0.52, 95% CI: 0.31-0.90, p-value=0.0181) and (Y537N: HR=0.40, 95% CI: 0.09-1.73, p-
value = 0.2213)], while participants carrying only the Y537S mutation showed the smallest
treatment effect (HR=0.79, 95% CI: 0.40-1.56, p-value=0.4954). These results suggest that while
the HR point estimates were < 1 across the evaluated ESR/ mutations, the magnitude of benefit
of imlunestrant compared to standard of care (SOC) may differ depending on the ZSR/ mutation
profile (Table 6.5). There are several limitations to these subgroup analyses. First, the study was
not designed to assess treatment effect within these subgroups, and these exploratory analyses
were not stratified for other factors such as prior treatments or visceral metastasis. Second, the
number of participants were small in some of these subgroups. Therefore, although these results
suggest variable response based on ESR/ mutations, a broad indication across different ESR/
mutations is justified. Further exploration of these relationships with other ESR/-targeting drugs

is warranted.
