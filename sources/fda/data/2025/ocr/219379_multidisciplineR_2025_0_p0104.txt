6.3.2. Clinical Pharmacology Questions

6.3.2.1 Does the clinical pharmacology program provide supportive evidence of
effectiveness?

Data:

Yes. The exposure-response analyses identified a clear relationship between mirdametinib
exposure and response within the range of actual doses used in the NF1 PN clinical studies.
Mirdametinib has a concentration-dependent impact on PN tumor growth and overall
response. The final tumor growth inhibition (TGI) model was used to generate simulations to
evaluate target doses (1, 1.5, 2, and 3 mg/m?) of mirdametinib BID by comparing efficacy
(response rate, with response defined as tumor volume reduction from baseline exceeding
20%, or percent change from baseline). Desirable clinical efficacy is predicted at target doses as
low as 1 mg/m? BID; however, substantial increases in both magnitude and time of response
are predicted at the recommended starting dose of 2 mg/m? BID. As the starting dose allows for
potential reduction due to tolerability, this relationship ensures high probability of response at
the initial dose and confidence that a clinically active dose is still expected in the event of
individual reductions.

The Applicant's Position:
The mirdametinib clinical pharmacology program supports that exposure resulting from the

proposed regimen 2 mg/m? dose (maximum 4 mg) BID on a 3 weeks on/1 week off schedule
provides effective reduction in tumor volume in both adult and pediatric patients with NF1 PN.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. Additionally, the proposed dosage forms of capsules
(1, 2 mg) and tablets (1 mg) are acceptable based on overlapping PK observed between adult
and pediatric patients following administration of 2 mg/m? BID via tablets or capsules, including
comparable PK in the overlapping ages of pediatric patients who received tablets vs. capsules
(age 5 to 14 years). Efficacy was observed across pediatric age groups (2 to< 12,12 to<18
years), while differences in TEAEs by dosage form appear attributable to age-related
development differences. The proposed unified dosing nomogram is also acceptable, based on
the PPK simulation which showed no expected clinically meaningful differences in systemic
exposures.
