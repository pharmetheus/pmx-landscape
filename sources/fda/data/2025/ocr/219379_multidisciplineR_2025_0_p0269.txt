Pediatrics and Assessment of Effects on Growth

Data:

Fifty-six pediatric participants aged 2 to 17 years were enrolled in the pivotal Phase 2b Study
MEK-NF-201. For all participants 2 to 17 years of age, radiographs of the distal femur and
proximal tibia were obtained at baseline. If the investigator determined that the physes were
open, these participants underwent a radiograph in accordance with the Treatment Phase
Schedule of Activities to monitor for changes in physeal growth plates.

Relevant bone events from 4 pediatric participants in Study MEK-NF-201 were identified, as
shown in Table 64. All 4 events were assessed as not related to mirdametinib treatment. The
femoral neck fracture was reported as serious, and Grade 3, while the other events were Grade
1 or Grade 2 and non-serious. The femoral neck fracture was reported by a 12-year old female
with a relevant medical history of protrusion acetabuli, leg length discrepancy, and
levoscoliosis, on Day 269. The verbatim term was reported as right pathologic femoral neck
fracture due to fall. The participant’s target tumor was located in the hip affected by the
fracture and was noted to be 426.60 mL at baseline (SW-000421). The location and size of the
participant’s tumor are confounding factors in this event.

A Grade 2 radius fracture was reported by a 6-year-old female on Day 735. No relevant medical
history was noted, though the participant’s target tumor was noted to be located in the upper
limb. Additional details concerning the event are not available, though forearm fractures are
the most common fracture in children under 16 years old, and account for approximately 40%
of all pediatric long bone fractures (Oh 2022). The location of the participant’s target tumor and
epidemiology do not suggest this event was caused by mirdametinib.

Grade 1 osteopenia was reported by a 5-year-old female (Study MEK NF 201) on Day 664. The
participant had a relevant medical history of bowing tibia, dextroscoliosis, and spinal fusion
rods, and reported a fall resulting in a fractured spinal fusion rod and requiring surgery from
Day 40 to Day 308. The participant’s target tumor is noted as occurring in the mediastinum with
intrathoracic involvement to the right upper extremity and cervical spine, with a tumor volume
at baseline of 140.59 mL. The clinical effect of the participant’s tumor, as evidenced by their
medical history, is considered a confounder to the event of osteopenia.

A Grade 1 spinal deformity, described as a mild dextroconvex curvature of the thoracolumbar
spine, with apex approximately at T11, and measuring 5 degrees, was reported in a 7-year-old
male (Study MEK-NF-201) on Day 636. The participant’s target tumor was located in the
paraspinal region. The location of the participant’s tumor is a confounder for this event.
