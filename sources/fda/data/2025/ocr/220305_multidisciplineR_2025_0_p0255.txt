Total deaths were higher in Study KO-MEN-001 versus the ISS population of pooled studies,
which is reflective of the ISS population containing 76 newly diagnosed patients enrolled to
Study KO-MEN-007 and the preliminary nature of the follow-up data for Studies KO-MEN-007
and KO-MEN-008. On-treatment deaths and grade 5 TEAEs were similar but slightly higher on
KO-MEN-001 versus ISS, again for reasons mentioned for total deaths. The high rates of all-
grade TEAEs, grade 3-5 TEAEs, SAEs, and all-cause discontinuation reflect the high
background rates of adverse events in patients with active AML with most patients (277/353) in
the ISS pool and all participants enrolled on KO-MEN-001 having R/R AML. Despite this high
background rate of TEAEs, the observation that only 15-22% of patients across the studies had
TEAESs leading to discontinuation suggests that most toxicity observed was attributed to the
disease or expected complications of active AML and not to study treatment.

The safety profile of the most common TEAEs in Study KO-MEN-001 were generally consistent
with those that occur in AML patients with active disease and consistent in the safety subset of
NPMIm patients and the ISS population (FDA Table 58). The general categories for common
TEAEs often observed in active AML included infections, cytopenias, bleeding events, and GI
toxicity. TEAEs that are not associated with AML itself and induced or likely worsened by
ziftomenib treatment were differentiation syndrome and QT prolongation.
