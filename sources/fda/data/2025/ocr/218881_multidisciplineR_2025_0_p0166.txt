EMBER-3 was an active-controlled trial with a replacement design. For such trials even a
modest improvement in an efficacy endpoint may support a favorable regulatory action provided
the control arm is active, and the investigational treatment is not more toxic than the control. At
the time that the study was designed single agent endocrine therapy was an acceptable option for
second line therapy. Subsequently, it has been observed that fulvestrant monotherapy
underperforms after progression on a CDK4/6i therapy.

The secondary OS endpoint in the ESR/m population did not reach its prespecified significance
alpha level. However, there was a trend favoring the imlunestrant arm in the ESR/m population
(AR 0.91, 95% CI: 0.56-1.47). OS could not be tested in the ITT population since PFS failed to
meet its prespecified threshold. The exploratory endpoint of objective response rate also favored
imlunestrant (14% versus 8%). A postmarketing commitment (PMC) has been issued by the
FDA and agreed to by the Applicant to obtain the next prespecified interim and final OS results
for the ESRJm population

Finally, the approval of elacestrant for patients with an ESR/m and emerging data from other
oral SERDs, summarized in Section 2.2, suggest that the benefit of these therapies is greater for
patients with an ESR/m.

The FDA concluded that clinical benefit was demonstrated for imlunestrant in the ESR/m
population. Although the median PFS improvement was modest it should be interpreted in the
context of a replacement design. The PFS results were robust across multiple sensitivity analyses
and supported by no potential trend toward OS detriment. The FDA revised the indicated
population to patients with evidence of disease progression after endocrine therapy based on the
population included in the trial.
