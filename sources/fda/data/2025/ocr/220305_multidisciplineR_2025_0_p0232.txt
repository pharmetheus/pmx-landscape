8.3.3. | Adequacy of the Safety Database

The FDA agrees that the size of the safety database is adequate to identify common AEs.
However, due to the number of patients treated for the proposed indication (monotherapy for
R/R AML with NPM1-m) and the recommended dosage (600 mg daily) (n=112), rare events
may be missed. Additionally, the duration of treatment is too short to establish the safety profile
with long-term use as most patients were treated for less than 90 days. Therefore, additional data
for long-term use may be required.

Adequacy of the Applicant’s Clinical Safety Assessments
The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. See the discussion concerning laboratory AEs
versus laboratory value shift analysis in the FDA comments below.

Issues Regarding Data Integrity and Submission Quality
The quality of the safety data submitted was adequate to allow substantial primary review.

Categorization of Adverse Events

The FDA agreed with mapping of reported terms to preferred terms per MedDRA 27.0. Terms
that referred directly to relapse, persistence of disease, or progression of AML were excluded.
from FDA’s analyses. TEAEs were assessed by frequency (event per patient) and maximum
grade reported.

The FDA reviewed the Applicant’s grouped terms as above but also developed a list of grouped
terms (GTs) based on preferred terms (PTs) that were reported in the ISS pool (see Appendix
14.5 for a table of FDA grouped terms used throughout FDA safety analyses unless otherwise
noted). FDA assessed laboratory abnormalities as both reported AEs and by shifts from baseline
labs. Since all patients in Study KO-MEN-001 presented with R/R acute leukemia, most patients
had some level of abnormal baseline labs induced by the disease process. Therefore, analysis
anchored from this abnormal baseline would not identify labs that normalized with control of the
acute leukemia but later became abnormal again. Therefore, analysis of both lab shifts and lab
abnonnalities reported as TEAEs were evaluated to identify abnormal signals.

Routine Clinical Tests
The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. See the discussion conceming laboratory AEs
versus laboratory value shift analysis in the FDA comment above.
