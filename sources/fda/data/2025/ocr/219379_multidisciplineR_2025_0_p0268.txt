NDA/IND Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 219379/219389
Mirdametinib (GOMEKLI)

The Applicant's Posit
No human carcinogenicity studies have been conducted with mirdametinib.

The FDA's Assessment:
The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment.

Human Reproduction and Pregnancy

Data:

Studies with mirdametinib have not been conducted in women who are pregnant or lactating.
Reproductive animal toxicology studies have been completed with mirdametinib and confirm
the reproductive toxicity of mirdametinib, consistent with effects observed in nonclinical
studies with other MEK inhibitors. In embryo-fetal developmental toxicity studies in pregnant
rats and rabbits, oral dosing of mirdametinib induced early resorptions, post-implantation loss
and decreased fetal body weights in rats at dosages 23 mg/kg/day and in rabbits at dosages
20.3 mg/kg/day; these exposures were equivalent to the human exposure or approximately 0.4
times the human exposures at the recommended dose, respectively. In the rat study, at 3
mg/kg/day, a single fetus had extremity malformations.

There are no data on the presence of mirdametinib in human milk or the effects of
mirdametinib on a breastfed child or milk production.

The Applicant’s Position:

Mirdametinib should not be used during pregnancy. If a patient becomes pregnant while
receiving this drug, treatment with mirdametinib should be stopped and the patient should be
apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.

Since drugs are commonly excreted in human milk and because of the potential for serious
adverse reactions in nursing infants, women should not breastfeed during treatment with
mirdametinib and for at least 1 week after the last dose of mirdametinib. Given a terminal
elimination half-life of approximately 28 hours, it is expected that mirdametinib would no
longer be present 1 week after the last dose.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s statement. There were no patients who discontinued from the
study due to pregnancy, and birth control measures during treatment and ongoing pregnancy
screening were enforced to ensure that no fetus was exposed to study drug.
