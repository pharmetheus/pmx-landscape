The Applicant’s Position:

Several in vitro and ex vivo studies were conducted in order to characterize the secondary
pharmacodynamic properties of sevabertinib.

The specificity of sevabertinib and its metabolite BAY 3306572, was tested in vitro by
investigating potential interactions with up to 77 mostly human off-targets, including

G protein-coupled and nuclear receptors, ion channels, transporters, and enzymes in radioligand
binding and biochemical assays at concentrations up to 10 mol/L (Module 4.2.1.2, Report R-
13832 and Module 4.2.1.2, Report R-14379). Sevabertinib did not display significant effects
(>50%) on 72 out of 76 targets at 10 jzmol/L. Interactions with the following four off-targets
were observed: inhibition of binding to the human adenosine A1 (Ki ~0.52 mol/L) and A3
receptors (Ki ~1.03 mol/L), motilin (Ki ~1.32 mol/L), and inhibition of the human PDE4D2
activity (ICso ~2.77 mol/L). In two follow-up functional in vitro assays (Al: GTPyS in CHO
cells; motilin: intracellular Ca?* in recombinant CHO cells), sevabertinib displayed functional
antagonistic activity at the human adenosine A1 receptor (ICso ~4.3 jrmol/L) and at the human
motilin receptor (ICso ~4.9 mol/L) (Module 4.2.1.2, Report R-13833 and Module 4.2.1.2,
Report R-13933). This off-target activity was observed at concentrations >6-fold higher than the
human plasma concentration (Cmaxuss ~91 nmol/L) at the clinically relevant dose of 20
mg/patient BID.

The metabolite, BAY 3306572, did not display significant effects (>50%) on 72 out of 77 targets
at 10 pmol/L. Functional interactions with the following 5 human off-targets were observed:
inhibition of the adenosine Al (ICso ~4.0 pmol/L) and A3 receptors (ICso ~2.7 pmol/L),
adenosine transporter (uptake ICso ~0.46 jumol/L), cyclooxygenase-2 (ICso ~2.2 jumol/L), and 15-
lipoxygenase (ICso ~0.25 mol/L) (Module 4.2.1.2, Report B002421).
