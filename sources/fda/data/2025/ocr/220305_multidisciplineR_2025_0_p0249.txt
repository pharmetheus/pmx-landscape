8.3.7. Safety Analyses by Demographic Subgroups

Drug-Demographic Interactions

TEAEs by Sex
FDA Table 52 shows TEAEs from the main safety population separated by sex and with at least

10% difference between males and females. It is interesting to note that, while the overall
occurrence of TEAEs is 100% in either males or females, the occurrence of grade 3 and 4 events
occurred more frequently in females (76% in females versus 61% in males). Moreover, almost
all the risk differences that were observed were for more frequent occurrences in female patients,
with the notable exception of DS, which was more frequent in males (31% of males versus 19%
of females). As discussed above, most of the TEAEs that were observed on Study KO-MEN-001
are events that frequently occur in patients with R/R AML. Therefore, it is not clear that the
events that occurred more often in females were due to ziftomenib treatment or how
complications of the underlying AML vary by sex.
