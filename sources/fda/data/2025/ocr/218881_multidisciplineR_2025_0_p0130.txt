Tnluriyo (imlunestrant)

The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of patient disposition in the ITT and
ESRIm populations with the following additions:

Early discontinuations:

The FDA performed an analysis of patients in the intention-to-treat population who withdrew
consent or were withdrawn by their treating physician between randomization and 56 days (2
cycles) of therapy. Given the open-label design of the EMBER-3 trial, patients may have
considered not starting therapy or discontinuing from the study early if they were assigned to the
TPC arm because of the added requirements on the trial to receive open-label approved
therapies.

Five patients (1.5%) assigned to the TPC arm and two patients (0.6%) assigned to the
imlunestrant arm withdrew from the study before starting therapy. An additional patient (Subject
ID: ©) assigned to the TPC arm withdrew after two doses of fulvestrant based on
their physician’s decision that “other treatment may be more beneficial.” Finally, three patients
(0.9%) withdrew consent on the TPC arm versus two > patients (0.6%) on the imlunestrant arm
within 56 days. One patient (Subject ID: °) on the TPC anm was diagnosed with a
germline BRCA mutation by Guardant360 testing and withdrew consent; however, no
information on subsequent therapy was provided. Overall, discontinuations between
randomization and the first two doses of study medication were rare but were numerically higher
on the TPC arm. It is unlikely that these small differences led to imbalances between the two
arms.

The FDA did not evaluate patient dispositions on Arm C since this was out of scope for the
proposed indication under this NDA.

Protocol Violations/Deviations

Data:

Protocol deviations were identified, evaluated, and categorized as important or nonimportant
prior to unblinding. A nonimportant deviation was one that did not significantly affect data
reliability or patient’s rights, safety, or well-being. An important deviation was one that could
have significantly affected the completeness, accuracy, and/or reliability of data or potentially
affected a patient’s rights, safety, or well-being.
