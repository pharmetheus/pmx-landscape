For the 24 participants who achieved a CR or CRh, the median time to first response was 2.7
months (range: 0.9 to 15 months), and 21 (88%) participants did so within 6 months of initiating
the study treatment. The majority of these participants achieved their best response at their first
remission; only 1 participant (subject ID © with the longest time to respond) achieved first
response of CRh at 15 months prior to best response of CR at 18.7 months of treatment.

The transfusion independence data are summarized in FDA Table 36 for Pooled Efficacy Cohort,
with 21.2% of participants achieving transfusion independence, and 26.1% of participants
maintaining transfusion independence. These results appeared similar to those in the Phase 2
portion, where the transfusion independence rate was 17/75 (22.7%) and transfusion maintenance
rate was 2/17 (11.8%). These low rates may be explained in part by the short duration of
treatment (median 9.6 weeks). In addition, the population of patients with disease that had a best
response of CR + CRh showed much higher transfusion independence and maintenance rates
than those whose disease achieved lesser or no response.

Clinical TL Comment: Of particular concern is the lack of substantive achievement of
transfusion independence or maintenance of transfusion independence in participants not
achieving a disease status CR or CRh. This suggests that the benefit of ziftomenib is limited to
participants who achieve disease control with ziftomenib monotherapy.

The median OS was estimated similarly in the Pooled Efficacy Cohort and its Phase 2 subset: 6.1
(95% CI: 3.8, 8.6) and 6.6 (95% CI: 3.6, 8.6) months, respectively. OS is not interpretable in a
single-arm trial, so OS was not considered further, except to observe that this appears to be a
numerical improvement in OS compared to historical controls, as described by the Applicant
