Differentiation syndrome emerged as a notable adverse event across ziftomenib clinical studies,
with incidence varying by genetic subtype and dose. In the KO-MEN-001 study focusing on
patients with NPM/-mutated AML receiving 600 mg daily monotherapy, DS occurred in 27
patients (24%) with any grade severity, with 15 patients (13%) experiencing grade 3-4 events.
When examining genetic subtypes, patients with KM724-r demonstrated a higher overall
incidence of DS (42%, 15/36 patients) compared to patients with NPM1-m (24%, 29/120
patients), while no cases were observed in the "Other" genetic subtype group (0/16 patients). The
severity distribution showed that most cases were grades 1-3, with patients with KM724-r
experiencing 11% grade 5 events (4/36) compared to only 2% (2/120) in patients with NPM/-m.
Across the broader ISS, including participants who received both monotherapy and combination
therapy (n=353), DS occurred in 55 participants (16%) at any grade and 31 participants (9%) at
grade 3-4, suggesting this AR represents a manageable but clinically significant consideration in
ziftomenib treatment. The rate of DS was similar between the key safety population and the
