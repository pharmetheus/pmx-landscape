BLA 761458 Reviewer: David Klein, PhD

1 Executive Summary

1.1. Introduction
The Sponsor is developing GSK3511294 (depemokimab), a humanized monoclonal
antibody that blocks human IL-5 binding to its receptor for the treatment of

asthma, add-on-maintenance treatment of asthma in adult and pediatric patients aged
12 years and older with type 2 inflammation characterized by an eosinophilic phenotype
on medium-to high-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) plus another asthma controller,

Depemokimab differs from the approved
product mepolizumab by only 7 amino acids located in the heavy chain sequence.
There were 4 changes made with the goal of increasing affinity for IL-5, and 3 changes
were introduced to the Fc region to increase the half-life (YTE modification).

The focus of this review was to evaluate the extractables and leachables studies with
the container closure system as well as a new pharmacology study submitted to the
BLA.

1.2 Brief Discussion of Nonclinical Findings
The new pharmacology study indicated that depemokimab did not bind to mouse, rat,
rabbit, or dog IL-5.

The safety evaluation consisted of extractable and leachable studies for depemokimab.
The drug product is stored in a clear glass pre-filled syringe (PFS) housed in either an
autoinjector (Al) or safety syringe device (SSD) before subcutaneous administration.
The Sponsor considered the CCS to be consisted of the syringe stopper, needle shield,
and glass syringe with stainless-steel needle, although the needle shield does not come
into direct contact with the product. The glass syringe with stainless-steel needle does
not contribute organic leachables into the drug product. However, the syringe stopper
has the potential to contribute organic leachables.

Extractable studies were conducted with the syringe stopper and needle shield as well
as some manufacturing products that could potentially provide leachables. The Sponsor
tested the drug product for leachables following inverted storage for 6 months at 40°C
(accelerated degradation) or up to 12 months at 25°C with 24 and 36 time points being
planned for September 2025 and 2026.

The extractables and leachables for the depemokimab drug product did not appear to
pose any safety concerns.
