The FDA’s Assessment:

Phototoxicity: Although the Sponsor concludes that mirdametinib has little risk of phototoxicity
given that it is not retained in the eye or skin, there were two cases of photosensitivity and one
case of photophobia reported clinically. In animals, there was skin toxicity (sores, crust,
neutrophilic infiltrates) observed in repeat-dose toxicology studies, but it is unclear if these
were related to phototoxicity. In the clinic, dermatologic reactions, such as dermatitis
acneiform and non-acneiform rashes, were also reported; however, these findings are
consistent with MEK inhibitors class effects, suggesting that the majority of dermatologic
disorders in patients may be related to a pharmacological effect.

Metabolites: Two major metabolites of mirdametinib with pharmacological activity have been
identified (M22 and PD-0315209) that represent >10% of mirdametinib in human plasma. M22
(O-glucuronide conjugate) is a unique human metabolite while PD-0315029 (carboxylic acid)
was detected in repeat dose studies in mice, rats, and dogs. M22 is a Phase 2 glucuronide
conjugate and Phase 2 conjugation reactions generally render a compound more water soluble
and pharmacologically inactive; thus, M22 is not expected to have an increased risk of
carcinogenicity and further evaluation is not warranted. Genetic toxicity of PD-0315209 was not
directly tested; however, it is expected that rats were exposed to PD-0315209 in the in vivo
micronucleus test as rats can generate PD-0315209, though levels PD-0315209 were not
measured in this study. FDA clarifies that toxicokinetics analyzing PD-0315209 levels during the
6-month carcinogenicity study were conducted in non-transgenic littermates.
