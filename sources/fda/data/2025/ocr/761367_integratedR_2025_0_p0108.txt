13.1.1. Pharmacology (Primary and Secondary)

The nonclinical pharmacology studies were originally reviewed under the IND. Summaries are
provided here.

Primary Pharmacology

In vitro

Garadacimab is a fully humanized, affinity matured monoclonal antibody that was developed to
bind specifically to activated FXII (FXIla) and render it inactivated, thereby arresting kallikrein-
kinin system activation and contact-mediated thrombosis without affecting hemostasis. Using an
amino acid mutagenesis approach, it was shown that garadacimab binds to the catalytic site of
FXIa.

Effects of garadacimab on Factor XII (FXII) activity were evaluated in human, cynomolgus
monkey, mouse and rabbit plasma blood samples (Table 40, Table 41, Table 42, and Table 43).
Inhibition of FXII activity was similar in human and monkey. In contrast, inhibition of FXII
activity was lower in mouse and rabbit. Inhibition of FXII with garadacimab was observed in all
nonhuman species tested. The affinity matured aFXII antagonist, garadacimab, has high affinity
to human BFXIIa (140pM) and is cross-reactive with mouse, rabbit, and cynomolgus BFXIla
(700pM, 400pM and 19nM, respectively).
