Vital Signs
The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of vital signs.

QT/Electrocardiograms (ECGs)

The FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s assessment that ziftomenib is not associated with
clinically meaningful QTc prolongation. Please see the discussion of FDA modeling of
ziftomenib concentration and QTc prolongation in Section 6. As shown in FDA Table 48, QTc
prolonged was noted to occur at any grade in 12% of the key safety population with grade 3
events in 8% of the population.

Clinical Reviewer’s Comment: It is important to note that grade 3 OTc prolongation requires
either an average OTc > 501 ms or a > 60 ms increase from baseline. Moreover, for a OTc
prolongation event to rise to grade 4, an associated clinical event of Torsade de pointes,
polymorphic ventricular tachycardia, or other signs/symptoms of serious arrhythmia is required.
While the treatment of patients in this single-arm study makes it difficult to attribute all QTc
prolongation events to ziftomenib alone, since most patients were receiving concomitant
medications (azole antifungals, antiemetics, antibiotics) commonly used in this population, the
FDA decided that the combination of the concentration-dependent increase in QTc noted above,
combined with the potential for inadvertent ziftomenib concentration increases from drug-drug
interactions or food effects and standard use of supportive medications with OTc-prolonging
potential, required the addition of a OTc prolongation warning and precaution to the label to
mitigate this risk in clinical use.

Immunogenicity

The FDA agrees with Applicant’s position. The risk of immunogenicity for small molecules is
low, and there was no indication in the TEAE analysis of an issue with immunologic problems.
