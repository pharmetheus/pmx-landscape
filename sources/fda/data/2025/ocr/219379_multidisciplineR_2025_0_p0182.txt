Mirdametinib (GOMEKLI)

The exploratory PRO endpoints demonstrated improvements in mobility, upper extremity
function, strength, range of motion, and 6-minute walk test within the pediatric cohort. Median
TTP and PFS were both not reached. Additionally, the tablet formulation achieved high scores
on the P-OMAQ-P and P-OMAQ-C, indicating high acceptability among the pediatric cohort.

Exploratory efficacy endpoints also supported clinical benefit of mirdametinib in the adult
cohort. The exploratory PRO endpoints demonstrated improvements in mobility, upper
extremity scores, strength, range of motion, and 6-minute walk test. Median TTP and PFS were
both not reached.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA did not independently verify results for some exploratory endpoints including DCR, time to
progression or progression-free survival.

As described above, there were observed general improvements in clinical outcome
assessments however the same limitations apply: these results should be interpreted with
caution given the small sample size, single arm design of the study, heterogenous population in
terms of age and tumor location, and compliance rate at cycle 13. Because of these limitations
and to better assess the clinical meaningfulness of tumor shrinkage, FDA also reviewed
individual patient narratives submitted by the Applicant for all responders, given the
heterogeneity in target PN location and associated morbidities. On review of the individual
narratives, the majority of responders had improvement in at least one symptom or clinical
outcome measure. Key information from the responder narratives for patients in the pediatric
and adult cohorts, including evidence of clinical benefit for each patient, in ReNeu is
summarized in Table 43 and Table 44, respectively.
