Secondary Pharmacology

The Applicant’s position:

In a kinase selectivity assay with a select panel of 104 kinases (FRO950040003), 100

kinases were not inhibited after treatment with 1 4M mirdametinib. The other 4 kinases
showed little to no kinase activity, including human MEK1 (21% activity), human B-Raf

(0% activity), human B-Raf V599E (-1% activity), and human C-Raf (0% activity). Of note, the
MEK1, B-Raf, B-Raf(V599E), and C-Raf kinases are linked assays exploiting kinase cascades in
which the reaction mixtures contain components of the MAPK pathway, including MEK1.
Therefore, B-Raf and C-Raf inhibition is consistent with the nature of the linked assay format for
on-target inhibition by a MEK inhibitor.

In addition, an in vitro safety screening assay (TW04-0016273) comprising 87 targets (receptors,
enzymes, ion channels, and transporters) was conducted with 1 and 10 uM mirdametinib.

At 10 uM, mirdametinib inhibited only 2 targets which included the L-type calcium channel
(phenylalkylamine) inhibited by 54% and the peroxisome proliferator activated receptor gamma
inhibited by 53%. Mirdametinib did not inhibit any targets at 1 uM.

The screening assays covered a sufficient range of targets and did not demonstrate significant
mirdametinib-mediated activity against proteins other than MEK1/2. The 1 uM concentration of
mirdametinib tested in these assays is approximately 250-fold higher than the clinically
observed unbound maximum observed concentration (Cmax; 0.0039 4M) for mirdametinib in
the MEK-NF-201 clinical study.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s assessment, except that based on a human plasma
binding of 99.7% and a Cmax of 189 ng/mL (392 nM), the free Cmax is 1.2 nM. Thus, the 1 uM
concentration of mirdametinib tested in secondary pharmacology assays is approximately 800-
fold higher than the free Cmax.
