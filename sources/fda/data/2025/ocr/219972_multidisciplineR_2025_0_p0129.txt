No clinical data are available regarding a potential effect of sevabertinib on CYP1A1 substrates.
However, sevabertinib is a strong inhibitor of CYP1A1 at clinically relevant concentrations in
vitro. This indicates that co-administration of sevabertinib may increase the plasma
concentrations of CYP1A1 substrates. Therefore, the related recommendation in the product
information of these substrates (e.g. riociguat, granisetron) should be followed when co-
administered with sevabertinib.

No clinical data are available regarding a potential effect of sevabertinib on CYP2C8 substrates.
However, sevabertinib is a weak inhibitor of CYP2C8 at clinically relevant concentrations in
vitro. The clinical relevance of this finding is unknown.

No clinical data are available regarding a potential effect of sevabertinib on MATE] or
MATE2K. However, sevabertinib is an inhibitor of MATE] and MATE2-K at clinically relevant
concentrations in vitro. This indicates that co-administration of sevabertinib may affect renal
clearance of substrates of these transporters. Therefore, the related recommendation in the
product information of these substrates (including, but not limited to metformin, cisplatin) should
be followed when co-administered with sevabertinib.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment that sevabertinib should be taken with food and can
be given with acid reducing agents. FDA also agrees with the Applicant’s assessment regarding
concomitant use of sevabertinib with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, strong CYP3A4 inducers, and
certain substrates of CYP3A4 substrates and P-gp.

FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s assessment using the population PK model to evaluate
the effect of mild and moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors and inducers on the PK of sevabertinib.
According to FDA analysis, the population PK estimates are not robust or reliable as the
treatment duration of the CYP3A4 inhibitors and inducers is unknown and the mild and
moderate categories are not separated. Clinical DDI Study 22250 demonstrated that sevabertinib
AUC increased 2.3-fold and Cmax 1.6-fold following concomitant use of itraconazole (strong
CYP3A4 inhibitor). Moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors may also increase the exposure of sevabertinib
and thus lead to increased adverse reactions. However, a PMR clinical DDI trial to assess the
effect of moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors is not required given the dosage modifications based on
adverse reactions have been specified in the label and the outcome of a DDI study with moderate
CYP3A4 inhibitors may not change the labeling recommendations as currently recommended.
