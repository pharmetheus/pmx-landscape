I. Executive Summary
1. Overview
1.1. Summary of Regulatory Action
This new drug application (NDA), 217370 for Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003%,
was submitted in accordance with Section 505(b)(1) of the Federal Food Drug and Cosmetic Act
and 21 CFR 314.50 of the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, by Alcon Laboratories, Inc.
(Alcon), for the treatment of the signs and symptom of dry eye disease (DED) in adults when
dosed twice daily in each eye.
The Applicant submitted three studies supporting the efficacy of Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic
solution) 0.003% (one Phase 2b and two Phase 3 pivotal studies). Each of these studies was
prospective, randomized, and double-masked. Initial eligibility was assessed at the Screening
visit, based on signs and symptoms of DED (anesthetized Schirmer test score, total corneal
fluorescein staining score, global Symptoms Assessment iN Dry Eye [SANDE] score, and ocular
discomfort score [ODS]). Subjects also had to demonstrate an increase in signs and symptoms of
DED following exposure to a controlled adverse environment (CAE) at Screening. All qualified
subjects at the Screening visit entered a 2-week run-in period with the vehicle. Following
requalification of subjects based on signs and symptoms of DED, all eligible subjects were
randomized to twice daily (BID) treatment in both eyes (OU) with acoltremon ophthalmic
solution or vehicle for up to 3 months.
Both Phase 3 studies, COMET-2 and COMET-3, demonstrated safety and efficacy for the
treatment of dry eye disease for the primary efficacy endpoint, percentage of subjects achieving a
10-mm increase or more in Schirmer’s tear test scores. NDA 217370 was reviewed by a
multidisciplinary team as a Standard Review and was not presented at an advisory committee.

1.2. Conclusions on Substantial Evidence of
Effectiveness
Substantial evidence of effectiveness (SEE) was established with two or more adequate and wellcontrolled clinical investigations.
Phase 3 studies COMET-2 and COMET-3 demonstrated safety and efficacy for the treatment of
dry eye disease for the primary efficacy endpoint, percentage of subjects achieving a 10-mm
increase or more in Schirmer’s tear test scores.

2.2. Conclusions Regarding Benefit-Risk
Studies COMET-2 and COMET-3 demonstrated that Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution)
0.003% dosed twice daily in each eye was statistically superior to vehicle in the proportion of
subjects experiencing a ≥10-mm increase in Schirmer score on Day 14. These were identical,
randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled studies evaluated in adult subjects with dry eye
disease.
The primary efficacy endpoint, the proportion of subjects achieving a ≥10-mm increase in
Schirmer’s tear test scores, is considered a clinically significant change without a corresponding
symptom change. The amount of change in tearing has been associated with a change in clinical
symptoms.
The safety of Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% was assessed in over 766
subjects dosed twice a day. The mean for treatment exposure was 141.3 days; the median was
92 days. The safety profile of Tryptyr is acceptable. The most common treatment-emergent
adverse event experienced during the clinical studies was instillation site pain (burning or
stinging) events: 49.7% in the Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% group and 4.1%
in the vehicle group. The majority of instillation site pain (burning or stinging) events were mild
in severity: 97.6% of these events were mild in the Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution)
0.003% group, and 96.4% in the vehicle group.

The published literature established that acoltremon is a transient receptor potential melastatin 8
(TRPM8) agonist like menthol. TRPM8, a calcium-permeable cation channel, is a cold sensor in
humans. The activation of TRPM8 opens the channel and allows influx of calcium ions into the
cell, resulting in cold thermal transduction and pain detection, among other effects. The

5.2. Clinical Pharmacology/Pharmacokinetics
Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% is proposed for topical ophthalmic use and the
site of action is the eye. Acoltremon systemic exposure was evaluated in a subset of subjects
with DED in the ongoing long-term safety study COMET-4, in which subjects received
acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% or vehicle one drop in each eye twice daily for 12
months. Plasma samples were collected on Days 1, 14, and 90 from 25 subjects dosed with
acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003%. Acoltremon was detected in 6 samples from 3 subjects
with plasma concentrations ranging from 22.8 pg/mL to 213 pg/mL but was below the limit of
quantification (lower limit of quantification [LLOQ] of 20 pg/mL) in all other samples (refer to
Section 14 for more details). Given the negligible systemic exposure of acoltremon following
topical ocular administration, the impact of intrinsic and extrinsic factors on acoltremon
pharmacokinetics (PK) has not been assessed.

6. Efficacy (Evaluation of Benefit)
6.1. Assessment of Dose and Potential
Effectiveness
Based on nonclinical studies, the high protein binding of acoltremon, and negligible systemic
(plasma) exposure following the clinically intended route of administration (topical ocular), there
is limited potential for clinically significant systemic effect levels with topical ocular dosing. A
systemic pharmacokinetic evaluation was incorporated in the long-term safety study (Phase 3,
COMET-4). Results from this study are included in Module 2.7.2 and the final analysis reported
included in Module 5.3.5.4.
Initial dose finding and dosing frequency were evaluated in the Phase1/2a study conducted by
the prior sponsor (AVX012-CT-001). Results demonstrated that both 0.0007% and 0.0014%
acoltremon administered as a topical ophthalmic solution three times a day (TID), had an
acceptable safety profile. Additionally, after 28 days of either BID or TID administration of
0.0014% acoltremon ophthalmic solution, both DED symptoms (SANDE score) and signs (tear
production) were improved. Review of the totality of efficacy data concluded that TID
administration provided no additional treatment benefit relative to BID, and BID dosing was
chosen to maximize subject compliance.
The concentration of acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% used in the final formulation was
based on results from the Phase 2b study (COMET-1), which evaluated 0.0014% and 0.003%
acoltremon. In this study, the 0.003% concentration yielded the most consistent and robust
treatment effects across multiple signs and symptoms. This included a dose dependent increase in
tear production on Days 1 and 14 based on unanesthetized Schirmer scores (Day 1: least squares
[LS] mean difference from vehicle in change from baseline: 12.2 and 6.1 mm for 0.003% and
0.0014%, respectively; Day 14: LS mean difference from vehicle in change from baseline: 13.4
and 9.2 mm for 0.003% and 0.0014%, respectively) (COMET-1 clinical study report Table
14.2.3.10.1), and reduction in global SANDE scores on Days 28 and 84 (Day 28: LS mean
difference from vehicle in change from baseline: -7.9 and -4.3 mm for 0.003% and 0.0014%,
respectively; Day 84: LS mean difference from vehicle in change from baseline: -8.9 and -1.1
mm for 0.003% and 0.0014%, respectively).
Overall, evidence from preclinical safety and clinical studies support the concentration and
dosing frequency of acoltremon chosen for Phase 3 development studies. The recommended
dosage for Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% is one drop in each eye, twice
daily.

6.1.1. Proposed Dosage Regimen
The proposed dosage regimen is Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% one drop
instilled twice daily in each eye (approximately 12 hours apart). This dosing regimen was tested
in two independent Phase 3 studies (COMET-2 and COMET-3) as well as a Phase 2 study and a
long-term safety study.

Three randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled studies were conducted to support the

to demonstrate efficacy for the treatment of dry eye disease. Refer to Section 15 for a detailed
description of the study design and endpoints.
Subject inclusion and exclusion criteria were the same among all studies. Subjects 30 years and
older with a history of artificial tear use for DED were qualified based on signs and symptoms of
DED at Screening as outlined below, and were requalified at Baseline (Day 1) following a 14day vehicle run-in.
Both of the following signs of DED in the same eye:


Total corneal fluorescein staining score of ≥2 and ≤15 (modified National Eye Institute), and
no region >3 at Screening only



Anesthetized Schirmer test score ≥2 and <10 mm at 5 min at both Screening and Baseline
visits



ODS- visual analog scale (VAS) and global SANDE ≥50 at both Screening and Baseline

allowed in any study. Overall, any use of medications likely to bias the study results was
minimized.
Eligible subjects were randomized at baseline to administer acoltremon ophthalmic solution
0.003% or vehicle as one drop BID OU in the morning and evening. Treatment compliance was
measured by count of used and unused vials with subjects deemed noncompliant entered as

6.3. Key Efficacy Review Issues
6.3.1. Uncertainty Regarding the Duration of the
Treatment Effect
Issue
The duration of the treatment effect was not assessed.
Background
The primary endpoint was met in two randomized, double-masked, well-controlled studies. The
FDA has previously accepted drugs for the treatment of signs and symptoms of dry eye disease
with two replicated studies that succeeded in demonstrating a statistically significant difference
between the percentage of subjects achieving a 10-mm increase or more in Schirmer scores,
without requiring a demonstration of symptom relief. Because this magnitude of increased tear
production has been associated with a reduction in symptoms, the Division has accepted this
primary efficacy endpoint without a corresponding symptom endpoint to support a
demonstration of safety and efficacy.
Assessment
The postdrop Schirmer tests were all initiated at 2 minutes and 45 seconds postdrop for the first
eye and 3 minutes postdrop for the second eye. The Schirmer test strips were removed 5 minutes
later, then evaluated and compared to baseline predrop Schirmer. See the study instructions for
predrop and postdrop unanesthetized Schirmer test below.

The data obtained using this methodology provides an 8-minute sampling to assess the benefit in
tear production in a drug seeking every 12 hours dosing.
The only tear production measurements that were measured at an interval exceeding 8 minutes
were the predrop unanesthetized Schirmer tear tests performed at every visit in the COMET-2,
COMET-3, and COMET-4 studies. Predrop unanesthetized Schirmer tear tests when performed
at each visit, in both Tryptyr and vehicle groups when compared to Day 1 predrop
unanesthetized Schirmer tear tests demonstrated <1 mm mean change from baseline in every
study. These post Day 1 predrop Schirmer tests do tell us that any increase in tear production that
had been present after taking the second drop on the previous day was no longer inducing
increased tear production at the time of the testing visit. We do not know the time of the most
recent drop prior to the testing visit so we don’t have information to provide a degradation curve
between the first 8 minutes of increased tear production and the unknown interval that led to the
<1-mm increase the following day.
At each visit, the postdrop Schirmer tear test showed a higher proportion of Tryptyr subjects than
vehicle subjects with a ≥10-mm mean increase postdrop on day of visit versus predrop at
baseline. This data demonstrates that Tryptyr provides a convincing repeated increase in tear
production after each drop in a 90-day period, with the effect of each drop lasting at least
8 minutes. The window of time between 8 minutes and 12 hours postdrop is not assessed.
The FDA has previously accepted drugs for the treatment of signs and symptoms of dry eye
disease with two replicated studies that succeeded in demonstrating a statistically significant
difference between the percentage of subjects achieving a 10-mm increase or more in Schirmer
scores, without requiring a demonstration of symptom relief.

This study demonstrated a lack of correlation between meeting the primary endpoint and

6.3.2. Clinical Significance of Corresponding Symptom
Relief
Issue
Is evidence of symptom relief clinically significant?
Background
A statistically significant treatment group difference in the change from baseline in SANDE
symptom score was only demonstrated in COMET-2. The Phase 2 study, COMET-1, failed its
primary endpoint; thus, the secondary endpoints including change from baseline in SANDE score
cannot be evaluated. In COMET-3, the change from baseline in SANDE score at Day 28 was not
statistically significant. Clinically significant symptom relief was not demonstrated in clinical
studies.
Assessment
Evidence of symptom relief represented by the global SANDE score was not demonstrated to be
statistically superior to vehicle in relief of symptoms in replicated studies.
Conclusion
A separate demonstration of safety with a symptom endpoint was not demonstrated in replicated
studies. However, the sign endpoint, the proportion of subjects who achieved a ≥10-mm increase
in Schirmer test score, is considered a clinically significant endpoint without a corresponding
symptom endpoint.

COMET-2, COMET-3, and COMET-4
Blood pressure and heart rate were measured in the Phase 2b and Phase 3 studies. However, the
Phase 2b collected vital signs only at Screening; therefore, they were not considered for the
evaluation of safety.
Vital signs were assessed on Day 1 (Baseline) and the Exit visit for all Phase 3 studies, and on
Days 14, 90, 180, and 270 in the Phase 3 LTSS. Heart rate was measured manually or by
automated methods, and blood pressure was measured using an appropriate sphygmomanometer
after the subjects were at rest (seated) for a minimum of 5 minutes.
Overall, data from the integrated safety population showed that vital signs remained stable
without significant changes throughout the treatment period. There were no identified safety
concerns with 0.003% acoltremon treatment related to vital signs.

was developed as a drug, in compliance with 21 CFR Part 312.

Pharmacology
The published literature establishes that acoltremon (as WS-12) is a TRPM8 agonist like
menthol. TRPM8, a calcium-permeable cation channel, is a cold sensor in humans. The
activation of TRPM8 opens the channel and allows influx of calcium ions into the cell, resulting
in cold thermal transduction and pain detection, among other effects (Izquierdo et al. 2021). The
Applicant verified this activity in vitro (using cells transfected with rat TRPM8) and ex vivo

Carcinogenicity
Carcinogenicity studies were not conducted with acoltremon. ICH S1A indicates that
pharmaceuticals administered by the ocular route may not require carcinogenicity studies unless
there is cause for concern or unless there is significant systemic exposure (March 1996).
Alcon consulted with the Agency at the pre-NDA meeting held on March 4, 2024, on supporting
data required for rodent carcinogenicity study waiver requests. Based on the Agency feedback,
the Applicant used the following integrated approach to assess potential carcinogenicity risk of
acoltremon to request a waiver of the studies. This rationale was presented to the Executive
Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee for review of the waiver request. The committee agreed
with the Division that in the absence of systemic exposure for acoltremon ophthalmic solution,
0.003%, there is no need to conduct carcinogenicity studies.
Carcinogenicity Data for Another Compound in the Same Class
Acoltremon is also widely known as WS-12, a menthol derivative, and Flavor and Extract
Manufacturers Association 4681. WS-12 is widely used as a food flavoring agent and is the most
common agent used to create the “cool mint” flavor. WS-12 is GRAS for oral food exposure
(refer to Pharmacology and Toxicology review of IND 147005 by Dr. McDougal).
The Applicant indicated the lack of test-article-related carcinogenicity findings in mouse and rat
2-year feeding studies conducted with the cooling agent dl-menthol by the National Toxicology
Program (National Toxicology Program 1979).
In these studies, B6C3F1 mice (50/sex/group) were administered vehicle control (basal diet
containing 2% corn oil) or 2000 or 4000 ppm mg/kg/day dl-menthol in the diet ad libitum 7 days
per week for 104 weeks. Assuming a 0.03-kg mouse consumes 5 grams of food per day, these
doses corresponded to 0, 334 and 667 mg/kg/day, respectively. Fischer 344 rats (50/sex/group)
were administered vehicle control (basal diet containing 2% corn oil) or 3750 or 7500 ppm
mg/kg/day dl-menthol in the diet ad libitum 7 days per week for 104 weeks; assuming a 0.4-kg
rat consumes 20 grams of food per day, these doses corresponded to 0, 188 and 375 mg/kg/day,
respectively. Animals were evaluated for mortality, clinical observations, masses, body weight,
and food consumption, gross and microscopic pathology.
All animals survived to the scheduled necropsy and there were no test-article-related effects on
clinical observations, body weight, food consumption, or gross pathology findings. There were
no test-article-related preneoplastic or neoplastic findings up to and including the highest doses
tested in mice (667 mg/kg/day) and rats (375 mg/kg/day).
Based on the available data, dl-menthol did not produce any evidence of a carcinogenic effect in
B6C3F1 mice and Fischer 344 rats up to the highest tested doses (mice: 667 mg/kg/day; rats:
375 mg/kg/day).

Study Design
Study COMET-2 was a Phase 3, multicenter, vehicle-controlled, double-masked, randomized
study conducted at 23 sites in the United States. The study consisted of Screening (Day -14) and
Baseline (Day 1) visits, and follow-up visits on Days 7, 14, 28, and 90 (Study Exit). In addition,
there was a Day 60 dispensing visit. All subjects were exposed to the CAE at the Screening visit
to assess eligibility.
At the end of the Screening visit, all qualified subjects were assigned to administer vehicle BID
to both eyes for 14 days (vehicle run-in period). After the vehicle run-in period, subjects were
reevaluated for signs and symptoms of DED. Only subjects who requalified, based on
inclusion/exclusion criteria, were enrolled in the study and randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive
0.003% acoltremon or vehicle to be administered BID as one drop in each eye for 90 days.
Efficacy was assessed at the Baseline visit and on Day 7, Day 14, Day 28, and Day 90. At the
end of the Day 90 visit, subjects exited the study. Safety assessments were conducted at each
study visit.

Method of Assigning Subjects to Treatment Groups
All subjects were centrally assigned to randomized study treatment using an interactive response
technology (IRT). Before the study was initiated, the login information and directions for the
IRT were provided to qualified personnel at each site. All qualified subjects were assigned to
receive acoltremon vehicle BID to both eyes for 14 days (vehicle run-in period).
Following the run-in period, all subjects who requalified at the Baseline visit were randomized in
a 1:1 ratio, within each site, to receive:


0.003% acoltremon



Acoltremon vehicle

The IRT provided the site with the specific kit number(s) for each randomized subject at the time
of randomization. Sites dispensed the study treatment according to the IRT instructions and the
Schedule of Visits and Assessments.
Rescue Medication
Disallowed medications and treatments were listed in the Exclusion Criteria. All medications and
treatments that were not allowed prior to the study were not allowed during the study, except that
occasional short-term use of topical ocular medications (specified above) was permitted provided
that no drops were used within 24 hours of the Screening visit or anticipated use within 24 hours
of any study visit.

Planned Statistical Analysis Methods
Sample Size Determination
Two hundred and two subjects per treatment group yields 99% power to conclude superiority of
0.003% acoltremon over vehicle in the proportion of subjects with ≥10-mm increase in
unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14, assuming a true difference of proportions (acoltremon
versus vehicle) of 80% versus 30% and a two-sided alpha =0.05.
Additionally, 202 subjects per treatment group yields 99% power to conclude superiority of
0.003% acoltremon over vehicle in the mean change from baseline in SANDE score on Day 28
assuming a true difference (acoltremon minus vehicle) of -7.9, a common standard deviation of
17.72, and a two-sided alpha =0.05.
Accounting for subject discontinuations, approximately 460 total subjects (230 per treatment
arm) were planned to be randomized assuming a dropout rate of 10%.
Analysis of Primary Endpoint
The primary comparison in this study was between 0.003% acoltremon and vehicle in the ITT
population using Estimand 1.
Analysis of primary efficacy was based on the difference of proportions using Pearson’s Chisquare test. Available data only were used if the discontinuation rate was <5% in each arm;
otherwise, an estimand based on multiple imputations was used for the primary analysis.
Inference across multiple imputed datasets was obtain by pooling Chi-square distributed
statistics as proposed by Ratitch (2013), Rubin (1987), and Li (1991) (Rubin D.B. 1987; Li 1991;
Ratitch et al. 2013).
The primary endpoint was tested as follows:


H01: The difference between study eyes treated with 0.003% acoltremon and study eyes
treated with vehicle, in the proportion of subjects with ≥10-mm increase in unanesthetized
Schirmer score on Day 14=0.



H11: The difference between study eyes treated with 0.003% acoltremon and study eyes
treated with vehicle, in the proportion of subjects with ≥10-mm increase in unanesthetized
Schirmer score on Day 14 ≠ 0.

The primary estimand (Estimand 1) was based on the ITT population targeting the treatment
effect attributable to the randomly assigned treatment, regardless of compliance to study
treatment and assuming subjects who discontinued due to lack of efficacy, AEs, or disallowed
concurrent treatments followed the behavior of subjects in the reference arm for the duration of
the study and subjects who discontinued for other reasons (outside of lack of efficacy, AEs, or
use of disallowed treatment) would follow the behavior of subjects who were still in the study
under same randomly assigned treatment. For further details on Estimands 1, 2, and 3 refer to
Statistical Analysis Plan.

Analysis of Secondary Efficacy Endpoints
The secondary efficacy endpoints were analyzed using the ITT population and repeated using the
PP population.
Analysis of secondary efficacy was conducted as described for the primary efficacy endpoints,
using the difference of proportions tests, or ANCOVA for difference of change from baseline
means. Available data only was used if the discontinuation rate was <5% in each arm and
multiple imputation otherwise. For details on Estimands 1, 2, and 3 as applicable to secondary
efficacy endpoints refer to Section 9.7.1.6.2.
Two-sample t-tests comparing treatment groups were performed as sensitivity analyses.
Wilcoxon rank sum tests were also performed as sensitivity analyses. The secondary
dichotomous efficacy endpoints were analyzed similarly to the primary endpoint.
To ensure Type 1 error rate was controlled at the 5% significance level (2-sided) over all set of
study hypotheses, a fixed-sequence hierarchical strategy was employed, requiring all preceding
endpoints in the testing hierarchy to be statistically significant at the 5% level before an endpoint
could be evaluated for statistical significance. For example, the primary endpoint must have
shown statistical significance at the 5% significance level before the first (key) secondary
endpoint could be evaluated for statistical significance and so on.

Protocols AR-15512-CS301 and AR-15512-CS302
These protocols were very similar in design with both studies being designed to assess the safety,
efficacy, and tolerability of Tryptyr (acoltremon) in the treatment of signs and symptoms of dry
eye disease over a 15-week study period.
Both studies shared the same efficacy objectives, treatment schedules, and subject qualification
criteria. These protocols were multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled,
parallel-group Phase 3 trials whose primary objectives were to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and
tolerability of Tryptyr (acoltremon) compared to placebo for approximately 13 weeks in the
treatment of signs and symptoms of dry eye disease.
The study consisted of six clinic visits over a period of 15 weeks. Eligible subjects were
randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive Tryptyr (acoltremon) or vehicle (placebo) once daily for 13
weeks. Clinical assessments of efficacy were conducted at the baseline visit and on Day 7,
Day 14, Day 28, and Day 90.
The primary efficacy endpoint of the study was the increase (postdrop on Day 14 versus predrop
at baseline) in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14.
Each protocol was conducted at 23 sites in the United States. For Protocol AR-15512-CS301,
there were 465 randomized subjects with the first subject being enrolled on May 23, 2022, and
the last subject completing the study on July 24, 2023. For Protocol AR-15512-CS302, there
were 467 randomized subjects with the first subject being enrolled on August 1, 2022, and the
last subject completing on October 17, 2023.
