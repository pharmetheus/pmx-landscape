1.3 Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA)
Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women worldwide. In the U.S., there are 316,950 new cases of breast cancer and
42,170 deaths from breast cancer estimated for 2025. Although rare (<1%), male patients can also develop breast cancer and may
present at a higher stage than female patients.
Histopathological subtypes of breast cancer are defined by expression of the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR),
and/or human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). Approximately 70% of patients with breast cancer will have ER-positive,
HER2-negative disease. Although associated with a better prognosis than other breast cancer subtypes, if ER-positive, HER2negative breast cancer is advanced and not surgically resectable, or metastatic, it is incurable and associated with a limited life
expectancy.

CDK4/6 inhibitor in combination with endocrine therapy (an AI or fulvestrant). At disease progression, FDA-approved options for
second and later-line treatment options may include endocrine monotherapy with fulvestrant, an AI, or tamoxifen (if not received as
first-line treatment); everolimus in combination with endocrine therapy; targeted therapy such alpelisib in combination with

inhibitors for BRCA-mutated tumors; and chemotherapy such as capecitabine. Inavolisib with palbociclib and fulvestrant was
approved in 2024 for adults with endocrine-resistant, PIK3CA-mutated, HR-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or
metastatic breast cancer, as detected by an FDA-approved test, following recurrence on or after completing adjuvant endocrine
therapy.

2 Therapeutic Context
2.1 Analysis of Condition
The Applicant’s Position:
It is estimated that 2.3 million new cases of breast cancer occurred worldwide in women in 2022
(Bray et al. 2024). Treatment options for patients with breast cancer are largely determined by
ER and HER2 status (Waks and Winer 2019; Cardoso et al. 2024; NCCN 2024). ER-driven
breast cancers remain the most frequent cancers among women and are a major cause of cancerrelated deaths worldwide (Bray et al. 2024).
ER is expressed in more than two thirds of breast cancers, and advances in ET targeting the ER
have reduced the risk of recurrence in EBC and prolonged survival in advanced or MBC,
henceforth referred to as ABC. However, ER+ ABC is incurable with a median OS of 3 to 5
years (Hortobagyi et al. 2022; Meegdes et al. 2023; Cardoso et al. 2024), representing an unmet
medical need to optimize ET options as monotherapy and in combination with targeted therapy.
The FDA’s Assessment:
The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s characterization of advanced and metastatic ERpositive (ER+), HER2-negative (HER2-) breast cancer. Advanced and metastatic ER+ breast
cancer is incurable, and the goal of therapy is palliative.

5 Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
5.1 Executive Summary
Imlunestrant (Inluriyo) is an estrogen receptor (ER) antagonist indicated for the treatment of
adult patients with ER-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative,
ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer, with disease progression following at least
one line of endocrine therapy. The recommended dose of Inluriyo is 400 mg orally once daily.
The nonclinical development program for imlunestrant consisted of studies in mice, rats, and
monkeys to evaluate the pharmacology (primary, secondary, safety), metabolism, general
toxicity, embryo-fetal development, and genotoxicity. During the nonclinical development,
imlunestrant was referred to as LY3484356 tosylate, LSN3484356, and LSN3513322.
Estrogen receptors (ERs) consist of nuclear ERs, extra-nuclear ERs, and G protein-coupled ERs.
Estrogen receptor-alpha (ERα) and estrogen receptor-beta (ERβ) are located in the nucleus and
are encoded by the ESR1 and ESR2 genes, respectively. ERα is expressed in breast, prostate,
uterus, liver, and bone tissues. Mutations in the ESR1 gene, such as Y537S/N and D538G,
contribute to ER+HER2- breast cancer resistance to endocrine therapy.
In primary pharmacology studies, imlunestrant bound to wild-type ERα, mutant (Y537S) ERα,
and wild-type ERβ with Ki values of 0.6 nM, 2.8 nM, and 0.1 nM, respectively. Imlunestrant
induced degradation of ERα in breast cancer cell lines with wild-type and ESR1-mutated
(Y537S/N) ERα and led to inhibition of ER-dependent gene transcription. In vitro, imlunestrant
inhibited the proliferation of both ERα wild-type and ESR1-mutated (Y537S/N) breast cancer
cell lines (IC50s 1.67 to 7 nM). In vivo, daily oral administration of imlunestrant (3 to 30 mg/kg,
28 days) to mice bearing ERα wild-type and ESR1-mutated (Y537S) breast cancer xenografts led
to ≤ 83% tumor growth inhibition (TGI) compared to control.
In secondary pharmacology studies, the binding affinity of imlunestrant to ER was at least 300fold higher than other nuclear hormone receptors such as human glucocorticoid receptor,
androgen receptor, mineralocorticoid receptor, or progesterone receptor (PR) suggesting unlikely
off-target activity at clinical concentrations. The Applicant conducted a non-GLP study in
immature female rats to evaluate the potential agonistic effects of imlunestrant on ERα. Oral
administration of imlunestrant at doses up to 200 mg/kg for 4 days did not result in remarkable
changes to uterine weight or the histopathology of uterus, cervix, and vagina when compared to
control. On the other hand, positive controls estradiol and tamoxifen led to severely increased
uterine weights and histopathology findings in female reproductive organs. The data suggests
that imlunestrant does not have agonistic effects on ERα.

5.3 Pharmacology
Primary pharmacology
Imlunestrant is a next-generation orally bioavailable, noncovalent, SERD and antagonist.
Imlunestrant binds with high affinity to wild-type and mutant (Y537S) ERα and wild-type ERβ
ligand-binding domains with Ki values of 0.64 nM, 2.8 nM, and 0.11 nM, respectively.
Imlunestrant is a potent degrader of ERα wild-type and mutant (Y537N/S) proteins in ER+
breast cancer cell lines, with an IC50 value of 3 nM in MCF7 ESR1 wild-type cells. In MCF7
ESR1 wild-type and Y537N mutant cells, imlunestrant potently inhibited ER-mediated PGR gene
transcription (IC50 41 nM and 13 nM, respectively). Imlunestrant is a pure antagonist and has not
shown agonist activity in MCF7 cells as measured by the absence of PGR expression induction.
Imlunestrant potently inhibits cell proliferation in MCF7 and T47D ESR1 wild-type and Y537N
mutant breast cancer cell lines as well as an ESR1-Y537S mutant PDX-derived cell line. In a
panel of breast cancer cell lines, 11 out of 12 ER+ breast cancer cell lines were sensitive to
imlunestrant (IC50 <100 nM), while all ER- cell lines tested were insensitive (IC50 >2 µM). In
vitro combination of imlunestrant with abemaciclib displayed synergistic anti-proliferative
activity in ER+ breast cancer cell lines.
Imlunestrant demonstrated dose- and time-dependent sustained in vivo target inhibition (>75%
PGR gene expression inhibition up to 96 hours) in ESR1 wild-type (MCF7) and ESR1-Y537S
mutant (ST941/C) xenograft tumors in mice. Imlunestrant was well tolerated and induced
significant tumor growth inhibition and tumor regressions in multiple ER+ breast cancer
xenograft models, including an ESR1-Y537S mutant PDX model. Combination of imlunestrant
with abemaciclib was well tolerated and exhibited enhanced antitumor activity in a T47D breast

General Toxicology
The Applicant’s Position:
The general toxicology profile across repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and monkeys
demonstrates that imlunestrant was well tolerated at doses and exposures that exceeded the
anticipated therapeutic levels in humans.
Single-dose toxicity studies
No single-dose toxicity studies of imlunestrant were conducted. Single-dose studies are typically
not conducted for modern drug development, as short pilot and range-finding studies generally
provide equivalent or more information. In pilot and range-finding studies, single doses were
well tolerated with no mortality or clinical observations indicating acute toxicity from Day 1 to
Day 2.

6 Clinical Pharmacology
6.1 Executive Summary
The FDA’s Assessment:
The Applicant is seeking approval of imlunestrant for the treatment of adults with estrogen
receptor (ER)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, estrogen
receptor-1 (ESR1)-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer with disease progression
following at least one line of endocrine therapy.
The clinical pharmacology review focused on the following key review issues:
1. Recommended dosage for the general population: 400 mg taken orally (PO), once daily
(QD) taken on empty stomach, at least 2 hours before or 1 hour after food. The
recommended dosage was selected based on safety, activity, pharmacokinetic (PK), and
pharmacodynamic (PD) data from Studies EMBER and EMBER-2 and supportive
exposure-response (E-R) analyses for safety and efficacy. Safety and efficacy of the
recommended dosage in the intended patient population was demonstrated in EMBER-3.
The recommendation for administration under fasting conditions is based on the positive
food effect observed when imlunestrant was administered with a low-fat meal and the food
conditions used in all three studies (i.e., imlunestrant at least 2 hours before or 1 hour after
food).
2. Recommended dosage for patients with hepatic impairment (HI): 200 mg QD for patients
with moderate (Child-Pugh B) or severe (Child-Pugh C) hepatic impairment. No dosage
modification is recommended for patients with mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A).
Imlunestrant is hepatically metabolized, and data were available from population PK
analysis (PopPK) and a dedicated HI study to support dosage recommendations for patients
with HI.
3. Dosage modifications for patients receiving concomitant strong CYP3A inhibitors or
inducers with imlunestrant (a substrate of CYP3A): Avoid concomitant use of imlunestrant
with strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers. However, if concomitant use cannot be avoided,
reduce imlunestrant dosage to 200 mg QD when used with strong CYP3A inhibitors, or
increase imlunestrant dosage to 600 mg QD when used with strong CYP3A inducers.

Dose selection discussion
In the Phase 1 EMBER study, imlunestrant was administered at 5 dose levels (200 to 1200 mg
QD). At the 200-mg dose, the trough concentrations of the majority of patients did not exceed
the TEC80 range (therapeutic effective concentration required for 80% target inhibition) and
thereby did not have sustained biologically relevant concentrations over the entire dosing
interval, which was also supported by the popPK study. This threshold has been associated with
tumor regression in xenograft models, and therefore, considered a relevant benchmark. With
doses at and above 400 mg, the TEC80 range was exceeded throughout the dosing interval; 400
mg being the lowest dose that satisfied the desired PK requirements. In addition, there were
notable differences in the frequency of low grade side effects (such as nausea) between 400 mg
and higher doses, which could have potentially impacted long-term adherence to imlunestrant.
Moreover, evidence of clinical activity was observed at the 400-mg dose, with 4 of 6
imlunestrant monotherapy partial responses observed across the study seen at the 400-mg dose
level. Therefore, this was chosen as the registration dose for the EMBER-3 study.
At the same time, the EMBER-2 window-of-opportunity study evaluated 3 dose levels (200 mg,
400 mg, and 800 mg) to assess for effects on key biologically relevant PD biomarkers in

Any Grade Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events
The safety profile of imlunestrant monotherapy was consistent with the mechanism of action of
SERDs and events commonly experienced by patients with ABC.
Incidence of any grade TEAEs was similar across treatment arms. The majority of TEAEs were
Grade 1 or 2.
The most common any grade TEAEs in the imlunestrant arm were fatigue, diarrhea, and
nausea, with fatigue and diarrhea occurring over 5% more frequently in the imlunestrant arm
compared to the SOC arm.
Diarrhea was a frequently reported TEAE for the imlunestrant arm (21.4%). The majority of
events were Grade 1 (60, 18.3%), occurred early in treatment (median time to first onset was 30
days), and did not lead to any discontinuations or dose reductions in either arm. The majority of
patients experienced only 1 episode of diarrhea.
Nausea was a frequently reported TEAE for the imlunestrant arm (17.1%). The majority of
events were Grade 1 (44, 13.5%), occurred during the first month of treatment (36, 11%), median
time to onset (19.5 days) and did not lead to any discontinuations, with 1 dose reduction. The
majority of patients experienced only 1 episode of nausea.
The most common any grade TEAEs in the SOC arm were arthralgia, fatigue, and nausea.
Injection site reaction was the only any grade TEAE to occur 5% more frequently in the SOC
arm compared to the imlunestrant arm (8.0% versus 0%).
Grade ≥3 Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events
The incidence of Grade ≥3 TEAEs was similar in the imlunestrant arm (17.1%) and the SOC
arm (20.7%). These events were predominantly Grade 3 (14.1% versus 16.7%), with lower but
similar incidences for Grade 4 (1.2% versus 2.2%) and Grade 5 (1.8% versus 1.9%) TEAEs for
imlunestrant and SOC arm, respectively. Grade 5 TEAEs are discussed further in the CSR.
The most common Grade ≥3 TEAEs in both the imlunestrant and SOC arms were associated
with laboratory investigations, which were reported with similar incidences in both arms. The
most common Grade ≥3 TEAEs for imlunestrant occurring >1% were anemia (2.1%),
neutropenia (2.1%), and gamma-glutamyltransferase increased (1.5%). The most common Grade
≥3 TEAEs for the SOC arm occurring >1% were anemia (2.8%), neutropenia (1.9%), gammaglutamyltransferase increased (1.9%), and thrombocytopenia (1.2%). There were no clinically
meaningful differences in Grade ≥3 TEAE incidences noted between treatment arms.

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
8.3 Statistical Issues
The FDA’s Assessment:
The randomization for EMBER-3 was stratified on the following factors: prior CDK4/6i therapy,
visceral metastases, and region. The ESR1 mutation status was not a stratification factor at the
time of randomization. Although the key measured demographics and baseline disease
characteristics are generally balanced between treatment arms, there is still a risk of potential
imbalance between treatment arms for unmeasured baseline characteristics.
EMBER-3 did pre-specify formal testing of treatment benefit of PFS and OS in the ESR1mdetected population. EMBER-3 demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in
investigator-assessed PFS in the ESR1m-detected population. These results were robust to
several sensitivity analyses, including a restricted mean survival time analysis. EMBER3 did not
meet its primary endpoint of investigator-assessed PFS in the ITT population and no benefit was
demonstrated in the ESR1m-nd population.
At the time of the primary PFS analyses, the IA1 for the key secondary endpoint of OS for the
comparison of the imlunestrant arm and the TPC arm in the ESR1m-detected population did not
reach statistical significance. However, no trend toward a survival detriment was observed in the
ESR1m-detected population. Therefore, the FDA agrees with the Applicant’s proposed indication
for patients with a detected ESR1m.
OS curves for the comparison of the imlunestrant arm and the TPC arm in the ESR1m-detected
population overlapped for the first 3 months, favored to the TPC arm from about 3 to 7 months,
and began to separate in favor of the imlunestrant arm from Month 7 onward. It was noted that
the number of OS events that occurred before Month 7 was small. Although OS curves began to
separate from Month 7 in favor of the imlunestrant arm, the censoring for OS was heavy starting
from Month 8 since many patients were administratively censored, therefore, there is still
uncertainty in the OS result and further review of the preplanned remaining interim and final OS
analyses for the comparison of the imlunestrant arm and the TPC arm in the ESR1m-detected
population with more mature data is important for both efficacy and safety. The Applicant
committed to submit SAP version 5 pre-specified interim and final OS results as a PMC.

8.4 Conclusions and Recommendations
The FDA’s Assessment:
EMBER-3 was a randomized, active-controlled, open-label, multicenter trial that enrolled 661
patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer to receive either
imlunestrant monotherapy or TPC. The trial demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically
meaningful improvement in investigator-assessed PFS in the ESR1m population. This finding
was further supported by a favorable trend in OS HR in the ESR1m population. The trial did not
meet the prespecified efficacy threshold for the ITT population and exploratory analyses
suggested that there was not benefit in the ESR1m-nd population.
Imlunestrant was associated with increased fatigue, gastrointestinal adverse events, and elevated
transaminases compared to treatment of physician’s choice.

imlunestrant in a fasted state. Additionally, orally administered drugs may be associated with
increased gastrointestinal toxicity.

19.4.2.7

Overall benefit-risk evaluation based on E-R analyses
The Applicant’s Position:
The proposed imlunestrant dose of 400 mg QD is associated with meaningful benefit
and an acceptable safety profile. A 2.5-fold increase in exposure from 400 mg QD is
not expected to have a major impact on safety. Dose reductions for tolerability from
400 mg QD to 200 mg QD are expected to maintain sufficient benefit to warrant
continued treatment. Overall, the totality of data from EMBER-3 demonstrates a
favorable benefit-risk profile for Imlunestrant 400mg QD and a reduced dose of
200mg QD when needed to maintain tolerability.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s initial statement that there was a significant relationship
between imlunestrant exposure and PFS due to the error in the initial analysis as described
above. There were no clear E-R relationships for efficacy or safety of imlunestrant. However, the
proposed dosage of 400 mg QD is supported by the totality of data including the E-R analyses.
The Applicant’s position regarding overall benefit-risk is stated in Section 8.4. See FDA’s
Assessment in Section 1 and Section 8.4.
