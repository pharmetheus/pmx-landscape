organs.

While no animal studies were conducted to assess the reproductive and developmental risks of
mosunetuzumab, a weight of evidence (WoE)-based risk assessment report was submitted. The
assessment is based on a variety of factors, including maternal and/or neonatal toxicities due to
depletion of B-cells and cytokine release-related events. Based on the drug’s mechanism of
action and pharmacodynamic effects, mosunetuzumab may cause depletion of B-cells and
impair infant immune response due to fetal exposure, increasing the risk of opportunistic
infections. Risks associated with T-cell activation include cytokine release and associated
inflammatory effects, which may adversely affect a pregnant woman, and maintenance of
pregnancy. Adverse events that may occur in pregnant animals are those that have been
described in general toxicology studies, e.g., diarrhea, cardiac events, acute phase protein
reactions and inflammatory response, B-cell depletion, etc. The recommendation for using
contraception in females of reproductive potential for 3 months after the last dose of the
product, as reflected in the product label, is based on 5 half-lives of the product.

The nonclinical data of mosunetuzumab submitted to BLA 761263 are adequate to support the
labeling and approval of mosunetuzumab for the proposed indication.
