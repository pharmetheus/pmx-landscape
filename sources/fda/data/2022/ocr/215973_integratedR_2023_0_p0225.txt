II. Interdisciplinary Assessment

3. Introduction

The Applicant has submitted two new drug applications (NDAs) for lenacapavir (LEN) seeking
the indication of treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral(s) A

LEN is a first-in-class human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) capsid inhibitor and has
been formulated for subcutaneous injection and as a tablet for oral use. The Applicant previously
submitted an original investigational new drug (IND) application in 2017 to investigate LEN for
injection and in 2018 to investigate LEN for oral use for the treatment of HIV-1 infection. (Refer
to Section III. 12 for additional regulatory history.) This interdisciplinary assessment combines
the review of NDA 215973 for LEN subcutaneous injection and NDA-215974 for LEN oral
tablets.

The Applicant conducted two clinical trials in support of the findings of safety and efficacy for
LEN, Study GS-US-200-4625 (Study 4625, also referred to as CAPELLA) and Study GS-US-
200-4334 (Study 4334, also referred to as CALIBRATE). Study 4625 is an ongoing phase 2/3
trial designed to evaluate the antiviral activity of lenacapavir administered as an add-on to a
failing regimen (functional monotherapy) for heavily treatment-experienced (HTE) people living
with HIV (PLWH). Study 4334 is a phase 2 trial conducted in a treatment-naive patient
population that serves as supplementary support for the efficacy and safety evaluation of LEN
for the indication of treating the HTE HIV-1 population.

Other currently available therapies used as part of the standard of care for the treatment of HIV-1
are discussed in more detail in Section 1.2 and are listed in Section II.12.1. When constructing
an ARV regimen for heavily-treatment experienced (HTE) PLWH, as noted by the U.S. HIV
Treatment Guidelines (NIH 2022), the expected potency and whether a new drug as part of a
complete regimen has a new mechanistic action are important considerations. Currently, no other
ARV products HIV-1 have been approved in the capsid inhibitor class, nor have any been
approved with a dosing interval as long as every 6 months.
