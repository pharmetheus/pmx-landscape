BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761263, Lunsumio (Mosunetuzumab)}

Other clinically important adverse reactions that occurred in less than 10% of patients treated
with mosunetuzumab include the following:

Blood and lymphatic system disorders: Febrile neutropenia (2%)
Infections and infestations: Urinary tract infection (7%), pneumonia (6%)
Investigations: Aspartate aminotransferase increased (7%)

Metabolism and nutrition disorders: Tumor lysis syndrome (1%)

Neoplasms benign, malignant, and unspecified (including cysts and polyps): Tumor flare
(2%)

The FDA’s Assessment:

Treatment emergent adverse events were assessed from the start of study drug until 30 days
after the last study drug administration. In the B11 RP2D cohort, any grade TEAEs were
reported in 98% and Grade 3 or 4 adverse events occurred in 66%. The FDA analysis of TEAEs
(>5%) using FDA grouped preferred terms (Section 20.5) is displayed in the table below in
decreasing order of incidence by system organ class and preferred term. The most common
TEAEs (excluding laboratory abnormalities) in the B11 RP2D cohort (220%) were cytokine
release syndrome, fatigue, rash, pyrexia, headache, cough and edema. The most common
Grade 3 or 4 adverse events (23%) were pneumonia, cytokine release syndrome, sepsis, and
renal insufficiency. For laboratory abnormalities, see the Laboratory Findings section below.

For adverse reactions, the FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s definition as stated above. Given
the single-arm trial design of GO29781, FDA considers all TEAEs as an adverse drug reaction
unless unequivocally due to the underlying disease or an extraneous cause. For the
mosunetuzumab prescribing information (USPI), the adverse reactions included are based on
the FDA definition.
