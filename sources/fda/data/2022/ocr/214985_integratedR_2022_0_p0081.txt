6.3.2.1. IDSIQ Sleepiness Fitness for Purpose

The development of IDSIQ was initiated in 2008. Several modifications were made to reach the
final version of IDSIQ. In the Advice Letter dated May 3, 2018, the final version of IDSIQ
sleepiness domain was considered acceptable for inclusion as a secondary efficacy endpoint in
phase 3 trials. Evidence from qualitative studies and other psychometric property evaluation
supports that the IDSIQ sleepiness subscale is fit for purpose. Content validity of IDSIQ
sleepiness domain was established and confirmed through literature review, qualitative input
from subjects with insomnia, Rasch analysis, and exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses.
Assessment results of interitem correlations, internal consistency, test-retest reliability, known
groups validity, responsiveness, and meaningful change showed acceptable measurement
properties of IDSIQ sleepiness domain.

The clinical construct it targets is meaningful, relevant, and nonredundant with other outcome
measures used. It comprises four items, one of which assesses sleepiness, which can be thought
of as representing sleep drive, and three of which assess fatigue, which can be thought of as
describing deficit in wake drive. Subjective assessment of these symptoms is expected to have
limited correlation with level of objective sleep deprivation because patients habituate to sleep
deprivation such that subjective effects plateau while performance impairments continue to
increase (Van Dongen et al. 2003). On the other hand, excess daytime sleepiness and fatigue are
common symptoms of sleep deficit and disturbed sleep architecture, and cause distress and
potentially objective impairment in function. Thus, while improvement in this domain may
exhibit independence from improvement in nighttime sleep (assessed either subjectively or
objectively), it is also a treatment outcome desired and valued by patients with insomnia
disorder.

It is reasonably sensitive to change. Anchor-based analyses also showed that there are clear
separations between the 50 mg treatment arm and placebo arm in the eCDF curves by treatment
arm at the estimated thresholds of clinical meaningful within patient change in IDSIQ sleepiness
domain scores from baseline to Month 1 and from baseline to Month 3. However, separation was
minimal between the 25 mg treatment arm and placebo arm in the eCDF curves at the estimated
