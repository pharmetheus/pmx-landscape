The FDA’s Assessment:

The comparison of response rates from a single-arm trial against a historical rate is challenging
because such a historical benchmark is difficult to verify and needs to be carefully reviewed and
agreed upon prior to conducting any hypothesis test. Therefore, the hypothesis test or
comparison against the historical control CR rate of {3% is considered as supportive only, and
the corresponding p-value is nominal.

Although CR rate was prespecified as the primary endpoint in the protocol and statistical
analysis plan, FDA considers the intermediate endpoint of ORR and duration of response as
assessed by an independent review facility according to standard criteria for NHL (Cheson 2007)
to support substantial evidence of efficacy. In addition, the Applicant did not submit data (trial-
level or patients-level) to support the use of CR rate as an intermediate endpoint to establish
efficacy.

As noted above, the Applicant based the efficacy hypothesis testing on a historical CR rate. In
single-arm trials, the FDA generally uses these historically based calculations for sample size
determination and does not use the demonstration of statistical significance as the ultimate
determination of effectiveness. In NHL, the observed ORR with a 95% Cl and duration of
response are used in the overall assessment of efficacy in the context of the observed safety
findings to inform the benefit-risk of a given product.

For the B11 FL RP2D expansion cohort, the ORR rate is 80% (95% Cl: 70.3%, 87.7%) and the
estimated median DOR is 22.8 months (95% Cl: 9.7, not estimable). The primary efficacy results
were able to be reproduced by the statistical reviewer.

Among 72 patients who achieved CR or PR, median time to response was 1.4 months (range:
1.1, 8.9); the majority of these responders (94%) achieved their response by 4 months since the
start of treatment and only 6% responded after 4 months. Among 54 patients who achieved CR,
median time to response was 3 months (range: 1.2, 18.9).
