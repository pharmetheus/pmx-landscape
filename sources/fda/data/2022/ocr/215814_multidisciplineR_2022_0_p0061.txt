6.2. Summary of Clinical Pharmacology Assessment
6.2.1 Pharmacology and Clinical Pharmacokinetics

Olutasidenib is a potent, selective, small-molecule inhibitor of mutated IDH1. In vitro,
olutasidenib significantly inhibited mutated IDH1-R132H and IDH1-R132C proteins without
inhibition of wild-type IDH1 or mutated IDH2 proteins. Olutasidenib inhibition of mutant IDH1
led to decreased 2-HG levels in in vitro and in vivo xenograft models. A significant and
sustained decrease in 2-HG levels, and a sustained reduction in blast counts with restored normal
cellular differentiation were also observed in blood of AML patients with IDH1 mutations
treated with olutasidenib 150 mg twice daily in clinical studies.

Absorption:

e The median tmax of olutasidenib following a single oral dose of 150 mg is approximately 4
hours.

e A high-fat meal (approximately 800 to 1,000 calories, with approximately 50% of total
caloric content of the meal from fat) increased olutasidenib Cmax by 191% and AUCins by
83%.

Distribution:

e@ Mean (SD) apparent volume of distribution of olutasidenib is 319 (89) L. The plasma protein
binding of olutasidenib is approximately 93% and the blood-to-plasma ratio is 0.5 to 0.7.

Elimination (Metabolism and Excretion):

e The mean half-life (t2) of olutasidenib is approximately 67 hours and the mean (SD)
apparent oral clearance (CL/F) of olutasidenib is 4 (2.5) L/h.

Metabolism:

¢ Olutasidenib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4, with minor contributions from
CYP2C8, CYP2C9 and CYP2C19.

e Based on a mass balance study, olutasidenib is the predominant circulating component
(92%) in plasma and no major circulating metabolites were observed. Olutasidenib
metabolic pathways involve N-dealkylation, demethylation, oxidative deamination
followed by oxidation, mono-oxidation with subsequent glucuronidation.

Excretion:

60
