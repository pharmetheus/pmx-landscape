5.1. Executive Summary

Mosunetuzumab (RO7030816; also known as BTCT4465A) is a full-length, humanized

immunoglobulin (Ig) G1 bispecific antibody, produced in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, and

directed against CD20 and CD3. |

both healthy and malignant B-cells, and the CD3 receptor is expressed on the surface of T-cells.
Mosunetuzumab promotes the T-cell dependent killing of B-cells through CD20 and therefore,
is being developed for the treatment of patients with B-cell hematologic malignancies.

The proposed mechanism of action of mosunetuzumab was demonstrated with a variety of in
vitro and in vivo pharmacology studies. A version of the antibody manufactured in OM was
used in early nonclinical studies prior to the development of the CHO-manufactured clinical
product. A bridging PK/PD study in transgenic mice showed that the exposure profile and effect
on B-cell levels were comparable between the 2 versions of the antibodies. In vitro activity
studies showed that binding to CD3 and CD20 (CD3 Kd =40 nM; CD20 Kd=68-83 nM), T-cell
activation (EC50 = 128-149 ng/mL), B-cell killing (EC50 = 36-60 ng/mL) and cytokine induction
(IL-2, IL-6, IFNy, and TNFa) were also comparable between the antibodies. The cynomolgus
monkey is a relevant species for toxicology studies based on mosunetuzumab binding to CD20

and CD3 in cynomolgus monkeys.

The Applicant conducted multiple single and repeat dose pilot toxicity studies to find a dose
and dosing regimen that would minimize ADA formation and cytokine-release syndrome (CRS)-
like toxicities. One such design was the step-up dosing approach which was used in the
repeated-dose study. The GLP studies in cynomolgus monkeys included single-dose and
