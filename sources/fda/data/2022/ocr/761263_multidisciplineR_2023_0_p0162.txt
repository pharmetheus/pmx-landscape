NAE due to Psychiatric System Disorders in the B11 FL Cohort

In the B11 FL cohort, NAEs under the psychiatric system disorders SOC, 23% of patients in the
FL cohort experienced a psychiatric system disorder. Grade 1 events occurred in 14% of
patients (13/90) and Grade 2 events in 10% (9/90). The most common manifestations were
insomnia (13%), anxiety (4%), and depression (3%).

There was 1 serious NAE due to a psychiatric system disorder in the B11 FL cohort due to fear.
There were no dose interruptions. In 4% of patients (4/90), the psychiatric disorder was
unresolved at the data cutoff, largely due to insomnia in 3% of patients.

ICANS

There were two patients in the B11 RP2D cohort who experienced ICANS, a Grade 1 event anda
Grade 2 event. The Grade 1 event occurred in the setting of a Grade 2 CRS event and resolved
within 3 days. There was no information provided by the Applicant on the manifestations of the
ICANS event. The Grade 2 event occurred in the setting of Grade 4 CRS and the primary
manifestation was a confusional state. The ICANS event, along with CRS, was treated with
corticosteroids (and tocilizumab) and resolved within 2 days.

Driving Restrictions
Prior to protocol version 10 (September 26, 2019), patients treated with mosunetuzumab were

advised to refrain from driving or operating dangerous machinery for the first 3 cycles of
treatment. Following September 26, 2019, no baseline driving restriction was in place for
patients with follicular lymphoma and an event-based restriction was in place. Essentially, any
patient that developed CRS or any adverse reaction that impairs consciousness should refrain
from driving or operating dangerous machinery. The table below summarizes the patients
enrolled before and after September 26, 2019.

Enrolled B11 FL B11 RP2D
N=90 N=218

Before September 26, 2019 28 (31%) 102 (47%)

After September 26, 2019 62 (69%) 116 (53%)

With the initiation of the event-driven driving restrictions, no additional safety events occurred
compared to the time in which the baseline driving restrictions were in place. Additionally, 53%
to 69% of the patients were enrolled during the event-driven approach. Therefore, the driving
restrictions to be included in the label will be consistent with the event-driven approach for
patients that experience CRS or any adverse reactions that impairs consciousness.
