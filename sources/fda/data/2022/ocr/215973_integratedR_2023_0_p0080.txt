On December 14, 2022, the Applicant responded to another request for information regarding
any available information of ISRs. Additional follow-up was presented through Week 80 of
Study 4334, Week 26 of Study GS-US-536-5816 (phase 1b, LEN + broadly neutralizing
antibodies), and Week 8 of Study GS-US-412-5624 (LEN for PrEP in cisgender women).
Overall, the findings were consistent with prior assessments as discussed above. In the Week 80
analysis of Study 4334, there were no new Grade 3 or higher ISRs reported. One additional
subject discontinued due to an ISR (induration, Grade 1); making a total of 4 subjects that
discontinued due to a LEN-related ISR.

Conclusion

ISRs can be characterized as having a short-term phase lasting a few days (e.g., pain, swelling,
and erythema) or a long-term phase (e.g., indurations and nodules), some of which were still
resolving over a period of weeks to months. Indurations and nodules were generally described as
palpable and elongated (1 to 4 cm), not painful, and not associated with any erythema. Nodules
were more visible in subjects who were leaner with little fat tissue in the abdomen. Several
subjects in Study 4625 had indurations or nodules that had not fully resolved at the time of the
interim data cut dates. Overall, it appears nodules tend to resolve more slowly than indurations;
however, there may be overlap in choice of preferred terms used by investigators that could also
be characterized similarly by terms such as “mass” or “granuloma”. Due to the lack of
understanding of the histopathology at the injection sites in patients who experienced longer
times to resolution of indurations and nodules, and the relationship with the unique properties of
the drug product solution, it is unclear what is driving persistence in some subjects. Given the
concern about the incompatibility of LEN with the borosilicate glass, compatibility with the

© vas further evaluated. The available nonclinical and clinical data do not support
that the presence of particulates are responsible for the development or persistence of ISRs. The
available dermatopathology has revealed expected responses to foreign body that are also
expected from the presence of the SC LEN drug depot.

Overall, DDD agrees with the Applicant’s assessment, that ISRs most likely the result of the
presence of the drug depot, and the proposed labeling describing ISRs, including injection site
nodules and indurations. In addition, DDD continues to recommend that human histopathology
(e.g., punch biopsy) be obtained in ongoing or future clinical trials; the presence of findings such
as necrosis, hypersensitivity, granulomas, foreign bodies, fibrous tissue, etc. can aid in
understanding the pathophysiology of nodule formation. Additionally, where possible, injection
site nodules should be followed to resolution or stabilization. Please also refer to the DDD
Memorandum completed November 18, 2022 for additional details.
