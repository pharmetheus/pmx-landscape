Drug Similarity VAS is a unipolar scale (0: definitely not, 100: definitely so). The data for Drug
Similarity VAS were collected at hour 12 post-dose. Drugs in the comparisons with each treatment in the
study were amphetamine, barbiturates, benzodiazepines, cannabis, cocaine, ecstasy, ketamine, lysergic
acid diethylamide, methamphetamine, opioids, phencyclidine, psilocybin, and sedatives. Table 13 shows
summary statistics for subjects’ responses to the comparisons between each treatment and each drug. The
N denotes the number of subjects who gave a response for such a comparison. For example, compared to
amphetamine 12 subjects gave responses to all treatments. Compared to barbiturates only one subject
gave a zero response to all treatments. Because 0 denotes “definitely not”, the N for each treatment
compared to each drug should be 63. It is not clear to this reviewer, how the investigator instructed
subjects to respond to questionnaires for Drug Similarly VAS, and why Ns are not all equal to 63.
Nevertheless, almost all subjects responded to all treatments when compared to benzodiazepines. Because
the Ns may be different, drug to drug comparisons for these summary statistics for a treatment is not
meaningful. For example, only one subject compared 150 mg suvorexant to ketamine with a similarity
score 84, and the mean score is 84 (84/1). Comparing 84 to the mean similarity score of 61.8 based on 63
subjects who compared 150 mg suvorexant to benzodiazepines is not meaningful.
