Daridorexant (Quviviq) for Insomnia

Dimension

Evidence and Uncertainties

Conclusions and Reasons

Current
Treatment
Options

« Psychotherapeutic and behavioral interventions are
considered first line, with Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for
Insomnia recommended by most practice guidelines.

© Two other dual orexin receptor antagonists are approved for
the treatment of insomnia disorder: suvorexant and
lemborexant

« Nonbenzodiazepine GABA receptor agonists are indicated
for the short-term treatment of insomnia (zolpidem) and
treatment of insomnia (eszopiclone).

© Doxepin, ramelteon, and certain benzodiazepines (e.g.,
quazepam) are also approved for the treatment of insomnia.

¢ Several barbiturates (e.g., secobarbital) are labelled for the
short-term treatment of insomnia.

° Efficacy of some pharmacological treatments are limited by
duration, development of tolerance, or are efficacious in only
sleep onset or sleep maintenance but not both.

* Safety concerns with available pharmacological treatments
include next-morning residual effects, fall risk, complex sleep
behaviors, physical dependence and withdrawal, and risk of
nonmedical use, abuse, and addiction.

« There are limitations to the efficacy and safety of available
treatments, and efficacy described in approved products is
largely based on measures of sleep onset and sleep
maintenance without evidence of effects on distress or
impairment.

Benefit

Primary efficacy endpoints

« Wake after sleep onset (WASO) Month 1 showed statistically
significant treatment effects for the 25 mg dose in Trial ID-
078A302 and the 25 mg and 50 mg doses in Trial ID-
078A301.

« Latency to persistent sleep (LPS) Month 1 demonstrated
statistically significant treatment effects for the 50-mg dose
and the 25-mg dose in Trial ID-078A301. A dose response
relationship between the 25 mg and 50 mg doses was
observed for effects on sleep parameters in Trial ID-
078A301. Sensitivity analysis using log-transformed data
(viewed as preferred handling of the observed data by the
statistical review team) in Trial ID-078A302 yielded a
statistically significant treatment effect for the 25-mg dose.

Primary efficacy endpoints

« The application provides substantial evidence of
effectiveness for the treatment of insomnia based on
statistically significant treatment effects on the two primary
efficacy endpoints, WASO and LPS, in two adequate and
well-controlled studies.

Secondary efficacy endpoints

« The subjective total sleep time (STST) supports the primary
efficacy endpoints and merits inclusion in the description of
the study results in the prescribing information (PI).
