6.3.2.2. Adequacy of Evidence

Given the evidence provided for the IDSIQ’s fitness for purpose, is there substantial
evidence for efficacy in this domain?

The 50-mg dose demonstrated superiority to placebo in the prespecified analysis for Trial ID-
078A301 on the IDSIQ sleepiness domain score. The treatment effect was approximately 2
points at both 1 and 3 months with approximately 10% of patients (placebo subtracted)
experiencing a clinically meaningful improvement on this measure (see page 5361). However, no
significant effect was found for the 25-mg dose in either Trial ID-078A301 or ID-078A302. See
Sections 6.2.2.2 and 6.2.2.3. Thus, the efficacy at 50 mg is not independently supported by o other

6.3.2.3. Conclusion

We find the IDSIQ sleepiness domain subscale to be fit for purpose. However, in light of only a
single study (Trial ID-078A301) and single dose (50 mg) supporting statistically significant
improvement on the IDSIQ sleepiness domain, we conclude that there is not sufficient evidence

independent substantiation of this finding. In addition, the 50 mg results are not supported by
other contextual factors such as another statistically significant result in another active treatment
arm at a different dose or in another closely related outcome measure. The small p-value on the
50 mg dose is not adequate to obviate the need for confirmatory evidence in this condition for
this outcome, because confirming the result would not be impracticable or unethical and even a
robust result can be a result of bias or chance. This shortcoming may be addressed by a second
adequate and well-controlled study to substantiate the effects of the 50-mg dose on next day
sleepiness in patients with insomnia disorder.
