Conclusions and Reasons

Benefit

Efficacy of lenacapavir (LEN) in HTE patients with multidrug
resistance (MDR) who were failing treatment due to resistance,
intolerance, or safety considerations was established in Study
GS-US-200-4625 (Study 4625), a phase 2/3 study conducted in two
cohorts.

The Study 4625 design was consistent with FDA’s Guidance for
Industry (EDA 2015).

Cohort 1 of Study 4625 was divided into two periods. During the first
period, the Functional Monotherapy Period (FMP), 36 subjects in
Study 4625 were randomized 2:1 to receive 14 days of oral LEN or
placebo, respectively, in addition to their failing regimen. During the
second period, the Maintenance Period, all subjects were unblinded
and dosed with LEN (via subcutaneous injection following the
14-day oral lead-in) plus an optimized background regimen (OBR)
through at least 52 weeks of follow-up. A second cohort enrolled 36
subjects, all of whom received open-label LEN plus an OBR, in
parallel with Cohort 1.

The primary efficacy endpoint for Study 4625 was the proportion of
subjects in Cohort 1 achieving a reduction in HIV-1 RNA of

20.5 logio copies/mL from baseline at the end of the 14-day FMP.
The difference in proportions in HIV-1 RNA logio copies/mL from
Day 1 to Day 14 (LEN minus placebo) was -70.8 (95% confidence
interval, -34.9, -90.0), demonstrating statistical superiority of LEN
compared to placebo.

Early HIV-1 RNA decreases of >0.5 log1o copies/mL in prior trials
have been associated with reduction in clinical disease progression
according to the FDA's Guidance for Industry (EDA 2015).

The key secondary efficacy endpoints assessed durability of
virologic response following the FMP. After 26 and 52 weeks, 81%
and 83% of participants continued to have HIV-1 RNA suppression
(<50 copies/mL), respectively.

LEN has demonstrated virologic activity in HTE
patients with MDR HIV-1 infection who have
limited treatment options.

The clinical study design and endpoints were
appropriate for the HTE population with limited
treatment options.

Reductions in HIV-1 RNA levels are highly
predictive of meaningful clinical benefit, and LEN
functional monotherapy was superior to placebo
for reducing HIV-1 RNA over 14 days.

Study 4625 was uncontrolled during the
maintence period, so there are limitations in
performing comparative analyses. However, the
Week 52 analysis suggests that LEN contributes
to the durability of virologic response when added
to an OBR.

Availability of LEN would provide a new and
effective first-in-class treatment option for HTE
patients with multidrug resistant HIV-1 infection
and limited alternative options due to resistance,
intolerance, or safety considerations. Patients
who achieve virologic suppression with LEN plus
an OBR would have a reduced risk of HIV/AIDS-

related morbidity and mortality.
