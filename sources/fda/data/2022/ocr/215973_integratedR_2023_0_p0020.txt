LEN is a first-in-class HIV-1 capsid (CA) inhibitor and has been formulated for subcutaneous
(SC) injection and as a tablet for oral use. The Applicant previously submitted an original
investigational new drug application in 2017 to investigate LEN for injection and in 2018 to
investigate LEN for oral use for the treatment of HIV-1 infection. (Refer to Section 12 for
additional regulatory history.) This interdisciplinary assessment combines the review of NDA
215973 for LEN SC injection and NDA 215974 for LEN oral tablets.

The Applicant conducted two clinical studies in support of the findings of safety and efficacy for
LEN, Study GS-US-200-4625 (Study 4625, also referred to as CAPELLA) and Study GS-US-
200-4334 (Study 4334, also referred to as CALIBRATE). Study 4625 is an ongoing phase 2/3
study designed to evaluate the antiviral activity of LEN administered as an add-on to a failing
regimen (functional monotherapy) for heavily treatment-experienced (HTE) people living with
HIV (PLWH). Study 4334 is a phase 2 study conducted in a treatment-naive patient population
and serves as supplementary support for the efficacy and safety evaluation of LEN for the
indication of treating the HTE HIV-1 population. Of note, the results of Studies 4625 and 4334
have been published [Week 26, (Segal-Maurer et al. 2022)] and presented at the Conference on
Retroviral and Opportunistic Infections 2022 [Week 52, (Ogbuagu et al. 2022) and Week 54,

Gupta et al. 2022)], respectively.

Other currently available therapies used as part of the standard of care for the treatment of HIV-1
are discussed in more detail in Section 2. When constructing an ARV regimen for HTE PLWH,
as noted by the U.S. HIV Treatment Guidelines (DHHS 2022a), the expected potency and
whether a new drug as part of a complete regimen has a new mechanistic action are important
considerations. Currently, no other ARV product in the CA inhibitor class for HIV-1 has been
approved, nor have any been approved with a dosing interval as long as every 6 months (DHHS
2022b).
