14C-FT-2102 After a Single Oral
Administration to Rats / 8362183

The distribution of olutasidenib was investigated in vivo in Sprague Dawley
(SD) and Long Evans (LE) rats administered radiolabeled olutasidenib
orally by using quantitative whole-body autoradiography (QWBA). Tissue
distribution of radioactivity following a single oral dose of 150 mg/kg of
[4C]-olutasidenib with specific activity of 100 uCi/kg was evaluated
through 72 hours (SD) or 168 hours (LE) after dosing.

The distribution of radioactivity was moderately widespread at 0.5 hours
post-dose. A notable difference was that a biphasic distribution was
observed in most tissues for SD rats, but not for LE rats. Tissues with the
highest radioactivity concentrations common to both rat strains were the
small intestine, cecum, and liver. Radioactivity concentrations were higher
in the uveal tract and pigmented skin of partially pigmented LE rats and
lasted for a longer duration than for albino SD rats, indicating that there was
an affinity of C-olutasidenib - derived radioactivity for tissues containing
melanin.
