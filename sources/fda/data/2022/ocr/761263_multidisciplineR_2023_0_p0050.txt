BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761263, Lunsumio (Mosunetuzumab)}

CNS microscopic lesions in this animal were more extensive than those identified in the
remaining animals and were considered causal for the convulsion. Following chronic dosing,
mosunetuzumab was associated with microscopic evidence of upper urinary tract and/or
enteric infection, which resulted in moribundity and early euthanasia in two animals. The
increased susceptibility to infection was considered to be secondary to prolonged B-cell
depletion, a pharmacologic effect of mosunetuzumab. With the exception of the above
findings, no other toxicities were observed following 26 weeks of mosunetuzumab
administration at the highest dose tested, corresponding to approximately 5-fold multiples
relative to the steady-state exposure (AUC) in human at the recommended clinical dose of
1/2/60/30 mg.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The GLP-compliant single-dose toxicity (IV/SC) and 26-week repeat-dose toxicity (IV) studies in
cynomolgus monkeys were reviewed and we generally agree with the adverse findings
described by the Applicant. Detailed reviews of the 26-week study and the summary of the
single-dose toxicity study are included.

Study title/ Study number: BTCT4465A: 26 Week Multiple Dose Intravenous (Infusion)
Administration Toxicity and Toxicokinetic Study in the Cynomolgus Monkey
Key Study Findings

e@ Two animals (one at 0.1 mg/kg and one at 0.5 mg/kg) were euthanized prematurely in
moribund conditions due to immune suppression/enteric infection.

¢ Clinical signs including red/swollen face, swollen eyes, warm to touch, subdued, and
emesis occurred mostly during the step dosing phase, consistent with BTCT4465A-
related cytokine release.

e Consistent with BTCT4465A pharmacological activity, markedly reduced B lymphocytes
in the blood and lymphoid tissues, transient cytokine release and T-cell activation, and
associated transient decrease in circulating T-cells and natural killer cells were
observed at both dose levels tested.

© Clinical pathology showed increased indicators of inflammation (monocyte,
lymphocyte, eosinophil, and basophil counts and globulin, CRP and fibrinogen
concentrations).

e BTCT4465A-induced toxicity was observed within the systemic vasculature
(vascular/perivascular inflammatory cell infiltration) in multiple tissues (brain, heart,
liver, kidney, gastrointestinal tract, gall bladder), lymphoid tissues (decreased germinal
centers/follicles/GALT), bone marrow (increased cellularity consistent with
inflammation), kidney (inflammatory infiltration/tubular basophilia, likely related to
ascending urinary tract infection), and pancreas (decreased exocrine granulation) in
animals administered 0.1 or 0.5 mg/kg.
