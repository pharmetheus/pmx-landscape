From a COA perspective, the PSAAD appears fit for purpose in this context of use and could be
described in labeling provided the clinical trial data are supportive. While the PP-NRS , in
general, is acceptable to evaluate itch severity at its worst in this context of use, in consultation
with Clinical and Biostatistical Reviewers, it was determined that the clinical trial data derived
from the scale was limited by substantial missing data due to (1) data transmission issues for
some subjects/sites and (2) subjects not bringing eDiaries to on-site visits. Similarly, while the
NTIS, in general, is acceptable to evaluate itch severity at its worst in this context of use, in
consultation with Clinical Reviewers, it was determined that this instrument did provide any
additional information than the PP-NRS and PSAAD item 1 (itch) as nighttime itch is assessed
within these instruments (See Section 2 for additional details).

2 REVIEW CONCLUSIONS

1.

From a measurement perspective, we do not find any issues that would preclude labeling
claims based on the PSAAD total score, provided the clinical trial data are supportive.
The PSAAD was reviewed for content validity and other measurement properties, as
well as the applicant’s proposed threshold for meaningful within-patient score change.
The PSAAD is appropriate for measurement of atopic dermatitis signs and symptoms
and validly and reliably measures clinically relevant and important atopic dermatitis
signs and symptoms to patients. While there were limitations in the anchor-based
methods due to anchor scale selection (see Section 5.5.6), the change from baseline in
the PSAAD total score showed separation between the treatment and placebo arm across
a range that likely includes a clinically meaningful change threshold for Studies
B7451012 and B7451013. Additionally, we note that the treatment arms appear to
experience change in every item compared to the placebo arm; the total score does not
seem to be overly influenced by specific items.

In general, the PP-NRS is acceptable to evaluate itch severity at its worst. However, due
to the single administration of the PP-NRS at the clinic visit, as well as, the large amount
of missing data from the trials (e.g., Studies B7451012 and B7451013 had missing PP-
NRS data ranging from 20% to 36% for the study treatment arms at Week 12), it is
difficult to interpret the data and may be misleading to include in the label without
supportive data. To provide supportive information on itch, it was recommended to
Clinical and Biostatistics that the PSAAD item 1 (itch) item be explored in conjunction
with the PP-NRS as it was administered daily and does not have high missingness.
Based on supportive analyses with PSADD item 1, it supported improvements in itch
consistent with PP-NRS. As such, with input from Clinical and Biostatistics, a
descriptive claim related to itch improvement appears reasonable.

The NTIS, while generally acceptable to evaluate night time itch severity at its worst,
was not formally tested in the statistical hierarchy and does not add any additional
information regarding itch severity beyond the PP-NRS and PSAAD item Mitch
