Conclusions and Reasons

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is the second most common non-Hodgkin
lymphoma.

Follicular lymphoma remains an incurable disease and relapse is nearly
universal.

Follicular lymphoma is a serious and life-
threatening disease.

Treatment options for relapsed and refractory follicular lymphoma include
anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies, immunochemotherapy, an
immunomodulatory agent, a PI3K inhibitor, an EZH2 inhibitor, and anti-
CD19 CAR-T cell therapy.

New treatments are needed for patients with
relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma.

Study GO29781 was an open-label, multicenter, multi-cohort study that
included 90 patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma who
had received at least 2 prior systemic therapies.

Mosunetuzumab was administered as follows: 1 mg on Cycle 1 Day 1; 2
mg on Cycle 1 Day 8; 60 mg on Cycle 1 Day 15 and Cycle 2 Day 1; and 30
mg on Cycle 3 Day 1 onwards, administered every 3 weeks.

Objective response rate (ORR) per independent review facility in the 90
patients with relapsed and refractory follicular lymphoma was 80% (95%
Cl: 70%, 88%), with 60% achieving a complete response.

With a median follow-up for duration of response (DOR) of 14.9 months,
the median DOR was 22.8 months (95% Cl: 10, not reached).

Based on ORR with durability, mosunetuzumab
has clinically meaningful activity in patients
with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma
who have received two or more lines of
systemic therapy.

Safety was evaluated in 218 patients treated at the recommended dose
and regimen (1/2/60/30 mg).

The median duration of treatment was 8 cycles.

The most common adverse events (220%) were cytokine release
syndrome, fatigue, rash, pyrexia, and headache.

The safety profile of mosunetuzumab is
acceptable in the intended population.
