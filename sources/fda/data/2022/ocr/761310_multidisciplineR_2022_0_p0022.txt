e In Study 0417, the most common (220%) adverse reactions that
occurred in patients treated with mirvetuximab soravtansine-gynx
were vision impairment, fatigue, increased transaminases, nausea,
keratopathy, abdominal pain, decreased lymphocytes, peripheral
neuropathy, diarrhea, decreased albumin, constipation, increased
alkaline phosphatase, dry eye, decreased magnesium, decrased
leukocytes, decreased neutrophils, and decreased hemoglobin. The
most common Grade 23 adverse reactions were keratopathy, vision
impairment, and abdominal pain.

e Fatal adverse reactions occurred in 2% of patients and included small
intestinal obstruction and pneumonitis.

© Ocular toxicity is an important safety signal associated with
mirvetuximab soravtansine-gynx, experienced by 61% patients
enrolled to Study 0417.

¢ There is a boxed warning to alert providers to the potential for severe
ocular toxicity in patients receiving mirvetuximab soravtansine in the
USPI.

e Additional important safety signals associated with mirvetuximab
soravtansine-gynx include pneumonitis, peripheral neuropathy, and
risk of embryo-fetal toxicity, which are listed under Warnings and
Precautions in the USPI.

The toxicity profile of mirvetuximab
soravtansine-gynx is acceptable for the
indicated patient population with platinum-
resistant ovarian cancer and limited
therapeutic options.

Safe use of mirvetuximab soravtansine-gynx
can be managed through labeling, which
includes ocular toxicity as a boxed warning
and the inclusion of pneumonitis, peripheral
neuropathy, and embryo-fetal toxicity under
Warnings and Precautions.

No REMS is indicated.
