5.5. Toxicology

5.5.1. General Toxicology

The Applicant's Posit

Mosunetuzumab has been evaluated in single-dose and repeat-dose toxicology studies up to
26 weeks in cynomolgus monkeys following IV administration. Key mosunetuzumab-related
toxicities included post-dose CRS-like toxicities that were primarily limited to the first dose,
reversible findings of vascular/perivascular inflammatory cell infiltrates, and increased
susceptibility to infection following chronic dosing due to sustained B-cell depletion. All of the
findings were considered pharmacologically-mediated effects.

The most consistent findings were CRS-like toxicities following IV administration. They were
primarily limited to the first dose and included tachycardia, fever, and acute phase reactions at
> 0.1 mg/kg, clinical signs (e.g., emesis, hypoactivity) at > 0.2 mg/kg, and hypotension and
evidence of minimal hepatocellular cell injury at 1 mg/kg. Microscopic findings of
vascular/perivascular inflammatory cell infiltrates were present primarily within the brain, with
decreased incidence in spinal cord and sciatic nerve in single and repeat-dose studies, and were
also present infrequently in other organs in the 26-week toxicity study. In the brain, the findings
were accompanied with local microglial reaction; however, no neuronal degeneration was
observed. These findings were reversible and were considered secondary to
cytokine/chemokine-induced up-regulation of chemokine receptors and adhesion molecules in
the vessels of the brain and immune cell activation, margination, and transmigration. In-life
neurologic abnormalities were limited to one incidence of convulsion in one animal on Day 8
following a total of 2 mg/kg dose, corresponding to Cmax and AUCpayo-7 values approximately
50-fold and 20-fold, respectively, higher than those in patients exposed to mosunetuzumab
