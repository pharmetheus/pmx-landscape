Following oral administration, daridorexant was quickly absorbed with Tmax values of 0.3 hours
in rats, 0.8 to 2 hours in dogs under fasted condition, and 2.5 hours in dogs under fed condition.

slightly higher under fed condition (1.3-fold relative to fasted condition). Plasma clearance was
also quick in both rats and dogs, with a terminal half-life T1/2 of 0.5 to 2.1 and 2.5 to 4.1 hours,
respectively.

In rats, drug exposure (AUC) increased slightly more than dose proportionally whereas in dogs,
drug exposure increased dose proportionally. Oral bioavailability was 10 to 30% and 31 to 58%
in rats and dogs, respectively. The clearance in female rats was slightly lower than males, leading
to higher exposure levels and higher oral bioavailability (30%) than male rats (~15%).

13.1.2.2. Distribution
13.1,2.2.1. Plasma Protein Binding

The protein binding of daridorexant is high in both animal and human plasma samples (>95%
bound in the mouse, rat, dog, and rabbit plasma; and >97% bound in human plasma, Table 52;
Studies B-13.202 and 17.061). In human samples, saturation of protein binding of daridorexant
was observed at concentrations >15 jtg/mL, which was likely due to saturable binding to the a1-
acid glycoprotein (a1 AGP, 98.1%, 93.3% and 65.7% bound at 1.5, 5, and 15 g/mL daridorexant
concentrations, respectively). However, it should be noted that at the proposed MRHD of 50 mg,
the clinical Cmax of daridorexant was ~1 j1g/mL; therefore, the saturable binding is unlikely to
oceur at clinically relevant levels. This was further supported by findings from the ex vivo assay
using samples collected from the clinical study (Study AC-078-101), in which consistently high
protein binding (99.7 to 99.9%) was observed without saturation in samples collected from the
25-mg and 200-mg dose groups.

Similarly, the protein binding of the M1 and M3 metabolites was high in both animal (mouse,
rat, dog, and rabbit) and human samples. The protein binding of the M10 metabolite was
relatively lower than that of daridorexant or the M1 and M3 metabolites in animal species (~70
to 80% bound) but remained to be relatively high in human samples (+92% bound, Table 52).
