Conclusions and Reasons

Current
Treatment
Options

* For patients with multidrug resistant (MDR) HIV-1 infection,
providers must individually tailor combination treatment
regimens based on previous antiretroviral (ARV) exposure,
cumulative viral resistance testing, drug safety and
tolerability, and comorbid conditions.

¢ The resulting ARV regimens are often burdensome, less
well-tolerated, and associated with inadequate HIV-1 RNA
suppression.

¢ HTE patients with limited treatment options need new and
effective therapies that lack cross-resistance with
commercially available products, have a favorable
tolerability profile, and allow for convenient dosing.

Benefit

© Efficacy of lenacapavir (LEN) in HTE patients who were
failing treatment due to multidrug resistance, intolerance, or
safety considerations was established in Study GS-US-200-
4625 (Study 4625), a phase 2/3 trial conducted in two
cohorts.

« The Study 4625 trial design was consistent with FDA’s
Guidance for Industry Human Immunodeficiency Virus-1
Infection: Developing Antiretroviral Drugs for Treatment
(November 2015).

* Cohort 1 of Study 4625 was divided into two periods. During
the first period, the Functional Monotherapy Period (FMP),
36 subjects in Study 4625 were randomized 2:1 to receive
14 days of oral LEN or placebo, respectively, in addition to
their failing regimen. During the second period, the
Maintenance Period, all subjects were unblinded and dosed
with LEN (via subcutaneous injection following the 14-day
oral lead-in) plus an optimized background regimen (OBR)
through at least 52 weeks of follow-up. A second cohort
enrolled 36 subjects, all of whom received open-label LEN
plus an OBR, in parallel with Cohort 1.

© The primary efficacy endpoint for Study 4625 was the
proportion of subjects in Cohort 1 achieving a reduction in
HIV-1 RNA of 20.5 logio copies/mL from baseline at the end
of the 14-day FMP. The difference in proportions in HIV-1
RNA logio copies/mL from Day 1 to Day 14 (LEN minus
placebo) was -70.8 (95% Cl, -34.9, -90.0), demonstrating
statistical superiority of LEN compared to placebo.

o Early HIV-1 RNA decreases of >0.5 logio copies/mL in prior
trials have been associated with reduction in clinical disease
progression according to the FDA’s Guidance for Industry,

e LEN has demonstrated virologic activity in HTE patients with
MDR HIV-1 infection who have limited treatment options.

¢ The clinical trial design and endpoints were appropriate for
the HTE population with limited treatment options.

* Reductions in HIV-1 RNA levels are highly predictive of
meaningful ical benefit, and LEN functional monotherapy
was superior to placebo for reducing HIV-1 RNA over
14 days.

« Because Study 4625 was uncontrolled during the maintence
period and the interim analysis included follow-up through
only Week 26, there is some uncertainty surrounding the
contribution of LEN to the durability of virologic response.

e Availability of LEN would provide a new and effective first-
in-class treatment option for HTE patients with HIV-1
infection and limited alternative options due to multidrug
resistance, intolerance or safety considerations. Patients
who achieve virologic suppression with LEN plus an OBR
would have a reduced risk of HIV/AIDS-related morbidity
and mortality.
