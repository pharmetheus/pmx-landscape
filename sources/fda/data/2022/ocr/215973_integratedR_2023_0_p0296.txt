7.6.4.5. Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events

Excluding injection site reactions (ISRs), the most common (>5%) treatment-emergent adverse
events (TEAEs) that were reported more frequently in the Total LEN group than the B/F/TAF
group included headache (12%), nausea (11%), lymphadenopathy (8%), diarrhea (6%),
depression (6%) and back pain (6%). The most common AEs (>1%) that were considered by the
investigator to be related to study drugs were nausea (5%), diarrhea (3%), fatigue (2%),
headache (2%), weight increased (1%), and abdominal pain (1%). No AEs of Grade 3 or higher
were reported for more than one subject in any treatment group. In the Total LEN group, 7% (11
of 157 subjects) had AEs of Grade 3 or higher compared to 4% (1 of 25 subjects) in the B/F/TAF
group. TEAEs occurring in at least 3% of subjects in the Total LEN group are listed in Table 47.
