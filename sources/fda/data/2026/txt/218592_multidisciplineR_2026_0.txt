1 Executive Summary
Product Introduction
Floretyrosine F 18, also termed F-18 fluoroethyltyrosine or F-18 FET, is a new molecular entity
radioactive diagnostic drug. Chemically it is a fluorine-18 radiolabeled tyrosine analog.
Floretyrosine is a substrate for the Large-neutral Amino Acid Transporter (LAT) 1 and 2 and
accumulates in cells expressing LAT. Viable glioma cells generally have greater uptake of
floretyrosine F 18 than normal brain tissue or brain tissue experiencing treatment-related
change, and the proposed indication is for the characterization of recurrent or progressive
glioma from treatment-related change (TRC).
The current resubmission addresses clinical, statistical, and product quality deficiencies from
the Complete Response (CR) issued for the initial NDA submission. See the prior NDA 218592
Multi-disciplinary Review dated April 25, 2025, for discussion of therapeutic context, including
analysis of the condition and current treatment options.

Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
With this resubmission, substantial evidence of effectiveness has now been demonstrated for
the ability of floretyrosine F 18 to differentiate recurrent or progressive glioma from TRC,
thereby addressing the clinical/statistical deficiency of the previous CR. Support for efficacy was
derived from two adequate and well-controlled trials submitted by the Applicant. The first trial,
18F-TLX101-CDx-203, was included in the initial NDA submission. The second trial, 18F-TLX101005, was newly included in the current resubmission.
Study 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 was a prospective, single-center study that evaluated floretyrosine F
18 brain positron emission tomography (PET) for detecting glioma in treated participants with
findings on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) that were equivocal for recurrence or
progression of glioma versus TRC. The reference standard was a composite of all available MRI,
histopathology slides and reports, and clinical data. The co-primary endpoint of positive percent
agreement (PPA) ranged from 58% to 73% and the co-primary endpoint of negative percent
agreement (NPA) ranged from 36% to 51%, both expressed as the lower bounds of the 95%
confidence intervals.
Study 18F-TLX101-005 was a multicenter study in which retrospectively collected floretyrosine
F 18 images previously acquired during clinical care of patients were prospectively read in a
blinded fashion for differentiating recurrent or progressive glioma versus TRC in treated
participants with indeterminate findings on MRI. Similar to 18F-TLX101-CDx-203, the reference
standard was a composite of all available MRI, histopathology slides and reports, and clinical
data. The co-primary endpoint of PPA ranged from 58% to 64% and the co-primary endpoint of
NPA ranged from 44% to 52% at the lower bounds of the 95% confidence intervals.

5 Sources of Clinical Data and Review Strategy
Two studies were provided as primary sources of effectiveness data:
1) Study 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 was a prospective, single-center study utilizing data licensed
from the UC-GlioFET study at UCSF. The FDA deemed this study adequate and wellcontrolled during the original NDA review, as discussed in the prior NDA 218592 Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation. A corrigendum of the study with updated datasets

resubmission review.

safety, an OSI audit was not requested for 18F-TLX101-005.

6 Clinical Pharmacology, Clinical, and Statistical Evaluation
Review of Relevant Individual Trials Used to Support Efficacy
18F-TLX101-005 Design
Trial Design
In 18F-TLX101-005, floretyrosine F 18 PET images that were previously acquired as part of
clinical care in treated glioma patients with MRI imaging that was indeterminate for glioma
recurrence or progression versus treatment-related changes were prospectively read in a
blinded fashion. Participants were enrolled from four sites in Germany (2 sites), the
Netherlands (1 site), and the U.S. (1 site). Potential participants for 18F-TLX101-005 were
consecutively selected starting from the most recently imaged patient in the database at each
site. The trial retrospectively enrolled adult and pediatric participants with a histologic
diagnosis or clinical suspicion of grade 1-4 glioma who had primary treatment of the tumor with
surgery, radiation therapy, or chemotherapy. Participants were required to have post-therapy
MRI that was equivocal for recurrence or treatment-related changes and a floretyrosine F 18
PET that was intended to address the MRI findings. Adults must have received an administered
activity between 3.6 - 7.7 mCi of floretyrosine F 18 while pediatric participants must have
received an administered activity adjusted based on body weight with the following calculation:
Administered activity = ([patient body weight in kg]/70 kg) × adult administered activity
(minimum 0.8 mCi, maximum 7.7 mCi)
Participants were excluded if they did not have follow-up MRI performed after the floretyrosine
F 18 PET.
Floretyrosine F 18 PET images were reviewed by three independent, central nuclear medicine
specialists, trained on a standardized interpretation workflow. Readers were blinded to site and
participant identifiers, participant demographics, on-site interpretation, participant treatment
outcome, and all other clinical information not deemed essential for independent review. One
static image set per participant, acquired between 5 to 50 minutes post-injection with a
minimum acquisition time of 20 minutes, was presented to the PET readers. Dynamic
acquisitions were reconstructed as a relevant static image set prior to presentation. Each
reader was provided with non-contrast T1 MRI if available for anatomic reference. Up to five
lesions with increased tracer uptake suspicious for disease were selected for evaluation. Each
reader initially made a visual assessment of whether each lesion was glioma recurrence or
progression (increased uptake of floretyrosine F 18) or treatment-related changes (nearbackground uptake of floretyrosine F 18) using a discontinuous color scale and window and
level settings based on normal brain background uptake. After the visual assessment was
completed, a semi-quantitative assessment was performed based on the maximum value of
tumor-to-background ratio (TBRmax) and mean value of tumor-to-background ratio (TBRmean).
A lesion was defined as positive if TBRmax ≥2.3 and/or TBRmean ≥2.0, with TBRmax serving as
the primary parameter. An overall assessment that integrated both the visual and semi12

Randomly selected cases were reviewed by the same PET reader at least two weeks after the
initial review of the floretyrosine F 18 PET images to allow assessment of intra-reader
variability. These cases were inserted into the ongoing primary reviews in a blinded fashion.
The reference standard was a composite standard of truth (CSOT) determined by a Truth Panel
from an integrated review of MRI, histopathology slides and reports, and clinical reports. The
Truth Panel consisted of specialists in clinical neuro-oncology, neuroradiology, and
neuropathology who were blinded to floretyrosine F 18 PET images and PET read results. They
were trained on a standardized interpretation workflow and assessment methodology. MRI
images obtained before, with, and after floretyrosine F 18 PET imaging, with the imaging closest
to PET imaging considered baseline, any pathology slides and reports from routine clinical care,
and clinical data including primary tumor type, dates and type of treatment, neurologic deficits,
corticosteroid use, performance assessments, and comorbidities were presented to the panel.
The timeframe for follow-up MRI was not specified, nor the number of follow-up MRI studies
that would be evaluated as part of the CSOT. One neuroradiologist performed a review of the
available imaging, adapted from RANO-HGG or RANO-LGG criteria (Wen et al. 2010, van den
Bent et al. 2011) as appropriate for the participant’s glioma. An independent pathology slide
review was performed by the neuropathologist in cases where relevant pathology slides were
available. Once the neuroradiology review and pathology review were completed, a
comprehensive standard of truth review was performed where all imaging, pathology, and
clinical data were reviewed to make an overall assessment for each target and non-target lesion
identified on the baseline MRI. A lesion was considered recurrent or progressive disease if there
was a ≥ 25% increase in the product of perpendicular diameters of that lesion compared to the
baseline measurement or if there was unequivocal tumor present as determined by clinical
data or pathology.
Lesion linking was performed by a trained imaging specialist who was not involved with the
floretyrosine F 18 PET reads or the CSOT review. The lesion linker had access to annotated
floretyrosine F 18 PET images and annotated baseline MRI but was blinded to PET read results,
all non-baseline reference standard information, and CSOT determinations. The lesion linker
first linked lesions among the PET readers. After the lesion linking among PET readers was
locked, lesions identified on the floretyrosine F 18 PET reads were linked to lesions identified on
MRI during the CSOT review. Challenging cases were evaluated by two independent
adjudicators, who were also not members of the Truth Panel and were blinded to non-baseline
reference standard information. If the two independent adjudicators did not agree on lesion
linking, then a third independent adjudicator made the final determination.

Because the reference standard used in 18F-TLX101-005 was a composite of histopathology,
imaging, and clinical data, it is not considered to be the gold standard for diagnostic
performance assessment. Accordingly, the terms positive percent agreement (PPA) and
negative percent agreement (NPA) are used in place of sensitivity and specificity, respectively,
throughout the remainder of the clinical review.
Secondary endpoints of interest for this review included PPA and NPA for participants who had
histopathology reference standard data, PPA and NPA in subgroups based on relevant
treatment factors, and inter-reader agreement by Fleiss’ kappa.
Statistical Analysis Plan
Protocol-defined analysis populations included the following:
• Full Analysis Set (FAS): all participants who were enrolled
• Intent-to-Treat (ITT) Population: all participants enrolled; population used for
primary efficacy analysis
• Per-Protocol Set (PPS): all participants in the ITT Population with no significant
protocol deviations; used for sensitivity analyses of efficacy endpoints
The predefined PPA and NPA goals were 60% and 50%, respectively, against which the lower
bounds of the 95% confidence intervals (CI) were compared. The study was to be considered
successful if the PPA and NPA goals were met by at least the same two out of three
independent readers.

Compliance with Good Clinical Practices
The Applicant indicated that the study was conducted in accordance with GCP and with

Financial Disclosure
The Applicant stated that no investigators for 18F-TLX101-005 had disclosable financial

Patient Disposition
The study screened 455 participants across the four sites and a total of 254 participants were
eligible for inclusion. The remaining 201 participants were classified as screen failures. Of these,
a total of 145 (72%) failed screening for missing MRI follow up imaging and 37 (18%) for
administered activity of floretyrosine F 18 outside the range of 3.6 mCi to 7.7 mCi.
Protocol Violations/Deviations
The Applicant reported that 68 participants had at least one protocol deviation, all of them
considered minor. Of these, 67 participants had a protocol deviation related to scan duration,
and one had a protocol deviation due to the acquisition window being out of the specified
range. Accordingly, the same 254 participants were included in FAS, ITT, and PPS.
Demographic Characteristics
Demographic information for 18F-TLX101-005 is summarized in Table 2. The sex and age
distributions appear reasonably representative of the population of intended clinical use. Race
and ethnicity results were very limited due to enrollment of participants in countries where
such data collection is prohibited. Participants were drawn from four sites, including one in the
United States, two in Germany, and one in the Netherlands. Only 11% of participants were from
the United States.

Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness
The Applicant has provided substantial evidence for the effectiveness of floretyrosine F 18 for
PET imaging to differentiate recurrent or progressive glioma from treatment-related change, in
conjunction with other diagnostic evaluations. Effectiveness is supported by two adequate and
well-controlled studies, 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 and 18F-TLX101-005, that included participants
who had treated glioma of any grade with findings on MRI that were indeterminate for
23

Reference ID: 5867747

NDA 218592 Multi-Disciplinary Review and Evaluation
Floretyrosine F 18
recurrence or progression versus treatment-related change. Both studies evaluated lesionbased, participant-level PPA and NPA against a composite reference standard.
Both studies failed to meet their predefined success criteria for the co-primary endpoints, and
both demonstrated marginal performance at the lower bounds of the 95% CIs for PPA and NPA.
18F-TLX101-CDx-203 had significant weaknesses that limit its persuasiveness, discussed in the
multidisciplinary review dated April 25, 2025, and not reiterated here. Notable design issues
with 18F-TLX101-005 include a retrospective collection of source images and reference
standard data, potential for the result of floretyrosine F 18 imaging to impact availability of
components of the composite reference standard, and exclusion of participants without follow
up MRI. This last issue raises concern for potential enrollment of participants who may not be
representative of the population of intended clinical use. However, the proportions of
participants with treatment-related change versus glioma recurrence or progression in 18FTLX101-005 were equal, consistent with a clinically relevant population with indeterminate
findings on MRI.
Among patients with treated glioma and an MRI that is indeterminate for recurrence or
progression versus treatment-related change, there are no well-established non-invasive
methods for diagnosis other than imaging and clinical follow up. Decisions regarding whether to
initiate therapy or otherwise change management are often delayed or made with great
uncertainty. Therefore, even a test with suboptimal performance could potentially contribute
to addressing this unmet diagnostic need. Further, for this orphan indication, there is decreased
likelihood of development of alternative agents.
The relatively low performance of floretyrosine F 18 will be addressed in labeling beyond clearly
presenting the performance results in Section 14 of the prescribing information. The
recommended indication statement positions the drug for use in conjunction with other
diagnostic evaluations, emphasizing the need to consider all available information before
making treatment decisions. A warning for risk of misinterpretation will be included that
highlights the possibility of false positive and false negative results.
The reading method used in the adequate and well-controlled studies integrated visual
assessment with semiquantitative metrics into an overall read, an approach adapted from the
scientific literature and multi-society procedure standards (Law et al. 2019). In both studies, the
overall read results and visual read results were very similar, while the semiquantitative reads
alone had lower PPA, higher NPA, and somewhat lower accuracy. There was a slight trend to
improved performance with combined versus visual reads for two readers in 18F-TLX101-CDx203 and no loss of accuracy with overall read compared to visual read in any reader in either
study. The overall read method was considered appropriate to describe in the labeled image
interpretation instructions, consistent with its use in the studies that supported approval. The
instructions in labeling also clearly indicate that semiquantitative assessments should not be
used in isolation.

The Applicant’s Primary Efficacy Analysis
As mentioned in Section 6.1.2, the Applicant’s primary analyses of lesion-based, participantlevel performance of floretyrosine F 18 used a multiple imputation (MI) approach for missing
data. Additional details, particularly regarding the MI approach, are presented below.
Each PET exam was classiﬁed as true positive (TP), true negative (TN), false positive (FP), or false
negative (FN) for each of three independent readers based on each reader’s overall assessment
as compared to the CSOT at the lesion-level. Lesion-level reader assessments were rolled up to
participant-level classiﬁcation using the predeﬁned hierarchy rule TP > FN > FP > TN for the
primary analysis. Missing data were imputed at the lesion-level for the primary analysis.
Among the 254 participants in the Efficacy Analysis Set, 219 participants had at least one lesion
that was matched between PET assessment and the CSOT for at least 1 reader, encompassing a
total of 544 evaluable lesions. Among those 219 participants, 213, 209, and 214 had evaluable
data for Readers 1, 2, and 3, respectively; the corresponding numbers of evaluable lesions were
525, 515, and 524 for Readers 1, 2, and 3, respectively. Lesion-level imputation was applied to
the missing data in all 254 participants, including the 219 participants who had both observed
and missing lesion-level data. Per the Applicant’s information request (IR) response dated April
17, 2026, there were six participants in whom the readers identified one or more lesions that
did not correspond to any lesions identified by the truth panel, implying a missing CSOT for one
or more reader-identified lesions in these six participants. However, lesions without a
corresponding CSOT were excluded from imputation. Therefore, in the Applicant’s primary
analysis, missing data referred specifically to missing PET results with an observed CSOT.

MI using fully conditional specification (FCS) was performed to impute missing lesion-level data
under the missing at random (MAR) assumption.
a) Imputation workflow:
1. The imputation model generated 1,000 imputed datasets. Each imputed dataset
contained a plausible imputed value for each missing lesion-level blinded read result
(TP, TN, FP, or FN).
o Lesions without a corresponding CSOT were excluded; the model considered only
lesions with an observed CSOT.
o A condition was applied such that if the CSOT = recurrence or progression, the
imputed value was restricted to TP or FN; if the CSOT = treatment-related change,
the imputed value was restricted to TN or FP.
2. The predefined hierarchy rule (TP > FN > FP > TN) was applied to derive a participantlevel result by combining lesion-based results for each participant.
3. Sensitivity and specificity were calculated for each imputed dataset and each reader.
4. The sensitivity and specificity results across the 1000 imputed datasets were combined
into a single set of pooled parameter estimates and confidence intervals using Rubin’s
rules.
b) FCS imputation model:
1. As noted above, the imputation incorporated a condition dependent on the CSOT: If the
CSOT was positive, imputed values could only be TP or FN, and if the CSOT was negative,
imputed values could only be TN or FP.
2. To account for within-subject correlation of the lesion-level data, the model included
observed data from other lesions within the same participant as predictors for the
lesion being imputed for each reader.
3. Logistic regression models for binary endpoints were employed in the FCS imputation
model, with age, sex, tumor grade, and other lesions as covariates and PET read as the
response variable.

Post-hoc Analyses of the Primary Efficacy Endpoints
Unlike Table 18, Table 17 does not include a 2×2 table data (i.e., TP, FP, FN, and TN counts) for
any reader, as the estimates were based on 1,000 imputed datasets. In response to the FDA
clinical and statistical IR dated April 3, 2026, the Applicant provided a table presenting lesionbased, participant-level observations (TP, FN, FP, TN, and missing values) for each of the three
readers for the primary analysis. For the primary analysis, the reader-level positive or negative
result for each participant was determined by applying the hierarchical rule to all lesions
identified by both the reader and the truth panel. Once a participant-level result was
determined, TP and FP were combined as PET positive, while TN and FN were combined as PET
negative. The CSOT result was determined by evaluating all truth panel-identified lesions: if any
lesion was CSOT positive, the participant was CSOT considered positive; if all lesions were CSOT
negative, the participant was considered CSOT negative. This approach was consistent with the
methodology used in the primary analysis but differed from that used in the secondary analysis,
in which only lesions identified by both the reader and the truth panel were considered.
FDA Statistical reviewer’s comment:
• The methodology used in the secondary analysis — in which only lesions identified by both
the reader and the truth panel were considered — resulted in inconsistent numbers of CSOTpositive and CSOT-negative participants across readers in the Efficacy Analysis Set (N = 254).
Specifically, Readers 1 and 3 each yielded 124 CSOT-positive and 130 CSOT-negative
participants, compared to 122 CSOT-positive and 132 CSOT-negative participants for Reader
2, as presented in Table 19 below.
• Upon further investigation, this discrepancy was found to be attributable to a subset of
participants, differing across readers, who had observed TN and/or FP lesions alongside
lesions with a positive CSOT and a missing PET read. When deriving participant-level
classifications from lesion-level data using only lesions with both a PET read and CSOT
available, these participants were classified as CSOT-negative (TN or FP). However, their true
participant-level CSOT should be positive, given that they had at least one lesion with a
positive CSOT, albeit with a missing PET read.
• To address this issue, the Applicant re-assigned participant-level TN to FN for participants
with TN lesions who also had lesions with a positive CSOT and a missing PET read, and reassigned participant-level FP to TP for participants with FP lesions who also had lesions with
a positive CSOT and a missing PET read, as reported in Table 4 on Page 1 of “annex-1-table4.pdf”. While the former re-assignment (TN → FN) was reasonable, the latter (FP → TP)
appeared optimistic. A more conservative approach for the latter would be to re-assign
participant-level FP to FN for those participants, based on the presence of lesions with a
positive CSOT and a missing PET read, and consistent with the prespecified hierarchical
algorithm TP > FN > FP > TN.

Conclusions and Recommendations
Two adequate and well-controlled studies assessed performance of floretyrosine F 18 to
differentiate recurrent or progressive glioma from treatment-related change. While
performance did not meet predefined thresholds, it was deemed clinically meaningful for
adjunctive use in this orphan indication where there is significant unmet medical need.
Floretyrosine F 18 was well tolerated in the clinical studies that assessed safety, and the risk
profile appears similar to other microdose PET drugs. The benefit-risk balance is considered to
be favorable and supports approval of floretyrosine F 18 for use with PET to differentiate
recurrent or progressive glioma from treatment-related change, in conjunction with other
diagnostic evaluations, in adults and pediatric patients 1 month of age and older.
7 Pediatrics
Glioma pathophysiology differs between pediatric and adult patients. However, the mechanism
of action of floretyrosine F 18, an amino acid analog that is accumulated via upregulated amino
acid transporters in tumors according to metabolic demand, is expected to be maintained in
both adult and pediatric glioma. Accordingly, an extrapolation approach from adult safety and
effectiveness data is reasonable. A total of 12 pediatric participants were included in the
adequate and well-controlled studies. Within the limit of this small sample, there were no
appreciable differences in safety or performance of the drug between pediatric and adult
participants.
The proposed weight-based dose of 0.081 mCi/kg for pediatric patients was used in the limited
group of pediatric patients in the adequate and well-controlled trials and further supported by
scientific literature submitted with the application (Dunkl et al. 2015, Marner et al. 2021,
Elahmadawy et al. 2023, Kertels et al. 2023). However, there was insufficient data to establish
dosing in patients less than 1 month of age. In addition, clinical data informing a minimum
administered activity for the smallest patients were not available. A minimum administered
activity is needed to ensure adequate image quality in very low-weight patients where the

Comments
Per the multi-disciplinary review findings, the combination of this resubmission with the initial
NDA submission supports an indication for floretyrosine F 18 for use with PET to differentiate
recurrent or progressive glioma from treatment-related change, in conjunction with other
diagnostic evaluations, in adults and pediatric patients 1 month of age and older.
The clinical and statistical deficiency that contributed to the CR for the initial NDA submission
centered on the lack of substantial evidence of effectiveness. The current clinical and statistical
review appropriately concluded that substantial evidence of effectiveness of floretyrosine F 18
was demonstrated for the recommended indication through the combination of the newly
submitted Study 18F-TLX101-005, which was considered to be adequate and well-controlled,
and Study 18F-TLX101-CDx-203, which was previously reviewed and also considered to be
adequate and well-controlled. Both studies did not meet prespecified targets for PPA and/or
NPA but demonstrated similar modest diagnostic performance that exceeded chance. While
additional weaknesses were present in both studies, the studies complemented one another to
a certain degree. For example, 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 involved participants from a single U.S. site
while 18F-TLX101-005 involved participants from multiple, predominantly European sites.
Given the unmet diagnostic need in the indicated rare population, the observed modest
diagnostic performance of floretyrosine F 18 PET is expected to provide clinical utility,
particularly when used in conjunction with other diagnostic evaluations, as specified in the
indication statement. A labeled warning regarding risk for misinterpretation further emphasizes
the need to correlate floretyrosine F 18 PET results with available diagnostic evaluations and
describes the possibility of false positive and false negative interpretations. Additionally,
Section 14 of the prescribing information details the observed PPA and NPA results of each
reader in both studies.
The reading method described in the prescribing information reflects the combined visual and
semiquantitative approach applied in the adequate and well-controlled studies. Per review of
the resubmission by the clinical pharmacology team, the labeled adult dosing and image
acquisition window were directly supported by these studies as well, along with additional
evidence provided by another study, 18F-TLX101-006.
The safety profile of floretyrosine F 18 from submitted study data raised no concerns and was
generally consistent with other microdose PET drugs. While safety monitoring in the submitted
studies was relatively limited, the safety database was considered to be sufficient, particularly
given the extensive research use of floretyrosine F 18 worldwide and its clinical use abroad.
While 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 and 18F-TLX101-005 were mainly conducted with adult participants,
efficacy and safety were reasonably extrapolated to pediatric patients, as supported by the
mechanism of action of floretyrosine F 18 and limited pediatric data collected in these studies.

1 Executive Summary
Product Introduction
Floretyrosine F 18, also termed F-18 fluoroethyltyrosine or F-18 FET, is a new molecular entity
radioactive diagnostic drug. Chemically it is a fluorine-18 radiolabeled tyrosine analog.
Floretyrosine is a substrate for the Large-neutral Amino Acid Transporter (LAT) 1 and 2 and
accumulates in cells expressing LAT. Viable glioma cells generally have greater uptake of
floretyrosine F 18 than normal brain tissue or brain tissue experiencing treatment-related
change, and the proposed indication is for the characterization of recurrent or progressive
glioma from treatment-related change.
(b) (4)

New drug applications, NDA 218592

Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
The data contained in this application do not constitute substantial evidence of effectiveness
for the proposed indication.
One adequate and well-controlled study, 18F-TLX101-CDx-203, was submitted. This study
examined floretyrosine F 18 brain positron emission tomography (PET) for detection of glioma
in patients who had treated glioma and findings on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) that
were indeterminate for recurrent or progressive tumor versus treatment-related change. The
study did not meet the predefined success criteria for the co-primary endpoints of sensitivity
and specificity. The performance was marginal, with sensitivity ranging from 58% to 73% and
specificity from 36% to 51% at the lower bound of the 95% confidence intervals. Additionally,
there were design (i.e., conduct at a single center) and execution (i.e., unblinded personnel
present for reference standard discussions) issues that further limit the persuasiveness of the
study.
Four published studies were submitted to provide confirmatory evidence of effectiveness for
the NDA. These studies have varying design weaknesses including reading methods that differ
from those proposed in the prescribing information, limited information regarding number,
blinding, or independence of readers, dosing that differs from that proposed in the prescribing
information, and unblinding of the reference standard evaluators to floretyrosine F 18 results.
Considering the limited persuasiveness of 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 as an adequate and wellcontrolled study, none of these published studies was assessed as providing sufficient strength

Benefit-Risk Assessment
Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
After treatment of glioma, it is not uncommon for MRI surveillance imaging to identify areas that are indeterminate for recurrent or progressive
glioma versus treatment-related change. Floretyrosine F 18 is a radioactive diagnostic drug intended to distinguish recurrent or progressive
glioma from treatment-related change.
Substantial evidence of effectiveness was not provided for this indication. The single adequate and well-controlled study showed marginal
diagnostic performance in addition to weaknesses in design and conduct and was not supported by sufficiently robust confirmatory evidence. A
second adequate and well-controlled efficacy study, which could be conducted in the same patient population or a closely related condition, or
other robust confirmatory evidence of efficacy is necessary.
While the safety risks are low, the evidence of benefit is insufficient to support approval.

2 Therapeutic Context
Analysis of Condition
Glioma is a type of central nervous system neoplasm that originates from glial cells, and is the
most common malignant brain tumor. There are many different subtypes of glioma, varying
widely in tumor grade and biologic behavior. In the 2021 revised WHO guideline, gliomas are
classified in six different groups, adult-type diffuse gliomas, pediatric-type diffuse low-grade
glioma, pediatric-type diffuse high-grade gliomas, circumscribed astrocytic gliomas,
glioneuronal and neuronal tumors, and ependymomas (Louis et al. 2021).
The current standard treatment options for glioma consist of surgery, radiation therapy, and
chemotherapy, often using temozolomide (NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology for
Central Nervous System Cancers V.5.2024 2024). As with many other cancers, imaging is a key
component of post-therapy surveillance for recurrence. In the case of glioma, magnetic
resonance imaging (MRI) is the most used modality. It is not uncommon for MRI to reveal
findings that may represent either tumor recurrence or changes caused by prior treatments.
These possibilities can usually be distinguished by continued imaging follow-up, however, this
follow-up requires weeks to months and delays potential changes in therapy.
One example of a treatment-related change is pseudoprogression, which is usually reflected by
increases in contrast enhancement on MRI. Pseudoprogression is often seen with
chemoradiation and can relate to transiently increased permeability of the tumor vasculature
and inflammation induced by radiation therapy, which may be exacerbated by temozolomide.
The incidence of pseudoprogression is high in the first 12 weeks after chemoradiotherapy for
glioblastomas, occurring in up to 30%-40% of patients (Wen et al. 2023). Another type of
treatment-related change is seen with therapies that reduce vascular permeability, such as
bevacizumab, a monoclonal antibody against vascular endothelial growth factor A. Because MRI
contrast drugs rely on abnormal vascularity for accumulation in central nervous system lesions,
anti-angiogenic therapies can give rise to pseudoresponse characterized by nonenhancing
tumor progression.
Floretyrosine F 18 is an analog of the amino acid tyrosine. Floretyrosine is a substrate of the
large-neutral amino acid transporter and accumulates in cells that express the transporter.
Presumably due to increased metabolic demand, gliomas generally have substantial activity of
the transporter. Floretyrosine F 18 has been studied for glioma imaging in multiple settings,
including in determination of recurrent tumor versus treatment-related change. Professional
society guidelines have been published for use of radiolabeled amino acid analogs in glioma
imaging (Law et al. 2019). Floretyrosine F 18 is also marketed in some European countries, for
example IASOglio (Curium) in France and Poland. There is potential for floretyrosine F 18,
through a mechanism of localization distinct from MRI contrast drugs, to aid in distinguishing
tumor from treatment-related change in patients who have indeterminate MRI findings.

Analysis of Current Treatment Options
MRI is the standard of care for imaging glioma, both before and after therapy. MRI is performed
with contrast agents when possible, as abnormal vascularity is an important finding for
recognizing tumor. All currently marketed gadolinium-based contrast agents except for Eovist
(gadoxetate) are indicated for visualization of central nervous system lesions. Advanced MRI
imaging techniques, including spectroscopy and perfusion imaging have been studied and may
be helpful in assessing patients with otherwise indeterminate MRI.
Fludeoxyglucose F 18 is a glucose analog that is indicated to assist in the evaluation of
malignancy in patients with known or suspected abnormalities. Fludeoxyglucose F 18 has been
used for differentiation of tumor versus treatment-related change in patients with glioma,
however, it is not ideal for brain tumor imaging because of the high background uptake of the
drug in normal brain parenchyma.
Fluorodopa F 18 is an amino acid analog approved for characterization of presynaptic
dopaminergic activity in patients with suspected Parkinsonian syndromes. Brain tumor imaging
is an unapproved use that has been studied, and fluorodopa F 18 is included in the same joint
guidelines publication previously cited (Law et al. 2019).
Given the limitations of MRI and currently available PET drugs, there is an unmet medical need
for differentiation of recurrent or progressive glioma from treatment-related changes.

Executive Summary
Fluoroethyl-L-tyrosine (18F) or floretyrosine F 18 is a radiolabeled analog of the non-essential
amino acid tyrosine. Like tyrosine, floretyrosine F 18 is a substrate for the large-neutral amino
acid transporter 1 and 2 (LAT1 and LAT2). The Applicant stated that LAT1 is overexpressed in a
number of tumors including brain tumors and gliomas. The Applicant is seeking the following
indication: “for use with PET imaging for the characterization of progressive or recurrent glioma
(b) (4)
from treatment-related changes”. The mass dose of floretyrosine F 18 will not exceed
ђg/dose (microdose). Tyrosine is present in food and supplements and can be safely ingested in
gram quantities, therefore, there are no safety concerns with a microdose of floretyrosine F 18
from a nonclinical perspective. As previously agreed, the Applicant provided the relevant
nonclinical information available from the public literature. Additionally, the Applicant

toxicity. However, nonclinical toxicology studies are not needed because a microdose of
floretyrosine does not pose safety concerns. In conclusion this NDA can be approved from a
nonclinical perspective.

Review Strategy
Study 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 was submitted as the primary evidence of effectiveness, with the
remaining studies intended to provide confirmatory evidence of effectiveness, or in the case of
the FIG study to provide safety data. The Applicant performed post-hoc analyses of 18F-TLX101CDx-203 termed 18F-TLX101-CDx-204 to assess the performance of MRI for detection of
recurrent or progressive glioma. These analyses were evaluated with the source study where
applicable.
As will be discussed in detail in Section 8.1, 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 was considered adequate and
well-controlled but upon review revealed important weaknesses in design and conduct and
marginal performance of floretyrosine F 18 for the proposed indication. When using one
adequate and well-controlled study and confirmatory evidence to demonstrate substantial
evidence of effectiveness, the quantity of confirmatory evidence needed is affected by the
features of and results from the adequate and well-controlled study (Demonstrating Substantial
Evidence of Effectiveness With One Adequate and Well-Controlled Clinical Investigation and
Confirmatory Evidence - Guidance for Industry September 2023). Because of the issues with
18F-TLX101-CDx-203, highly compelling confirmatory evidence or another adequate and wellcontrolled study would be needed to establish substantial evidence of effectiveness. Each of
the studies provided by the Applicant as confirmatory evidence have design issues that on their
face make them unable to provide the strength of evidence needed for this application.
Therefore, emphasis will be placed on design of these studies in the next section with only
limited presentation of their results.

18F-TLX101-CDx-203 Design
Trial Design
18F-TLX101-CDx-203 was a re-read of patients enrolled in a prospective, single center, open
label, phase 2 academic study named UC-GlioFET (“Fluoroethyltyrosine (FET) for the evaluation
of intracranial neoplasm”). UC-GlioFET included adult and pediatric (age >3 years) patients with
intracranial glioma or metastasis who had received either radiotherapy or surgery and who had
suspicion of recurrence on MRI (population 1). It also enrolled patients with intracranial
neoplasm of any type prior to a planned biopsy or surgical resection (population 2). Each
patient received a single PET/MRI (with option for repeat in adults) after intravenous
administration of 4-7 mCi ±10%, mass dose not more than 100 ђg floretyrosine F 18 for adults,
scaled to body weight for children. PET acquisition began promptly after administration and
included reconstructions covering the first minute, 10-20 minutes, 20-40 minutes, and 30-40
minutes. Images were reviewed by a nuclear medicine physician and neuroradiologist and a
clinical report was generated. The initial PET images were also reviewed by 3 nuclear medicine
physicians blinded to patient history and pathology results. The primary analysis was for
patient-level misclassification rate against a histopathology reference standard, evaluating
tumor recurrence versus treatment related change for patient population 1 and high-grade
glioma versus low grade glioma for population 2.
18F-TLX101-CDx-203 defined all patients from UC-GlioFET as eligible for safety analysis and the
subset of patient population 1 who had glioma as eligible for efficacy analysis. Floretyrosine F
18 PET images collected at 20-40 minutes after dosing and non-contrast T1 MR images for
anatomic reference were independently evaluated by three nuclear medicine specialists
blinded to clinical and additional imaging information. Readers were instructed to perform
three sequential evaluations. First, they were to identify up to five lesions and determine
visually whether they demonstrated increased uptake compared to background activity in the
cortex (recurrence). Next, semiquantitative maximum and mean tumor to background
(TBRmax, TBRmean) measurements of each lesion were made and compared to threshold
values of 2.3 for TBRmax and 2 for TBRmean, with greater than threshold values indicating
positive results. TBRmax was to be considered the primary value. Finally, the readers were
asked to provide an overall assessment of whether each lesion represented recurrence or
treatment-related change.
The reference standard for 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 was determined by a three-member panel that
included a pathologist and neuroradiologist. It was a composite of all available clinical data,
including histopathology, baseline and follow up imaging, and tumor board notes, with a binary
output of tumor or treatment-related change at the lesion-level. Floretyrosine F 18 PET images

Statistical Analysis Plan
Protocol-defined analysis populations included the following:
x Safety analysis set: All patients who completed initial floretyrosine F 18 imaging per
protocol.
x Efficacy analysis set: Glioma patients who completed initial floretyrosine F 18 imaging
per protocol and were under concern for recurrence or progression per conventional
imaging. Repeat floretyrosine F 18 imaging was excluded from evaluation.
x Per protocol set: Members of the efficacy analysis set without major protocol deviation.
The primary analysis was performed in the efficacy analysis set. Study success criteria were
defined as the lower bounds of the 95% confidence intervals of patient-level sensitivity and
specificity being greater than or equal to 0.70 and 0.68, respectively, for at least the same two
out of three independent readers.
Protocol Amendments
Version 1 of the 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 protocol, dated February 13, 2024, was amended once on
April 22, 2024. Key changes include making the primary analysis lesion-based, patient-level
(from patient-level), defining imputation methods, adding the per protocol set and associated
analyses, adding lesion linking methods, and clarifying the reference standard methods and
efficacy analysis set definition. Most of these changes were made in response to FDA
comments.

Patient Disposition
Of 143 patients in the safety analysis set, 16 were excluded from the efficacy analysis set due to
having metastasis rather than glioma (n=12), scan data error (n=3), or being scanned at initial
diagnosis (n=1), resulting in 127 patients evaluable for effectiveness.
Protocol Violations/Deviations
Of 143 patients in the safety analysis set, 8 (6%) patients reported at least one major protocol
deviation. Four patients had administered activity for floretyrosine F 18 outside the protocol
defined range. However, in later version of the UC-GlioFET protocol, the dose was modified
from 4-7 mCi to 4-7 mCi ± 10%, and all four patients were within the latter range. One patient
did not have residual activity measured, so the administered activity was estimated rather than
calculated. Three patients of childbearing potential did not have a pregnancy test at screening.

Data Quality and Integrity
After evaluating the application and inspection results, OSI identified concerns related to data
quality and integrity. First, the reference panel determinations were entered into the electronic
data capture system by the principal investigator, who was unblinded to floretyrosine F 18 PET
read results and present during reference panel deliberations. In response to an information
request, the Applicant stated that the principal investigator conducted a training session for the
panel to explain the determination process, but afterward he did not provide advice to panel
members or participate in deliberations.
The lesion linking process was not pre-specified and was conducted by individuals unblinded to
floretyrosine F 18 PET and reference standard results. In addition, the lesion linking results were
not reviewed by the lesion linking team for accuracy. To examine the potential impact of these
issues, a patient-level analysis was performed without attempting to match the location of PET
findings to those of the reference standard. Patients with more than one lesion were
considered positive on PET if any positive lesion was recorded. The overall (rather than per
lesion) reference panel result was used for the analysis. The same strategy of categorizing
missing PET or reference standard results as negative was employed as in the Applicant’s
primary analysis. As shown in Table 14, there was no difference in per reader sensitivity or
specificity compared to the lesion-based analysis.

Study Design
GBM-RET-01 was a re-read evaluation of patients who were enrolled in an expanded access
protocol at Indiana University entitled, “18F-FET PET/MR imaging for the optimal management
of patients with malignant brain tumors following standard of care therapy”. The expanded
access study enrolled adult and pediatric patients with malignant brain tumor treated with
surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy who had clinical question of tumor progression versus
treatment-related effect based on abnormal contrast enhancement on MRI. It also enrolled
other patient populations, such as patients with treated brain metastases and patients with
suspected malignant brain tumor. Each patient received a PET/MR scan after intravenous
administration of 15 to 30 mCi floretyrosine F 18, with pediatric administered activity scaled to
body weight. PET acquisition began promptly after administration and included dynamic list
mode acquisition for the first 10 minutes followed by sequential 3-5 minute acquisitions for the
duration of MRI imaging. Images were reviewed by a nuclear medicine physician and
neuroradiologist and a clinical report was generated. Patients were allowed to re-enroll in the
study if they continued to meet inclusion criteria.
GBM-RET-01 included the subset of patients from the expanded access study who had glioma
and clinical question of tumor progression versus treatment-related effect. Three readers
independently assessed 20 to 40 minute reconstructions of the floretyrosine F18 brain PET
images, which were accompanied by non-contrast T1 MR images for anatomic reference. The
readers were blinded to clinical data and conventional imaging. If a patient had more than one
floretyrosine F 18 PET, only the first scan was included in the primary analysis. The reading
method used was the same as for 18F-TLX101-CDx-203.
GBM-RET-01 did not create a new reference standard, but instead used a patient-level
reference standard derived for the expanded access protocol. It was determined by a reference
panel consisting of two neuroradiologists and a nuclear medicine physician who had access to
all clinical records for at least 6 months after the initial PET. The panel was blinded to the
results of the floretyrosine F 18 PET re-reads, but had access to the initial clinical read results.
Histopathology results were to take precedence in the reference standard if they were
available. Other criteria included:
• “If a follow-up MRI showed clear improvement or clear progression without any
change in clinical management, the case was reported as TRC or progression
accordingly.

Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness
Substantial evidence for the effectiveness of floretyrosine F 18 for PET imaging of the brain in
patients with glioma to differentiate recurrent disease from treatment-related change has not
been presented in this application. Study 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 is adequate and well-controlled,
however it has significant weaknesses that limit its persuasiveness. The remaining submitted
studies do not provide sufficient strength of confirmatory evidence to meet the substantial
evidence standard for this application.
18F-TLX101-CDx-203
Design weaknesses of 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 include its use of a single center and the potential
for the result of floretyrosine F 18 imaging to alter the components of the composite reference
standard that were obtained. It may not be feasible to design a high-quality reference standard
that is not impacted by the PET result for this combination of product and indication, but the
potential for bias from this issue must still be considered.
Clinical site inspections revealed issues with study execution, most importantly the presence of
the principal investigator, who was unblinded to the results of the PET reads, at the reference
panel deliberations. This is of particular concern because of the critical importance of blinding
of the PET readers and reference panel to each other’s work in this single arm study and
because of the financial interests disclosed by the principal investigator. The Applicant stated
that the role of the principal investigator at the reference panel meetings was strictly limited to
recording the results and that he did not participate in discussions. The risk of unblinding and

Review of Safety
Safety Review Approach
Safety data were evaluated for patients exposed to floretyrosine F 18 in two clinical studies,
18F-TLX101-CDx-203 and FIG (Table 7). 18F-TLX101-CDx-203 is described in Section 8.1. FIG
(FET-PET in Glioblastoma) is an ongoing longitudinal, prospective, multicenter, non-randomized,
phase 2 study enrolling adults with glioblastoma who have received surgery and are planning,
undergoing, or recently completed chemoradiation. Patients receive floretyrosine F 18 PET up
to three times: after surgery but before chemoradiation (if applicable), 4 weeks after
chemoradiation, and within 14 days of suspected progression on MRI. For each PET, 5.4 mCi ±
10% floretyrosine F 18 is administered. The Applicant licensed the available safety data through
August 31, 2024, from the study. The remaining four studies submitted as confirmatory
evidence were not considered for the integrated safety analysis because they did not report
safety data.
Review of the Safety Database
Overall Exposure
In 18F-TLX101-CDx-203, 143 patients received 165 doses of floretyrosine F 18. The mean
administered activity was 5.7 mCi with a range of 1.7 mCi to 7.6 mCi. Among adult patients, the

14 Division Director (Clinical) Comments
(b) (4)

The mechanism of action of floretyrosine F 18 is based on the enhanced uptake of amino acids
by tumors. Uptake by tumors is generally greater than uptake by normal tissues because of
increased levels of amino acid transporters in metabolically active cancer cells. Clinical studies
using radiolabeled amino acid analogues are being conducted to evaluate the utility of amino
acid PET for differentiation of cancer from normal tissue. Amino acid PET would be particularly
valuable for imaging tumors in the brain where active glucose metabolism limits the use of
fludeoxyglucose F 18.
The timely ascertainment of glioma recurrence is necessary for optimal patient management
and frequently cannot be achieved with current standard of care imaging. This unmet clinical
need in an orphan patient population was an important consideration in the benefit-risk
assessment of these applications.
I concur with the consensus assessment by the NDA review team that this application does not
provide substantial evidence of effectiveness for the use of floretyrosine F 18 to differentiate
recurrent or progressive glioma from alterations of brain tissue caused by the glioma
treatments. I concur that, whereas the principal efficacy study titled 18F-TLX101-CDx-203
generally met the criteria of an adequate and well-controlled study, it demonstrated borderline
diagnostic performance with lower bounds of the 95% CI ranging from 58% to 73% for
sensitivity and from 36% to 51% for specificity across the three blinded central readers.
Moreover, the study had design and conduct shortcomings such as use of a single clinical site
and potential for bias in truth standard determination. I take note of the assessment by the
clinical pharmacology reviewers that there is insufficient information to determine that the
administered activity proposed for use in the dosing section of the labeling is optimal.
I concur that adequate confirmatory evidence of efficacy is not provided by GBM-RET-01, a
study consisting of a re-read of images acquired in an expanded access study, or the three
published retrospective studies. GBM-RET-01 used a different administered activity of
floretyrosine F 18 than the activity proposed for labeling. Additional important concerns are the
potential for unblinding of the reference standard panel and handling of missing data for the
co-primary efficacy analysis. The three published studies also demonstrate design and conduct
issues.

performance and as demonstrated in this review document, failed to provide substantial
evidence of effectiveness of FET PET in the proposed indication. The other submitted data were
not sufficiently robust or relevant to represent Confirmatory Evidence. Therefore, obtaining
additional evidence to substantiate the effect of FET PET in this or a related indication is
recommended. Additional dosing explorations might also be needed. Finally, the Telix
application (NDA 218592) contains a series of manufacturing deficiencies that have to be
addressed as well.
