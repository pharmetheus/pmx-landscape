1. Executive Summary
1.1. Product Introduction
Florquinitau F 18 (proprietary name Tauklarify), also referred to as [18F]MK-6240, is a new
molecular entity (NME) and a radioactive diagnostic drug. It is a pyridine isoquinolin-amine
derivative that binds to the three repeat/four repeat (3R/4R) isoform of tau neurofibrillary
tangles (NFTs) in the brain, which is one of the pathological hallmarks of Alzheimer’s disease
(AD).
The recommended indication for florquinitau F 18 is for positron emission tomography (PET) of
the brain in adults with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for Alzheimer’s disease
to identify patients with tau NFT pathology. Florquinitau F 18 is intended to be administered
intravenously at a dose of 5 mCi. New drug application (NDA) 220496 for florquinitau F 18 was
submitted under the 505(b)(1) pathway.

1.2. Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
The Applicant’s submission demonstrates substantial evidence of effectiveness for florquinitau
F 18 for identifying patients with tau NFT pathology. Efficacy is supported by two adequate and
well-controlled studies, CE-20-01 and CE-23-01, conducted by the Applicant.
In both studies, the positive percent agreement (PPA) and negative percent agreement (NPA) of
florquinitau F 18 PET for the identification of patients with tau NFT pathology were assessed
against a binary (positive/negative) clinicoradiographic reference standard for tau, determined
by each participant’s baseline cognitive function and amyloid PET status. All five readers in both
studies met the prespecified threshold of 60% for the co-primary endpoints of PPA and NPA
and demonstrated acceptable diagnostic performance of florquinitau F 18. In CE-20-01, PPA of
florquinitau F 18 ranged from 80% to 88% across five readers, and the lower bound of the 95%
confidence interval (CI) ranged from 72% to 82%. NPA ranged from 98% to 99%, and the lower
bound of the 95% CI ranged from 94% to 96%. In CE-23-01, PPA ranged from 68% to 82% across
five readers, and the lower bound of the 95% CI ranged from 61% to 76%. NPA ranged from
93% to 99%, and the lower bound of the 95% CI ranged from 89% to 97%.

1.3. Benefit-Risk Assessment
Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a debilitating and ultimately fatal progressive neurodegenerative disorder. AD is reported to be the
sixth most common cause of death in the United States with an estimated 7.4 million Americans aged 65 and older currently living
with AD (1). The spread of tau pathology from the limbic regions in early AD to the neocortical areas in advanced AD is closely
associated with the onset of cognitive impairment and subsequent neurodegeneration and cognitive decline (2-4).
Florquinitau F 18 is a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical intended for use with PET imaging of the brain in adults with
cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for AD to identify patients with tau neurofibrillary tangle (NFT) pathology.
The effectiveness of florquinitau F 18 was evaluated in two adequate and well-controlled multicenter clinical studies, CE-20-01
and CE-23-01. Both studies were conducted with participants who were either cognitively impaired and amyloid PET positive or
were cognitively unimpaired and amyloid PET negative. In CE-20-01, the lower bound of the 95% CI for positive percent
agreement (PPA) ranged from 72% to 82%, and from 94% to 96% for negative percent agreement (NPA), across five readers. In
CE-23-01, the lower bound of the 95% CI for PPA ranged from 61% to 76%, and from 89% to 97% for NPA, across five readers. All
five readers in both studies met the prespecified threshold of 60% for the co-primary endpoints of PPA and NPA.
The results of the two studies demonstrate acceptable performance of florquinitau F 18 in identifying patients with tau NFT
pathology. Weaknesses of the clinicoradiographic reference standard, including limited representation of the population of
intended clinical use and related suboptimal evaluation of earlier stage disease, are also shared by the traditional autopsy
reference standard. The potential for lower performance in earlier disease stages and general risk of misdiagnosis related to false
positive and false negative results are addressed through warnings and precautions in the labeling. The performance of
florquinitau F 18 for assessing the distribution of tau pathology could not be determined with the patient-level reference
standard, and the labeling appropriately reflects this limitation by indicating florquinitau F 18 for the identification of patients

Conclusions and Reasons

Analysis of
Condition

• Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a debilitating and ultimately fatal
progressive neurodegenerative disorder and is reported to be the
sixth most common cause of death in the United States (1).
• It is estimated that 7.4 million Americans aged 65 and older are
currently living with AD, and this number is projected to reach
13.8 million by 2060 (1).
• While the accumulation of amyloid is thought to be the primary
event in the pathogenesis of AD, amyloid pathology has only a
relatively modest direct correlation with cognition (6-9). In
contrast, the spread of tau pathology from the limbic regions in
early AD to the neocortical areas in advanced AD is closely
associated with the onset of cognitive impairment and
subsequent neurodegeneration and cognitive decline (2-4). This
stronger correlation between tau pathology and clinical
symptoms underscores the importance of accurate tau detection
for diagnosis and evaluation of patients with cognitive
impairment.
• The progression of tau NFTs follows a distinct spatiotemporal
pattern that is described by Braak staging, which classifies tau
pathology into four stages (B0 to B3) based on topographical

biologically by the presence of
amyloid beta plaques and tau NFT
aggregation, with tau pathology
showing a stronger correlation with

2. Therapeutic Context
2.1. Analysis of Condition
2.1.1. Disease Overview and Pathophysiology
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a debilitating and ultimately fatal progressive neurodegenerative
disorder and is reported to be the sixth most common cause of death in the United States (1).
The clinical syndrome of AD is a continuum, and evidence suggests that there is irreversible
brain damage by the time patients manifest clinical symptoms and signs (30, 31). Therefore,
early diagnosis and intervention are needed to decrease further damage to the central nervous
system (CNS) (30, 31). The accuracy of clinical diagnosis of AD by dementia experts is modest
when compared to postmortem diagnosis (32). A definitive diagnosis of AD in vivo is possible by
the demonstration of extracellular deposition of amyloid beta plaques and intraneuronal tau
neurofibrillary tangle (NFT) aggregation, the two pathological hallmarks of the disease, in the
brains of patients presenting with cognitive and memory disturbances (11, 12, 33).
While the accumulation of amyloid is thought to be the primary event in the pathogenesis of
AD, amyloid pathology has only a relatively modest direct correlation with cognition (6-9). In
contrast, the spread of tau pathology from the limbic regions in early AD to the neocortical
areas in advanced AD is closely associated with the onset of cognitive impairment and
subsequent neurodegeneration and cognitive decline (2-4).
2.1.2. Tau Pathology
Tau is a microtubule stabilizing phosphoprotein with six isoforms that are categorized into two
functional groups based on whether there are three (3R) or four (4R) repeats of the
microtubule-binding domain (34-37). Hyperphosphorylation of tau leads to its abnormal
aggregation into protofibril assemblies that are classified into straight, twisted, or paired helical
filaments (PHFs) based on the presence or absence of periodicity of twists. The abnormal
aggregation of these protofibril assemblies forms NFTs. The NFTs in AD typically exist as PHFs
and their deposition follows a distinct spatiotemporal pattern starting in the transentorhinal
cortex before spreading to the medial and inferior temporal lobe, the parietal-occipital regions,
and the posterior cingulate cortex (11, 12, 38).
Braak staging describes the progression of tau NFTs, reflective of disease progression in typical
AD, based on the topographical distribution pattern in different brain regions, which was first
described in six stages and subsequently reduced to four stages (10-12) as follows:
B0 – Braak stage 0: no NFT pathology
B1 – Braak stages I/II (transentorhinal): NFTs predominantly in the entorhinal cortex;
associated with an asymptomatic period also known as preclinical AD

B2 – Braak stages III/IV (limbic): abundant NFTs in the hippocampus and amygdala and
extending slightly into the association cortex; associated with incipient and subtle
memory loss also known as mild cognitive impairment (MCI)
B3 – Braak stages V/VI (isocortical): NFTs widely distributed throughout the neocortex;
associated with objectively measured progressive cognitive impairment and advanced
dementia
This staging framework is described here as background to the underlying pathophysiology of
AD and does not reflect the intended use of florquinitau F 18, which does not provide a Braak
stage readout.
2.1.3. Current Diagnostic Framework
These pathological insights have fundamentally reshaped how AD is diagnosed and staged. The
most recent updates to the diagnostic criteria for AD and clinical practice guidelines for amyloid
and tau PET reflect the current understanding that AD is defined biologically by
neuropathologic findings, not based on clinical syndrome, and that the disease is diagnosed by
detection of abnormal AD neuropathologic biomarkers (i.e., amyloid beta and tau) (5, 13). It is
also understood that the disease continuum consists of a temporal pattern of changes in AD
biomarker abnormalities that begins with the appearance of brain pathology in asymptomatic
individuals and progresses through stages of increasing pathologic burden eventually leading to
the appearance and progression of clinical symptoms. The temporal relationship of AD
biomarkers can be described by the stereotypical sequence of abnormal amyloid pathology in
the earliest stages of the disease followed by the spread of tau pathology in higher biological
stages, leading to abnormal markers of neurodegeneration and increasing severity in cognitive
decline.
2.1.4. Clinical Populations
The distribution of tau pathology varies significantly across clinical populations. In individuals
with cognitive impairment and amyloid positivity, moderate to advanced tau NFT pathology
(corresponding to Braak stages III to VI) are nearly always present. Autopsy data from 4,637
symptomatic individuals (Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) >0) with Consortium to Establish a
Registry for Alzheimer’s Disease (CERAD) moderate/frequent neuritic plaque density scores
(potentially detectable on amyloid PET) demonstrated that 4,390/4,637 (95%) had Braak NFT
stage III to VI (B2/B3) (13). A small percentage (~5%) of B1 NFT is observed in cognitively
impaired amyloid positive patients, while the cognitively unimpaired, amyloid negative
population is expected to demonstrate predominantly B0 NFT (39).

2.2. Analysis of Current Treatment Options
The clinical diagnosis of AD is based on neuropsychological evaluations, often supplemented
with biological markers. These tools can be organized by their invasiveness and the type of
information they provide.

Clinical and Structural Assessment: Neuropsychological evaluations assess cognitive function,
while MRI provides structural imaging of brain atrophy in AD-related patterns (14).
Functional Imaging: [18F]FDG PET measures regional cerebral hypometabolism, providing
information about neuronal dysfunction, including in AD (15, 16).
Amyloid Biomarkers: Amyloid PET imaging measures the extent of aggregated neuritic amyloid
plaques in the brain, while fluid-based assays measure soluble amyloid peptide fragments, such
as Aβ42 and Aβ40, or composite amyloid/tau ratios, such as pTau181/Aβ42 and pTau217/Aβ42,
in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and plasma. Both modalities serve as biomarkers for the same
pathologic process of AD and are classified by the National Institute on Aging and the
Alzheimer’s Association as Core 1 biomarkers (13, 17). Currently FDA-approved amyloid PET
drugs include florbetapir F 18, flutemetamol F 18, and florbetaben F 18, while FDA-cleared
fluid-based amyloid assays include the Fujirebio Lumipulse G Aβ42/40 CSF ratio and
pTau217/Aβ42 plasma ratio, and the Roche Elecsys pTau181/Aβ42 CSF ratio and pTau181
plasma assay. These tests report a single threshold (positive/negative) or dual thresholds
(positive/negative/indeterminate) to reflect the degree of abnormality.
Tau Fluid Biomarkers: In vivo assessment of soluble forms of pathological tau can be measured
through assays that measure total (T-tau) and phosphorylated tau (P-tau) in the CSF and plasma
(18-22). P-tau reflects AD-specific tauopathy, whereas T-tau is a non-specific marker of general
neuronal injury. Importantly, no standalone tau-only fluid assay is currently FDA-cleared
specifically for AD diagnosis. The FDA-cleared assays incorporating tau analytes (e.g., pTau181
and pTau217) are composite ratio assays that are classified as amyloid/Core 1 biomarkers, as
described above. Tau fluid biomarker assays do not provide information on the spatial
distribution of tau pathology, a characteristic that may be relevant in the evaluation of
cognitively impaired patients being assessed for AD. Additionally, fluid biomarkers are currently
limited in their ability to evaluate biological changes within individuals over time.
2.2.1. Tau PET
There have been significant efforts to develop PET imaging ligands that bind to tau with high
affinity and enable the visualization, mapping, and quantification of tau in the living brain (3437). Selective in vivo tau imaging is relatively noninvasive and may have the ability to measure
the spatiotemporal distribution and progression of tau deposition.
Currently, Tauvid (flortaucipir F 18) is the only approved PET drug targeted to tau. It is indicated
to estimate the density and distribution of aggregated tau NFTs in adult patients with cognitive
impairment being evaluated for AD. Flortaucipir F 18 binds to intracellular, phosphorylated,
paired helical filamental tau that is specific for AD (40). Tauvid has been shown to exhibit offtarget binding in the choroid plexus, which may affect interpretation of medial temporal lobe
signal (23, 25), and off-target binding to monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A) and B (MAO-B) has also
been reported (24).

2.2.1.1. Appropriate Use Criteria for Tau PET
Recent appropriate use criteria developed through expert consensus by the Alzheimer’s

6. Clinical Pharmacology
6.1. Executive Summary
Florquinitau F 18 is a fluorine-18 labeled small molecule that binds to tau NFT in the brain.
Florquinitau F 18 is intended for PET of the brain in adults with cognitive impairment who are
being evaluated for AD to identify patients with tau NFT pathology. The Applicant’s proposed
activity of florquinitau F 18 is 185 MBq (5 mCi) as a bolus IV injection. The proposed mass dose
is not more than (b)
μg per dose. Imaging is proposed to be initiated at 90 minutes post(4)
administration, with an imaging duration of 20 minutes.
The efficacy of florquinitau F 18 was evaluated in two phase 3 studies: CE-20-01 and CE-23-01.
The proposed injected activity, mass dose, and imaging acquisition window and duration were
used in these pivotal studies. The co-primary efficacy endpoints of both studies were PPA and
NPA of florquinitau F 18 PET images for identification of participants with tau NFT pathology as
determined by five independent readers on a participant-level visual read compared to the
reference standard. Both co-primary endpoints met prespecified thresholds for both studies.
The safety of florquinitau F 18 was evaluated in pooled data from studies AIBL and POINTER,
and a meta-analysis of other studies and a global safety database. The adverse events
associated with florquinitau F 18 were generally uncommon and non-serious. See additional
details on efficacy and safety in Section 8.
The proposed injected radioactivity, mass dose, and imaging acquisition window are acceptable

Required for Subpopulations Based on Intrinsic Patient Factors?
Dosage adjustments are not required based on intrinsic factors (age and sex) according to
efficacy analyses by subgroups (Section 8.1). Participants with hepatic or renal impairment
were excluded from the phase 3 studies. The impact of hepatic or renal function on efficacy and
safety has not been evaluated. Nonetheless, hepatic or renal dysfunction is not expected to
impact florquinitau F 18 safety and efficacy. Safety concerns are minimal given the microdose
administration and the absence of major safety signals (Section 8.2). Additionally, the brain
imaging signal is predominantly derived from parent compound with minimal radiometabolite
entering the brain. Therefore, reduced metabolism or clearance would not compromise image
quality.

Five different neuroradiologists for each study independently assessed the florquinitau F 18 PET
images. All readers had no prior experience with florquinitau F 18 PET nor did they participate
in any other prior studies for florquinitau F 18. All readers were to assess the images in a
randomized fashion, blinded to all clinical and demographic information, including amyloid
pathology status, cognitive/disease status, brain MRI results, and image source study (TANGO,
AIBL, or POINTER).
Prior to image interpretation, all readers underwent a training program on a visual reading
methodology for florquinitau F 18 PET that was developed and validated as described in
Shuping et al. (2023) (43). Images used for reader training and testing were not used in any
other studies in the florquinitau F 18 development program, including CE-20-01 and CE-23-01,
or in the validation of the read methodology (43).

MTL Only: Elevated intensity in any structure in the MTL (defined to include

surfaces of the temporal lobe)
MTL And: Elevated signal in the MTL and at least one additional neocortical region in
either or both hemispheres. Uptake in the lateral temporal cortex (including
anterolateral tissue and fusiform), frontal, parietal, occipital, and/or cingulate regions all
contribute to “AND” (consistent with B2 or B3 NFT score)

Only one classification from the four categories above was allowed for each image. No
deviations from the four categories were allowed. Selection of an image category was required
before readers could progress to reviewing the next image. The readers performed the visual
interpretations based on the PET images alone without co-registration of MRI brain scans. The
image classifications were dichotomized into a positive or negative finding as follows:

Positive: MTL Only, MTL And (consistent with B1, B2, or B3 NFT score)
Negative: No Uptake, Outside MTL (consistent with B0 NFT score)

8.1.3. Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness
Two primary efficacy studies submitted in this NDA, CE-20-01 and CE-23-01, evaluated the
diagnostic performance of florquinitau F 18 for the identification of patients with tau NFT
pathology in the brain using multiple blinded, independent readers, and both studies met their
predefined statistical success criteria. Both studies used a clinicoradiographic reference
standard for tau NFT pathology classification rather than the histopathological truth standard
72

Reference ID: 5852154

NDA 220496 Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
Tauklarify (florquinitau F 18)
that has been relied upon to support approval of other PET drugs for the assessment of brain
pathology in AD. To construct the clinicoradiographic reference standard, the Applicant used
clinical evaluation of cognitive function together with amyloid status based on amyloid PET
imaging to identify:
•
•
•

Participants most likely to have NFTs related to AD (cognitively impaired and amyloid
PET positive (CI/A+));
Participants most likely not to have NFTs (cognitively unimpaired and amyloid PET
negative (CU/A-));
Participants unlikely to have AD-related tau NFTs (cognitively impaired and amyloid PET
negative (CI/A-) or cognitively unimpaired and amyloid PET positive (CU/A+)).

CI/A+ and CU/A- participants represent relatively predictable populations for tau positivity and
tau negativity, respectively. Specifically, CI/A+ represents predominantly B2 or B3 NFT stage
based on established neuropathological criteria (11) and updated diagnostic frameworks (13).
This classification is supported by neuropathology-based observations that B2/B3 tau
(moderate to advanced tau NFT pathology) is observed in ~95% of patients with amyloid
positive (moderate to frequent neuritic plaques), clinically impaired stages of AD (MCI and mild
AD dementia). Conversely, CU/A- is expected to represent predominantly B0 NFT (39). The
other populations, CI/A- and CU/A+, were excluded from the reference standard, as the
presence of NFTs is neither uniform nor predictable in either of these populations.
A notable weakness of the clinicoradiographic reference standard thus lies in its use of subjects
of essentially known disease status rather than a population of intended clinical use in which
disease status is unknown. Furthermore, CU/A- patients do not represent an intended clinical
population for tau PET imaging at all, as tau PET is not recommended in patients who are
cognitively unimpaired. Diagnostic performance estimates observed in the trials may therefore
differ from the performance in the full spectrum of patients in whom the test may be used
clinically.
However, use of a histopathological truth standard also involves weakness regarding the
relevance of the study population to the population of intended clinical use. Since brain
histopathology is typically acquired at autopsy, enrollment of subjects with limited life
expectancy is employed for trial feasibility. These patients tend to be of older age and skew the
distribution of AD towards later stages of disease, and thus more advanced tau pathology.
The clinicoradiographic reference standard is described in detail in Section 14 of labeling for
florquinitau F 18 PET. As discussed elsewhere in this review, PPA and NPA are reported as
(b) (4)
performance metrics in Section 14,
to reflect the use of
the clinicoradiographic reference standard. The study population is also emphasized in Section
5 of labeling in a warning for risk of misdiagnosis. This warning further identifies risks of false
positive and false negative results and suggests additional evaluation when clinical uncertainty
remains.
Another weakness of the clinicoradiographic reference standard is that a limited proportion of
patients with B1 NFT are expected in both the cognitively impaired, amyloid positive (tau
73

NDA 220496 Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
Tauklarify (florquinitau F 18)
positive reference standard) population and the cognitively unimpaired, amyloid negative (tau
negative reference standard) population. Thus, B1 NFT cannot be clearly differentiated from
either B0 or B2/B3 NFT. As such, the performance of florquinitau F 18 for identification of
patients within this particular early segment of the pathological spectrum could not be
determined. Of note, distinguishing B1 from B2 NFT by PET is technically challenging in general
due to overlapping anatomical distribution patterns, image resolution limitations, partial
volume effects, and patient movement-related artifacts. Furthermore, the B1 stage is known to
represent a short-lived, transient phase of tau pathology with a relatively low prevalence (44,
45).
Once again, weakness related to inadequate evaluation of early stages of tau pathology is also
shared with use of a histopathological truth standard. The above described skew of the autopsy
study population to more advanced stages of AD is suspected to increase diagnostic
performance estimates in related trials, as reflected in the warnings and precautions of the
flortaucipir F 18 prescribing information that states, “performance for detecting tau pathology
may be lower in patients in earlier stages of the pathological spectrum” (46). This same
statement is included in the Section 5 warning for risk of misdiagnosis in the florquinitau F 18
PET prescribing information.
Because the clinicoradiographic reference standard is not histopathologically confirmed, there
is a degree of uncertainty in the actual tau status of the study population. As mentioned above,
a limited proportion of the tau negative reference standard is actually expected to have B1 NFT.
Again, similar weakness related to uncertainty of actual tau status is associated with use of a
histopathological truth standard. Even when studying a population with limited life expectancy,
PET scans are often acquired significantly in advance of death and autopsy. Thus, to the extent
that pathology develops or changes over the interval, as expected in AD, the truth standard
determined at autopsy may not align with the histopathology that was actually present at the
time of tau PET. On this note, imaging-neuropathology correlative data suggest that tau PETderived Braak staging reflects an earlier neuropathologic Braak stage than what is observed
microscopically at autopsy (47, 48). The previously described labeled warning regarding risk of
misdiagnosis is applicable to the uncertainty in both the clinicoradiographic reference standard
and histopathological truth standard.
Unlike a histopathological truth standard, the clinicoradiographic reference standard relies on
subject-level characteristics. Thus, an additional weakness of the clinicoradiographic reference
standard is that it cannot assess performance of florquinitau F 18 PET for determining regional
tau distribution, such as Braak stage. This weakness is not shared by a histopathological truth
standard but is addressed through the wording of the indication statement, which states the
recommended use for identifying patients with tau NFT pathology rather than the Applicant’s
(b) (4)
initial proposal of detection of tau NFTs

initially proposed by the Applicant in Section 2 of labeling was

An additional labeling approach regarding effectiveness was the placement of a limitation of

To summarize, while the clinicoradiographic reference standard has several weaknesses
including suboptimal representation of the population of intended clinical use, limited ability to
assess diagnostic performance in early disease, and additional inherent uncertainty in actual
tau pathology status, these weaknesses are generally shared to some extent by the traditional
histopathological truth standard and are addressed in labeling. The additional weakness of the
subject-level basis of the clinicoradiographic reference standard is also addressed in labeling,
particularly through the wording of the indications statement.
In conclusion, Study CE-20-01 and CE-23-01 are considered adequate and well-controlled and
demonstrate substantial evidence of effectiveness of florquinitau F 18 for PET of the brain in
adults with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for Alzheimer’s disease to identify
patients with tau neurofibrillary tangle (NFT) pathology. Florquinitau F 18 is expected to have
utility in certain clinical scenarios, such as evaluation of patients with suspected AD but atypical
presentation, as reflected in current appropriate use criteria for tau PET (5).

8.2.11. Integrated Assessment of Safety
Safety of this product was primarily evaluated in 1,734 participants in two studies, AIBL and
POINTER. Safety of this product was also evaluated in 4,230 participants in other studies.
Observed adverse reactions across Study AIBL, Study POINTER, and the other studies were
uncommon and non-serious. Weaknesses in the safety database related to lack of detailed data
for vital sign and laboratory value analyses are offset by the relatively large number of exposed
subjects and overall benign safety profile. The most common adverse reactions in Studies AIBL
and POINTER were headache, nausea, injection site reactions, dizziness, and abdominal
discomfort.
Radiation exposure is typical of PET imaging and is estimated to impart insignificant risk in the
context of use. This issue was included as a warning, “Radiation Risk,” in the product labeling.
Another safety issue is the risk of misdiagnosis (see Sections 1.3, 8.1.3, and 11.1). This was
included as a warning, “Risk of Misdiagnosis in Patients Being Evaluated for Alzheimer’s
Disease”, in the product labeling.

8.3. Statistical Issues
This NDA submission includes two phase 3 studies, CE-20-01 and CE-23-01. Neither study
administered florquinitau F 18 to participants; instead, five blinded central readers in each
study read existing images sampled from the TANGO (all reference standard (RS) positive
subjects) and POINTER (all RS negative subjects) studies for Study CE-20-01, and from the AIBL
(all RS positive subjects) and POINTER studies for Study CE-23-01. The primary objective for
both studies was to determine the sensitivity and specificity of florquinitau F 18 PET images for
identification of tau NFT as determined by five independent central readers on a subject-level
visual read. Both studies also shared the same primary analysis method and success rule.

Statistical Analysis Plan for Co-Primary Endpoints
Co-primary endpoints were the subject-level sensitivity and specificity of florquinitau F 18 PET
images for identification of tau NFT as determined by five independent readers on a subjectlevel visual read compared to the RS.
Point estimates of the sensitivity and specificity for each reader were to be provided, along with
95% CIs calculated using the Wilson score method. If the lower bound of the 95% CI for
sensitivity was greater than 0.6 and the lower bound of the 95% CI for specificity was greater
than 0.6, then the null hypotheses were to be rejected in favor of the alternatives for an
individual reader.
The studies were considered to successfully demonstrate efficacy if at least the same three out
of five readers rejected both null hypotheses.
The Applicant did not plan for missing data imputation.
FDA Terminology Clarification: The co-primary endpoints are referred to as PPA and NPA,
rather than sensitivity and specificity, respectively. This terminology change reflects the
absence of histopathology results as the standard of truth and acknowledges that the employed
reference standard does not constitute the ideal reference standard for diagnostic performance
assessment, although we note that autopsy-based histopathology is not without limitations.

Investigation of Randomization Deviation
To evaluate the potential effect of randomization deviation in Study CE-20-01, the nature of the
misalignment of the randomization order was evaluated, as summarized in Table 51. For both
Reader 1 and Reader 2, who shared similar deviation patterns, the two readers performed
image interpretation consecutively by decreasing VRTPID, a system-assigned 5-digit ID number
for a scan, instead of following the assigned randomized read order. The first 92 to 93
consecutive reads completed by both readers were from POINTER data (all RS Negative); the
following 70 consecutive reads completed by both readers were from TANGO data (all RS
Positive). Such grouping could introduce bias. The last 116 to 117 reads completed by the two
readers did not show obvious patterns in the dataset.
The reviewer conducted an analysis reporting the co-primary endpoint results separately for
two groups: the first 163 subjects versus the last 116 subjects for Reader 1, and the first 162
subjects versus the last 117 subjects for Reader 2 (Table 52 and Table 53). The division was
made based on the presence or absence of patterns in the dataset in the actual read order. The
reviewer noted the following:
•

•

Numerically lower PPA and higher NPA were observed for Reader 1 in the initial 163
reads compared to the last 116 reads. Numerically higher PPA and NPA, and thus
numerically overall better performance, were observed for Reader 2 in the initial 162
reads compared to the last 117 reads. Whether Reader 1's performance was affected is
difficult to conclude due to the trade-off in PPA and NPA, but the dataset pattern in the
actual read order had the potential to influence Reader 2's performance more favorably.
The PPA and NPA from the subgroup of the last 116 to 117 subjects for Reader 1 and
Reader 2, where no pattern was observed, achieved the individual reader success rule,
with the lower bound of the 95% CI exceeding the respective PPA and NPA thresholds.

8.3.3. Statistical Team’s Conclusions and Recommendations
Both study CE-20-01 and study CE-23-01 met prespecified success criteria for the co-primary
endpoints. The co-primary endpoints are referred to as PPA and NPA in this review, rather than
as sensitivity and specificity, respectively, as proposed by the Applicant. This terminology
change reflects the absence of pathology-confirmed results as the standard of truth. Similarly,
the term “identification” is preferred over “detection” for drug labeling as the reference
standard does not support a confirmed pathology detection claim.
The clinical data from CE-20-01 and CE-23-01 were derived from three image source studies,
TANGO, POINTER, and AIBL. Only cognitively unimpaired/amyloid-negative (CU/A-) and
cognitively impaired/amyloid-positive (CI/A+) participants in the source studies were included
in CE-20-01 and CE-23-01. Cognitively unimpaired/amyloid-positive (CU/A+) and cognitively
impaired/amyloid-negative (CI/A-) subjects were not included. This non-representative
sampling is a weakness of the reference standard and related study population that could
introduce spectrum bias and may limit applicability of observed performance characteristic
estimates across the full spectrum of patients being evaluated for AD. See Section 8.1.3 for
further clinical discussion.
Based on collaborative discussion between the clinical and statistical review teams, the
recommended harmonized indication statement is as follows:
“TAUKLARIFY is indicated for positron emission tomography (PET) of the brain in adults with
cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for Alzheimer’s disease to identify patients with
tau neurofibrillary tangle (NFT) pathology”.

8.4. Conclusions and Recommendations

Drug Interactions

Based on the findings from Study e0073, the review team recommended adding a DRUG
INTERACTIONS section. The key points supporting this decision are outlined below.
- Supporting Evidence: The study demonstrated that tobacco smoke, a wellestablished inducer of the enzyme CYP1A2, affects Tauklarify imaging. Active
smokers in the study showed a reduction in brain regional signal intensity, an effect
that was reversed after they stopped smoking. This observation is consistent with
the known pharmacokinetic consequences of CYP1A2 induction.
- Clinical Relevance: Despite the small sample size of the study, the review team
considered the finding to be clinically relevant. This is due to the strong alignment
between the observed imaging effects and the established biological mechanism of
CYP1A2 induction.
- Guidance for Patients: The new DRUG INTERACTIONS section recommends that
patients avoid using CYP1A2 inducers, including tobacco smoking, for a minimum of
7 days prior to the administration of Tauklarify.
The final, agreed-upon language for this drug interaction is as follows:
CYP1A2 Inducers
Avoid use of CYP1A2 inducers, including tobacco smoking, at least 7 days before
TAUKLARIFY administration. Use of CYP1A2 inducers can be resumed after
completion of imaging.
Florquinitau F 18 is a substrate of the CYP1A2 enzyme [see Clinical Pharmacology
(12.3)]. Concomitant use of florquinitau F 18 with CYP1A2 inducers, including
tobacco smoking, may decrease the florquinitau F 18 parent fraction in plasma and

Comments
Per the multi-disciplinary review findings, this NDA submission supports an indication for
florquinitau F 18 for PET of the brain in adults with cognitive impairment who are being
evaluated for AD to identify patients with tau NFT pathology.
As detailed in the OPQ Integrated Quality Assessment, no outstanding product quality issues
are present, with substance precursor characterization, drug product specifications,
manufacturing process validation, facility compliance, and microbiological controls being
adequately addressed. Per the pharmacology/toxicology review, nonclinical characterization is
sufficient. As discussed in the clinical pharmacology review, PK evaluation, administered activity
and mass dose selection, and proposed image acquisition time appear adequate. An effect of
CYP1A2 inducers, including tobacco smoking, on florquinitau F 18 levels is noted and addressed
in Section 7 of the prescribing information.
The clinical and statistical review appropriately concludes that substantial evidence of
effectiveness of florquinitau F 18 was demonstrated for the recommended indication through
the success of two adequate and well-controlled trials, CE-20-01 and CE-23-01. The participantlevel basis of the clinicoradiographic reference standard utilized in both trials is reflected
through the indication of florquinitau F 18 PET for identifying patients with tau NFT pathology
rather than for evaluating regional distribution of tau NFTs in the brain. The relevant binary
reading method that was used to generate diagnostic performance estimates in CE-20-01 and
CE-23-01 is appropriately included in Section 2 of labeling. With input from the FDA’s Center for
Devices and Radiological Health, similar recommendations are reflected in the Applicant’s
reader training materials.
Weaknesses of the clinicoradiographic reference standard utilized in both trials, including
suboptimal representation of the population of intended clinical use, limited ability to assess
performance in early disease, and inherent uncertainty of actual tau pathology status, are also
