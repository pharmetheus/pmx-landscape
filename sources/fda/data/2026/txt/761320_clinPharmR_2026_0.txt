1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Outlook Therapeutics, Inc. is developing ONS‐5010 (bevacizumab‐vikg; the proposed brand name,
LYTENAVA), a recombinant humanized immunoglobulin gamma 1 (IgG1) monoclonal antibody (mAb)
that selectively binds to all isoforms of human VEGF, for the treatment of patients with neovascular
(wet) age‐related macular degeneration (nAMD). The proposed dosage is 1.25 mg per eye to be
administered intravitreal (IVT) injection once a month.
The pivotal evidence of effectiveness of ONS‐5010 is based on a single, randomized, double‐blind,
active‐controlled trial (Study ONS‐5010‐002, or NORSE TWO), in which patients with nAMD received
ONS‐5010 1.25 mg or ranibizumab (Lucentis®) 0.5 mg IVT injection monthly for 1 year. The Applicant
also submitted supportive data including a proof‐of‐concept trial (Study ONS‐5010‐001, or NORSE ONE)
and a systematic review of the use of Avastin (bevacizumab) (Report ONS‐5010‐GMK) as general medical
knowledge where the Applicant concluded Avastin has been used off‐label for AMD with IVT injection
for a long time.
In Studies ONS‐5010‐001 and ONS‐5010‐002, in a subset of subjects with nAMD (N=14),
pharmacokinetics of ONS‐5010 following 1.25 mg IVT injection was evaluated indicating minimal
systemic exposure following IVT injection. In the subset of subjects, ADA samples were collected and
none of them showed positive ADA status.

2. SUMMARY OF CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY ASSESSMENT
2.1 Pharmacology and Clinical Pharmacokinetics
Mechanism of Action
ONS‐5010 is a VEGF inhibitor that binds VEGF and prevents the interaction of VEGF to its receptors (Flt‐1
and KDR) on the surface of endothelial cells. A human VEGF inhibitor that binds to all isoforms of VEGF‐A
suppresses endothelial cell proliferation, neovascularization, and vascular permeability.
Pharmacokinetics
Following intravitreal administration of ONS‐5010, the systemic exposure to ONS‐5010 is minimal. In 14
subjects who received a single 1.25 mg dose of ONS‐5010, the serum ONS‐5010 concentration was
assessed up to Day 4. The serum ONS‐5010 concentrations gradually increased over time, so the highest
concentrations observed were approximately at Day 4 (the last collection time) ranging from 33 to 270
ng/mL, which was <1% of the Cmax after intravenous infusion of 2 mg/kg bevacizumab‐vikg. However,
due to the limited sample collection, Cmax or AUC could not be adequately characterized.
Immunogenicity
The immunogenicity of ONS‐5010 was low. None of the subjects who received ONS‐5010 and were
subsequently evaluated for immunogenicity had detectable antidrug antibodies (ADA) at any timepoint.

2.2 Dosing and Therapeutic Individualization
2.2.1 General dosing
The proposed dosing regimen for ONS‐5010 is 1.25 mg (0.05 mL of 25 mg/mL solution) IVT injection per
eye once a month. Dosing regimen used in the clinical trials in patients with nAMD (Studies ONS‐5010‐
001 and ONS‐5010‐002) is identical with the proposed dosing regimen.
2.2.2 Therapeutic individualization
The Applicant did not propose any therapeutic individualization. The available clinical pharmacology
information (i.e., limited systemic exposure following IVT injection) does not warrant a need for
therapeutic individualization.

2.3 Outstanding Issues
There are no outstanding issues from a clinical pharmacology perspective.

3. COMPREHENSIVE CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY REVIEW
3.1 Overview of the Product and Regulatory Background
Avastin® (bevacizumab for IV injection), a recombinant humanized IgG1 mAb specific for human VEGF, is
currently approved for the first‐ or second‐line treatment of various cancer including metastatic
colorectal cancer and non‐squamous non‐small cell lung cancer, but it has not been approved for use in
ophthalmic indications. Per the Applicant’s literature review (Report ONS‐5010‐GMK), Avastin has been
used off‐label when injected intravitreally for neovascularizing ophthalmic diseases including nAMD.
The proposed product, ONS‐5010, is an ophthalmic preparation of bevacizumab‐vikg developed for the
treatment of nAMD to meet the need of intravitreal bevacizumab. ONS‐5010 is a preservative free,
sterile, ocular solution in a single‐use glass vial to deliver 0.05 mL of 25 mg/mL (1.25 mg). The proposed
dosage is 1.25 mg per eye every month.
Although the active moiety of ONS‐5010 is bevacizumab‐vikg, this BLA was submitted under Section
351(a) of the PHS Act, i.e., a “stand‐alone” application because the route of administration and the
indication are different from those of Avastin. Therefore, this BLA cannot rely on published literature
describing studies of Avastin to fulfill a requirement for licensure.

3.3 Clinical Pharmacology Questions
3.3.1 Does the clinical pharmacology information provide supportive evidence of effectiveness?
The clinical pharmacology information provides neither pivotal nor supportive evidence of effectiveness.
ONS‐5010 is administered via IVT route and the side of action is eye. Systemic exposure of bevacizumab
is minimal following IVT injection. Clinical efficacy in terms of the best corrected visual acuity (BCVA)
evaluated in Study ONS‐5010‐002 (NORSE TWO) provides pivotal evidence of effectiveness.

3.3.2 Is the proposed general dosing regimen appropriate for the general patient population
for which the indication is being sought?
The proposed dosing regimen of ONS‐5010 is 1.25 mg (0.05 mL of 25 mg/mL solution) IVT injection per
eye once a month. The regimen used in the clinical trials in patients with nAMD (Studies ONS‐5010‐001
and ONS‐5010‐002) is consistent with the proposed regimen, which is deemed appropriate.
There were no other dosing regimens evaluated in the clinical trials and no dose‐finding studies were
separately conducted. The Applicant determined the dose to be used in the clinical trials based on the

3.3.3 Is an alternative dosing regimen and/or management strategy required for
subpopulations based on intrinsic/extrinsic factors?
The Applicant did not propose any therapeutic individualization based on intrinsic/extrinsic factors.
Given the IVT route of administration and limited systemic exposure following IVT administration, any
alternative dosing regimens are not warranted.

3.3.4 What is the immunogenicity of the proposed drug product?
The immunogenicity of ONS‐5010 was low. None of the subjects who received ONS‐5010 and were
subsequently evaluated for immunogenicity had detectable antidrug antibodies (ADA) at any timepoint.
Clinically significant effect of ADA is not expected given the minimal systemic exposure and negative
ADA status following IVT injection.
In a total 14 subjects with nAMD (11 subjects from Study ONS‐5010‐001 and 3 subjects from Study ONS‐
5010‐002) whose PK was evaluated, ADA samples were collected prior to drug administration on Day 0,
again on Days 1, 2, 3, 4, prior to study drug administration on Day 30, again on Days 31, 32, 33, 34, and
prior to study drug administration on Day 240.
A total 128 samples were analyzed, of which none of them were confirmed positive for ADA.
• Study ONS‐5010‐001 – 95 samples from 11 subjects were analyzed yielding 2 screened positive

reported as negative for presence of ADA
• Study ONS‐5010‐002 – 33 samples from 3 subjects were analyzed yielding no screened positive
samples, therefore all individuals tested were reported as negative for the presence of ADA
Given all negative ADA status, further analysis for ADA effect on PK, efficacy, or safety was not

4.1 Clinical Pharmacokinetics and Immunogenicity
IVT administration in Subjects with nAMD (Studies ONS‐5010‐001 and ONS‐5010‐002)
Both Studies ONS‐5010‐001 and ONS‐5010‐002 were multicenter, randomized, double‐masked,
controlled studies designed primarily to evaluate the efficacy and safety of intravitreally administered
ONS‐5010. Subjects randomized to receive ONS‐5010 were administered a monthly intravitreal injection
of 1.25 mg of ONS‐5010 in the study eye for up to 12 months until study completion.
As a PK subset population, a total 14 subjects (N=11 from Study ONS‐5010‐001, N=3 from Study ONS‐
5010‐002) who received ONS‐5010 in the study eye only underwent serum sampling for PK analyses and
immunogenicity testing.
Samples for PK analysis were collected prior to study drug administration on Day 0, and again on Days 1,
2, 3, and 4. Samples for ADA analysis were collected prior to study drug administration on Day 0, again
on Days 1, 2, 3, 4, prior to study drug administration on Day 30, again on Days 31, 32, 33, 34, and prior
to study drug administration on Day 240.
The mean (SD) serum concentrations of ONS‐5010 versus time profiles are presented in Figure 1.
Overall, following intravitreal administration of a single 1.25 mg dose of ONS‐5010, mean serum
concentrations of ONS‐5010 gradually increased with time until the last sample collection on Day 4. The
individual serum concentration‐time profiles are presented in Figure 2.

IV administration in Healthy Subjects (Study ONS‐1045‐001)
Study ONS‐1045‐001 was a randomized, single‐dose, double‐blind, parallel study to define the PK of
ONS‐1045 (i.e., IV dosage form of ONS‐5010 delivered at a concentration of 100 mg/4 mL) in comparison
with the US and EU reference forms of Avastin and to demonstrate PK bioequivalence of bevacizumab‐
vikg to the US and EU forms of Avastin (bevacizumab). In this study, subjects were randomized to 1 of 3
study drug groups. After a single 2.0 mg/kg IV infusion of study drug, safety, immunogenicity, and PK
data were collected and reviewed for a total of 98 days after study drug administration.

4.2 Bioanalytical Method Report
The quantitation of ONS‐5010 in human serum was performed using a validated
electrochemiluminescent (ECL) method, for which the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) was 2 ng/mL.
The bioanalytical method validation was acceptable (Table 7).
Serum samples were stored for a maximum of 345 days between sample collection and analysis
(including incurred samples), within the 359 days demonstrated long‐term storage stability in human
serum at ‐80 °C. Incurred sample reanalysis (ISR) was performed for approximately 20% of the samples,
in which all samples showed a relative percent difference of ± 30%).
