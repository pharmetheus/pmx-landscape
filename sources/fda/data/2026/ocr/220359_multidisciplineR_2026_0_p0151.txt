Based on PBPK modeling, camizestrant is considered a strong inhibitor of CYP2C9 and a
strong inhibitor of CYP2C19 (13- and 8-fold increases in substrate exposure,
respectively). See Section 19.4.3.4.3 for detailed PBPK assessment. The significant
change in estimated exposure of substrates of CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 may be clinically
televant for less sensitive substrates; therefore, FDA recommends avoiding concomitant
use of camizestrant with all substrates of CYP2C9 and CYP2C19.

FDA agrees that camizestrant is an inhibitor of CYP3A and that dosage adjustment of
CYP3A substrates where minimal increases in concentration may lead to serious adverse
reactions may be necessary. Part A of clinical DDI study D8532c00006 adequately
evaluated the effect of camizestrant on the PK of a sensitive CYP3A substrate
(midazolam). The study contained 3 treatment parts. During Treatment Period 1,
participants received a single oral 1 mg dose of midazolam (Day 1), during Period 2
participants received camizestrant 75 mg QD from Days 2 to 6, and during Period 3
participants received a single oral 1 mg dose of midazolam and a single 75 mg dose of
camizestrant on Day 7 with PK sampling on Day 7 to Day 8.

The FDA reviewer’s independent analysis was consistent with the Applicant’s findings
demonstrating a 1.5-fold and 2-fold increase in midazolam AUC and Cmax GMR,
respectively, when administered concomitantly with camizestrant. These results support a
conclusion that camizestrant is a weak to moderate inhibitor of CYP3A. More AEs were
reported in participants when administered midazolam (22%) or camizestrant (27%)
alone compared with when midazolam and camizestrant were administered
concomitantly (4.5%). These data do not raise any new safety concerns.

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment that camizestrant is an inhibitor of CYP2B6
at clinically relevant concentrations in vitro. A PMR will be issued to conduct clinical
drug interaction trials to assess the effect of camizestrant on the PK of substrates of
CYP2B6 to inform an appropriate drug interaction management strategy for
coadministration with camizestrant.

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position that camizestrant is not a substrate of CYP1A2
at clinically relevant concentrations in vitro.
