The safety profile of camizestrant in combination with CDK4/6i is generally acceptable except
for the need to better characterize the QTc interval prolonging effects of camizestrant and to
better understand the cardiac risk associated with this product. A PMR will be issued for further
evaluation and a Boxed Warning will be included in labeling.

Overall, SERENA-6 met its primary PFS endpoint, but there is uncertainty regarding
clinical benefit and verification is needed.

The central issue raised by SERENA-6 is whether it is beneficial for patients to switch treatment
upon detection of ESR/m or switch treatment at the standard timepoint of radiographic
progression. One PMR will be issued for the Applicant to conduct a new trial in patients with
HR+HER2- MBC who have received an AI and CDK4/6i for at least 6 months. Patients will be
tested approximately every 3 months and if ESR/m is detected, will be randomized to either
switch treatment to camizestrant with continued CDK4/6i or continue treatment with AI and
CDK4/6i and switch treatment to camizestrant with or without CDK4/6i at radiographic
progression. The primary endpoint will be TSF defined as time from randomization until disease
progression by RECIST v1.1 criteria during treatment with camizestrant with or without
CDK4/6i, or death, whichever occurs first.

An additional PMR will be issued for final OS results from SERENA-6. While OS, which is
measured from randomization at detection of ESR/m, will not allow us to determine whether an
early switch in treatment at detection of an ESR/m is better than the standard switch at
radiographic disease progression, the review team believes that a statistically significant
improvement in OS measured from any starting point could still provide evidence of clinical
benefit.
