with drugs that have QTc interval prolonging effects, such as ribociclib, one of the CDK4/6i options on SERENA-6, further elevates the risk of
developing a life-threatening arrhythmia. Ribociclib also increases the exposure of camizestrant through a drug-drug interaction, which could
further potentiate camizestrant’s heart rate lowering and QTc interval prolonging effects, increasing the risk of arrhythmia. One patient enrolled
in the phase 1 trial SERENA-1 received camizestrant and ribociclib at the doses used in SERENA-6 and experienced bradycardia, QT
prolongation and a life-threatening arrhythmia (TdP). Only 23 patients enrolled to SERENA-6 received camizestrant and ribociclib, limiting
further evaluation of the cardiac safety for this combination. Ribociclib is emerging as the preferred 1L CDK4/6i in the U.S. due to demonstration
of OS benefit in MONALEESA-2 (Hortobagyi et al 2022). Further evaluation of camizestrant’s effect on the QTc interval is needed to better
understand the cardiac risk associated with this product, and a PMR will be issued.

Visual disturbances occurred in 34% of patients who received camizestrant and CDK4/6i compared to 16% who received AI and CDK4/6i. The
most common visual disturbance adverse reaction (AR) was photopsia (20% in the camizestrant and CDK4/6i arm and 8% in the AI and
CDK4/6i arm). All cases of visual disturbances were Grade 1 and 2, except for one case of Grade 3 photopsia. Photopsia cases were generally
reported as brief periods (<1 min/episode) of flashing lights that were reversible and not associated with structural findings on ophthalmic
examination. The visual PROs supported the clinician-reported observations that the events were not severe, of short duration, and did not have
significant impacts on daily living. Overall, the most common ARs (=10%) for camizestrant and CDK4/6i in SERENA-6 included neutropenia
(55%), musculoskeletal pain (35%) visual disturbance (34%), fatigue (23%), anemia (17%), dry eye (12%) and nausea (10%).

Overall, the review team concludes that SERENA-6 met its primary PFS endpoint, but because PFS is measured from randomization at ESR/m
detection, there is uncertainty as to whether this result is indicative of clinical benefit and verification is needed. The toxicity profile was
generally as expected for an ET and CDK4/6i and considered acceptable, although further evaluation of camizestrant’s effect on the QTc interval
is needed to understand the risk of arrhythmia. To mitigate the cardiotoxicity risk, a Boxed Warning and ECG monitoring guidance is included in
labeling, and a PMR will be issued to better characterize camizestrant’s impact on the QTc interval. The benefit-risk assessment is considered
favorable in the setting of an incurable disease with unmet medical need for therapies to improve long-term outcomes.
