Two PMRs will be issued for verification of clinical benefit. The main uncertainty with SERENA-6 is whether switching treatment at detection
of ESRIm is better for patients compared to switching treatment at the standard timepoint of radiographic progression. In the first PMR, the
Applicant agreed to conduct a new trial to compare the early treatment-switching strategy to the standard treatment-switching strategy. Patients
with HR+HER2- MBC with an ESR/m detected after at least 6 months of treatment with an AI and CDK4/6i will be randomized to either switch
treatment to camizestrant and CDK4/6i or continue with AI and CDK4/6i and switch to camizestrant with or without CDK4/6i at radiographic
progression. The primary endpoint will be time to strategy failure (TSF), defined as time from randomization to either progressive disease by
RECIST v1.1 while receiving camizestrant with or without CDK4/6i, or death, whichever occurs first. This trial will not isolate the effect of
camizestrant, but it will allow for assessment of the treatment strategy.

A critical aspect of the accelerated approval pathway is timely verification of clinical benefit. Typically, the confirmatory trial should be well
underway at time of regulatory action, which will not be the case for this new trial. FDA may use trials in the same setting or a related setting for
verification of clinical benefit but the ongoing trials for camizestrant in the adjuvant or metastatic setting do not necessarily address the
underlying uncertainty regarding the SERENA-6 treatment strategy. FDA is willing to exercise flexibility in terms of timing of the confirmatory
trial because the review team believes a new trial is necessary that is designed to specifically address whether early treatment-switching strategy
is beneficial for patients compared to the standard treatment-switching strategy.

The second PMR is for the final OS results from SERENA-6. Although the FDA does not typically require a statistically significant improvement
in OS for regular approval, due to the uncertainties in the SERENA-6 trial design and results, an OS benefit could help overcome some of these
shortcomings and provide evidence of clinical benefit.
