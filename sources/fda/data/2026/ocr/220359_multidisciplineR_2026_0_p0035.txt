SERENA-6 is an ongoing, randomized (1:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled, two-part clinical trial in patients with ER+HER2- MBC who had
been treated with an AI and CDK4/6 inhibitor for at least six months. Approximately 3,256 patients were tested for ESR/m in ctDNA, with
testing occurring every two to three months. A total of 315 patients with tumor ESR/m detected and no evidence of disease progression on AI and
CDK4/6i were enrolled to the second part and randomly assigned (1:1) to either switch ET to camizestrant with continued CDK4/6i or continue
AI in combination with CDK4/6i. Stratification factors included: visceral disease (yes or no), timing of ESR/m detection (first or subsequent
test), time from initiation of AI and CDK4/6i (<18 months or >18 months), and CDK4/6i choice (abemaciclib, palbociclib, or ribociclib). The
primary endpoint was PFS, defined as time from randomization at detection of ESR/m until radiographic disease progression or death. PFS2 and
OS were key secondary endpoints.

At DCO1, the trial met its primary PFS endpoint with a HR of 0.44 (95% CTI: 0.31, 0.60; p<0.00001) and an estimated median of 16 months (95%
CI: 12.7, 18.2) on the camizestrant and CDK4/6i arm and 9.2 months (95% CI: 7.2, 9.5) on the AI and CDK4/6i arm. At DCO3, the trial met its
key secondary PFS2 endpoint. OS data were not statistically significant at DCO3 and remain immature with a 58% information fraction. A final
OS analysis is still planned and anticipated to occur in 2028. Despite SERENA-6 meeting its PFS and PFS2 endpoints, the review team believes
there is uncertainty in whether clinical benefit with camizestrant has been established.

The review team’s main concern is that the treatment approach implemented in SERENA-6, where patients start a new treatment regimen at
detection of ESR/m and prior to radiographic progression, does not have established clinical benefit. The current treatment paradigm for patients
with HR+HER2- MBC includes receiving one treatment regimen until experiencing disease progression and then starting a new treatment
regimen, as outlined above. It is unknown whether switching treatment at the earlier time point of ESR/m detection improves long-term
outcomes such as OS. One concer is that shortening the duration of a treatment that a patient is tolerating well and on which they are clinically
stable could have unintended consequences on long-term outcomes. The SERENA-6 trial was not designed to compare whether changing
