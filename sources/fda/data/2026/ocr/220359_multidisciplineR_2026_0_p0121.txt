e Population PK (PopPK) analyses indicate no clinically significant
differences in PK of camizestrant in patients with mild HI (total
bilirubin < ULN and AST > ULN or total bilirubin > 1x to 1.5x
ULN and AST of any value (n=209) compared to patients with
normal hepatic function (n=546).

e FDA recommends monitoring patients with moderate or severe HI
for increased adverse reactions given the positive E-R relationships
for bradycardia and visual adverse events.

Renal Impairment (RI):

e No dosage modification is recommended for patients with CLcr 30
to <90 mL/min. The effect of severe RI and end-stage renal disease
is unknown; however, renal clearance of camizestrant is low
(4.8%).

e PopPK analyses indicate no clinically significant differences in PK
of camizestrant in patients with mild RI (CLer 60 to < 90 mL/min)
and moderate RI (CLcr 30 to < 60 mL/min) compared with patients
with normal renal function (CLer > 90 mL/min).

Other Intrinsic Factors:

Based on PopPK analyses, the other evaluated intrinsic factors i.e., age (29
to 89 years), body weight (34 to 150 kg), race (White 86%, Asian 8.5%) do
not have clinically meaningful impact on the exposure of camizestrant. No
dosage adjustment is recommended based on these intrinsic factors.

Drug
interactions

CYP3A is the primary CYP450 enzyme involved in camizestrant
metabolism.

e Camizestrant AUC increased 1.9-fold and Cmax increased 1.4-fold
following concomitant use of itraconazole (strong CYP3A
inhibitor) 200 mg QD. Camizestrant AUC and Cmax both increase
2-fold when camizestrant is used in combination with ribociclib
(strong CYP3A inhibitor) 400 or 600 mg QD.

e Camizestrant AUC decreased to 30% and Cmax decreased to 40%
following concomitant use of carbamazepine (strong CYP3A
inducer) 100 mg QD for 3 days, followed by 200 mg QD for 3
days, and then 300 mg QD for 6 days.
