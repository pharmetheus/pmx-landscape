Pharmacogenomics:

Different ESR/ mutations may confer different sensitivities to SERDs, due to the mutations’
distinct effects on protein conformation and binding sites. Furthermore, co-occurring alterations
in other biological pathways, such as activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, may also
influence SERD therapeutic response. The Applicant provided exploratory analyses for
individual ESR/ mutations and those with co-occurring PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN mutations. In the
SERENA-6 trial, patients provided ctDNA blood sample for central testing of ESR/ mutation
status every 2 to 3 months using the Guardant360® CDx assay. The assay tested for the most
prevalent ESR/ mutations in patients with metastatic HR-positive breast cancer: E380Q,
V422del, S463P, L536H/P/R, Y537C/D/N/S, and D538G. The majority of patients (81.6%) had
a single ESR/ mutation and 11.4% had two co-occurring ESR/ mutations. FDA Table 8
summarizes the prevalence of ESR/ mutations, with D538G (48.3%), Y537S (38.4%), Y537N
(17.1%) and E380Q (10.5%) being the most prevalent. The frequencies of mutations were
generally balanced between arms.
