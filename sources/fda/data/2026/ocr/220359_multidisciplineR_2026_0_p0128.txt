e Elimination: Following a single oral dose of 75 mg [C]-camizestrant in healthy
post-menopausal participants, the mean total recovery of radioactivity was 16.7% from urine
and 61.4% from feces, suggesting fecal elimination to be the predominant elimination
pathway.

e Metabolism: Results from in vitro studies suggest that camizestrant undergoes multiple
oxidation reactions in addition to direct N-glucuronidation and is primarily metabolized by
CYP3A and UGT1A4. Human metabolites of camizestrant were detected and quantified in
pharmacokinetic samples from healthy female volunteers receiving a single oral dose of
75 mg [4C]-camizestrant (0.67 MBq). The major drug-related circulating materials were the
N-glucuronide conjugate of camizestrant, the N-glucuronide of the acid metabolite, and
camizestrant which accounted for 20.1%, 11.2%, and 13.6% of the circulating radioactivity
in plasma, respectively. The other eight minor metabolites in plasma, each of which had a
relative abundance lower than 10% of the total drug-related materials in plasma were either
oxidative metabolites or glucuronides of oxidative metabolites. No active metabolites have
been identified.

e Dose proportionality: Camizestrant exposure increases in a greater than dose proportional
manner over the dose range of 25 to 450 mg.

Data from SERENA-1 and SERENA-3 demonstrated that dosing with camizestrant modulated
ER expression and impacted downstream biomarkers.

Paired tumor biopsies from SERENA-1 (D8539C00001) were analyzed using IHC for
expression of ERa, PgR and Ki67. Degradation of ERa, reduced expression of the downstream
transcriptional target PgR, and suppression of the functional proliferation biomarker Ki67 was
observed across the dose cohorts, with no apparent trend as a function of dose.

The data from SERENA-3 demonstrated that the 75 mg dose of camizestrant achieved maximal
levels of ER degradation, reduction of PgR expression (ER antagonism), and downstream
inhibition of Ki67 labeling index (anti-proliferative effect).
