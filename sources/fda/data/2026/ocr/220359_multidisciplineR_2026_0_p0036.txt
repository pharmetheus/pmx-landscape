Furthermore, the review team believes there is uncertainty as to whether the PFS results show clinical benefit. Although FDA has based regular
approvals in HR+HER2- MBC on PFS improvements, this is usually from trials which randomize patients at metastatic diagnosis or after
radiographic progression on prior therapy. In SERENA-6, randomization occurred at detection of ESR/m, which is not generally associated with
signs or symptoms of disease progression. It is not clear that patients need a new treatment at this time, and the clinical meaningfulness of a PFS
improvement measured from ESR/m detection and the magnitude which may translate into improved long-term outcomes are unknown. The
review team also did not view the PFS2 findings as interpretable as evidence of efficacy for the following reasons. The PFS2 endpoint does not
isolate the treatment effect of camizestrant, the PFS2 information is unreliable due to heterogeneity in subsequent treatments and disease
assessments, and PFS2 does not assess whether an early switch in treatment at ESR/m detection is better than a standard switch in treatment at
radiographic progression. OS data are immature.

The safety profile of camizestrant and CDK4/6i in SERENA-6 was generally as expected for an ET and CDK4/6i, with a few notable exceptions.
Important safety signals for camizestrant included bradycardia, QT prolongation, and visual disturbances. The review team was most concerned
about the potential for camizestrant to cause life-threatening arrhythmias, particularly when administered with other drugs that could prolong the
QTc interval or increase camizestrant exposure.

Camizestrant was associated with heart rate lowering effects (median decrease of 14 beats/min [bpm]) which can increase the risk of clinically
significant bradycardia. Camizestrant also was associated with QTc interval prolonging effects, and at the recommended dosage of 75 mg once
daily, a mean increase in the QTcF interval of > 20 msec cannot be ruled out. Bradycardia and QTc interval prolongation are both independent
risk factors for the development of a clinically significant arrhythmia such as ventricular fibrillation or Torsades de Pointes (TdP), and when
