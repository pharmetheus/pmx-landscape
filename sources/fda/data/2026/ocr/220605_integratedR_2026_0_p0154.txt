Distribution

Dedicated distribution studies conducted in male Sprague Dawley and Lister Hooded rats
evaluated the tissue and fluid distribution of rusfertide following a single subcutaneous (SC)
administration of [‘*C]-radiolabeled rusfertide at 10 mg/kg.

Plasma and blood cell radioactive concentrations peaked at 24 hours post-dose (Cmax) and
declined steadily thereafter. Rusfertide distributed broadly across tissues, with the highest early
concentrations observed in the kidney, liver, lung, lymph nodes, and blood, and the highest later
concentrations in fat, lymph nodes, skin, and kidney cortex. Radioactivity persisted in most
tissues through 840 hours post-dose, while blood concentrations declined more rapidly after 48
hours and fell below the limit of quantification by 840 hours.

Notably, rusfertide penetrated the central nervous system (CNS), with radioactivity detected in
the brain and spinal cord as early as 8 hours post-dose and remaining quantifiable at 840 hours
post-dose. Comparison between pigmented and non-pigmented rats revealed similar
radioactivity concentrations in melanin-containing tissues, indicating negligible melanin-binding
affinity.

Blood partitioning behavior was species-dependent: rusfertide partitioned predominantly into
plasma in rats and monkeys, whereas it was sequestered in red blood cells (RBCs) in humans,
a distinction with potential implications for pharmacokinetic interpretation across species.
