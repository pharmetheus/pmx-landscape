1.3. Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA )

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women worldwide. In the U-S., there are 321,910 new cases and 42,140 deaths from breast cancer
estimated for 2026 (American Cancer Society 2026). Although rare, male patients can also develop breast cancer and may present at a higher
stage than female patients. Histopathological subtypes of breast cancer are defined by expression of ER, PR, and/or HER2. Approximately 70%
of patients with breast cancer will have HR+ HER2- disease. Although associated with a better prognosis than the other breast cancer subtypes, if
HR+HER2- breast cancer is advanced (and surgically unresectable) or metastatic, it is incurable and associated with a limited life expectancy.

All treatment for HR+HER2- MBC is palliative and the purpose of therapy is to prolong patients’ survival while reducing disease symptoms and
preserving their ability to function. Nearly all patients will need treatment for the rest of their lifetimes. Typically, a patient receives one
treatment regimen until experiencing disease progression, and then a new treatment regimen is started. The goal is to maximize each line of
treatment to ultimately extend life. For most patients with HR+HER2- MBC in the U.S., preferred first-line (1L) treatment is ET, most typically
an AI, in combination with a CDK4/6i. After experiencing disease progression on 1L treatment, options for next lines of therapies include ET
alone (more below), ET in combination with targeted agents, antibody-drug conjugates, and chemotherapy.

ESR1 is the gene that encodes ERa, and ESR/m is an acquired resistance mutation that a tumor develops after being exposed to AI treatment.
Only approximately 5% of patients with HR+HER2- MBC will have a tumor ZSR/m at metastatic diagnosis. However, after experiencing
disease progression on an AI, approximately 40-50% of patients will have a tumor ZSR/m. Three oral estrogen receptor antagonists, elacestrant,
imlunestrant, and vepdegestrant, are FDA approved as monotherapy for treatment of patients with HR+HER2- MBC and tumor ESR/m after
disease progression on an AI and CDK4/6i (Elacestrant USPI, Imlunestrant USPI, Vepdegestrant USPI). Emerging data also suggest that
combining oral estrogen receptor antagonists with CDK4/6i after disease progression on 1L AI and CDK4/6i may become a treatment approach
in the future (Jhaveri et al 2024). It is unknown if starting new therapy earlier, at detection of ESR/m, offers long-term benefit to patients.
