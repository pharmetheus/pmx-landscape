This nonclinical review supplements the integrated Assessment of NDA 220605 for rusfertide, a
hepcidin mimetic indicated for the treatment of adults with polycythemia vera (PV).

Primary Pharmacology

Polycythemia vera (PV) is a rare disorder where excess red blood cell (RBC) production is a
central feature. Excess RBCs increase the risk of clotting, stroke and heart attack. The standard
of care for PV is phlebotomy, a time-consuming and burdensome procedure. Hepcidin is the
primary endogenous hormone regulator of systemic iron availability, where it acts to limit iron
uptake in the gut and blocks release of stored iron from macrophages (See Figure 1).

Nonclinical pharmacology studies (see Tables 1 and 2, below) were completed with rusfertide, a
new molecular entity hepcidin mimetic peptide, to investigate the mechanism of action of
hepcidin mimicry and to explore the potential clinical benefit associated with limiting iron
availability for erythropoiesis to reduce the need for phlebotomy in patients with PV.
