The safety profiles of the treatment arms in Study 302 were differentiated by their distinct
mechanisms of action. As with other PD-1 CPIs (Kojima et al 2020; Kato et al 2019),
tislelizumab safety was characterized by immune-mediated adverse events, such as
hypothyroidism, pneumonitis, and skin adverse reactions. The events were generally low grade
and responsive to corticosteroids or hormone therapy as needed. Tislelizumab had notably lower
rates of adverse events than ICC (mainly hematologic, gastrointestinal, and neurologic toxicities,
constitutional symptoms, and alopecia). As evidenced by the higher rates of treatment
discontinuation or delay/interruption due to adverse events with ICC compared with
tislelizumab, these toxicities can be dose-limiting for ICC. The overall tislelizumab safety
profile appeared consistent across key ESCC patient demographics. No new signals for
tislelizumab were identified when safety findings were evaluated in the larger pooled All Doses
All Indications population.

The favorable safety results in this submission provide an ample dataset to adequately support
the use of tislelizumab monotherapy in the treatment of patients with unresectable recurrent
locally advanced or metastatic ESCC after prior systemic therapy.

The Applicant’s Position:
The totality of the safety package sufficiently support the use of tislelizumab 200 mg

intravenously once every 3 weeks as a single agent to treat patients with unresectable recurrent
locally advanced or metastatic ESCC after prior systemic therapy.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees that Study 302 adequately characterized the safety of tislelizumab for the second
line treatment of patients with ESCC. In general, the adverse reactions observed with
tislelizumab were consistent with the mechanism of action and toxicity observed in prior clinical
studies with tislelizumab and the pharmacological class. The primary risks related to tislelizumab
are immune related reactions. These serious risks are adequately addressed in the Wammings and
Precautions and Dosage Modifications sections of tislelizumab product labeling. The major
safety risks of tislelizumab are toxicities that oncologists frequently manage and the toxicity
profile of tislelizumab is considered acceptable when considering the improved survival
observed in a patient population with a serious and life-threatening condition.
