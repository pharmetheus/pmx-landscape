FDA agrees with the frequency of IRRs in Study 302 noted by the Applicant. Eight patients
(3.1%) in the tislelizumab arm and 11 (4.6%) patients in the ICC arm experienced an IRR. In the

of these were Grade 1 events except for one Grade 2 event of pyrexia.

Treatment Emergent Adverse Events and Adverse Reactions

Data:

In Study 302, the overall safety profile of tislelizumab compared favorably with that of
chemotherapy (Table 34). Almost all patients in both arms experienced an adverse event: 95.7%
for the Tislelizumab Arm and 98.3% for the ICC Arm. The most patients in the Tislelizumab
Arm had only low grade (Grades 1 or 2) adverse events; > Grade 3 adverse events occurred in
46.3% of patients in that arm. In contrast, the most patients in the ICC Arm had adverse events
> Grade 3 (67.9%). Treatment-related adverse events across categories were consistently lower
in the Tislelizumab Arm compared with the ICC Arm.

Fewer patients in the Tislelizumab Arm experienced adverse events leading to treatment
discontinuation (19.2%) or adverse events leading to dose modification (22.7%) compared with
patients in the ICC Arm (26.7% and 47.9%, respectively). Serious adverse events occurred at a
similar incidence in both arms (41.2% versus 43.8%), although the exposure-adjusted serious
adverse events were lower in the Tislelizumab Amn (12.5 per 100 person-months) than in the
ICC Arm (28.9 per 100 person-months). Deaths due to any cause in Study 302 occurred in fewer
patients who received tislelizumab than ICC (77.0% versus 83.2%, respectively) (Table 27).
Deaths due to adverse events occurred in 14 patients in each arm (5.5% tislelizumab; 5.8% ICC).

In the All Doses All Indications population, a similar safety profile was observed compared with
the Study 302 population suggesting consistency of the tislelizumab safety profile across
multiple tumor types (Table 35)
