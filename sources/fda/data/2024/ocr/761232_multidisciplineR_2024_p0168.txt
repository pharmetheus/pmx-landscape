BLA 761232 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
TEVIMBRA - tislelizumab-jsgr

Tislelizumab Arm experienced lower risk of clinically meaningful worsening compared to the
ICC Arm in physical functioning (stratified HR: 0.67 [95% CI: 0.45 to 1.00]), measured via
EORTC QLQ-C30, and symptoms of reflux (stratified HR: 0.50 [95% CI: 0.32 to 0.80]),
dysphagia (stratified HR: 0.76 [95% CI: 0.53 to 1.07]) and pain (stratified HR: 0.89 [95% CI:
0.59 to 1.35]), pre-measured via EORTC QLQOES18. No clinically meaningful differences in
GHS between the Tislelizumab Arm compared to the ICC Arm were observed (stratified HR:
0.96 [95% CI: 0.65 to 1.41]).

The Applicant’s Position:

These findings show that tislelizumab treatment improved symptoms of pain and eating,
decreased the risk of worsening of EC symptoms, particularly reflux, dysphagia, physical
functioning, and pain in PDL1 sub population, compared with ICC.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA does not agree with the applicant’s position that tislelizumab treatment improved symptoms
of pain and eating or decreased the risk of worsening ESCC symptoms. No conclusions can or
should be drawn regarding HRQoL given that no alpha was allocated to these endpoints and
results are to be considered exploratory only. All p-values presented by the applicant with
respect to HRQoL analyses are considered nominal only. Furthermore, FDA does not agree with
the proposed 10-point threshold for clinically meaningful worsening. See FDA’s comments in
Section 8.1.2 regarding the HRQoL secondary endpoints for more details.

8.2.9. Safety Analyses by Demographic Subgroups

Data:
Age
The overall incidence of adverse events was similar among the 3 age subgroups (< 65 years, > 65

to < 75 years, and > 75 years). No clear pattern of higher incidence of adverse events with
increasing age was observed.

Numerically higher incidence of constipation, nausea, diarrhea, pyrexia, fatigue, and pruritus was
observed for the > 75 years of age subgroup compared with the other two subgroups; these
results should be interpreted with caution due to the small sample size of the > 75 years of age
subgroup.

Numerically higher incidence of diarrhea, dysphagia, and pyrexia was observed in the > 65 to
<75 years of age subgroup compared with the < 65 years of age subgroup.

Numerically higher incidence of aspartate aminotransferase increased, alanine aminotransferase
increased, decreased appetite was observed in the < 65 years of age subgroup compared with the
other two subgroups.
