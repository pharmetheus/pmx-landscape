One subject in the sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid arm of Trial 301 (subject ow) a 28-
year-old woman with a medical history significant for obesity and use of an oral contraceptive
pill, had postbaseline ALT elevation >5= ULN. Liver function tests were normal at baseline
except for a mild elevation of AP at 125 U/L. At the TOC visit, the AP had increased further
(223 U/L), and ALT was elevated to 208 U/L (normal range 6 to 41 U/L). The subject was
asymptomatic. Laboratory testing was repeated 5 days later with a decrease in ALT to 46 U/L
and a downward trend in the AP to 169 U/L. The cause of the ALT elevation was not known, but
potentially could have been related to sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid.

In the sulopenem arm of the four phase 3 trials, 9 (0.3%) subjects and 1 (<0.1) subject had ALT
elevations of >5x ULN and >10x ULN, respectively. Of these subjects, one was in Trial 301 as
described above and eight subjects were in Trials 302 and 303. On review of the narratives of
these eight subjects, attribution of causality to sulopenem appeared confounded by the subjects’
underlying medical conditions and/or concomitant medications.

One subject from Trial 302 (Subject oo, discontinued IV sulopenem due to the AE of
toxic hepatitis. The subject was a 47-year-old woman with pyelonephritis who had normal ALT,
AP and bilirubin at baseline and mild elevation of AST. On Study Day 5, while on IV
sulopenem, ALT and AST were >3X ULN. On Day 7, the subject was diagnosed with probable
toxic hepatitis, and sulopenem was discontinued. AST and ALT trended down at Day 10 with
normnalization of values at TOC. Sulopenem may have contributed to the elevations in liver
enzymes, but attribution of causality is confounded by the concomitant medications of
paracetamol and methimazole.
