NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {NDA 219249}
{Inavolisib}

4. Lack of E-R relationship for efficacy
Given the lack of dosage optimization and potential for improved benefit-risk profile with a
lower dose of inavolisib, a PMR will be issued to study a lower starting dosage. See Section
6.1.2.1 for details.

6.3.2.3. Is an alternative dosing regimen or management strategy
required for subpopulations based on intrinsic patient factors (e.g., race,
ethnicity, age, performance status, genetic subpopulations, etc.)?

Data and the Applicant’s Position: No, please refer to Section 6.2.2.2.
The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s position.

e Renal impairment: As detailed in Section 6.1.2.2, dose reduction (to 6 mg PO QD) is
recommended in patients with moderate renal impairment. Dosing recommendations
for patients with severe renal impairment is not currently available and will be
dependent on results of the ongoing renal impairment study.

e Hepatic impairment: A >20% contribution of hepatic elimination cannot be ruled out for
inavolisib. Therefore, it is not known at this time whether an alternative dosing regimen
or management strategy is required in patients with moderate or severe hepatic
impairment. A PMR will be issued to evaluate the PK and safety of inavolisib in patients
with moderate and severe hepatic impairment. Refer to Section 6.2.2.2 for details.

e Race and ethnicity: The available data were insufficient to determine any potential
differences in PK or PD between patients of different races (other than White or Asian)
and ethnicities. Clinical pharmacology data will be collected in a diversity PMC. Refer to
Section 8.1.1 for details.
