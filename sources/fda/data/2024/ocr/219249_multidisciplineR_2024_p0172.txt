NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {NDA 219249}
{Inavolisib}

13 Postmarketing Requirements and Commitment

The FDA’s Assessment:

The following postmarketing requirements (PMR) and postmarketing commitments (PMC) were issued
by FDA and agreed to by the Applicant:

PMRs:

1. PMR 4718-1: Conduct a multicenter, randomized clinical trial to further characterize known
serious risks with inavolisib including severe hyperglycemia, stomatitis/mucosal inflammation,
and diarrhea, and compare the safety and activity of inavolisib 9 mg daily versus a lower daily
dose in patients with P/K3CA-mutant, hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-negative locally
advanced or metastatic breast cancer.

Draft Protocol Submission: 04/2025
Final Protocol Submission: 10/2025
Trial Completion: 10/2028
Final Report Submission: 04/2029

FDA Rationale: Conducting additional inavolisib dosage optimization is needed to identify whether
alternative dosing would reduce the rate of the known serious risk of increased severe adverse
events including increased blood glucose, stomatitis/mucosal inflammation, and diarrhea in the
target patient population. The Applicant has not provided sufficient justification and supportive data
that inavolisib 9 mg PO QD is the optimized dosage for the target patient population in combination
with palbociclib and fulvestrant. There are extremely limited clinical data at lower inavolisib dosages
with only 20 patients treated at dosages lower than the proposed 9 mg QD dosage across the entire
development program of inavolisib. For the proposed inavolisib combination therapy with palbociclib
and fulvestrant, all patients were treated at the proposed 9 mg QD inavolisib dosage. At the
proposed 9 mg QD inavolisib dosage in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant, multiple serious
ARs in the pivotal study (i.e., severe cases of increased blood glucose, stomatitis/mucosal
inflammation, and diarrhea) occurred at high rates. Toxicities led to dosage modification in 69% of
patients treated with the proposed recommended dose regimen. Finally, while there were no
apparent exposure-response (E-R) relationships for efficacy endpoints, there were multiple positive E-
R relationships for safety endpoints including Grade 22 anemia, Grade 22 hyperglycemia, Grade 23
adverse reactions, and inavolisib dosage modifications due to adverse reactions. Given the limited
dose finding, high rates of serious ARs, poor tolerability, and positive E-R relationships for multiple
safety endpoints, the proposed inavolisib dosage of 9 mg PO QD is not adequately supported as the
optimized dosage. Therefore, the Applicant should conduct additional dosage optimization to
identify whether an alternative inavolisib dosage would reduce the risk of serious toxicities.

2. PMR 4718-2: Complete the ongoing renal impairment clinical trial and evaluate the
pharmacokinetics and safety of inavolisib in participants with normal renal function and patients
with severe renal impairment, to evaluate the serious potential risk of increased drug exposure.
