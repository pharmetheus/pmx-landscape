Aqneursa (levacetylleucine)

Thrombocytopenia

There were no AEs of thrombocytopenia reported; however, there were subjects in both
treatment groups with low platelets, called thrombocytopenia (typically defined as

<150 10%3 cells/uL), identified via routine laboratory analysis during the trial. Patients with NPC
can have thrombocytopenia related to their underlying diagnosis. Baseline values in

Trial IB1001-301 ranged from 65 to 345 10%3 cells/uL (mean 170.45 10%3 cells/uL).
Additionally, labelling for miglustat (Zavesca) lists thrombocytopenia as a known risk with its
use. All subjects in Trial IB1001-301 with laboratory values consistent with thrombocytopenia
received concomitant miglustat therapy.

During Treatment Period I, there was an equal number of subjects with platelets

<140 10°3 cells/uL in those randomized to levacetylleucine (13/30, 43.3%) as those randomized
to placebo (13/30, 43.3%). A similar proportion of subjects also had platelets <125 10%3 cells/uL
(7/30 subjects randomized to levacetylleucine and 6/30 subjects randomized to placebo). There
were four subjects randomized to levacetylleucine who had platelets <100 10%3 cells/uL,
compared to two subjects randomized to placebo.

For these four subjects with platelets <100%3 cells/ uL, two had baseline values of

<100 10%3 cells/uL that then fluctuated throughout the course of Treatment Period I (on
levacetylleucine) and Treatment Period II (on placebo), ranging from 53 to 112 103 cells/uL),
as detailed in Table 38. There were no values <50 103 cells/uL. Two subjects had baseline
values >100 1043 cells/uL that decreased during the treatment period to 81 and 60 103 cells/uL,
respectively. However, in both instances, their platelet levels increased again to

>100 10%3 cells/uL during Treatment Period II.

While there are no symptoms typically associated with thrombocytopenia with platelets

>50 10%3 cells/uL because patients with NPC are at risk for thrombocytopenia as part of their
underlying disease and miglustat use is common, the potential for any additional risk associated
with levacetylleucine may be clinically important. These laboratory findings will be included in
labeling.
