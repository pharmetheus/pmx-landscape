Nonclinical in vitro data, clinical endogenous biomarker data, and physiologically-based
pharmacokinetic (PBPK) modeling suggest that clinically relevant DDI between inavolisib and
CYP3A4 substrates is unlikely. Therefore, inavolisib can be co-administered with CYP3A4
substrates at their respective indicated dose levels.

Based on the ADME properties of inavolisib, the role of P-gp and breast cancer resistance
protein (BCRP) transporters in in vivo disposition of inavolisib is not expected to be clinically
significant. Therefore, inavolisib can be co-administered with inhibitors/inducers of P-gp and/or
BCRP at their respective indicated dose levels.

Similarly, based on the ADME properties of inavolisib, hepatic impairment is considered not to
have a clinically significant impact on the PK of inavolisib. Hence, dose adjustment of inavolisib
in patients with hepatic impairment (including mild, moderate, and severe) is not warranted.

Population PK modeling suggested that mild renal impairment does not have a clinically
significant impact on inavolisib exposure. Therefore, no dose adjustment is needed in patients
with mild renal impairment. To assess if the dose of inavolisib needs to be adjusted in patients
with moderate or severe renal impairment, a dedicated clinical renal impairment study is being
conducted in these patients.

The Applicant's Position: No therapeutic individualization is needed in the proposed indication
based on demographic factors (body weight, age, sex), lack of food effect, lack of potential
DDIs, and lack of clinically relevant impact of hepatic impairment (mild, moderate, severe) or
renal impairment (mild) on inavolisib PK. Moderate or severe renal impairment may affect
inavolisib PK, hence a dedicated clinical renal impairment study is being conducted in these
patients.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant that no dose adjustment is necessary in the proposed indication
based on body weight (39 to 159kg), age (27 to 85 years), sex, mild renal impairment (eGFR 60
to <90 mL/min), and mild hepatic impairment. In addition, FDA agrees that neither a high-fat
meal (approximately 1,000 calories with 50% from fat) nor concomitant use of a proton pump.
inhibitor have clinically significant effects on the PK of inavolisib (see Section 6.3.2.4 for
details). FDA also assessed therapeutic individualization in the following clinical pharmacology
