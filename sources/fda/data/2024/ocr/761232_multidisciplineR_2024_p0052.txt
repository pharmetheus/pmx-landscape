BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — BLA 761232
TEVIMBRA (tislelizumab)

The Applicant’s Position:
The clinical pharmacology of tislelizumab has been well characterized, and the outcomes of the

exposure — efficacy and exposure — safety analyses for tislelizumab support the use of the
recommended dose in the target indication.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position. Clinical data in Study 302 were limited with
tespect to the conduct of analyses on efficacy and safety based on race/ethnicity as the trial
enrolled only Asian and White patients. Limited data were available in other racial or ethnic
groups to assess the potential for differences in PK or PD.

6.2.2. General Dosing and Therapeutic Individualization
6.2.2.1. General Dosing

Data:

The recommended dose of 200 mg administered intravenously once every 3 weeks was selected
based on safety, efficacy, and PK assessments in the first-in-human (FIH) dose escalation Study
001. In this study, dose ranges of 0.5 mg/kg through 10 mg/kg (once every 2 or 3 weeks) were
studied in patients with advanced solid tumors and comparable safety and efficacy profiles were
observed between the 2 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg levels. Also, no significant E-R relationships were
observed between exposure and efficacy (ie, ORR), or safety endpoints across the dose range
tested in this study. Based on this observation, a flat dose of 200 mg (corresponding to a dose
between 2 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg) once every 3 weeks was chosen as the clinical dose because
tislelizumab concentrations with this dose largely overlapped with those observed with the 2
mng/kg and 5 mg/kg once every 3 weeks. The less frequent regimen of once every 3 weeks
regimen had similar efficacy and safety events compared with once every 2 weeks. The efficacy
and safety with the 200 mg once every 3 weeks dose regimen has been subsequently studied in
clinical studies including the pivotal study 302 that forms the basis of this Biologics License
Application (BLA) submission.

The E-R analyses using data from ESCC patients from the tislelizumab cohort in Study 302
demonstrated that there were no clinically meaningful relationships between exposure and
efficacy endpoints (ORR and OS), and exposure and safety endpoints (imAE, IRR, AE with
Grade >3, AE leading to dose modification, and AE leading to drug discontinuation).
Comparable efficacy and safety response were observed across White and Asian ESCC patients.
Similarly, no clinically relevant E-R relationships were observed for efficacy endpoint of ORR
and safety endpoints, which include the same endpoints as above, based on tislelizumab-treated
patients in Supportive studies (001, 102, 203 and 204). Taken together, the recommended dose
of 200 mg Q3W demonstrated a favorable risk-benefit profile in 2L ESCC patients.
