BLA 761232 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
TEVIMBRA - tislelizumab-jsgr

The Applicant’s Position:

The size of the safety database in this submission is adequate to support the use of tislelizumab
monotherapy in the treatment of patients with unresectable recurrent locally advanced or
metastatic ESCC after prior systemic therapy.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the summary description of the studies used to assess the safety of tislelizumab
and agrees that the size of the safety database in this submission is adequate to characterize the
safety profile of tislelizumab in this setting.

8.2.2. Safety Review Approach

The safety data package in support of this submission included the following 3 patient
populations:

Study 302 population (N = 495) included patients from the global Phase 3 study evaluating
tislelizumab monotherapy (Tislelizuamab Arm; n = 255) versus ICC of paclitaxel, docetaxel, or
irinotecan (ICC Arm; n = 240) as the treatment for patients with ESCC after prior systemic
therapy.

The ESCC population (N = 307) included patients with ESCC who received tislelizumab from
Study 302 (N = 255), Study 102 (N = 26), and Study 001 (N = 26). The safety results from the
ESCC population were similar to that of the Study 302 population.

The All Doses All Indications population (N = 1972) included patients treated with tislelizumab
monotherapy from studies evaluating tislelizumab in multiple tumor types with available CSRs.
This included the above 3 studies plus Studies 303, 208, 204, and 203. Patients who received
comparator agents in these studies are not included in this population.

The goal of this analysis of an integrated safety population is to provide a comprehensive safety
profile for tislelizumab and to provide supportive data for the safety profile of tislelizumab in a
larger population, as well as to identify possible rare safety signals. The analysis of this
population reflects the full experience with tislelizumab in advanced cancers, including ESCC.

This integrated safety population includes patients with different tumor types who received > 1
dose of tislelizumab monotherapy. Of these, 77.8% of patients received tislelizumab 200 mg
once every 3 weeks. Patients in the supportive Studies 102, 303, 208, 204, and 203 received
200 mg once every 3 weeks, while patients in Study 001 received a variety of dose regimens,
including 200 mg once every 3 weeks.

All patients in Study 102 received 200 mg once every 3 weeks; 57 of the 300 patients (PK
substudy) had a first-cycle duration of 28-days instead of 21 days, which was not considered to
be a different dose regimen.
