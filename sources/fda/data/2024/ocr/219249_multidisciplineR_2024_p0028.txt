NDA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {NDA 219249}

ITOVEBI {inavolisib}

Dimension

Evidence and Uncertainties

Conclusions and Reasons

¢ About 70% of breast cancers are hormone receptor (HR) positive.

e Approximately 35-40% of patients with HR-positive breast cancer
have tumors that harbor mutations in PIK3CA.

e Endocrine resistant breast cancer is considered to be a negative
prognostic factor as these patients tend to have worse survival
outcomes compared to patients with endocrine sensitive breast
cancer.

e Advanced or metastatic HR-positive, HER-negative breast cancer
remains incurable.

e Treatment for advanced or metastatic breast cancer is palliative in
intent with the goals to prolong survival, delay progression, and
reduce symptoms from cancer.

¢ Treatment with a CDK 4/6 inhibitor plus endocrine therapy
(fulvestrant or aromatase inhibitor) is considered standard of care
first line treatment for patients with advanced or metastatic HR-
positive, HER-negative breast cancer.

For patients that have progression of disease with/after CDK4/6
inhibitor + endocrine therapy, treatment options include endocrine
monotherapy or combination endocrine therapy + targeted agents
(alpelisib, capivasertib, elacestrant, everolimus) depending on the
presence or absence of specific genetic alterations. Second line
treatment with CDK 4/6 inhibitor + endocrine therapy may be
considered for patients that did not receive it in the first line. Other
options include chemotherapy (capecitabine, eribulin, ixabepilone,
paclitaxel protein-bound, gemcitabine), and/or an antibody-drug
conjugate fam-trastuzumab-deruxtecan, for patients with HER2 low
(IHC1+ or IHC2+/ISH-) advanced or metastatic breast cancer

There are no FDA approvals specifically for
the first line treatment of patients with
endocrine resistant, PIK3CA mutated, HR-
positive, HER-negative advanced or
metastatic breast cancer. These patients are
treated with currently available options for
HR-positive, HER2 negative breast cancer. All
treatment options are palliative and there is
an unmet medical need to improve outcomes
in these patients.
