Treatment with tislelizumab was associated with a better overall HRQoL in Study 302. The
better outcomes in HRQoL in the Tislelizumab Arm compared with those in the ICC Arm were
related to either a reduction in symptoms (e.g., eating and pain) or slower and milder worsening
(e.g., dysphagia and reflux). Improvement in physical functioning was observed in the
Tislelizamab Arm compared with the ICC Ann.

Furthermore, in the ITT population, analysis of the time to deterioration showed, patients treated
with tislelizumab were at a lower risk for clinically meaningful worsening symptoms of
dysphagia (stratified HR of 0.76 [95% CI: 0.53, 1.07]) and reflux (0.50 [95% CI: 0.32, 0.80] ) as
well as physical function (0.67 [95% CI: 0.45, 1.00]) through the course of treatment. The PD-L1
vCPS > 10% patients treated with tislelizumab were at lower risk for pain (0.52 [95% CI: 0.24,
1.13).

The Applicant’s Position:
In addition to the statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in the primary

endpoint of OS in the ITT population, tislelizumab also demonstrated statistically significant
improvement in the key secondary endpoint of OS in the PD-L1 vCPS > 10% population. The
survival benefit of tislelizumab over ICC was accompanied by favorable landmark PFS rates at
6,9 and 12 months, higher ORR, longer DOR and improvements in HRQoL assessment in the
key PRO endpoints of eating, pain, and physical functioning and slower and less notable
deterioration in dysphagia and reflux.

The FDA’s Assessment:

Although FDA confirmed the data as presented above, FDA’s interpretation of these results is
different. As stated above, given the missing PD-L1 expression data on 19% of patients in
RATIONALE-302 and that PD-L1 status was not a stratification factor, FDA notes that the
analysis of survival in patients based on PD-L1 expression should be interpreted with caution.

FDA also acknowledges the results of the secondary endpoint of PFS, which are considered
exploratory. The p-value for PFS is considered nominal. As noted by the Applicant, median PFS
was slightly shorter on the tislelizumab arm compared to the ICC arm but the Kaplan-Meier
curves for PFS start to separate in favor of the tislelizumab arm approximately 3 months after
randomization. FDA notes that the delayed separation of the PFS curves suggests that the
proportional hazards assumption may have been violated.
