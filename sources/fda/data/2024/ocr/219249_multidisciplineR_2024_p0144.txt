The Applicant's Po:
Pivotal Study WO41554: Overall, clinically relevant shifts (defined as shifts from Grade 0, 1, or 2

at baseline to Grade 3 or 4 post-baseline) in blood chemistry, coagulation, and hematology
during study treatment were infrequent and comparable between the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv and
Pbo+Palbo+Fulv arms. The most commonly occurring clinically relevant hematology shift from
baseline was low total absolute neutrophils (81.7% Inavo+Palbo+Fulv vs. 78.5%
Pbo+Palbo+Fulv). Regardless of baseline fasting blood glucose, the majority of patients (64.8%)
in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm maintained fasting blood glucose <160 mg/dL. There was 1 patient
in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm and 3 patients in the Pbo+Palbo+Fulv arm who fulfilled the
laboratory criteria for potential Hy’s law. However, all of these cases were confounded by
concurrent worsening of liver metastasis and did not meet the criteria for drug-induced liver
injury.

Supportive Study GO39374: For the overall safety population, the most commonly occurring
clinically relevant hematology shift from baseline was low total absolute neutrophils (48
patients [25.5%]), while the most commonly occurring clinically relevant biochemistry shifts
from baseline were high fasting glucose (34 patients [18.3%]) and high glucose (15 patients
[12.8%]).

The FDA’s Assessment:

The most common laboratory abnormalities in the INAVO120 trial were observed in the
hematologic parameters. The hematologic abnormalities were noted in both treatment arms
and are known to occur with palbociclib treatment. Platelets decreased (all grade and grade 3-
4) were higher in the inavolisib treatment arm compared to the placebo arm.

Increased fasting glucose occurred in a larger proportion of patients on the inavolisib treatment
arm compared to placebo. Increased fasting glucose corresponds with a known toxicity of
hyperglycemia for PI3K inhibitors, including inavolisib. Patients were required to have a HbA1c
<6% and fasting blood glucose <126 to be eligible for the INAVO 120 trial.
