The Applicant’s Position:
The OS benefit of Tislelizumab use (ie, HR < 1) was consistent across all prespecified

subgroups, including age, gender, smoking status, ICC option, ECOG PS score, region, race, and
baseline PD-L1 expression. Imbalances in PD-L] at baseline and metastatic status at diagnosis or
of missing PD-L1 results were demonstrated to have had no impact on the primary endpoint.
Thus, the improvement in overall survival with Tislelizumab was observed across all pre-
specified and post hoc analyses.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA notes that results of the post-hoc analysis to assess potential violation of proportional
hazards and sensitivity analysis to address imbalances in metastatic status at diagnosis have not
been presented by the Applicant in this Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation. In general,
FDA acknowledges that the OS benefit of tislelizumab appeared consistent across various
subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses (both pre-specified and post-hoc) conducted by the
Applicant and by FDA but emphasizes that these analyses are all considered exploratory (except
for the key secondary endpoint of OS in PD-L1 vCPS>10%, which should also be interpreted
with caution for reasons discussed in the Efficacy Results sections).

8.1.3. Integrated Review of Effectiveness

The FDA’s Assessment:

Study 302 was designed to compare OS in patients with unresectable/metastatic ESCC that has
been previously treated with one prior line of systemic therapy by comparing patients who
receive tislelizumab to those who receive ICC of either paclitaxel, docetaxel, or irinotecan.
Study 302 demonstrated a statistically significant, clinically meaningful improvement in OS for
patients randomized to tislelizumab (n = 256) compared to ICC (n = 256). The median OS of
patients in the tislelizumab arm was 8.6 months [95% CI: 7.5, 10.4] and the median OS of
