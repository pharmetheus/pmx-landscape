Based on data from the literature and its mechanism of action, treatment with tislelizumab has
the potential for enhanced immune responses to infection or impaired microbial immune
responses. The absence of PD-1 pathway signaling in mouse models of lymphocytic
choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) infection led to fatal CD8+ T cell-mediated killing of virally
infected vascular endothelial cells, systemic vascular leakage, and cardiovascular collapse
(Frebel et al., 2012; Mueller et al., 2010). In PD-1 deficient mice (C57BL/6), infection with MZ.
tuberculosis resulted in decreased survival that correlated with uncontrolled bacterial
proliferation in the lungs. It is unclear whether decreased in survival in this model resulted from
an inability to mount an appropriate antibacterial response or a failure to control the immune
response leading to normal tissue destruction and organ failure (Lazar-Moluar, et al., 2010). As
this data represents a potential mechanistic risk associated with the use of a PD-1 signaling
inhibitor, a brief description of this data is included in the proposed label under Section 13.2.

5.3. Pharmacology

Primary pharmacology

The Applicant determined the binding affinity of tislelizumab for human PD-1 using surface
plasmon resonance (Study BLGC-004). The binding affinity (Kd) of tislelizamab for human PD-
1 was 0.15 nM.

Cynomolgus monkeys were a pharmacologically relevant species for toxicity assessment based
on sequence homology and binding. As assessed by ELISA, tislelizumab bound to human and
monkey PD-1 in a dose-dependent manner (EC50 of 17.3 ng/mL and ECS50 of 27.4 ng/mL,
respectively). Tislelizumab did not bind to mouse PD-1 (see Figure 1; Study BLGC-003).
