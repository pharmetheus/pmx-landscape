The Applicant's Position:

Pivotal Study WO41554: A numerically higher proportion of patients in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv
arm (69.1%) compared with the Pbo+Palbo+Fulv arm (34.6%) experienced AEs leading to the
dose interruption of inavolisib/placebo. A numerically higher proportion of patients in the
Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm (23 patients [14.2%]) compared with the Pbo+Palbo+Fulv arm (5 patients
[3.1%]) experienced AEs leading to the dose reduction of inavolisib/placebo (Table 34). The
most common PT leading to dose reductions (2.5%) or interruptions (24.7%) in the
Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm was hyperglycemia.

Supportive Study GO39374: In the overall safety population, AEs leading to dose modification or
interruption of inavolisib were reported in 64.7% of patients, with hyperglycemia (36.8%) being
the most frequently reported AE (Table 35). Hyperglycemia was the most commonly reported
AE leading to dose modification or interruption of inavolisib in Arm B (27.3%), Arm E (40.0%)
and Arm F (55.0%).

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees that a higher proportion of patients on the inavolisib treatment arm in the
INAVO120 Trial (WO41554) required a dose interruption or dose reduction compared to the
placebo arm. The most common AE leading to an interruption of inavolisib treatment was
hyperglycemia, followed by COVID-19, neutropenia, diarrhea, and mucosal inflammation. The
most common cause leading to dose reduction in the inavolisib treatment arm was
hyperglycemia (in 2.5% of patients). Hyperglycemia, diarrhea, and stomatitis/mucosal
inflammation are known side effects of PI3K inhibitors, and these adverse events occurred at a
higher incidence in the inavolisib treatment arm compared to the placebo arm. These toxicities
could be managed with dose interruption or dose reduction of inavolisib without permanent
discontinuation of treatment.
