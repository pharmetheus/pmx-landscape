Aqneursa (levacetylleucine)

Trial IB1001-202

Trial IB1001-202 was a phase 2 study of levacetylleucine in 30 subjects with GM2
gangliosidosis, a lysosomal storage disorder with some shared pathophysiological mechanisms
that could potentially benefit from treatment with levacetylleucine. The trial design was identical
to that of Trial IB1001-201 in subjects with NPC, and the results from Trial IB1001-202 are
based on the rated-blinded CI-CS in the parent study. Similar to Trial IB1001-201, the Tnal
IB1001-202 parent study also met its primary endpoint CI-CS. The mean CI-CS in the treatment
period was 0.34 (SD=1.59, median=0.50), while the mean CI-CS in the washout period was
-0.36 (SD=1.33, median=-0.50). The Hodges-Lehmann estimate was 0.75 (90% CI: 0.00, 1.50),
indicating a nominally significant effect for levacetylleucine in terms of improvement during the
treatment period followed by deterioration during washout.

Given the similar pathophysiological mechanisms of NPC and GM2 gangliosidosis and the same
expected MOA of levacetylleucine in the two diseases, the results from Trial IB1001-202 may
provide some support for the effectiveness of levacetylleucine in lysosomal storage disorders
such as NPC. However, since levacetylleucine has not been approved in the United States for the
treatment of GM2 gangliosidosis (and thus, the efficacy has not been established), the review
team determined that the results of IB1001-202 are not adequate to be used as CE of
effectiveness of levacetylleucine for NPC.

Conclusion

The several different sources of data described above were submitted to serve as CE of the
treatment benefit of levacetylleucine observed in Trial IB1001-301. Each of these sources of data
has considerable limitations, as detailed above, that preclude their acceptability to serve as CE on
their own. However, the review team determined that the integration of the clinical data from
Trial IB1001-201 along with the nonclinical data together constitute acceptable CE for this
application, which included a highly persuasive clinical trial.
