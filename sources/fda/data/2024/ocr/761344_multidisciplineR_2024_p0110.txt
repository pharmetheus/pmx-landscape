NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

Due to limited clinical data on dose delay, the dosage recommendation for restarting
doses or repeating doses were assessed based on a PK simulation. Tarlatamab
exposure after each dose delay scenario was simulated with the popPK model and
compared to the reference, tarlatamab exposure after the recommended schedules
without delays. From a PK perspective, the Ctrough at the maximum allowed dose delay
interval should be similar (20%) or higher than the Ctrough for next lower dose level (e.g.,
the step-up doses) to avoid risk of rebound CRS.

The simulated Ctrough was higher than reference except in the first dose delay scenario
(Table 24). The predicted Crrough following the first step-up dose C1D1 dose delay for 14
days was approximately 59% of the reference value. Figure 11 shows when patients
received 1 mg on Day 1 and followed by dose interruption, under with and without
repriming scenario. Although the initial Ctrough followed by the dose interruption is lower
than the reference, the concentration reaches to that of reference once next target
dose is administered. The initial drop in the concentration is not likely to be clinically
relevant. The proposed 14 days dose delay cutoff was further supported by safety data
observed in study 20160323. During the dose escalation phase, cohort 6 in the dose
escalation evaluated the dosing regimen of 1 mg Q2W in 8 patients without a step dose.
Overall, CRS was lower than 1/10 mg cohorts, supporting the 14-day cutoff before re-
priming in scenario 1 (Table 14).

Based on the simulated PK profile and available clinical data, FDA agrees proceeding
with 10 mg doses when the dose interruption < 14 days following the 1 mg step-up
dose.
