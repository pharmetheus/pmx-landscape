Serious Adverse Events

Data:

In Study 302, serious adverse events (SAEs) were reported with similar overall incidence in the
two treatment arms (41.2% Tislelizumab Arm versus 43.8% ICC Arm). The exposure adjusted
event rates for SAEs were lower in the Tislelizumab Arm compared with the ICC Arm at 12.5
per 100 person-months versus 28.9 per 100 person-months, respectively.

By preferred term, pneumonia was the most common SAE reported for both treatment arms
(7.1% in both arms) (Table 30). Dysphagia was the only SAE that occurred with > 2% higher
incidence in the Tislelizumab Arm compared with the ICC Arm (4.7% versus 1.7%,
respectively); the events were generally associated with the underlying ESCC and were not
considered treatment related.

SAEs reported with > 2% lower incidence in the Tislelizamab Arm compared with the ICC Arm
included diarrhea (0% versus 3.3%), decreased appetite (0.4% versus 2.5%), febrile neutropenia
(0% versus 3.3%), neutrophil count decreased (0% versus 4.2%), and white blood cell decreased
(0% versus 3.3%).

In both arms, most SAEs were considered unrelated to study drug. The incidence of treatment-
telated SAEs was lower in the Tislelizumab Arm than in the ICC Arm (14.1% versus 19.6%,
respectively) even without taking exposure-adjusted event rates into consideration.
