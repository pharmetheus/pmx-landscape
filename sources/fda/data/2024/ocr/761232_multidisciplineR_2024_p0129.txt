Given the differences in exposure between the two arms described above, exposure-adjusted

events) of the Study 302 population in addition to overall event incidence.

The exposure for patients in Study 302 was lower than the exposure for patients in the All Doses
All Indications population for median duration (2.76 months versus 4.11 months, respectively),
and fewer patients in Study 302 received at least 6 months of treatment (25.5% versus 37.3%).
This may reflect differences in study population, line of therapy, study timing, and tumor type.
For example, Study 302 enrolled more patients with ECOG PS score of 1 (74.5%) and had a
population with more comorbidities associated with tobacco and alcohol use (73.3% current or
former smoking status, 70.6% current or former alcohol use) than All Doses All Indications
population (66.6% ECOG PS score of 1, 56.8% current or former smoking status, 30.8% current
or former alcohol use); these are recognized differences between advanced ESCC and other
tumor types.

A difference in tislelizumab regimen existed between the Study 302 population and the All
Doses All Indications population. The Study 302 population was treated with 200 mg once
every 3 weeks regimen; the All Doses All Indications population was treated with 200 mg once
every 3 weeks regimen (Studies 302, 102, 303, 208, 204, 203, and 001 [Part 3]) or weight-based
regimens including 0.5, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0 mg/kg once every 2 weeks, 2.0 and 5.0 mg/kg once every 3
weeks (Study 001 Parts 1 and 2). Only a small proportion of patients (3.2%) in the All Doses All
Indications population were treated with a regimen outside of these 3 dose regimens; hence, the
difference in tislelizumab regimen is unlikely to impact the assessment of tislelizumab safety.

The Applicant’s Position:

The Tislelizumab Arm had greater treatment exposure than the ICC Arm with longer median
duration of exposure, a higher proportion of patients receiving > 6 months of study treatment,
and a higher median relative dose intensity. These differences are likely the result of an
improvement in efficacy and a better tolerability profile for tislelizumab compared with
chemotherapy.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA verified the results summarized above. Median treatment duration in the tislelizumab arm
was 2.8 months compared to the ICC arm (1.5 months) and with the approximately 10% higher
median relative dose intensity in the tislelizumab arm compared to the ICC arm in Study 302.
