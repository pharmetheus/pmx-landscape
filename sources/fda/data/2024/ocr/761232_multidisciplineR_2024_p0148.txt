In the All Doses All Indications population, infusion-related reactions occurred in 83 patients
(4.2%) of the 1972 patients treated with tislelizumab. The most frequent PTs were pyrexia (40
patients, 2.0%), rash (5 patients, 0.3%), and nausea (5 patients, 0.3%). Events of infusion-related
reactions were typically Grade 1 or 2 in severity; only 6 patients (0.3%) in the All Doses All
Indications population had events of > Grade 3. A Grade 2 infusion-related hypersensitivity
reaction occurred in | patient (0.1%) in the All Doses All Indications population; no other
infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions were reported. Five patients (0.3%) and 21 patients
(1.1%) experienced an infusion-related reaction leading to tislelizumab discontinuation or dose
modification, respectively. Twenty-six of the 83 patients (31.3%) who experienced infusion-
related reactions were treated with corticosteroids, and 79 of 83 cases (95.2%) were resolved.

The Applicant’s Position:

Immune-mediated adverse events were generally low grade and responsive to corticosteroids or
hormone therapy as needed. Infusion-related reactions were infrequent, low grade, and occurred
at a similar incidence in both arms.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the summary results described in the Applicant’s Table 32. As noted above,
however, FDA considers that there was an event of autoimmune hepatitis leading to fatal hepatic
failure; although the Applicant did not flag this event as an imAE flag, inclusion of this patient
would not change the incidence of imAEs significantly (55 patients or 21.6%).

Similarly, FDA analysis demonstrates 10 patients (3.9%) who experienced a > Grade 3 imAE
compared to the Applicant’s calculation of 9 patients (3.5%) who experienced a > Grade 3
imAE. The following were the Grade > 3 imAEs:

hepatitis (3 patients)

pneumonitis (3 patients)

myositis/rhabdomyolysis (2 patients)

myocarditis (1 patient)

hypothyroidism (1 patient)

All of these were Grade 3 events except for 1 of the events of hepatitis (Grade 5, as discussed
above) and an event of Grade 4 hypothyroidism. FDA analysis agrees with the remaining data in
Table 32 including the use of systemic steroids, hormone therapy, and drug discontinuations.
Additionally, though not noted in the Applicant’s Table 33, above, FDA found 1 patient who
experienced immune-mediated pituitary dysfunction (Grade 2). That patient was treated with
systemic steroids and was not discontinued from study therapy. This information is consistent
with the Applicant’s Table 19 in the Clinical Overview document submitted to the BLA.
