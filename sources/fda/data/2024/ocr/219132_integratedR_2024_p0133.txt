Assessment

During the review cycle the Applicant provided real world data from 17 subjects who were
under the age of 5 years old (ranging from 4 months to 3 years old) and who had received
Tanganil (N-acetyl-DL-leucine) as a component of their clinical care as reported to the
Applicant by Dr. Michael Strupp, a neurologist in Munich, Germany. Just three of these
subjects had a diagnosis of NPC; the remainder had other genetic diagnoses including
Tay-Sachs disease, Sandhoff disease (GM2 gangliosiodoses), and Ataxia-Telangiectasia.
Dosages ranged from 1 gram per day to 2.5 grams per day, divided into BID of TID doses.
Treatment durations ranges from 2 to 73 months. The three subjects with NPC were

18 months, 2 years, and 3 years old at the time of treatment initiation. All three were reported
to have been on treatment with Tanganil for 12 months or more (range: 12 to 73 months).
There were no drug-related AEs reported, although notably these subjects are not being
monitored in a clinical trial during which AEs would be systematically reported. No PK
values were provided.

Anecdotal reports submitted by subject/caregivers to an online registry were also provided.
All these subjects had a diagnosis of GM2 gangliosidosis, were between the ages of 8 months
and 4 years old, and the only AEs noted were gastrointestinal upset or potential
gastrointestinal reflux; most reported no side effects.

For subjects in the placebo-controlled pivotal Trial IB1001-301, there were no serious safety
signals. Subjects <10 years old did not experience significantly more or different AEs than
subjects >10 years old.

Based on the PopPK analyses (see Section 6.3.2), the exposures following the proposed dose
in subjects <10 years old and weighing >15 kg are expected to be similar to the exposures in
subjects >10 years.

Conclusion

Levacetylleucine is expected to be well-tolerated at the to-be-marketed doses for individuals
<10 years old and weighing >15 kg. There are limited clinical safety data for subjects

<10 years old, and no data for levacetylleucine in subjects <5 years old or <20 kg. However,
important considerations are the relatively benign safety profile of levacetylleucine overall in
the available clinical trials, anecdotal reports of the to-be-marketed or higher doses in
subjects <4 years old with the related product Tanganil, as well the similar PK profile
following the to-be marketed dose in subjects >10 years old compared to the subjects

>10 years old. These data are considered sufficient to support a limited amount of
extrapolation of safety to support use in pediatric patients weighing >15 kg. Further
considerations included the favorable benefit/risk profile, that NPC is a genetic disease with
the same pathophysiology across the age spectrum, that while there can be heterogeneity in
manifestations, there was similarity in response to treatment in terms of safety across the
studied populations, and that the drug is not anticipated to behave differently in slightly
younger children. Thus, it is anticipated that there will be similarity in the safety profile
