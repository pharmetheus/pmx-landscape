BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — BLA 761232
TEVIMBRA (tislelizumab)

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees that esophageal cancer is a serious and life-threatening illness and acknowledges the
differences discussed by the Applicant with respect to esophageal cancer histologies (esophageal
adenocarcinoma [EAC] and ESCC) in high-risk regions of the world compared to in lower-risk
regions of the world (including the U.S). In the U.S., approximately 20,640 new cases of
esophageal cancer are expected in 2022, and approximately 16,410 deaths are expected. The
mnale:female ratio is 4:1 for both diagnoses and deaths (American Cancer Society 2022). The
estimated 5-year survival rate for U.S. patients with esophageal cancer is only approximately
20% (Siegel 2020; American Cancer Society). EAC is more common in Western populations
(Torre 2016) and ESCC accounts for less than 30% of all esophageal cancers in the U.S.
(American Cancer Society).

In a retrospective analysis of the Surveillance Epidemiology and End Results (SEER) database
data from the years 2004 — 2015, in the US 23,804 patients with EAC and 13,919 patients with
ESCC were diagnosed. The most common stage of presentation for both adenocarcinoma
(36.9%) and squamous cell (26.8%) carcinoma was Stage IV. In this analysis, 66% of patients
with ESCC were men, 56% were White, 26% were Black, 8% were Hispanic and 9% were Asian
or Pacific islanders. Important for this BLA, as per the analysis of the SEER database, there are
no differences in outcomes for patients with ESCC based on race or ethnicity (Then EO, 2020).

2.2. Analysis of Current Treatment Options

Prior to the use of checkpoint inhibitors (CPIs), the therapeutic approach for unresectable
recurrent locally advanced or metastatic ESCC after prior systemic therapy was based on the
single-agent chemotherapy regimens (second-line treatment), such as docetaxel, paclitaxel, or
irinotecan (NCCN 2017),. These regimens were generally recommended for patients with good
performance status, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status (PS)
score of 0 or 1 and were often associated with key toxicities that led to treatment interruptions or
delays, dose reductions, and/or treatment discontinuations. Fewer than 20% of patients receiving
second-line chemotherapy respond to treatment, with median overall survival (OS) ranging from
5 to 7 months (Abraham et al 2020). Hence, an improvement in the second-line setting was
needed that provided a longer survival time, with fewer high-grade adverse events.

Phase 3 study results demonstrating benefit over standard chemotherapy coupled with a more
tolerable safety profile are available for 3 immune CPIs including Study 302 for tislelizumab
(also known as BGB-A317), the most recently completed study, and 2 previously completed
studies, ATTRACTION-3 for nivolumab and KEYNOTE-181 for pembrolizumab. Results of
ATTRACTION-3 or KEYNOTE-181 were not available at the time of initiation of Study 302.

In ATTRACTION-3, nivolumab demonstrated a 23% reduction in risk of death (OS HR of 0.77
[95% CI: 0.62 to 0.96, p-value = 0.019; median OS: 10.9 months compared with chemotherapy
median OS: 8.4 months) in previously treated patients with advanced ESCC (Kato et al 2019).
