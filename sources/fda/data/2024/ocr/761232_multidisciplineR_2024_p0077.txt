* Results of standard-of-care laboratory tests and physical examinations performed before
obtaining informed consent and < 7 days before randomization could be used for screening

assessments rather than repeating such tests. A fresh tumor biopsy was permitted if no

archival tumor tissue was available.

Cycle 1 treatment was required to be given < 3 business days before randomization. From

Cycle 2, there was a 3-day window for all study treatment given on a 21-day cycle unless

otherwise noted. There was a 2-day window for treatments given on a weekly schedule.

© If routine laboratory tests (eg, hematology, serum chemistry) were completed < 7 days before
the End-of-Treatment Visit, the tests were not required to be repeated. Tumor assessment
was not required at the End-of-Treatment Visit, provided that fewer than 6 weeks had passed
since the last assessment.

4 The End-of-Treatment Visit could be combined with the Safety Follow-up Visit, provided
that the end of treatment occurred 30 days (+ 7 days) after the last study treatment; the
assessments for both visits were required to be performed.

© Included history of treatment for the primary diagnosis, including prior systemic, radiation
treatment, and surgical treatment. Radiographic imaging that was performed before study

entry could be collected for review by the investigator, as clinically appropriate.

Concomitant medications included any prescription medications, over-the-counter

medications, herbal supplements, and intravenous medications and fluids. All concomitant

medications received < 30 days before the first dose of study treatment and 30 days after the
last infusion of study treatment were required to be recorded. At subsequent visits, changes to
current medications or medications that were used since the last documentation of
medications were recorded. Only new anticancer treatment was collected at the Survival

Follow-up Visits.
