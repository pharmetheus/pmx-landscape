The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees based on results from study 20200491 and study 2016323, tarlatamab represents a
therapeutic approach intended for addressing unmet medical needs in patient with SCLC, a life-
threatening condition.

FDA agrees that the proposed dose regimen of 1 mg on cycle 1 day 1, followed by 10 mg on
cycle 1 day 8, 15 and every 2 weeks did not raise any significant safety concerns. CRS risk was
effectively mitigated by a single 1 mg step-up dose and retreatment plans after dose delay. The
E-R efficacy and safety relationships generally support the proposed dosage regimen.

However, a positive E-R relationship was identified between tarlatamab exposure and
neutropenia or neurologic toxicity including ICANS with a higher risk of any grade neutropenia
or neurologic toxicity including ICANS at higher exposure. Therefore, the appropriate risk
management strategies may be necessary to ensure a favorable risk/benefit balance.

A positive E-R relationship between tarlatamab exposure and efficacy endpoints was observed
across the dose levels evaluated (3-100 mg). The efficacy appears to reach plateau at doses of
10 mg and above, supporting the proposed target doses.

Furthermore, FDA agrees that no clinically relevant impact on QTc interval was observed.
Although Applicant’s analysis determines a statistically significant positive C-QTc relationship,
this relationship is very shallow, not clinically significant, and unlikely to be due to interaction
with hERG channel since tarlatamab is a large therapeutic protein.
