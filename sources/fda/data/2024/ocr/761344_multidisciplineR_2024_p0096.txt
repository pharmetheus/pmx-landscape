Specific Population

Intrinsic Factor

No clinically meaningful differences in the PK were observed
based on age (32 to 82 years), BW (35 to 149 kg), sex, race
(White and Asian), mild to moderate renal impairment, or mild
hepatic impairment. Although BW and race were identified as
statistically significant covariates on tarlatamab volume of
distribution and clearance, the impacts of these covariates on
tarlatamab exposure were not considered clinically relevant.
Positive ADA was also identified as a significant time-varying
covariate on tarlatamab clearance. Due to low occurrence of
ADA, the clinically relevant effect of ADA on tarlatamab’s PK,
efficacy and safety is not conclusive. None of the covariates
evaluated above found to have important effect on E-R
relationships for efficacy. Therefore, no dose adjustment is
needed based on these intrinsic or extrinsic covariates.

No difference in ORR was observed at the DLL3 cut points of <
75% vs > 75% and < 25% vs > 25% at moderate (2+) and strong
(3+) staining intensities (see section 8.1.6). These results
support treatment of patients at 1/10 mg irrespective of
baseline DLL3 expression status.

The effects of severe renal impairment, end-stage renal disease,
moderate to severe hepatic impairment, ADA, or ethnicity (e.g.,
Hispanic or Latino) are unknown due to limited or unavailable
data.

PD Characteristics

Pharmacodynamics Transient elevation of serum cytokines IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, and

IFN-y were observed at a tarlatamab dosage of 0.3 mg Q2W
and above. Peak elevation of cytokines was generally observed
20-24 hours following the initial dose of tarlatamab at 1 mg on
Cycle 1 Day 1 and generally returned to baseline levels prior to
the next infusion on Cycle 1 Day 8.

Exposure-
Response for

Higher exposures (e.g., Cavg,first cycle) Were associated with higher
ORR. The efficacy reached a plateau at the higher exposures of
target dosage 10 mg Q2W supporting the proposed dosage.
