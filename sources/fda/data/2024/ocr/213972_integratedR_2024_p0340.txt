The Applicant interpreted this footnote to mean that susceptibility profiles or molecular testing
(such as WGS) could be used to differentiate between baseline and follow-up isolates of the
same genus and species. As a result, a subject with a follow-up isolate at TOC that was the same
genus and species as that at baseline could be considered a microbiological eradication (and
eligible for assessment as an overall responder) if the two isolates had different susceptibili

profiles or WGS results.

Statistical, Clinical and Clinical Microbiology Reviewers’ Comment: This is not consistent with
our assessment of microbiological success/persistence, which is based on the genus and species
of the organism. It is possible that there may be different (i.e., hetero-resistant) subpopulations
of the same bacterial pathogen present at baseline which exhibit different antibacterial drug
susceptibility profiles and have different WGS results. These subpopulations may not be
identified because only a single colony was selected for analysis. As a result, the Division does
not agree with the revised definition; if the post-baseline urine culture grew the same bacterial
species present at baseline at 210? CFU/mL, it was considered a microbiological failure. In FDA’s
analyses, we use the original SAP (version 1) dated September 30, 2019, which was written
before the second interim analysis.
