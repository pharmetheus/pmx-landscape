NDA 217686
TRYVIO (aprocitentan)

2.2. Conclusions Regarding Benefit-Risk

Aprocitentan at doses of 12.5 mg daily and 25 mg daily was demonstrated to lower blood
pressure in patients taking other antihypertensive medications, and thus is expected to reduce the
tisk of cardiovascular events in these patients. Although two doses were studied in the Phase 3
trial, the 25 mg dose does not appear to provide a meaningful improvement in blood pressure
reduction as compared to the 12.5 mg dose.

Based on data from animal reproduction studies with endothelin receptor antagonists,
aprocitentan can cause embryo-fetal toxicity when administered during pregnancy and a REMS
will be required to mitigate this risk by ensuring prescribers are educated about appropriate
patient counseling (i.e., need for contraception and pregnancy testing). Although the available
data indicate that aprocitentan can cause liver injury, the injuries seen in the aprocitentan clinical
trials were modest and reversible. Similar to macitentan, hepatotoxicity will be included as a
labeled risk for aprocitentan, and periodic liver tests will be recommended. Other risks (i-e., fluid
retention, hemoglobin decrease, and decreased sperm counts) will also be described as Warnings
in the label. To mitigate the risks of fluid retention and hemoglobin decrease, the label will also
include periodic monitoring for signs and symptoms of fluid retention, weight gain, and
worsening heart failure, and periodic assessments of hemoglobin, respectively.

Given the measures that will be put in place, aprocitentan’s benefits outweigh its risks. Because
of aprocitentan’s safety profile, as well as the availability of other antihypertensive agents with
more benign safety profiles, the benefit-risk profile is considered favorable only when used in
combination with other agents to lower blood pressure in patients who are not adequately
controlled on other drugs (i.e., not as first-line therapy).! For these patients, the clinical benefit of
reducing cardiovascular risk outweighs risks that are either manageable (fluid retention,
hemoglobin reduction, liver injury) or have rigorous risk mitigation requirements (embryofetal
toxicity).
