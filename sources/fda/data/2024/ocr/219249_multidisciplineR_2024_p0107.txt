At study entry, the majority of patients had metastatic disease (99.1%) with the remainder
locally advanced disease (0.9%). The majority of patients had an ER+/ PR+ hormone receptor
status (69.5%). Per eCRF data, most patients had secondary endocrine resistance (65.5%) vs.

organs involved at baseline. Only 0.6% had known CNS metastases at baseline. Per eCRF data,
the majority of patients had measurable disease (non-measurable/ evaluable disease, including
bone-only, was excluded) (96.6%), visceral disease (80%; defined as lung, liver, brain, pleural,
and peritoneal involvement) and liver metastases (51.7%). The majority of patients had
received prior (neo)-adjuvant chemotherapy (82.8%) and/or prior (neo)-adjuvant ET (99.4%);
very few patients had received prior adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibitor therapy (1.2%).

The Applicant’s Position: Study WO41554 enriched a patient population with poor prognosis
clinicopathological characteristics. The two treatment arms were comparable with respect to
baseline disease characteristics and breast cancer history and are representative of a patient
population with endocrine-resistant P/IK3CA-mutated, HR+, HER2— advanced breast cancer
receiving treatment in a first-line setting.

The FDA’s Assessment:

In general, FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment. The patient population enrolled on this
trial had endocrine resistant/refractory tumors that are considered to have a poor prognosis.
The majority of patients had “secondary” endocrine resistance. Primary endocrine resistance
was defined as relapse while on the first 2 years of adjuvant endocrine therapy and secondary
endocrine resistance was defined a relapse while on adjuvant endocrine therapy after at least 2
years or relapse within 12 months of completing adjuvant endocrine therapy. In addition, all
patients had a PIK3CA mutation, which is also considered a poor prognostic factor.
