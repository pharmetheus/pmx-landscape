In the Phase 2 study (Study AC-080A201), aprocitentan doses of 10, 25, and 50 mg decreased
sitting systolic/diastolic unattended automated office blood pressure from baseline to Week 8,
with placebo-corrected reductions of 7.1/4.9, 9.9/7.0 and 7.6/5.0 mmHg, respectively, compared
with a reduction of 4.8/3.8 mmHg with lisinopril 20 mg. For those with valid ambulatory blood
pressure readings, aprocitentan 10, 25, and 50 mg significantly decreased placebo-corrected 24-
hour systolic/diastolic blood pressure by 4.0/4.0, 4.8/5.9, and 3.7/4.5 mmHg, respectively.

The doses for the Phase 3 study (PRECISION) were based on data from the Phase 2 study and
the PD Phase 1 study, AC-080-102, which evaluated the effect of aprocitentan on body weight in
healthy male subjects on a high-sodium diet. While noninferiority versus placebo in change from
baseline to Day 9 body weight could not be demonstrated for the 25 and 50 mg doses of
aprocitentan, only the 50 mg dose significantly increased body weight compared to placebo
(p=0.017). Taken together, the Applicant selected a 12.5 mg and 25 mg once daily dose for the
Phase 3 study, believing that aprocitentan in the range of 10 to 25 mg could provide the best
risk/benefit profile.

In the PRECISION study, the mean (+ SE) change in SBP at 4 weeks was -15.3+0.9 mmHg for
aprocitentan 12.5 mg, -15.2+0.9 mmHg for 25 mg, and -11.5+0.9 mmHg for placebo, for a
difference versus placebo of -3.8+1.3 mmHg (97.5% CI: -6.8, -0.8; p =0.004) and -3.7+1.3
mmHg (97.5% CI: -6.7, -0.8; p=0.005), respectively. Based on the data for the primary efficacy
endpoint, it is unclear if the 25 mg dose would provide any additional benefit compared to
