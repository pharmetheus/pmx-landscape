TEVIMBRA - tislelizumab-jsgr

Infusion-related reactions were summarized based on the adverse event verbatim terms reported
by the investigator and the list of symptoms/signs of infusion-related reactions outlined in the
protocol and their association with a tislelizumab infusion in terms of causality and onset time.

If the adverse event term did not include the term infusion-related reactions, but met all of the
following criteria:

¢ one or more of the infusion related reactions-associated symptoms per protocol
e occurred up to 1 day after the onset of the event
e deemed related to tislelizumab by investigator

the terms were firstly reviewed by the medical monitor, and then the investigator was queried to
clarify if the event was considered an infusion related reactions, in which case, the adverse event
term would have been updated to include the term infusion-related reactions. Infusion-related
reactions were summarized by SOC, PT, and worst grade. Infusion related reactions leading to
treatment discontinuation or dose modification were summarized by PT level.

The Applicant’s Position:
Adverse events were collected and analyzed as described above.

The FDA’s Assessment:

National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE),
Version 4.03 were used to assess the severity of all TEAEs. The MedDRA version 23.0
dictionary was used to code TEAE data. TEAEs were assessed from the time of study treatment
initiation and for 30 days after the last dose of study treatment. Because of the long half-life of
monoclonal antibodies and the potential for late-onset treatment-emergent imAEs beyond the 30-
day period following the last dose of tislelizumab, imAEs were also followed for 90 days after
the last dose of study treatment per the study protocol.

Note that FDA, as indicated above, does not consider Applicant’s attribution of whether an AE
was drug-related or not when reviewing safety data.
