e Progression-Free Survival (PFS): Similar statistical methods described in the primary
efficacy analysis of OS were applied to the PFS analysis. According to the primary
censoring rule, PFS were censored in patients with 1) no baseline or any post-baseline
tumor assessments and without death within two tumor assessments from randomization;
2) no progression at the time of data cutoff or withdrawal from study; 3) new anticancer
treatment started before disease progression; and 4) death or progression after more than
one missed tumor assessment.

e Duration of response (DOR): The same censoring rule for PFS primary analysis was
applied to DOR. Median of DOR per treatment arm with 95% confidence interval was
constructed with a generalized Brookmeyer and Crowley method.

e Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQoL): PRO key endpoints included the global health
status/QoL (GHS) and physical function as measured by the EORTC QLQ-C30 and
esophageal cancer main symptoms of pain, eating, dysphagia and reflux as measured by
EORTC QLQ-OES18. Descriptive analysis (mean [standard deviation], median, range
and change of mean scores) for the EORTC QLQ-C30 and the EORTC QLQ-OES18
scales and the EQ-5D-5L VAS were summarized by treatment arm. Time to clinically
meaningful worsening for key PRO endpoints were evaluated by treatment arm. All
HRQoL measures were summarized in the ITT Analysis Set and the patients with PD-L1
vCPS = 10%.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the applicant’s presentation of the statistical analysis plan for efficacy analyses.
The overall type I error rate of 0.025 (one-sided) was controlled by testing the primary endpoint
of OS in the ITT population and the key secondary endpoint of OS in the PDL1 vCPS >10%
patient subpopulation sequentially. According to the study protocol there was no formal interim
analysis planned for the primary and key secondary endpoint. Other secondary endpoints of PFS,
ORR, DOR and HRQoL were not formally tested with alpha allocation and are considered
descriptive only.
