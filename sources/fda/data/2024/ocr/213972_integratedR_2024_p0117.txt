In the original NDA submission, the Applicant provided attestation that Trial 301 was conducted
in compliance with good clinical practice guidelines and all local regulatory requirements were
followed. The trial protocol and amendments were reviewed by institutional review boards. The
Applicant certified that none of the investigators for Trial 301 had any financial interests or
arrangements with the Applicant.

A review of clinical sites for Trial 310 was conducted by the Office of Scientific Investigations
(OSI) to ensure data quality. Three sites (Site 115 in California, Site 129 in Arkansas, and Site
251 in Florida) were inspected based on high enrollment and high treatment response. Per the
Clinical Inspection Summary by Dr. John Lee, the data from the inspected sites appear to be
acceptable in support of the proposed indication for sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid. The
primary and key secondary efficacy endpoint data were verifiable, and no significant
underreporting of AEs was observed. The trial appeared to have been conducted in compliance
with good clinical practice principles and regulations at Sites 115 and 129. A GCP deficiency
(lack of consistent documentation of adequate contraception history and negative pregnancy
testing) was noted at Site 251 but was unlikely to be important to efficacy analysis of study data.
For additional details, please refer to Section 22 and the clinical inspection summary written by
Dr. John Lee from OSI, dated October 7, 2024, in DARRTS.

For Trial 301, routine PDUFA surveillance inspections were conducted for four clinical
investigators. In addition, combined PDUFA/for-cause inspections of two clinical sites (Sites
202 and 218) were conducted to address specific data reliability concerns raised by the
Applicant. The Applicant listed several reasons for their exclusion of Site 202: (1) pulse-field gel
electrophoresis (PFGE) analysis of some urinary isolates identified from the baseline urine
bacterial cultures were either genetically indistinguishable or closely related; (2) a high
contamination rate of urine samples; and (3) artificial intelligence software that suggested
intentional patient nonadherent behavior. The FDA inspection could not confirm that a single
urine sample was used to enrolled multiple subjects at Site 202. In addition, it was concluded that
the medication adherence software results may have been misinterpreted as many subjects with
“ted flag” events were well-documented to have taken the dose. Finally, the decision to exclude
Site 202 was made after unblinding of the interim analysis. FDA concluded that there did 1 not
appear to be sufficient cause to exclude data from Site 202.

For these reasons, FDA agreed with the
Applicant that data from Site 218 should be excluded. For details on clinical site inspections for
Trial 301, please refer to the NDA Multi-Disciplinary Review and Evaluation {NDA 213972}
dated July 22, 2021, and the clinical inspection summary written by Dr. Cheryl Grandinetti from
OSI, dated June 18, 2021, in DARRTS.
