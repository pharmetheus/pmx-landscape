NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

The pharmacokinetics (PK) of tarlatamab was characterized after single intravenous (IV) bolus
injections (Study 121985 / eCTD Location: Module 4.2.2.2), short IV infusions (Study 121424 /
eCTD Location: Module 4.2.3.2), IVs slow bolus administration (Study 122516 / eCTD Location:
Module 4.2.3.2), and subcutaneous administration in cynomolgus monkeys (Study 157064 /
eCTD Location: Module 4.2.3.6). After a single IV bolus injection, serum concentrations of
tarlatamab declined in a biphasic manner with a mean terminal half-life of 234 hours (Study
121985 / eCTD Location: Module 4.2.2.2).

The toxicokinetics (TK) of tarlatamab was characterized in cynomolgus monkeys after weekly IV
doses of 50, 500, and 4500 mg/kg for up to 3 months. The studies have been described in detail
in Section 2.2 of Module 2.6.6, Toxicology Written Summary. Briefly, tarlatamab exposure
increased with dose and with repeated dosing. No difference (within 2-fold) in exposure, as
assessed by the area under the curve from the time of dosing to the last measurable
concentration (AUCjast) and calculated drug concentration at time 0 after IV bolus dose (Co) was
observed between male and female animals.

The TK of muS757 was characterized in BALB/c mice after weekly IV doses of 50, 500, and 4500
mg/kg for up to 28 days. The study has been described in detail in Section 2.2 of Module 2.6.6,
Toxicology Written Summary. Briefly, muS757 exposure was dose-proportional and increased
over time, particularly at the > 500 mg/kg dose levels. There was no sex-based difference in
exposure in AUCiast and maximum observed concentration (Cmax) on days 1 and 22.

muS757 exposure increased approximately in proportion to dose in both females and fetuses.
At the single sampling time point included (GD 13, 120 hr) fetal serum concentrations were
22.6- to 29.5-fold higher than female serum concentrations (Reference Table 8 and Table 9).
