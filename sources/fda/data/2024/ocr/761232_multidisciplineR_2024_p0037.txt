(Applicant Figure Reproduced from Study BLGC-008)

To determine whether BGB-A317 can functionally block PD-L1-mediated inhibitory signaling and activate cytokine
release in human T cells, a PD-1-expressing human T-cell line HuT78/PD-1 and a target cell HEK293/OS8-PDL1
were generated. In HEK293/OS8-PD-L1 cells, a membrane-bound form of anti-CD3 scFv (OS8) and a PD-1 ligand,
PD-L1 were stably transfected. The resulting signal-sending cell is capable of engaging TCR and PD-1
simultaneously. The assay was carried out by co-culture of HuT78/PD-1 and HEK293/OS8-PDL1] cells in the
presence of BGB-A317. Secretion of IL-2 in media was analyzed by ELISA.

Anti-tumor activity was measured in three human cancer xenograft models (A431 human
epidermoid carcinoma, Study RO1-VIVO-126; BCCO-028 colon cancer, Study RO1-VIVO-127);
and BCLU-054 NSCLC models, Study RO1-VIVO-128) and in a human PD-1 transgenic mouse
model with B16/F10 mouse melanoma cells overexpressing murine granulocyte macrophage
colony-stimulation factor (mGM-CSF) (Study RO1-VIVO-135).

Tislelizumab (1, 3, or 10 mg/kg, once per week) had anti-tumor activity in anA431 xenograft
model in non-obese diabetic (NOD)/severe combined immune deficient (SCID) mice.
