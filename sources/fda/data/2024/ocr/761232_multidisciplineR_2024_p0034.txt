BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — BLA 761232
TEVIMBRA (tislelizumab)

5. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Tislelizumab is an IgG4« monoclonal antibody that recognizes PD-1 and blocks its interaction
with PD-L1 and PD-L2. Tislelizumab has an approximate molecular weight of 148 kDa. Binding
of the PD-1 ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2 to the PD-1 receptor found on T cells inhibits T-cell
proliferation and cytokine production. Upregulation of PD-1 ligands occurs in some tumors and
signaling through this pathway can contribute to inhibition of active T-cell immune surveillance
of tumors. Based on the nonclinical data submitted in the BLA, the established pharmacological
class (EPC) of “programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) blocking antibody” is scientifically valid.

In vitro studies showed tislelizumab bound to human and cynomolgus monkey PD-1 with
nanomolar affinity and absence of cross reaction with mouse PD-1, supporting the use of
cynomolgus monkeys as a pharmacologically relevant species for toxicity assessment.
Tislelizumab blocked the binding of human PD-1 to human PD-L1 and PD-L2 ina cell-based
assay and promoted T-cell activation measured by IL-2 secretion by a PD-1 expressing cell line.
Tislelizumab did not have in vitro complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) activity or
antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) activity. Anti-tumor activity was observed in
three xenograft models (A431 human epidermoid carcinoma, BCCO-028 colon cancer, and
BCLU-054 non-small cell lung cancer). Anti-tumor activity was observed in human PD-1
transgenic mice with B16/F10 mouse melanoma cells.

Tislelizumab was evaluated in a 13-week repeat-dose toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys.
Monkeys received once weekly intravenous administration of tislelizumab at doses up to 30
mg/kg. There was no mortality. Findings included an increase in lymphoid aggregates and
immune cell infiltration in various tissues.

Embryo-fetal developmental toxicology studies were not conducted with tislelizumab. The
Applicant provided a literature-based assessment of the potential reproductive toxicity of
tislelizumab. Data from the literature indicates the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway plays a critical role in
maintaining maternal immunological tolerance to the fetus during pregnancy. In allogeneic
mouse models of pregnancy, blockade of PD-1/PD-L1 signaling resulted in an increased
incidence of fetal loss but no fetal malformations. There are no reports of fetal malformations
associated with PD-1 deficiency in mice; however, late onset autoimmunity can occur. Based on
these findings, potential risks of administering the product during pregnancy include increased
rates of abortion or stillbirth and an increased risk in offspring developing immune-related
disorders or altered immune response. Labeling states the product can cause fetal harm if
administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. Effective contraception for 4
months after the last dose is recommended based on 5S half-lives. The Applicant did not evaluate
