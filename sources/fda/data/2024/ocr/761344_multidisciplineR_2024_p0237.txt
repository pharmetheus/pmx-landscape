NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

ICANS is an important identified risk for tarlatamab. In the tarlatamab monotherapy studies (n =
457), ICANS and associated neurological adverse events using the ICANS AMQ broad search
strategy were observed in 72 subjects (15.8%). At the 10 mg tarlatamab dose (n = 160), ICANS
events were observed in 15 subjects (9.4%). Across the tarlatamab monotherapy studies, most
ICANS events were mild to moderate. No fatal events have been reported. Most of the events
were self-limiting without the need for treatment discontinuation or dose reduction.

The FDA’s Assessment:

Based on FDA analysis in the pooled safety population, neurologic toxicity including ICANS
occurred in 47% of patients who received tarlatamab, including 10% Grade 3; there were no
Grade 4 events. The most frequent neurologic toxicities were headache (14%), peripheral
neuropathy (7%), dizziness (7%), insomnia (6%), muscular weakness (3.7%), delirium (2.1%),
syncope (1.6%) and neurotoxicity (1.1%).

ICANS occurred in 9% of patients and recurrent ICANS occurred in 1.6% of patients. Most
patients experienced ICANS following cycle 2 day 1 (24%). Following Day 1, Day 8, and Day 15
infusions, 0.5%, 0.5% and 3.7% of patients experienced 2 Grade 2 ICANS, respectively. The
median time to onset of ICANS from the first dose of tarlatamab was 29.5 days (range: 1 to 154

days). The median time to resolution of ICANS was 33 days (range: 1 to 93 days).
a)
FDA did not agree with the Applicant’s initial proposal in the USPI to monitor patients for

hours after first infusion and to remain within 1 hour of an appropriate healthcare setting for
hours after each tarlatamab infusion on Cycle 1 Day 1 and Cycle 1 Day 8. In Part 3 of the
pivotal study (modified monitoring cohort), patients were hospitalized for 24 hours post
tarlatamab infusion for Cycle 1 day 1 and Cycle 1 Day 8 doses. Based on the data provided, FDA
agreed that monitoring of patients from the start of tarlatamab infusion for 22-24 hours on
Cycle 1 Day 1 and Cycle 1 Day 8 in an appropriate healthcare setting would be appropriate to
mitigate risk. FDA also recommend patients remain within 1 hour of an appropriate healthcare
setting for a total of 48 hours from the start of the infusion with tarlatamab following Cycle 1
Day 1 and Cycle 1 Day 8, accompanied by a caregiver.

FDA carefully considered the need for Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies (REMS) given
the major identified safety findings of neurologic toxicity including ICANS and have determined
that a REMS is not needed. Based on our review, we have determined that the toxicity profile of
the tarlatamab can be adequately conveyed through labeling (including a Boxed Warning for
serious or life-threatening CRS and neurologic toxicity, including ICANS). In addition, enhanced
pharmacovigilance and a Dear Healthcare Provider letter are non-REMS adjuncts recommended
to help convey these risks. Refer to Section 12 for rationale for not requiring a REMS.
