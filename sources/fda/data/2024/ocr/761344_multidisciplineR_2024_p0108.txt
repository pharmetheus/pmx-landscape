The proposed restart recommendations after dose delay were implemented in

Study 20200491. In Study 20200491, there were 6 episodes of repriming with 1 mg step-
up dose: 2 episodes of > 21 days of dose delays following the 1 mg step-up dose, 2
episodes of >28 days of dose delay follow the first full dose of 10 mg on Day 8, and 2
episodes of >36 days of dose delay following third and later 10 mg Q2W. No CRS was
reported in any of these episodes of repriming.

FDA’s assessment on the adequacy of the proposed recommendations for restarting
therapy after dose delay, as detailed below, include evaluations of observed rebound
CRS data after dose delay and PK simulations following different dosing delay scenarios.
In Table 22Table 27, evaluation of rebound CRS events following dose delay in 20200491
indicated that no increase in the risk of CRS following dose delays that were within the
proposed cut-off for repriming and no patients experienced Grade 2 or higher CRS upon
the resumption of treatment. The overall risk of rebound CRS is assessed to be low.
However, there is sparse data to support evaluation of longer dose delay intervals
greater than proposed cut-off for repriming. There were three patients experienced
dose delays ranging from 15 to 21 days following the first step-up dose with no CRS, a
Grade 1 CRS and a Grade 2 CRS, respectively. One patient had dose delay ranging from
22 to 28 days following the first treatment dose and five patients had dose delays great
than 28 days following the subsequent full treatment dose. None of these patients
developed rebound CRS. The overall clinical data is not concerning regarding rebound
CRS or against the proposed re-treatment plan.
