NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

For the 100 mg target dose, consistent with the initial ISS analysis, the most frequently reported
(2 5% of subjects) grade > 3 adverse events by preferred term for subjects treated with
tarlatamab monotherapy were neutropenia, anemia, fatigue, and hyponatremia (6.7% each);
and CRS (6.1%).

The Applicant's Position:

The types of grade > 3 adverse events reported for subjects with SCLC treated with tarlatamab
at the 10 mg dose were generally similar to those reported for subjects treated at the 100 mg
dose, although the incidences of certain grade 2 3 events (hyponatremia, neutropenia, and CRS)
were higher in the 100 mg group than in the 10 mg group. The incidence of grade 2 3 adverse
events with the 90-day safety update was similar to that seen in the initial ISS analysis.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA’s analysis of significant adverse events in the pooled population included CRS, neurologic
toxicity including ICANS, and cytopenias, which are further described in section 8.2.5 of this
review. Other significant adverse events included infections, hepatotoxicity and hypersensitivity
which are included in the Warnings and Precaution section of the label.

Infections in the pooled safety population, including opportunistic infections, occurred in 41%
of patients who received tarlatamab. Grade 3 or 4 infections occurred in 13% of patients. The
most frequent infections were COVID-19 (9%, majority during the COVID-19 pandemic), urinary
tract infection (10%), pneumonia (9%), respiratory tract infection (3.2%), and candida infection
(3.2%).

Hepatotoxicity in the pooled safety population included elevated ALT occurring in 42% of
patients with Grade 3 or 4 ALT elevation occurring in 2.1% of tarlatamab-treated patients.
Elevated AST occurred in 44% of patients, with Grade 3 or 4 AST elevation occurring in 3.2%.
Elevated bilirubin occurred in 15% of patients, with Grade 3 or 4 total bilirubin elevations
occurring in 1.6% of patients.

Hypersensitivity was also added to the Warnings and Precautions section of the USPI as a
potential risk. Clinical signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity may include, but are not limited
to, rash and bronchospasm. There were 4 patients identified with hypersensitivity adverse
events in the review of overall safety data (3 patients in the 100 mg dose group and 1 patient in
the 10 mg dose group). The first patient received 10 mg on Cycle 4 Day 1 and 8 days later
developed Grade 3 bronchospasm attributed to a mucous plug. The event resolved and no
action was taken with tarlatamab. The second patient received 100 mg on Cycle 2 Day 15 and
was subsequently hospitalized for maculopapular rash and treated with prednisolone,
zeroveen, and betamethasone valerate. The rash resolved and no action was taken with
