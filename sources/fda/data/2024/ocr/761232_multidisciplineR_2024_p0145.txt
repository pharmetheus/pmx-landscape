The Applicant’s Position:
Fewer adverse events leading to dose modification were observed in patients in the tislelizumab

group compared with patients in the ICC group.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the assessment above in that fewer dose interruptions were observed in the
tislelizumab arm (23% by FDA calculation) compared to the ICC arm (~57% by FDA
calculation). Management of dose modifications was different between study arms; while the
standard of care with immune checkpoints is dose interruptions until recovery, with
chemotherapy, often dose delays are followed by dose reductions.

Significant Adverse Events
Data:

Immune-Mediated Adverse Events

Overall, immune-mediated adverse events of any grade were reported in 54 patients (21.2%)
treated with tislelizumab (Table 32). The prespecified adverse event searches for Study 302
included events of immune-mediated hypothyroidism, immune-mediated pneumonitis, immune-
mediated skin reactions, immune-mediated colitis, immune-mediated hepatitis, immune-
mediated myositis/rhabdomyolysis, immune-mediated adrenal insufficiency, immune-mediated
myocarditis, and immune-mediated pituitary dysfunction. Note that the terms “immune-related
adverse event” and “immune-mediated adverse event” are used interchangeably across various
documents in the submission. Also note that the protocol did not specify collection of immune-
mediated adverse events for ICC.

Consistent with other anti-PD-L1 inhibitors, tislelizumab was associated with immune-mediated
adverse events. In tislelizumab-treated patients in Study 302, 21.2% reported immune-mediated
adverse events of any grade, and 3.5% reported immune-mediated adverse events of > Grade 3
(Table 32). Serious immune-mediated adverse events were reported for 5.9% of tislelizumab
treated- patients. None of the events was fatal.
