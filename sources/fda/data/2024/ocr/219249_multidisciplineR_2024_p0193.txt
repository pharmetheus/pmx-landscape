19.4.2. Population PK Analysis
19.4.2.1. Executive Summary
The FDA’s Assessment:

The Applicant submitted a population PK report entitled “Population PK Analysis of Inavolisib in
Patients with Locally Advanced or Metastatic PIK3CA-Mutant Hormone-Receptor Positive
Breast Cancer” to characterize the pharmacokinetics of inavolisib after oral administration in
patients from studies GO39374 and WO41554. The final population PK model was used to
estimate the PK parameters and exposure of inavolisib as a single agent or in combination with
other agents in patients with locally advanced or metastatic PIK3CA-mutated solid tumors and
in patients with locally advanced or metastatic, PIK3CA-mutated breast cancer. The potential
effect of the intrinsic and extrinsic factors on inavolisib were also assessed.

The population PK model for inavolisib was developed based on PK observations from a total of
336 subjects, with 187 patients from Study GO39374 and 149 patients from Study WO41554.
The final population PK model of inavolisib was a three-compartment model with first-order
absorption, linear elimination, and a mixed proportional and additive error model. Effects of
eGFR on CL, body weight and race on Vc and formulation on Ka were included in the final
model. The typical values of CL and V of inavolisib were 8.83 L/h and 155 L, respectively.

eGFR effect on CL, weight and race (Asian) effects on Vc were identified as significant
covariates, with lower CL for subjects with lower eGFR. The rate of absorption was found to be
slower with the phase 1 formulation and the Phase 1 resupply formulation compared to the
phase 3 formulation. No impact from combination therapy (fulvestrant, palbociclib, letrozole)
was identified.

Patients with moderate renal impairment (N=27) showed higher exposure than patients with
normal renal function (N=185) (GMR of steady-state AUCox: 1.42). Patients with mild renal
impairment (N=124) and normal renal function had comparable exposure. No impact on
inavolisib exposure was observed for patients with mild hepatic impairment (N=111) vs normal
hepatic function (N=224).
