NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

The E-R safety analyses indicated that there was an exposure-response relationship between
tarlatamab exposure and neutropenia or neurologic toxicity including ICANS with a higher risk
of any grade neutropenia or neurologic toxicity including ICANS at higher exposure.

Multivariate logistic regression analyses indicated that the higher sum of tumor lesion diameter
at baseline and exposure to prior anti-PD1 treatment were associated with higher probability of
neurologic toxicity including ICANS, while the ECOG status at baseline was associated with
higher probability of neutropenia.

In addition, the observed proportion of patients experiencing neutropenia and neurologic
toxicity including ICANS following a 100 mg target dose, was higher than those observed in 10
mg target dose. This dose-response trend was consistent with the estimated E-R relationship
for safety.

19.4.4.4. ER (safety) Assessment Summary

The Applicant's Position:

No clear trends for exposure-response relationships were identified for grade 2 3 adverse
events, grade 2 3 treatment-related adverse events, and grade 2 3 adverse events of interest of
neurological events, ICANS, and CRS. A shallow trend was observed for higher percentage of
subjects experiencing grade > 3 neutropenia with increasing tarlatamab exposures. No clinically
relevant impact on QTc interval was observed at the proposed clinical regimen of 10 mg Q2W.

The results of the exposure-response analysis suggest that the proposed clinical regimen of

10 mg Q2W reaches the plateau of efficacy with a limited impact on neutropenia and provides
a reasonable balance of benefit-risk in this patient population. None of the covariates evaluated
in this analysis resulted in clinically meaningful changes in the drug’s effect on efficacy and
therefore do not warrant a dose adjustment.
