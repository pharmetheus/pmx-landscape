Based on exposure-safety analysis for study GO39374 using logistic regression analysis, positive
exposure-response relationships were observed for the probability of grade > 3 AE, grade > 2
hyperglycemia, and inavolisib dose modification due to AE increased when AUC or Cmax were
used as exposure metrics.

Based on exposure-safety analysis for study WO41554 using logistic regression analysis, no
exposure-response relationship was observed for most adverse events of interest (grade > 3 AE,
grade 2 2 neutropenia, grade 2 2 hyperglycemia, grade 2 2 thrombocytopenia, inavolisib dose
modification due to AE, grade >=2 diarrhea, and grade >=2 stomatitis). However, the probability
of grade > 2 anemia was higher with increasing inavolisib Cmax values. Similar to the E-R efficacy
analysis, these results should be interpreted with caution given that only one inavolisib dose
level was evaluated.

19.4.3.2. E-R (efficacy) Assessment Summary

The Applicant's Position:
e Based on the exposure-efficacy analysis on a total of 149 patients with model-predicted

exposure parameters in the inavolisib arm from Study WO41554, PFS, ORR, BOR, DOR, CBR,
and time to TFST did not show an association with inavolisib exposure, after adjustment to
baseline covariates (including baseline neutrophil count, AST, liver metastasis, and body
surface area). Although similar OS was seen across Cmin quartiles 1 - 3, numerically shorter
OS was observed for quartile 4. Given that OS data could be confounded by the treatments
received after study treatment discontinuation, and that OS data was not yet mature, TFST
was investigated as another measure of long-term outcome. As outlined above,
exposure-efficacy relationship was not significant for TFST.

Based on the exposure-efficacy analysis on a total of 183 patients (Arm A to F) with
model-predicted exposure parameters from the supportive study GO39374 (Dose range: 3,
6, 9, and 12 mg, with limited data from 3, 6, and 12 mg), ORR, BOR, DOR and PFS were not
associated with inavolisib exposure, after adjustment to baseline covariates. However,
these results should be interpreted with caution given that the data was pooled across
multiple arms of Study GO39374 which include different combination partners across these
arms.
