8.2.3.3. Effect of Sulopenem Etzadroxil/Probenecid on
Valproic Acid PK

Concomitant administration of carbapenem antibacterial drugs and valproic acid has been
associated with clinically significant reductions in serum valproic acid concentrations, resulting
in a loss of seizure control. Because sulopenem (a penem) is structurally similar to carbapenems,
the Applicant conducted a DDI study (Study 104) with valproic acid using the sulopenem
etzadroxil/probenecid bilayer tablet, a standalone sulopenem immediate release tablet, and IV
sulopenem. In this study, multiple dose administration of 500 mg sulopenem etzadroxil with
valproic acid decreased valproic acid AUC and Cy, by approximately 25% and 19%,
respectively. Similarly, coadministration of 1000 mg IV sulopenem with valproic acid also
resulted in reductions of valproic acid plasma AUC and C,,,. of 33% and 28%, respectively.
However, when administered concomitantly with sulopenem etzadroxil and probenecid, less than
10% reduction in valproic acid plasma exposures were observed. These results indicate that
probenecid negates the reduction in valproic acid plasma concentrations caused by sulopenem.
These findings were corroborated in the Applicant’s phase 3 cUTI trial in one patient in the
sulopenem arm who received concomitant valproic acid treatment. Following the IV sulopenem
treatment phase of the study (i.e., Days 1-5), valproic acid plasma/serum levels decreased to
21000 ng/mL (in serum) from a baseline of 54800 ng/mL (in plasma). However, following the
switch to oral step-down therapy with sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid, valproic acid levels
recovered to 50100 ng/mL (in plasma) and 50600 ng/mL (in serum) at the end of treatment visit.

8.3. Plans for Pediatric Drug Development

There are currently no clinical data available on the safety and efficacy of sulopenem in pediatric
patients <18 years old. An Agreed initial Pediatric Study Plan (iPSP) was finalized on February
21, 2018.
