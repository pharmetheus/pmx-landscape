BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — BLA 761232
TEVIMBRA (tislelizumab)

In KEYNOTE-181, although pembrolizumab failed to demonstrate a statistically significant
improvement in OS over chemotherapy in patients with previously treated ESCC (OS HR of
0.78 [95% CI: 0.63 to 0.96], p = 0.0095; median OS: 8.2 months versus 7.1 months), it showed
an improvement in OS compared with chemotherapy (OS HR of 0.64 [95% CI: 0.46 to 0.90];
median OS: 10.3 months versus 6.7 months) in patients with previously treated ESCC with
tumor Combined Positive Score (CPS) > 10 (Kojima et al 2020).

Both pembrolizumab and nivolumab were approved for use in advanced ESCC based on the
above Phase 3 study results. In July 2019, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
approved pembrolizumab for patients with recurrent, locally advanced or metastatic, ESCC
whose tumors express programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) CPS =>10 with disease progression
after one or more prior lines of systemic therapy. In June 2020, the US FDA approved
nivolumab for patients with unresectable advanced, recurrent or metastatic ESCC after prior
fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-based chemotherapy.

The survival benefit of cytotoxic chemotherapies, as standard of care second-line palliative
systemic treatment for recurrent advanced or metastatic ESCC, is marginal. Moreover, they are
associated with significant toxicity with substantial morbidity and mortality for patients and
frequent treatment interruptions, delays, and dose reductions that increases the risk of disease
progression. More recently, immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 axis have
further shaped the treatment landscape for patients with ESCC. PD-1 inhibitors, including
tislelizumab, have demonstrated survival improvement over chemotherapy in advanced or
metastatic ESCC patients with disease progression after prior systemic therapies.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The major efficacy outcome measure in KEYNOTE-181 was OS evaluated in patients with
ESCC, patients with tumors expressing PD-L1 CPS >10, and all randomized patients. The
observed OS hazard ratio was 0.77 (95% CI: 0.63, 0.96) in patients with ESCC (not 0.78 as
stated above), 0.70 (95% CI: 0.52, 0.94) in patients with tumors expressing PD-L1 CPS >10, and
0.89 (95% CI: 0.75, 1.05) in all randomized patients (Keytruda UPSI). Based on the results in
patients whose ESCC tumors expressed PD-L1 (CPS =10), an improvement in OS was observed
among patients randomized to pembrolizumab as compared with chemotherapy with an observed
OS hazard ratio of 0.64 (95% CI: 0.46, 0.90).

Per the current USPI, pembrolizumab is approved, in combination with platinum and
fluoropyrimidine for the treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic esophageal or
GE] (tumors with epicenter 1 to 5 centimeters above the GEJ) carcinoma that is not amendable
to surgical resection or definitive chemoradiation, and as a single agent after one or more prior
lines of systemic therapy for patients with tumors of squamous cell histology that express PD-L1
(CPS =10) as determined by an FDA-approved test. Nivolumab is approved for unresectable
advanced, recurrent or metastatic ESCC after prior fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-based.
