5.5.2. Genetic Toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Inavolisib was considered not mutagenic in S. typhimurium strains
(TA98, TA100, TA1535, and TA1537) or E. coli (WP2 uvrA) in the absence or presence of an
exogenous metabolic activation system (S-9) (GLP Studies 15-3356 and 16-1286/4.2.3.3.1 In
Vitro Genotoxicity).

Inavolisib induced micronuclei in vitro in human peripheral blood lymphocytes (via a clastogenic
mechanism) at concentrations > 180 jig/mL in the absence of a rat liver metabolic activation
system (S-9) when tested for 24 + 24 hours (incubation plus recovery), with the no-effect level
between 120 and 180 j1g/mL. Inavolisib did not induce micronuclei when tested up to the limit
of solubility (350 t1g/mL) for 3 + 21 hours in the absence or presence of S-9 (GLP Study
15-3357/4.2.3.3.1 In Vitro Genotoxicity).

There was no evidence of inavolisib-induced clastogenicity or aneugenicity in polychromatic
erythrocytes of the bone marrow, or DNA damage in the liver of female Sprague-Dawley rats
(which showed higher exposure than males) up to the MTD of 40 mg/kg, with a corresponding
Day 1 Cmax of 1740 ng/mL and area under the concentration-time curve from 0 to 24 hours
(AUCo-24) of 17,100 ng hr/mL (“16.1% clinical exposure at 9 mg) (GLP Study 16-0989/4.2.3.3.1 In
Vivo Genotoxicity).
