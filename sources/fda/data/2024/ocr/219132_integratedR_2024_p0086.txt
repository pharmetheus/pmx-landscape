Trial IB1001-201 included a 6-week initial treatment period with a rater-blinded primary
endpoint, followed by continued open-label treatment with levacetylleucine for up to a total of
2 years (with a 6-week washout period between years 1 and 2 of additional open-label
treatment). The primary endpoint for the EP of Trial IB1001-201 was also the change in
SDNPCCSS from baseline to the EOT. A total of 19 subjects enrolled in the EP and 13 subjects
completed 2 additional years of treatment. After 12 months of treatment with levacetylleucine
from the start of the EP, the mean change on the SDNPCCSS for those with available
assessments at baseline (Visit 7) and Visit 10 was 0.88 (SD=2.60) based on 17 subjects. After 2
years of EP (Visit 7 versus Visit 12), the mean change on the SDNPCCSS was 1.75 (SD=2.56)
based on 12 subjects.

As detailed in Section 6.2.1, there was a high drop-out rate for the trial that was likely impacted
by the COVID-19 pandemic and associated health concems as well as travel and activity
restrictions, thus the results of the EP are difficult to interpret. Additionally, because the
neurological symptoms of NPC are expected to progress, a worsening in 5DNPCCSS scores over
the course of 2 years is not unexpected; however, without a concurrent control group for
comparison, we could not make definitive conclusions regarding the results of the EP of Trial
1B1001-201.

Conclusion

The 12 weeks of placebo-controlled data from Trial IB1001-301 followed by high-level
summary data from the EP suggesting continued mean improvement and/or relative stability in
neurological symptoms after 1 year provide reasonable scientific justification for the long-term
use of levacetylleucine. Although some uncertainty remains regarding the long-term efficacy
(greater than one year) of levacetylleucine, the data are adequate to support a labeled indication
for chronic therapy.
