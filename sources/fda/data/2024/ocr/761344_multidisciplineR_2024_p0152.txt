NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

The Applicant's Position:

The observed ORR in the pivotal study, which was rapid and durable, represents a clinically
meaningful benefit when considering the intended patient population with life-threatening
disease and limited available therapies (Section 2).

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA considers the ORR endpoint by BICR as the primary endpoint, while DoR is a supportive
secondary endpoint. Note that analysis of DCR and proportion of tumor shrinkage is generally
considered exploratory since the DCR is not a well-established measure of clinical benefit.

FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s summary of the efficacy results above since the results were
updated from re-adjudication of previous target lesion assessments and none of the 25
previously unsubmitted scans submitted for BICR factored into any update of response (see
Section 4.1 for details). In the updated data submitted to FDA on March 15, 2024, by the
Applicant, the best overall response of patient OO was changed from stable disease
to partial response, and the ORR by BICR was consequently updated from 40% to 41%. In the
FDA’s analyses, the number of responders by BICR in the 10 mg target dose group in Parts 1 and
2 remains 40, and the ORR by BICR was 40% (95% Cl: 31%, 51%). Among the 40 responders in
the 10 mg target dose group in Parts 1 and 2, the median DoR was not reached at the data
cutoff date of October 2, 2023 (90-day update). To better characterize the DoR, the data was
summarized with additional follow-up time, as requested by FDA. At the latest data cutoff date
of January 12, 2024, the median DoR was 9.7 months (95% Cl: 6.9, NE and range: 2.7, 20.7+);
and 68% and 40% of the responders had an observed DoR 26 months and 212 months,
respectively. The median follow-up time for DoR was 15.1 months (estimated using the reverse
Kaplan-Meier Method). See Table 34 for the FDA’s summary of the efficacy results.
