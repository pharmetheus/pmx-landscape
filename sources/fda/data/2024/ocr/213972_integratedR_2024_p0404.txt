NDA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {NDA 213972}
{sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid}

Results

All formulations of sulopenem were well-tolerated. Of the 46 subjects enrolled in the study, 2
subjects discontinued the study (for reasons unrelated to AEs). In the fasted state, 2 subjects
(14.3%) in the bilayer tablet cohort and 7 (35.0%) in the PIB cohort experienced TEAEs. Only 1
of these AEs were gastrointestinal (GI) in nature; all others were characterized as abnormal
urine odor. In the fed state, only 1 subject receiving sulopenem etzadroxil reported TEAE, which
again was abnormal urine odor.

The absolute bioavailability of sulopenem for the PIB formulation was 31% in the fasted state
and 38% in the fed state. Absolute bioavailability for the bilayer tablet (i.e., sulopenem
etzadroxil in combination with probenecid) was 40%, as shown in Table 127. During Period 2, a
subject in Cohort 1 registered unusually low plasma concentrations of sulopenem and
probenecid in the fed state in spite of having adequate concentrations of both drugs in the
fasted state. No definitive explanation for this observation could be provided, except for
potential subject non-compliance. Therefore, this subject was re-dosed in the fed state. When
PK data from redosing this subject in the fed state was included in analyses, it was determined
that a high fat meal increased the mean absolute bioavailability of sulopenem for the bilayer
tablet to 64.4% (Table 128). Sensitivity analysis conducted by exclusion of data from this subject
resulted in a similar estimated bioavailability (i.e. 64.6%) in the fed state.
