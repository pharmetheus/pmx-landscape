NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {NDA 219249}
{Inavolisib}

6.1. Executive Summary

The FDA’s Assessment:

The Applicant is seeking approval of inavolisib ]

The proposed recommended dosage is inavolisib 9 mg orally (PO) once daily (QD) in
combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant at their approved recommended dosages.

The clinical pharmacology review addressed the following three key review issues:

Dosage Acceptability: Although the clinical pharmacology review concluded that the proposed
dosage is approvable from the clinical pharmacology perspective, the proposed dosage of
inavolisib 9 mg PO QD may not be optimized due to 1) high rates of adverse events (AEs) and
dose modifications, 2) positive exposure-response (E-R) relationships for safety, 3) limited
clinical data at lower dosages, and 4) lack of E-R relationships for efficacy in patients who
received the proposed dosage in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant.

Intrinsic Factors: Dosage reduction is recommended for patients with moderate renal
impairment. A postmarketing requirement (PMR) will be issued for the complete study report
(CSR) and datasets for the ongoing renal impairment study to include evaluation of severe renal
impairment. FDA cannot rule out >20% contribution of hepatic elimination; therefore, a PMR
will be issued for a dedicated hepatic impairment study. Lastly, there was underrepresentation
of patients from diverse racial and ethnic groups including Black/African American or
Hispanic/Latino patients in the inavolisib development program. A postmarketing commitment
(PMC) to evaluate inavolisib in a diverse patient population representative of the US population
will include collection of clinical pharmacology data to assess potential impacts of race and
ethnicity on inavolisib pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD).

DDI Potential: There is low risk for CYP-based DDIs for inavolisib as an object given the lack of
CYP enzyme metabolism. However, in vitro studies showed that inavolisib is a time-dependent
inhibitor of CYP3A and inavolisib induces CYP3A and CYP2B6. PMCs will be issued to evaluate
inavolisib clinical induction potential on CYP3A and CYP2B6. Depending on the results of the
CYP3A study, additional studies may be warranted to evaluate the potential for inavolisib to
induce CYP2C enzymes and interaction with combined oral contraceptives (COC).
