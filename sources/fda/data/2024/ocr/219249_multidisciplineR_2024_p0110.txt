FDA queried the Applicant about subgroup results for the North America/West Europe (NA/WE)
patient population. In an Information Request Response dated May 20, 2024, the Applicant
stated that they “observed a relatively large extent of imbalances in some baseline
characteristics with known prognostic impact between the two arms in the NA/WE subgroup
compared to the FAS population, which may negatively impact the performance of the
inavolisib arm relative to the placebo arm in this subgroup and potentially explain the
numerically larger hazard ratio for PFS.” These included a lower proportion of patients with 1
organ site (lower overall disease burden) in the inavolisib arm compared with the placebo arm
(8.1% vs. 25.4%) in the NA/WE subgroup, which would be considered a better prognostic factor
compared to >1 organ site involvement. In addition, they noted a higher proportion of patients
with liver metastases (56.5% vs 46%) and progesterone receptor (PR)-negative disease (30.6%
vs 20.6%) in the inavolisib arm compared with the placebo arm in the NA/WE subgroup,
respectively. Both the presence of live metastases and PR negative disease may be poor
prognostic factors. The Applicant stated the difference in OS results for NA/WE subgroup when
compared to other subgroups could also be attributed to these same imbalances noted for the
PFS results and due to the limited number of OS events. FDA agrees that these imbalances may
have impacted the subgroup results but again notes these subgroup analyses should be
interpreted with caution.
