19.4.4. Physiologically Based Pharmacokinetic Modeling Review
19.4.4.1. Executive Summary

The FDA’s Assessment: The objective of this review is to evaluate the adequacy of the
Applicant’s physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) analyses to evaluate the drug-drug
interaction (DDI) potential of inavolisib as a precipitant of the CYP3A substrate midazolam.

The Division of Pharmacometrics has reviewed the PBPK report (1129287) and related model
summary reports, response to FDA information request dated 15 July 2024 (seq0027), and the
modeling supporting files, and concluded that PBPK analysis cannot rule out the induction
potential of inavolisib on the sensitive CYP3A substrate midazolam. The magnitude of inavolisib
induction on the exposure of substrates of CYP3A cannot be confidently predicted due to the
uncertainties about the induction parameters and methodology.

19.4.4.2. Methods

The FDA’s Assessment: All simulations were performed using the PK/PD Profiles mode in the
Simcyp® Simulator (Versions 22, Certara, Sheffield, UK) with the healthy subject population
(sim-Healthy Volunteers) model. Age, sex, subject number, and dosing regimens were
consistent with the actual trial design. Ten trials were simulated for each simulation scenario.
Schemes of the PBPK modeling and simulation strategy are shown in Figure 16, which
summarizes the studies used for inavolisib model development and verification, and model
applications in predicting DDI of inavolisib as a precipitant of CYP3A4 induction or inhibition.
The final model input parameters were summarized in Table 50. The inavolisib PBPK model
consists of an Advanced Dissolution, Absorption and Metabolism (ADAM) model for describing
drug absorption in each gut segment, a full PBPK model (method 3) for distribution, and an
enzyme kinetics model and renal clearance for elimination.

The Simcyp library models of midazolam, triazolam, itraconazole, rifampin and erythromycin
were used without any modification, except for CYP3A4 TDI parameters for erythromycin (see
Results, section 2).
