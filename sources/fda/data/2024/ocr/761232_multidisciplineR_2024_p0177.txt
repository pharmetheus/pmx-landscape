conclusions, the review team agrees that the study results are applicable to the US population.
Based on FDA Clinical Pharmacology analyses, no clinically meaningful difference in PK of
tislelizumab were found for in patients based on race (Asian, White, and Black); a flat
relationship between exposure and efficacy and safety was observed.

Another review issue considered was whether labeling would inform of the results of the key
secondary endpoint of OS in patients with PD-L1 expression > 10% and whether the indication
should be restricted based on tumor PD-L1 expression subgroups. The primary endpoint was OS
in the ITT population but the key secondary endpoint was OS in the vCPS > 10% population. A
survival benefit from tislelizumab was observed in both populations with a HR for OS 0.70 in
the ITT population and 0.53 in the vCPS > 10% population. However, approximately 19% of
patients in Study 302 had missing vCPS information and the Applicant did not submit data
supporting a 10% vCPS cutoff versus other cutoffs in patients with ESCC with disease
progression after first-line treatment. In addition, the subset of patients with PD-L1 expression <
1% was too small to derive conclusions. Thus, the indication recommended for approval is based
on the ITT population.
