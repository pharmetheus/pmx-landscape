BLA 761232 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
TEVIMBRA - tislelizumab-jsgr

The FDA’s Assessment:

Overall, the routine clinical laboratory tests and physical exam elements that were performed in
Study 302 are considered adequate for the evaluation of safety of tislelizumab in the target
patient population.

8.2.6. Safety Results
Deaths

Data:

Deaths due to adverse events (not due to ESCC) were reported with similar incidence in both
arms (5.5% [14 patients] in the Tislelizumab Arm versus 5.8% [14 patients] in the ICC Arm)
(Table 28).

Table 27 provides a summary of all fatal adverse events as documented on the Study 302 adverse
event case report form, including events in 21 patients in the tislelizumab arm for whom “disease
under study” (ie, ESCC) was reported as the primary cause of death. A review of the events
indicates no clustering of event types, except for effects typically associated with ESCC disease
progression. None of the deaths was associated with an immune-associated adverse event or an
infusion related-reaction. Three patients (1.2%) in the Tislelizumab Arm and 1 patient (0.4%) in
the ICC Amn died of ‘multiple organ dysfunction syndrome’. These 3 multi-organ events in the
Tislelizumab Arm were considered by the Investigator to be due to the underlying ESCC and
were unrelated to study drug, whereas the multi-organ failure in the ICC Arm was considered
related to study drug (irinotecan). Deaths reported as due to “indeterminate” cause (7.1% in each
treatment arm) or “other” (2.7% Tislelizamab Arm; 2.9% ICC Arm) occurred with an onset of

> 30 days after the last dose of study drug (ie, not treatment-emergent). Although the exact
causes of deaths remain unclear, they were not reported by the Investigator to be due to adverse
events and are presumed likely therefore to be disease related or of unknown etiology.

To account for the difference between the treatment arms in duration of exposure, an exposure-
adjusted assessment of adverse events leading to death was conducted, which showed a notably
lower rate in the Tislelizumab Arm (2.8 deaths per 100 person-months) compared with the ICC
Arm (4.7 deaths per 100 person-months).

An assessment of deaths was conducted by geographic region and by baseline PD-L1 expression
status. The overall trends and reasons for death in these subgroups were similar to those of the
overall population for both the Tislelizumab and ICC Arms.
