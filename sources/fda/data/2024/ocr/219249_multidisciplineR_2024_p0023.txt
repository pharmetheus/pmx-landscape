ITOVEBI {inavolisib}

1.3. Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA)

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Breast cancer is the second most common cancer in women worldwide after skin cancer. In the U.S., 310,720 new cases of breast cancer and
42,780 deaths from breast cancer are estimated for 2024. Histopathological subtypes of breast cancer are defined by expression of the
estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and/or human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). Approximately 70% of patients
with breast cancer will have hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-negative disease. Although associated with a better prognosis than the
other breast cancer subtypes, if HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer is advanced or metastatic, it is incurable and associated with a
limited life expectancy.

Endocrine therapy is the mainstay for HR-positive breast cancer. However, resistance may occur to endocrine therapy and a major challenge to
treating HR-positive breast cancer is overcoming endocrine resistance. Activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway may lead to endocrine resistance.
Endocrine resistant breast cancer is not uniformly defined but generally refers to cancer that grows in the presence of endocrine therapy (ET)
or soon after completing ET. Endocrine resistant breast cancer is considered to be a negative prognostic factor as these patients tend to have
worse survival outcomes compared to patients with endocrine sensitive breast cancer.

While there are no specific therapies approved by FDA for treatment of patients with ET resistant HR-positive advanced or metastatic breast
cancer, generally the treatment options after first line therapy can be considered for this population.

The preferred first-line treatment for patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer in the U.S. is a CDK4/6
inhibitor in combination with endocrine therapy (an aromatase inhibitor [Al] or fulvestrant). At disease progression, FDA-approved options for
second and later-line treatment include endocrine monotherapy with fulvestrant, an Al, or tamoxifen (if not received as first-line treatment);
everolimus in combination with exemestane; alpelisib in combination with fulvestrant in patients with a PIK3CA mutated tumor; capivasertib in
combination with fulvestrant for patients with tumors that have a PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alteration, and chemotherapy such as capecitabine.
Sequential endocrine based therapy is preferred because it is typically associated with less toxicity than chemotherapy. Endocrine
monotherapy is also associated with less toxicity than combinations with targeted agents. In addition to these therapies, the antibody-drug-
conjugate, fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan, is now available for patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-low (IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH-) breast
cancer who have received prior chemotherapy in the metastatic setting or developed disease recurrence during or within 6 months of
