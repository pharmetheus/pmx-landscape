Stomatitis occurred at higher rates in the inavolisib treatment arm (51%) compared to the
placebo arm (27%), including 6% of patients on the inavolisib arm having grade 3 events. There
were no grade 4-5 events or SAEs. Stomatitis was managed with dose modifications and
required permanent discontinuation in one patient. For patients treated with inavolisib, 38%
used a mouthwash containing corticosteroid for management or prophylaxis of stomatitis.
Patients should be monitored for stomatitis and have treatment instituted as indicated.
Stomatitis is labeled in the warnings and precautions section of the USPI.

Diarrhea occurred at higher rates in the inavolisib treatment arm (48%) compared to the
placebo arm (16%), including 4% of patients on the inavolisib arm having grade 3 events. There
were two SAEs and no grade 4-5 events or colitis. Diarrhea was managed with dose
modifications and no patients required treatment discontinuation due to diarrhea. Anti-
diarrheal medicines were used in 28% of patients treated with inavolisib to manage symptoms.

Rash occurred in 26% of patients treated with inavolisib compared to 19% of patients on the
placebo arm. There were no grade 3-5 events or SAEs. Few patients needed dose modifications
due to rash (3 patients had dose interruption and one patient had dose reduction) and no
patients required treatment discontinuation.

Ocular toxicity occurred in 22% of patients treated with inavolisib compared to 13% on the
placebo arm. The most common toxicity was dry eye (9%). There were no grade 3-5 events, no
SAEs and ocular toxicity did not lead to dose modifications (discontinuation, reduction, or
interruption).

Hematologic toxicities were seen in both treatment arms and are known toxicities with
palbociclib. Neutropenia and anemia were seen in a similar proportion of patients regarding all
grade toxicity. However, grade 3-4 toxicity was slightly higher in the inavolisib treatment arm.
Thrombocytopenia (including platelet count decreased) was also higher in the inavolisib
treatment arm compared to the placebo treatment arm.
