Hyperglycemia occurred at much higher rates on the inavolisib treatment arm (85%) compared to the placebo arm (43%). There were no grade
4-5 cases of hyperglycemia or cases of DKA and HHNK. However, patients that enrolled on the trial were required to fasting blood glucose (FBG)
<126 and HBA1c<6%. Stomatitis occurred at higher rates in the inavolisib treatment arm (51%) compared to the placebo arm (27%), including
6% of patients on the inavolisib arm having grade 3 events. There were no grade 4-5 events or SAEs. Diarrhea occurred at higher rates in the
inavolisib treatment arm (48%) compared to the placebo arm (16%), including 4% of patients on the inavolisib arm having grade 3 events.
There were two SAEs and no grade 4-5 events or colitis. Rash occurred in 27% of patients treated with inavolisib compared to 19% of patients
on the placebo arm. There were no grade 3-5 events or SAEs. Ocular toxicity occurred in 22% of patients treated with inavolisib compared to
13% on the placebo arm. The most common toxicity was dry eye (9%). There were no grade 3-5 events, no SAEs and ocular toxicity did not lead
to dose modifications (discontinuation, reduction, or interruption).

Hematologic toxicities were seen in both treatment arms and are known toxicities with palbociclib. Neutropenia and anemia were seen ina
similar proportion of patients regarding all grade toxicity; however, grade 3-4 toxicity was slightly higher in the inavolisib treatment arm.
Thrombocytopenia (including platelet count decreased) was higher in the inavolisib treatment arm compared to the placebo treatment arm.
Other adverse events (including laboratory abnormalities) noted in >20% of patients treated with inavolisib + palbociclib + fulvestrant include
decreased calcium, fatigue, decreased potassium, increased creatinine, increased ALT, nausea, decreased sodium, decreased magnesium,
decreased albumin, decreased appetite, Covid-19, and headache.

In addition, the Applicant evaluated expected symptomatic adverse events using patient-reported outcomes. Symptomatic adverse events
related to likely toxicity from the combination of inavolisib, palbociclib, and fulvestrant (diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, mouth sores,
decreased appetite, and rash), were selected and assessed from the patient reported outcome (PRO)-CTCAE item library, along with an
additional single item assessing bother due to side-effects of treatment. In general, these patient-reported symptoms were observed to be
worse in the inavolisib arm, with the greatest difference observed for diarrhea, mouth sores, decreased appetite, and rash. Although the PRO
data collection was considered exploratory, these symptoms were considered supportive of the clinician reported safety information and
informative for labeling and were included in Section 6 of the USPI.

meaningful improvement in PFS for the patient population enrolled on INAVO120. Results for secondary endpoints were supportive of the
