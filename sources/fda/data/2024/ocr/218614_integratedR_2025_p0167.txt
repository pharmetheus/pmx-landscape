Consultation with the Division of Hepatology and Nutrition (DHN) and the Division of
Applied Regulatory Science (DARS

Consultation with DHN and subsequently DARS was sought to further explore these hepatic
transaminase increases. DARS concluded that there was (1) no suggestion of olezarsen on-target
or non-specific off-target sequence effects on mRNAs in the liver that may be related to
hepatic/hepatobiliary dysfunction and toxicity, (2) the olezarsen O® tinker is
unlikely to be directly associated with hepatobiliary injury, (3) clinical data has shown that
APOC3 inhibition by volanesorsen resulted in reduced hepatic fat fraction (HFF) in patients with
diverse etiologies of severe hypertriglyceridemia (hypertriglyceridemia, familial partial
lipodystrophy, and FCS), and (4) there is no evidence linking high uptake of GaINAc-
conjugated-ASOs and slow/delayed release from hepatic endosomes with changes in liver
function/injury or whether ASO on-target effects on the lipodome can influence endosomal
function or endosomal-lysozyme systems in hepatic cells.

However, the relationship between APOC3 and liver steatosis or metabolic dysfunction-
associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is conflicting across different studies. Information on
the impact of chronic administration of olezarsen in subjects with impaired liver function is not
available and has not been assessed for volanesorsen (which is an approved FCS therapy outside
of the US). DARS suggests that because olezarsen and volanesorsen differ only in the |
GalNAc3-conjugate, an exploratory comparison of these ASOs in a suitable in vitro cell system
(e.g., hepatic microphysiological systems, 3D-spheroids, and/or organ-on-chip approaches) may
provide further information on the relationship between potential liver changes or injury and
endosomal trafficking and release, and reduced APOC3-mediated changes in the transcriptome
and lipodome following ASO degradation. The review team is considering this as a possible post
marketing commitment.

The DHN consult, in DARRTS dated December 16, 2024, concludes that the concerning
adverse events of hepatic failure or cirrhosis that occurred in FCS patients do not appear to be
directly attributable to olezarsen. However, the consequence of lifetime treatment with olezarsen
in FCS is unknown, particularly in those patients with underlying liver disease. DHN
recommends considering a PMR or PMC assessing the potential for drug induced liver injury
with Tryngolza with evaluation including baseline and periodic laboratory tests and hepatic
imaging over 3 to 5 years. Description of these modest aminotransferase elevations should be
included in labelling.
