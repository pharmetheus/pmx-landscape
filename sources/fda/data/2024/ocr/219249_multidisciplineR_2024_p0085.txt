NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {NDA 219249}
{Inavolisib}

Based on FDA’s QT-IRT analysis of the clinical data from the Phase 1 Study GO39374, inavolisib
is unlikely to cause 220 msec mean increase in QTc interval at the proposed recommended
dosage of 9 mg PO QD. Refer to the QT-IRT review (dated 8/1/2024) for details.

Pharmacogenomics:

FDA’s pharmacogenomics analysis suggested inavolisib plus palbociclib and fulvestrant
treatment is effective for patients regardless of PIK3CA mutation status. See Section 6.3.2.3 for
details.

6.3.2. Clinical Pharmacology Questions

6.3.2.1. Does the clinical pharmacology program provide supportive
evidence of effectiveness?

Data and the Applicant’s Position: Yes, please refer to Section 6.2.2.1.

The FDA’s Assessment:

Yes. The pivotal evidence of effectiveness is the improvement in PFS for patients who received
inavolisib + palbociclib + fulvestrant compared with patients who received placebo + palbociclib
+ fulvestrant in Study WO51554 (refer to Section 8.1.2).

There were no apparent exposure-response (E-R) relationships for efficacy endpoints (PFS,
DOR, TFST, CBR, ORR, and BOR) identified; however, this finding should be interpreted with
caution because the proposed triplet combination (inavolisib + palbociclib + fulvestrant) was
only studied at one dose level of inavolisib (9 mg PO QD). See Section 19.4.3.2 for details on the
E-R efficacy analysis.

6.3.2.2. Is the proposed dosing regimen appropriate for the general
patient population for which the indication is being sought?

Data and the Applicant's Position: Yes, please refer to Section 6.2.2.1.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA agrees that inavolisib 9 mg PO QD is an effective dosage based on improved PFS for
inavolisib 9 mg PO QD + palbociclib + fulvestrant compared with placebo + palbociclib +
fulvestrant in patients with HR+, HER2- locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer in Study
WO051554. However, it is not clear whether the proposed dosage is optimized due to:

1. High rates of AEs and dosage modification

2. Positive E-R relationships for safety

3. Limited clinical data at lower dosages
