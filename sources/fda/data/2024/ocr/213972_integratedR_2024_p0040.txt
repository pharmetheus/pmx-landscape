sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid
5.1.1. Summary of Nonclinical
Pharmacokinetic/ Pharmacodynamic Information

Dose fractionation studies were conducted in the murine thigh and in vitro 1-compartment
models to determine the PK/PD index that best correlates with sulopenem’s activity. Although
initial findings from the murine thigh infection model suggested that sulopenem exhibits
elements of both time and concentration-dependent killing, results were largely uninterpretable
due to concerns with study design, data integrity and the Applicant’s atypical analysis of the
ensuing results (see Section 14). Subsequent in vitro studies corroborated previous findings that
sulopenem does indeed display both time and concentration-dependent activity with coefficient
of determination (1?) values of 0.89 and 0.90 for free sulopenem peak concentration to minimum
inhibitory concentration (MIC) free-drug maximum plasma concentration(/Cmax)/MIC and the
percentage of the dosing interval that free-drug concentrations exceed the MIC (%fT>MIC),
respectively. Given that traditionally, %fI>MIC is the index that best correlates with the activity
of beta-lactams, the Applicant’s eventual use of %fI>MIC to characterize sulopenem’s activity
is acceptable.

Similar to the dose fractionation studies, concerns with the Applicant’s initial approach for
PK/PD target determination in the murine thigh infection model rendered the results unreliable
(see Section 14). Subsequent PK/PD target determination studies were conducted in the in vitro
1-compartment model using a panel of 10 Enterobacterales isolates with sulopenem MICs
ranging from 0.03 to 0.5 g/mL. In this model, the magnitude of median targets for stasis, 1-log
kill, and 2-log kill were 40.9, 50.2 and 62.6% /T>MIC, respectively.

Additionally, the Applicant evaluated the bactericidal activity of sulopenem urinary
concentrations associated with the proposed 500 mg sulopenem etzadroxil/500 mg probenecid
dosage for the treatment of uUTI. The simulated urinary concentrations suppressed the growth of
4 E. coli isolates (with sulopenem MICs ranging from 0.03 to 0.5 jg/mL) and prevented the
development of resistance for the entire duration of a 5-day study in a hollow-fiber infection
model.
