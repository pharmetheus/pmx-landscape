BLA 761232 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
TEVIMBRA - tislelizumab-jsgr

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS

8.3. Statistical Issues
The FDA’s Assessment:
No major statistical issues were identified during review of the data and results submitted in this
application. However, FDA notes that there are some uncertainties in evaluating the efficacy
results for PDL1 subgroups, including the key secondary endpoint of OS in patients with PD-L1
vCPS > 10%, due to missing PDLI status of 19% of the patients in the ITT population. FDA
further notes that only ~10% of the patients in the ITT population had vCPS <1% compared to
~71% of the patients with vCPS >1%.

8.4. Conclusions and Recommendations
The FDA’s Assessment:

Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness (SEE) was established with one adequate and well-
controlled clinical investigation with highly persuasive results that is considered to be the
scientific equivalent of two clinical investigations.

Based on the totality of evidence, the product quality, clinical pharmacology, clinical, and
statistical review teams determined that the evidence submitted provides substantial evidence of
the effectiveness of tislelizumab for the treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic
ESCC after prior systemic therapy that does not include an anti PD-(L)1 inhibitor and
recommends approval. The recommended dosage regimen for this indication is 200 mg IV once
every 3 weeks.

The data presented in this BLA demonstrating a modest but statistically significant improvement
in OS in patients with ESCC was derived from RATIONALE-302, a multicenter, randomized,
open-label, active-controlled trial. Patients were randomized to receive tislelizumab or
investigator’s choice of cytotoxic chemotherapy (paclitaxel, docetaxel, or irinotecan). The
median OS was 8.6 months [95% CI: 7.5, 10.4] in the tislelizumab arm compared to 6.3 months
[95% CI: 5.3, 7.0] in the ICC arm with a stratified hazard ratio of 0.70 [95% CI: 0.57,0.85, p=
0.0001]. Tislelizumab was generally well tolerated and fewer adverse events were reported than
in patients receiving chemotherapy. Immune-related adverse events, characteristic of anti-
PD1(L1) drugs, including hypothyroidism, pneumonitis, and rash, occurred in approximately
20% of patients receiving tislelizumab. The adverse reaction profile observed in patients
receiving tislelizumab in RATIONALE-302 was consistent with the known tislelizumab profile
in different studies across indications and the pharmacologic class. These adverse reactions were
generally manageable with dose delays and supportive care in most patients, although at least
one fatal event was reported.
