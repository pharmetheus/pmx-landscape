Conclusions and Reasons

MC is clinically diagnosed, but it can be confirmed via biopsy or
microscopic examination of a crush preparation of a lesion. Lesions may
occur anywhere on the body except the palms and soles and are
commonly seen on the trunk, axillae, antecubital and popliteal fossae, and
crural folds. The infection usually resolves spontaneously, but clearance
can take anywhere from 6 months to 4 years.

YCANTH (cantharidin) is the only FDA-approved drug products for the
treatment of MC. Various modalities have been used to treat MC,
including mechanical/chemical destruction, topical or intralesional
injections of immune-modulators, and antiviral drugs.

Depending on the chosen therapy, treatment can be time consuming or
can result in pain, irritation, dyspigmentation, or scarring. Robust
evidence for the efficacy and safety of these treatments is lacking.
Although MC is a self-limiting disease health care providers (HCPs)
recommend treatment of lesions to prevent disease transmission and
spread by autoinoculation.*
