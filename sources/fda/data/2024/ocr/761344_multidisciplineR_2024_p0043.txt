NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

4 Significant Issues from Other Review Disciplines Pertinent to Clinical
Conclusions on Efficacy and Safety

4.1. Office of Scientific Investigations (OSI)

An OSI audit was conducted for this BLA. The review division (DO2) and OSI selected three
investigator sites, and the imaging contract research organization (CRO) © For clinical
inspection. The three investigators selected were Dr. Byoung Chul Cho (Site ID #25054), Dr.
Myung-Ju Ahn (Site ID #66264) and Dr. Enrique Felip Font (Site ID #29404).

The inspection of Dr. Ahn revealed protocol violations, including enrollment of two patients
who did not meet eligibility criteria and underreporting of adverse events (AEs). Specifically,
Patient © had anew diagnosis of adenocarcinoma and should not have been
enrolled in the study, as the patient did not fulfill inclusion criterion to have “histologically or
cytologically confirmed relapse/refractory SCLC.” This patient received the tarlatamab 100 mg
dose. Patient © had abdominal aneurysm with thrombosis and should have been
excluded from study based on the exclusion criteria. Based on the available information, this
protocol deviation did not appear to have impact on this patient’s safety.

Inspection of Dr. Ahn’s site also revealed underreporting of AEs in 14 out of 19 patients enrolled
at the site. A total of 61 AEs including three Grade 3 AEs and two Grade 5 AEs (death due to
underlying disease) were not reported to the BLA in the Applicant's data listings. The root cause
of the violations was due to lack of human resources at the site as well as failure in the
Applicant’s monitoring activities. On January 31, 2024, OSI, in conjunction with the review
Division, requested that the Applicant provide a root-cause and impact analysis for the
eligibility and AE discrepancies at Dr. Ahn’s site, and to perform a source record review of
subject’s eligibility and adverse events for all subjects enrolled at all 5 sites in South Korea.
Based on the Applicant’s source record review of the Korean sites, an additional 42 unreported
AEs at 3 of the 4 remaining sites and involving 12 patients were identified.

Root cause analysis showed the following factors that caused inadequate AE reporting by the
site: 1) lack of Good Documentation Practices for adverse events tracking — study coordinators
entered adverse events information on an undisclosed source document, 2) Lack of electronic
medical record (EMR) access by study coordinators - study coordinators could not enter
adverse events into EMR, 3) Undisclosed source documents to the clinical research associate
(CRA) - source document was not disclosed to the CRA when inquiring about potential adverse
events under-reporting and 4) Under-resourcing of study coordinators at site during high
enrollment periods. Site 66264 remains on hold in the Applicant’s clinical trials until the
Applicant is satisfied that the robustness of the site’s processes will prevent recurrence of GCP
inspection findings identified.
