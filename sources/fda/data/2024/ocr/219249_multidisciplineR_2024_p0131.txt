The FDA’s Assessment:

In general, FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of deaths in the INAVO120 trial
(W0O41554). FDA agrees that the most common cause for death on either treatment arm was
progressive disease.

The death narratives were not reviewed for GO39374.

Following are a summary of the 8 deaths (6 on the inavolisib treatment arm and 2 on the
placebo arm) due to an adverse event in the INAVO120 Trial (WO41554). The FDA included
these deaths in the USPI as contribution of inavolisib cannot be ruled out for the 6 deaths
(3.7%) that occurred on the inavolisib + palbociclib + fulvestrant arm.

(b) 6)

Patient ID# --Applicant Attribution: Cerebral hemorrhage

60-year-old White female randomized to the inavolisib treatment arm was diagnosed with
Grade 3 acute coronary syndrome (ACS) on Study Day 493. On Study Day 499, the event of ACS
was considered resolved with sequelae (unspecified). There was no change in study treatment
due to ACS. On Study Day 500, the patient was diagnosed with Grade 4 cerebral hemorrhage.
No treatment was given for the event and the patient died on Study Day 506 due to cerebral
hemorrhage. The patient also had six episodes of Grade 2 hyperglycemia during study
treatment, all of which resolved. It was unknown if an autopsy was performed.

The Investigator considered acute coronary syndrome and cerebral hemorrhage to be unrelated
to inavolisib, fulvestrant, and palbociclib and related to other cause (unspecified).

Concurrent condition at the time of study entry included blood cholesterol increased. FDA
cannot rule whether inavolisib contributed to the ACS event and subsequent cerebral
hemorrhage. It is not clear if the patient was on an anticoagulant during or after the diagnosis
of ACS. The patient did have reported episodes of Grade 1 platelet count decreased; however, it
is unlikely this contributed to the event of cerebral hemorrhage.

Patient ID# © Applicant Attribution: Acute Coronary Syndrome

57-year-old Asian female randomized to the inavolisib treatment arm was diagnosed with life
threatening cardiac arrest on Study Day 4. The patient’s condition deteriorated in the
ambulance on the way to the hospital and died in the ambulance. An autopsy was not
performed.
