Covariates and Clinical Relevance

‘Same as exposure-efficacy analysis

Various baseline covariates were identified for the safety endpoints, such
as Hemoglobin for Grade >2 anemia (probability and time to onset),
Neutrophil count for Grade >2 Neutropenia (probability and time to
onset), and Platelet count for Grade >2 Thrombocytopenia (probability and
time to onset). The identification of those covariates in the model were
expected because as lab values they are highly relevant to the respective
AE endpoints. Race was also identified as cov: te for Grade >2
Hyperglycemia and Thrombocytopenia (probability and time to onset), and
for Grade >2 anemia and Grade 23 AE (time to onset), with higher
probability or early on-set of those AEs in Asian patients, however, the rate
of inavo dose modification or dose reduction in Asian or Chinese patients
are comparable to those in the global patients.

Visualization of E-R relationships

The relationship between inavolisib exposure metric and the AE endpoints

Overall Clinical Relevance for ER

‘Although positive relationship between inavolisib exposure and safety
endpoints (Grade >3 AE, hyperglycemia Grade >2, and inavolisib dose
modification) were revealed in Study G039374, no positive relationship
was found for WO41554 (except Anemia Grade >2). This can be explained
by a wider exposure range in study GO39374 compared to Study
WO041554, broader HbA1c and fasting blood glucose eligibility criteria in
Study GO39374 compared to Study WO41554, and better AE management
in WO41554.
