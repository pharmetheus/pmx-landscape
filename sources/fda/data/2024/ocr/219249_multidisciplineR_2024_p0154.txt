8.2.7. Safety Analyses by Demographic Subgroups

Data and the Applicant’s Position:

Pivotal Study WO41554: Given the relatively small number of patients included in the analyses
by age, gender, race, and ethnicity, these subgroup comparisons should be interpreted with
caution.

Overall, the incidences of SAEs and AEs leading to dose modification/interruption of any study
treatment was numerically higher in elderly patients (65-74 years and = 75 years vs. < 65 years
age groups) which could be due to higher prevalence of comorbidities in elderly patients and/or
reduction in the reserve capacity of the organs in general.

Consistent with the disease demographics for breast cancer, the vast majority of patients
treated with Inavo+Palbo+Fulv were female (n=157) compared with male (n=5). In the male
patients, no AEs leading to death or AEs leading to withdrawal of treatment were reported.

The majority of the 162 patients treated with Inavo+Palbo+Fulv were White (n=94, 58.0%)
followed by Asian (n=62, 38.3%), Unknown (n=5, 3.1%), and Black or African-American (n=1,
0.6%). AEs leading to withdrawal from any study treatment or dose modification/interruption
of inavolisib were comparable between the White and Asian racial subgroups, and across
regions, supporting the manageability and tolerability of inavolisib in combination with
palbociclib and fulvestrant in both White and Asian racial subgroups and across geographical
regions.
