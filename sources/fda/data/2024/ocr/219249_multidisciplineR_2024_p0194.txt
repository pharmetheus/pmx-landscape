19.4.2.2. PPK Assessment Summary

The Applicant’s Position: PopPK analysis was performed using 3863 samples from 336 patients
(187 from Study GO39374 and 149 from Study WO41554). The final model was a three-
compartment model with first-order rate of absorption and linear elimination, a combined
additive and proportional error model, a block omega on CL and Vc and between-subject
variability (BSV) on Ka, and Vp.

The typical values of CL and Vc of inavolisib were 8.83 L/h and 155 L, respectively. For the
peripheral compartments, the typical values of Q, Q2, Vp and Vp2 were 7.50 L/h, 0.14 L/h, 68 L,
and 31 L, respectively. The rate of absorption was estimated at 0.92 h* for Phase 3 formulation.
The population PK model included a significant effect of eGFR on CL, with an exponent of 0.441
[i.e., (eGFR/86)°**"]. This suggests a higher clearance for subjects with higher eGFR. Body
weight was significant on Vc, with an exponent of 0.635 [i.e., (WT/65)°5]. Subjects with a
higher body weight had therefore a higher volume of distribution. The volume of distribution
was also found to be approximately 25% lower in Asian subjects compared to non-Asian
subjects. The rate of absorption was found to be approx. 51% slower with the Phase 1
formulation and phase 1 resupply formulation compared to the phase 3 formulation. No
apparent trend remaining between random effects of parameters and the covariates were seen
in the final model.

Based on the goodness-of-fit plots and prediction-corrected visual predictive checks, the popPK
model was considered adequate to derive the patient-level exposure metrics (AUC, Cmax, Cmin)
for subsequent exposure-response analyses. The exposure metrics were also used to compare
exposures between the sub-groups of interests by simulations.
