Source: FDA Analysis

Although the tislelizumab exposure-response relationship for efficacy was inconclusive, the
finding of effectiveness of tislelizumab was supported by an improvement in overall survival of
tislelizumab monotherapy against ICC with a stratified HR of 0.70 (95% CI 0.57, 0.85). In
addition, a flat exposure-response for safety was observed in patients with ESCC with PK
exposure (i.e., Cavg.dosei and steady state Cavg, Cmax, Cmin) observed at 200 mg Q3W. Thus, the
recommended dosage of 200 mg Q3W is acceptable from a clinical pharmacology perspective.

Reported in vitro binding activities are generally comparable among tislelizumab, nivolumab,
and pembrolizumab.

6.2.2.2. Therapeutic Individualization

Data:
Please see Section 6.3.1.- Sub-paragraph “Clinical Pharmacokinetics”.

The Applicant’s Position:
No therapeutic individualization is needed in the proposed indication based on demographic

factors (body weight, age, gender, race) or in special populations (hepatic or renal impairment).

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position. In addition, FDA’s PopPK analyses using
PK data from all clinical trials (n=1,995) suggests that race is not a significant covariate on the
CL and Vc of tislelizumab and has no clinically meaningful effect on tislelizumab PK exposure
(Le. steady state AUC and Cmin). Asian, White, and Black patients administered 200 mg Q3W
tislelizumab had comparable PK exposure (Figure 9).
