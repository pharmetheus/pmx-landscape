{Inavolisib}

8.2.6. Clinical Outcome Assessment (COA) Analyses Informing
Safety/Tolerability

Data and the Applicant’s Position:
Pivotal Study WO41554: Seven AEs deemed most applicable to inavolisib, palbociclib, and

fulvestrant based on preliminary safety or published studies (i.e. diarrhea, nausea, vomiting,
fatigue, mouth sores, decreased appetite, and rash), were selected from the PRO-CTCAE item
library for assessment, along with an additional single item assessing bother due to side-effects
of treatment.

In both arms, worst post-baseline levels of “moderate”/ “occasionally” or less were reported by
>70% of patients for decreased appetite, nausea, and vomiting, and by >60% of patients for
mouth sores, diarrhea and fatigue. Results of bother from treatment side effects showed a
similar pattern with >55% patients in both arms reporting no more than moderate levels of
bother, suggesting that inavolisib did not contribute increased treatment burden when added
to fulvestrant and palbociclib.

Higher, i.e. worse, ratings of post-baseline severity/frequency (“severe”/ “frequently” or “very
severe”/ “almost constantly”), were reported by a greater proportion of patients in the
Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm than the Pbo+Palbo+Fulv arm, particularly for diarrhea
(Inavot+Palbo+Fulv: 32.9% vs. Pbo+Palbo+Fulv: 10.1%), mouth/throat sores (30.2% vs. 8.9%),
and decreased appetite (27.0% vs. 12.7%). The proportion of patients reporting post-baseline
rash (rated in terms of presence as “Yes”/ “No”) was 53.9% vs. 40.5% in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv
and the Pbo+Palbo+Fulv arm, respectively. These higher rates reported by patients in the
inavolisib arm mirror the safety data showing higher prevalence of stomatitis, diarrhea, and
rash associated with inavolisib. Despite greater proportions of patients in the inavolisib arm
reporting higher/worse levels of treatment-related symptoms post-baseline, no additional
detriment to their baseline levels of day-to-day functioning or HRQoL was observed while on
treatment, further demonstrating the tolerability of the inavolisib regimen.

The FDA’s Assessment:

The Applicant collected PRO-CTCAE items as described above. In general, these patient-
reported symptoms were observed to be worse in the inavolisib arm, with the greatest
difference observed for diarrhea, mouth sores, decreased appetite, and rash. These symptoms
are supportive of the clinician reported safety information and were considered informative for
labeling.

The Applicant also collected overall side effect impact using the MBI which prompted patients
“lam bothered by side effects of treatment”. At baseline, the proportion of patients with MBI
responses of “not at all” were similar between arms. Throughout the trial, patients in the
