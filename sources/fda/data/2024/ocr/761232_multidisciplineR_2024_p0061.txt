6.3.2. Clinical Pharmacology Questions

6.3.2.1 Does the clinical pharmacology program provide supportive evidence of
effectiveness?

Data:
Refer to section 8 for efficacy results from Study 302.

The exposure-efficacy relationships were evaluated in previously treated advanced or metastatic
ESCC patients receiving tislelizumab monotherapy in the pivotal Study 302. In the intention-to-
treat (ITT) Population in Study 302, a longer OS was observed in patients in the highest
exposure quartile compared with those in the lower three quartiles. However, the lack of a
consistent trend in OS by exposure quartiles, the confounding effects of baseline characteristics
like tumor size and albumin on OS and tislelizumab clearance, and the imbalance in distribution
of these baseline characteristics across exposure quartiles suggest there is not a meaningful
association of exposure with efficacy.

In addition, no exposure-safety relationships were observed for immune-mediated adverse events
(imAEs), infusion-related reactions (IRRs), any adverse event (AE) > Grade 3, AE of special
interest (AESI, imAE + IRR), and AE leading to dose modification or drug discontinuation
within the exposure range at tislelizumab 200 mg once every 3 weeks dosing regimen as
monotherapy in ESCC patients in the pivotal Study 302.
