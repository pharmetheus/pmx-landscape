7.1.2 Developmental and Reproductive Toxicity

Oral administration of sulopenem etzadroxil to pregnant females was associated with maternal
and fetal toxicity in embryo-fetal development studies in mice, rats, and rabbits.

In mice, sulopenem etzadroxil administered to pregnant females in oral doses of 100, 400, and
2000 mg/kg/day during the period of organogenesis was associated with maternal toxicity
(clinical signs and reduced maternal body weights) and fetal toxicity including reduced fetal
weights and a fetal malformation. Matemal clinical signs including respiratory distress,
abdominal distension, decreased motor activity and reduced maternal body weight gain occurred
in the 2000 mg/kg/day group. In the same group, reduced fetal weights were observed, and a
fetal malformation, cleft palate, occurred with an increased fetal and litter incidence relative to
control values.

Sulopenem etzadroxil administered in oral doses of 100, 400, and 2000 mg/kg/day to pregnant
females during the period of organogenesis in a rat embryo-fetal development study was
associated with maternal weight loss and reduced maternal weight gain and reduced fetal body
weights. Maternal toxicity occurred in the mid- and high-dose groups receiving 400 and

2000 mg/kg/day, respectively. Fetal weight loss occurred in the 2000 mg/kg/day group.

In rabbits, oral doses of sulopenem etzadroxil (5, 15, and 50 mg/kg/day) administered to
pregnant females during the period of organogenesis were associated with reduced maternal
weight gain occurring in a dose-dependent manner in all the treatment groups. In addition, post-
implantation loss was increased and reductions in the number of viable fetuses, gravid uterine
weights, and fetal body weights occurred in the mid- and high-dose groups receiving 15 and
50 mg/kg/day, respectively.
