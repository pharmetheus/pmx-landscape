Racial and ethnic minorities reflective of the U.S. population of patients with breast cancer were significantly underrepresented in INAVO120.
INAVO120 was a multinational trial with just 16 patients (4.9%) enrolled from the US. Almost all patients were female (98%) and the median
age of enrolled patients was 54 years. The majority of patients on study were White, with only two Black patients and 20 Hispanic/Latino
patients enrolled on study. Due to the lack of diversity in the enrolled population, a Post Marketing Commitment (PMC) is being issued to
further characterize the efficacy and safety of inavolisib for the treatment of breast cancer in study populations whose demographics are more
representative of the population of U.S. patients for the proposed indication.

Data from a phase 1 trial GO39374 was submitted by the Applicant |

G039374 is an ongoing, open-label,
multicenter, trial designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and PK of inavolisib administered orally as a single agent in patients with locally
advanced or metastatic PIK3CA-mutated solid tumors, including breast cancer, and in combination with standard-of-care endocrine and/or
targeted therapies in patients with PIK3CA-mutated locally advanced and metastatic breast cancer. The applicant submitted data from 3
cohorts. Arm B (N=33; inavolisib in combination with palbociclib and letrozole), Arm E (N=20; inavolisib in combination with palbociclib and
fulvestrant), and Arm F (N=21; inavolisib in combination with palbociclib, fulvestrant, and metformin). Arms B, E and F did not require that
patients have endocrine resistant breast cancer. The confirmed ORR results in patients with measurable disease in Arm B (N=25), Arm E (N=15),
and Arm F (N=18) were 52% (95% Cl: 31%, 72%), 40% (95% Cl: 16%, 68%), and 17% (95% Cl: 0%, 41%), respectively. While activity was observed
across the three treatment arms based on the confirmed ORR and DOR, given the small sample sizes and wide Cl of ORR, the contribution of
