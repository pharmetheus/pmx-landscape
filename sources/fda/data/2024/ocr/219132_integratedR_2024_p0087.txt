Aqneursa (levacetylleucine)

(0) (4)

As detailed in Section 6.3.1, the 4-domain fSARA that assesses gait, stance, sitting, and speech
disturbance with modified scoring is the Agency-recommended approach to using the SARA for
the endpoint and efficacy analyses in Trial IB1001-301. |

The Agency independently applied the Applicant’s approach of examining each SARA domain
and considering the neurological features and functional changes measured for domains | to 4 of
the fSARA. Whether these changes may be influenced by other neurological symptoms of NPC,
rather than ataxia alone, was then considered. The symptoms in subjects with late-infantile onset
of disease or later (>2 years of age) are primarily neurological, including: ataxia, vertical
supranuclear gaze palsy, dysarthria, cognitive impairment, dysphagia, dystonia, seizures,
cataplexy, and hypotonia (Patterson 1993). For each of the 4 fSARA domains, there are score
tesponse options that could reflect either ataxia or another neurological symptom (or, in many
cases, likely a combination of several neurological symptoms). Examples are tandem gait or
standing with feet together being impacted by dystonia, sway while sitting being impacted by
low truncal tone, and speech being impacted by a combination of cognitive impairment and
orofacial dyskinesia. The SARA was originally intended to evaluate cerebellar ataxia and was
developed in patients with spinocerebellar ataxia. While each category is geared toward
identifying features of ataxia specifically, in patients with NPC, there is more likely a
combination of ataxia and other heterogeneous neurological symptoms contributing to their
scores.

With respect to the other, non-neurological symptoms of NPC, the most common systemic
symptoms include neonatal jaundice, hepatomegaly, and splenomegaly. In general, the visceral
symptoms tend to predominate and are more severe with early onset of disease symptoms.
However, while the proportion of systemic manifestations declines with late age of onset, some
patients have a history of neonatal jaundice and/or hepatosplenomegaly as early as in infancy,
before the onset of neurological symptoms as late as in adulthood. The systemic manifestations
of NPC were not assessed in Trial IB1001-301. Finally, the inclusion criteria for Trial IB1001-
301 selected for subjects with neurological symptoms defined as a SARA score of 7 to 34 (out
of 40) points and either a score of 2 to 7 (0 to 8) on the SARA Gait domain or were able to
perform the 9-Hole Peg Test in 20 to 150 seconds.

In terms of the proposed MOA of levacetylleucine and nonclinical studies that include published
data from studies of Npc1~- mice treated with levacetylleucine that had reduced ataxia and
improved gait and mobility, the impact of levacetylleucine on the neurological symptoms of
NPC as evidenced by Trial IB1001-301 is plausible.
