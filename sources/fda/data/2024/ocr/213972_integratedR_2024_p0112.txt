As FDA-approved labeling for probenecid indicates that it is contraindicated for
pediatric patients under the age of 2 years, the Applicant planned to request a partial waiver from
conducting a phase 3 study in pediatric patients <2 years of age with uUTI. Additionally, given
that UTIs in pediatric patients <2 years of age are generally considered complicated, the
Pediatric Review Committee (PeRC) and the Division had previously agreed (November 8,
2017) that it would be reasonable for the Applicant to request a waiver for uUTI studies for
sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid in pediatric subjects <2 years of age. The Applicant also
planned to request deferral of phase 1 and phase 3 pediatric studies for patients aged 2 to <18
years until 3Q2024 as efficacy had not yet been established in adults. Additionally, the iPSP was
updated with results from juvenile rat toxicology studies.

Of note, to assess the appropriateness of the partial waiver request for pediatric patients under the
age of 2 years based on the labeled contraindication to probenecid, the clinical review team
reviewed the literature, available copies of hand-written records from the original probenecid
NDA (07898) review in 1951, and a consult response from the Division of Pediatric and
Maternal Health (DPMH) dated April 30, 2021. In their review, DPMH concluded that studying
probenecid in pediatric patients <2 years of age could be considered as no apparent scientific
basis for this contraindication was found. However, given that PeRC and the Division had
previously determined that UTIs in pediatric patients <2 years of age are generally considered
complicated, the Applicant’s request to waive uUTI studies for sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid
in pediatric subjects <2 years of age was deemed acceptable.

Consequently, in their revised proposed amended iPSP dated September 10, 2023, the Applicant
