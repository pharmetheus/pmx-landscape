BLA 761232 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
TEVIMBRA - tislelizumab-jsgr

Noting the differences in tumor types, disease status, event reporting rules, and durations of
follow-up between safety populations, deaths in the All Doses All Indications population, as in
Study 302, predominantly occurred > 30 days after the last dose of study drug and were likely
due to the underlying malignancies. Treatment-emergent adverse events leading to death
occurred in 2.3% of patients in the All Doses All Indications population. Most deaths in the All
Doses All Indications population were considered to be unrelated to study drug.

The Applicant’s Position:

The majority of deaths were due to disease under study. Deaths due to adverse events (not due to
ESCC) were reported in low and similar proportions for both arms (tislelizumab [5.5%] and
chemotherapy [5.8%]). The overall trend for death and reason for death were consistent across
geographic region and baseline PD-L1 expression status.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA’s analysis identified the same number of total deaths and deaths beyond 30 days after the
last dose as the Applicant’s Table 27. For deaths within 30 days after the last dose, FDA’s
analysis counted fewer deaths than the Applicant for both the tislelizumab arm (n = 13) and the
ICC arm (n= 11). The reason for this discrepancy is the Applicant counting deaths due to disease
progression as fatal AEs in its ADAE dataset (but not the ADSL dataset). See also the discussion
of Table 29, below. Regardless, FDA agrees that there were more deaths in the ICC arm
compared to the tislelizumab arm and that, in both arms, deaths were most often due to
progression of disease.

FDA further examined the total number of deaths in the tislelizumab arm that were classified in
the datasets as being due to “indeterminate” (n = 18), “other” (n = 7), and “concurrent illness” (n
= 4). After reviewing these CRFs and/or narratives, FDA categorizes deaths in the tislelizumab
arm as below (Table 29) with deaths in “other” and “concurrent illness” being re-categorized to
either disease under study/progression of disease, adverse event, or indeterminate.
