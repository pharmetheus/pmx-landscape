e Ocular Toxicity
Rare but serious ocular toxicities have been reported with anti-PD1(L1) drugs. A total of 18 (7%)
and 8 (3%) of patients experienced eye disorders in the tislelizumab and ICC arms, respectively.
None of these events were Grade > 3. In the tislelizumab arm, In one patient, Grade 2 uveitis was
considered to be an imAE on preliminary assessment and treated with topical steroids but this
was not adjudicated ultimately by the Applicant as an imAE. The PTs in the tislelizumab arm
were visual impairment, vision blurred, uveitis, retinopathy, retinal tear, retinal edema, retinal
exudates, ocular hypertension, macular degeneration, lacrimation increased, dry eye,
conjunctival hyperemia, chalazion, and cataract. All retinal toxicities occurred in 5 individual
patients and were all Grade 1 events. Only 1 of these events was considered recovered/resolved.
In the ICC arm, all 9 events (8 patients) were Grades 1-2 and the PTs were: visual acuity
reduced, lacrimation increased, eyelid ptosis, blepharitis, vision blurred, visual impairment, and
conjunctival pallor.

Although relatively rare, ocular toxicities are a known risk of anti-PD1(L1) drugs and patients
must be monitored for any eye or vision complaints.

Study 302:

Hematology: Overall, ICC was associated with a worsened hematology profile relative to
tislelizumab. A greater number of shifts of > 2 NCI-CTCAE toxicity grades for decreased values
of hemoglobin and white blood cell counts occurred in the ICC Arm compared with the
Tislelizumab Arm. Patients in the ICC Arm experienced a reduction from baseline in mean
hemoglobin over time; in contrast, there was an overall increase from baseline in the mean
hemoglobin in the Tislelizumab Arm. Patients in the ICC Arm also experienced greater
reduction from baseline in mean neutrophil counts over time. There was no clinically
meaningful difference between the 2 arms in the means of the lowest postbaseline values for
lymphocytes or platelets.
