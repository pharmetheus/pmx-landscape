2) Moderate and severe hepatic Impairment

The Applicant concluded that a dedicated clinical study of inavolisib in patients with moderate
or severe hepatic impairment is not warranted due to the following:

In vitro studies showed that inavolisib is metabolically stable in human liver microsomes
(HLM). Of the metabolites recovered in excreta, the majority seem to be formed due to
hydrolysis of amide and only *2% were CYP-mediated metabolites.

For drugs like inavolisib with low extraction ratio and low protein binding, hepatic
clearance is mainly dependent on intrinsic clearance due to hepatic enzyme activity in
the liver but not on the hepatic blood flow.

FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s conclusion that a hepatic impairment study is not
warranted based on the following reasons:

Per the Mass Balance and Absolute Bioavailability Study GP42652, the absolute oral
bioavailability of inavolisib is 76%.

Circulating metabolites were minimal with the majority (99.5%) of drug-related material
in plasma being inavolisib in the human mass balance study. Urine and feces excreta
accounts for 96% of total administered dose (Table 13). A mean of 48% of the dose (40%
as the parent drug and 8% as metabolites) was recovered in urine, and 48% (10% as the
parent drug and 38% as metabolites) was recovered in feces. The amount of drug
recovered as metabolites in feces and urine account for approximately 46% of the
administered dose. Given the bioavailability and the percentage of inavolisib recovered
as metabolites, >20% contribution of hepatic elimination cannot be ruled out.
Therefore, a PMR will be issued to evaluate the PK and safety of inavolisib in patients
with moderate and severe hepatic impairment.
