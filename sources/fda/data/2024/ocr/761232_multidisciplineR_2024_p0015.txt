BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation — BLA 761232
TEVIMBRA (tislelizumab)

Patients were treated until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or 2 years of tislelizumab
treatment. The stratification factors for randomization were geographic region (Asia [excluding
Japan] vs. Japan vs. US/EU), ECOG performance status (0 vs. 1) and ICC choice (paclitaxel vs.
docetaxel vs. irinotecan). PD-L1 expression was not a stratification factor. The primary endpoint
of the trial was overall survival (OS) in all patients; the key secondary endpoint was OS in
patients with PD-L1 > 10%. Additional efficacy outcome measures (no alpha allocated) included
objective response rate (ORR), duration of response, and progression-free survival (PFS) in all
patients and in the subpopulation with PD-L1 > 10%.

In RATIONALE-302, tislelizumab provided a modest but statistically significant improvement in
OS compared with chemotherapy. The OS HR in the ITT analysis was 0.70 (95% CI 0.57, 0.85;
one-sided p-value = 0.0001) with a median OS of 8.6 months (95% CI 7.5, 10.4) in the
tislelizumab arm and 6.3 months (95% CI 5.3, 7) in the ICC arm. Although the secondary
endpoint of OS in the PD-L1 >10% subpopulation (n: 157 patients, 31% of the overall
population) was statistically significant at the prespecified alpha level (stratified HR 0.53, 95%
CI: 0.37, 0.77; one-sided p-value = 0.006), ascertainment of PD-L1 status was missing in 19% of
patients, less than a third of patients had PD-L1 >10%, and PD-L] status was not a stratification
factor for this study. Therefore, although supportive of the primary efficacy outcome, the positive
results in the PD-L1 >10% should be interpreted with caution in the context of these limitations.
The ORR was 20.3% (95% CI 15.6, 25.8) in the tislelizumab arm and 9.8% (95% CI 6.4, 14.1) in
the ICC arm. Duration of response was prolonged in the tislelizumab arm when compared to the
ICC arm (7.1 vs. 4 months). The HR for PFS in the ITT was 0.83 (95% CI 0.67, 1.01). These
results are supportive of the primary outcomes and consistent with immune checkpoint inhibitors
effects (modest effect on PFS and ORR) in ESCC.

Although worldwide ESCC is the dominant histological subtype (~ 90%) of esophageal cancer,
approximately 35% of patients in the US present with this histology (~5000 new patients/year).
The study was conducted in several geographic regions; however, only 21% of patients in
RATIONALE-302 were enrolled in non-Asian countries. Although only exploratory in nature,
subgroup analyses of demographic subgroups of the ITT efficacy population do not indicate
differential activity based on race/ethnicity (of those explored) or for patients in Europe/North
America. Importantly, based on the SEER database analysis, there are no differences in outcomes
for patients with ESCC based on race or ethnicity (Then EO, 2020). Although minorities have
not been adequately represented in the study, based on the epidemiological data, tislelizumab
mechanism of action, known safety profile of the drug class, and the clinical pharmacology
conclusions, the review team agrees that the study results are applicable to the US population.

The submitted evidence meets the statutory evidentiary standard for the approval of tislelizumab
for the treatment of patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic esophageal
squamous cell carcinoma with disease progression on or after prior chemotherapy. The observed
improvement in overall survival with a HR of 0.70 is statistically robust. This finding is
supported by consistent results on prespecified exploratory subgroup analyses and secondary and
