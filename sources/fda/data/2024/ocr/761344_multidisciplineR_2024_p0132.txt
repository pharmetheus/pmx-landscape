NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
{Tarlatamab}

Protocol Violations/Deviations

Data:

Study 20200491: As of the data cutoff date of 27 June 2023, a total of 81 subjects (36.8%) had
at least 1 important protocol deviation (IPD) (Section 9.2 of Study 20200491). Overall, the most
frequently reported (> 10% of subjects overall) IPDs were other GCP deviations (19.5%) and
missing predose assessments during treatment period (10.5%). No IPDs due to COVID-19
control measures were reported. Details for all important protocol deviations are provided by
subject in Listing 16-2.2.1 of Study 20200491.

The Applicant's Position:
The above mentioned IPDs did not affect the overall results of the study.
The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA reviewed the full list of protocol deviations provided by the Applicant. For the tarlatamab
10 mg dose, 32% of patients had at least one important protocol deviation. Overall, the most
frequently reported (> 10% of subjects overall) IPDs were other GCP deviations (20.2%) and
missing predose assessments during treatment period (10.1%). Deviations listed under the
“other GCP deviations” category were generally in regard to samples being collected without
consent being obtained, samples being erroneously collected that were not required per
protocol or late SAE reporting.

There were also 2 patients who received the incorrect dose of study treatment. Review of these
protocol deviations showed that 1 patient received 1 mg of tarlatamab instead of 10 mg of
tarlatamab and a second patient received 10 mg of tarlatamab instead of 1 mg. Lastly, there
were 4 patients who had eligibility criteria violations. Two patients received prior anticancer
therapy within 30 days of the first dose of tarlatamab; one was within 28 days and the other
was within 29 days. Two additional patients did not receive at least 2 prior lines of treatment
(only received 1 prior line with platinum and PD-(L)1 therapy); one of these patients had a
response to treatment.

In addition, as noted in the summary from the Office of Scientific Inspections, 1 patient who
received the tarlatamab 10 mg dose was found ineligible following inspection of |
(patient me). The patient was noted to have a partial thrombosis of the abdominal
aorta at screening which violates the exclusion criteria of having a history of arterial thrombosis
within 12 months of the first dose of tarlatamab treatment. The patient had a response to
tarlatamab treatment, but FDA assessed that this eligibility violation is unlikely to have had an
impact on response to tarlatamab treatment as the violation was in relation to a criteria related
to patient safety. The eligibility violation did not appear to have an impact on the patient’s
safety.
