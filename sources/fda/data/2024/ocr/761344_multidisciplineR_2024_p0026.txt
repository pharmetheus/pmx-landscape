NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761344}
IMDELLTRA - Tarlatamab

For this application, the proposed indication, SCLC with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy, differs from the
population enrolled in Study 20200491, patients with SCLC with disease progression after receiving previous treatment with platinum-based
chemotherapy and at least one other line of prior therapy. Overall, the ORR and DoR data from Study 20200491, which enrolled a more heavily
treated patient population (3 line or greater), is greater than the ORR and DoR observed with topotecan, the only available therapy with
traditional approval from the FDA for patients with SCLC previously treated with first-line chemotherapy (ORR of 24% in platinum-sensitive
disease and 2-7% in platinum-resistant disease). Review of prior therapies in of the primary efficacy population from Study 20200491 showed
that 52% of patients received a topoisomerase | inhibitor as second line therapy. Of the remaining patients, the grand majority (89%) received
second line therapies considered treatment options per U.S. standard of care. The ORR in the subgroups of patients who received prior
topoisomerase | inhibitor and who did not receive prior topoisomerase | inhibitor (including and not including patients who received standard
of care therapies), were generally consistent with the response rate observed for the overall study population. Furthermore, it is not expected
that treatment with additional therapies following platinum-based chemotherapy would increase the likelihood of response to tarlatamab
relative to treatment with tarlatamab used as second-line therapy following platinum-based chemotherapy. In light of the observed results and
particularly considering the ORR observed with tarlatamab in patients with platinum-resistant SCLC, which far exceeds ORR reported with
standard of care therapies, the available data for tarlatamab supports the proposed indication for treatment of SCLC with disease progression
on or after platinum-based chemotherapy.

Of note, the primary efficacy population included no Black/African American patients and one Hispanic/Latino patient, which is an
underrepresentation of the incidence of SCLC in these racial and ethnic minority groups. Given this, a postmarketing commitment (PMC) will be
issued to provide further analyses from ongoing or planned clinical trials to further characterize the safety, efficacy and pharmacokinetics of
tarlatamab in these patient populations.

The safety assessment for tarlatamab is primarily supported by a pooled safety population which included 187 patients from Study 20200491
and Study 20160323 with ES-SCLC who were treated with tarlatamab at the dosage proposed for marketing. The most common adverse
reactions (220%) were cytokine release syndrome, fatigue, pyrexia, dysgeusia, decreased appetite, musculoskeletal pain, constipation, anemia,
and nausea.

The key safety concerns for tarlatamab are cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and neurologic toxicity, including immune effector cell-associated
neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), both of which will be included in a Boxed Warning in the USPI. Other safety concerns include hepatotoxicity,
