NDA 218614
Tryngolza (olezarsen sodium)

II. Interdisciplinary Assessment

3. Introduction

This review serves as the interdisciplinary assessment for NDA 218614, olezarsen sodium
(Tryngolza) injection.

The Applicant (Ionis Pharmaceuticals, Inc.) is developing olezarsen as an adjunct to diet to
reduce triglycerides (TG) in adults with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS). The
recommended dosage of olezarsen is 80 mg administered by subcutaneous (SC) injection once
monthly.

FCS is a rare, autosomal recessive condition affecting 1 to 10 individuals in a million.
Individuals with FCS have persistent elevated levels of TG, which increases their risk of
pancreatitis, a potentially life-threatening consequence of this condition. The onset of symptoms
in individuals with FCS usually occurs in childhood or adolescence and includes eruptive
xanthomas, lipemia retinalis, episodic abdominal pain, and hepatosplenomegaly. A restrictive
low-fat diet is the mainstay of therapy and is effective, but adherence is difficult. TG-lowering
drugs, such as fibrates and fish oils, are largely ineffective in reducing TG in this population.
There are no FDA-approved products for the treatment of FCS.

Olezarsen is an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) inhibitor of apolipoprotein C-III (ApoC-II])
production, covalently linked to a ligand containing three N-acetyl galactosamine (GalNAc)
residues to enable delivery of the ASO to hepatocytes. Olezarsen causes degradation of apoC-IIT
mRNA through binding to apoC-J/7 mRNA, which results in a reduction of serum ApoC-III
protein. ApoC-III is a key regulator of TG metabolism via lipoprotein lipase (LPL) dependent
and independent mechanisms. By reducing ApoC-III production, olezarsen decreases the
negative effect of ApoC-III on TG clearance, thereby lowering TG levels. The lipid biomarker
TG has historically been used as a surrogate for reduction in risk of pancreatitis and the basis of
full approval for TG-lowering drugs in patients with hypertriglyceridemia (HTG) (TG >500
mg/dL).

Olezarsen shares the same nucleotide sequence as its parent drug, volanesorsen. Volanesorsen
received a conditional marketing authorization for patients with FCS at high risk for pancreatitis
by the European Medical Agency in 2019 but received a complete response letter from the
Agency in 2018, |
(EMA 2019). Olezarsen is differentiated
