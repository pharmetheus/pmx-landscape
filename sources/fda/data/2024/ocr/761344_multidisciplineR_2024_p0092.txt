{Tarlatamab}

6.2.2.2. Therapeutic Individualization
Data:

The influence of demographic and subject specific characteristics on the PK of
tarlatamab were investigated using population PK. Various demographic variables were
evaluated in the covariate testing within the population PK analyses framework. Key
demographic factors (age, sex, ethnicity), estimates of renal function and liver function,
number of prior lines of therapies, baseline disease burden or Eastern Cooperative
Oncology Group (ECOG) status, did not affect tarlatamab PK.

Consistent with other biologics, body weight was correlated with tarlatamab clearance,
as was positive binding ADA status with increase in body weight or positive ADA binding
status associated with increase in clearance. However, at the proposed dosing regimen
of 1 mg on day 1 followed by 10 mg on day 8, day 15, and Q2W thereafter, neither body
weight nor ADA status had a clinically relevant impact on tarlatamab efficacy as
assessed by confirmed ORR or DCR. Race (Asian/Black/Others vs White) was found to be
correlated with central volume of distribution; however, race had a negligible impact on
exposures. The exposure-response relationship for ORR, DCR, best tumor size response
(BTSR), and PFS was not impacted by evaluated intrinsic or extrinsic factors (see Section
6.3.2 and Section 3.5 of Module 2.7.2, Summary of Clinical Pharmacology).

Exposure-response relationships for key efficacy measures (ORR, DCR, BTSR, and PFS) or
safety measures were not impacted by any of the evaluated covariates (see Section
6.3.2). Higher sum of tumor lesion diameter at baseline (independent of tarlatamab
serum exposures) was significantly associated with higher hazard for OS.

Detailed results are provided in Report 157688 (Population PK Report) (Module 5.3.3.5)
and Report 157687 (Exposure-response Analysis Report) (Module 5.3.4.2).

The Applicant's Position:
None of the covariates evaluated in this analysis resulted in clinically meaningful

changes in efficacy or safety and therefore do not warrant a dose adjustment.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position that no dose adjustment for tarlatamab is
needed for intrinsic and extrinsic factors including age, BW, sex, race (White and Asian),
mild to moderate renal impairment, or mild hepatic impairment, as the popPK analyses
showed no clinically important effect of these covariates on the PK or E-R efficacy
relationship of tarlatamab. Due to limited data, the effects of severe renal impairment,
end-stage renal disease, moderate to severe hepatic impairment, ADA, or ethnicity (e.g.,
Hispanic or Latino) on tarlatamab PK are unknown.
