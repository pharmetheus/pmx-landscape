Clinical Microbiology Reviewer’s Comments: The Agency differed with the Applicant’s approach
for resolving discordant cipro-susceptibility results and whether or not to assign these subjects
to the MMITTR population. The Agency provided guidance that if a subject is found with a cipro-
resistant isolate from either a central lab IHMA culture or IHMA confirmatory identification of
the local laboratory isolates, then the subject should be included in the MMITTR population. The
ciprofloxacin susceptibility should be determined by IHMA based on the MIC broth microdilution
assay only. The IHMA in vitro MIC susceptibility results should have priority irrespective of the
gyrase PCR results. The Agency found that the proposed PCR for gyrase mutation was not
sufficient to determine cipro-susceptibility since other gene mutations may also be involved in
determining quinolone-resistance (e.g., parC, parE) as per current literature. Additionally, the
presence of other plasmid-mediated quinolone resistance genes may also confer quinolone
resistance.

Summary of Baseline Culture Results: ITT Population

Approximately 67% of the ITT patients (1072/1590) from both treatment arms had a baseline
urine culture with at least one target uropathogen in a quantity of 210° CFU/mL, thus qualifying
for the MMITT population. The most-frequently isolated uropathogens from urine at
concentrations 210° CFU/mL in the ITT population are summarized in Table 189. More than half
the pathogens isolated were E. coli (57.2%; 910/1590); whereas 6.2% were K. pneumoniae,
2.1% were P. mirabilis, and less than 1% were S. saprophyticus.
