TEVIMBRA - tislelizumab-jsgr

tislelizumab monotherapy in patients with advanced or metastatic ESCC who had been heavily

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA acknowledges the applicant’s presentation of OS results from supportive Studies 101 and
102. FDA did not independently verify the results since the results are supportive and
exploratory. Further, OS results from single arm trials are not interpretable.

Secondary Endpoints

Progression-Free Survival (PFS)

The median PFS was 2.0 months (95% CI: 1.3 to 4.2 months) in Study 001 and 2.1 months (95%
CI: 2.0 to 4.2 months) in Study 102. The estimated PFS rates at 3 months and at 6 months were
38.5% and 23.1%, respectively, in Study 001, and 32.4% and 18.5%, respectively, in Study 102.

Objective Response and Disease Control Rate

Studies 001 and 102 supported tumor responses observed with tislelizumab in Study 302 with an
encouraging ORR in patients with advanced or metastatic ESCC who had been heavily treated
previously. The ORRs were 19.2% (95% CI: 6.6% to 39.4%) and 7.7% (95% CI: 0.9% to 25.1%)
in Studies 001 and 102, respectively. The DCRs were 42.3% (95% CI: 23.4% to 63.1%) and
34.6% (95% CI: 17.2% to 55.7%) in Studies 001 and 102, respectively. More patients in Study
102 (38.5%) received > 3 lines of treatment than in Study 001 (11.5%), which may have
contributed to the lower ORR and DCR in Study 102 compared with Study 001. However, the
ORR in Study 102 was considered favorable when compared with the historical ORR of 0.4% to
3% observed in patients in the second- or third-line treatment setting (Dutton et al 2014). In
general, the antitumor response observed in Studies 001 and 102 was comparable to the results in
the Phase 2 studies with pembrolizumab (the KEYNOTE-180 study) and nivolumab (the
ATTRACTION-1 study) in similar heavily pretreated populations.

Duration of Response

Studies 001 and 102 supported the durable antitumor activity of tislelizumab by showing an
encouraging 12-month DOR rate in patients with advanced or metastatic ESCC who had been
heavily treated previously. In Study 001, the median DOR was 4.2 months (95% CI: 2.8 months
to NE) and the 12-month DOR rate was 40.0%. In Study 102, the median DOR was not reached,
as 1 of 2 patients who achieved a response continued to respond to treatment as of the cutoff
date; both patients achieved a DOR of > 12 months.
