4) Lack of E-R Relationships for Efficacy

No apparent E-R relationships for efficacy endpoints (PFS, duration of response [DoR], time to
first subsequent cancer therapy after treatment [TFST], clinical benefit rate [CBR], overall
response rate [ORR], and best overall response [BOR]) were identified based on analysis of data
from Study WO41554; however, the E-R efficacy results should be interpreted with caution
given that the proposed triplet combination (inavolisib + palbociclib + fulvestrant) was only
studied at one inavolisib dose level (9 mg PO QD). While the Applicant notes numerically higher
ORR for inavolisib 9 mg PO QD compared to inavolisib 6 mg PO QD in Study GO39374, there
were too few patients treated with starting dosages <9 mg PO QD to evaluate dose-activity
relationships.

Taken together, the available data support approval of the proposed recommended dosage of
inavolisib 9 mg PO QD but do not support the conclusion that the inavolisib dosage was
optimized. A PMR will be issued to study a lower dosage of inavolisib in patients with PIK3CA-
mutant, HR+/HER2-locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer to determine whether a lower
dosage may improve the benefit-risk profile compared to inavolisib 9 mg PO QD.

6.2.2.2. Therapeutic Individualization

Data: Following 9 mg QD dosing, body weight, age, race (Asian vs. non-Asian), mild hepatic
impairment, and mild renal impairment were not associated with clinically relevant differences
in inavolisib exposure parameters. Given the totality of data, dose adjustment of inavolisib in
these specific populations is not warranted.

No clinically relevant food effect (high-fat meal) was observed on inavolisib PK in Study
GO39374. Hence, inavolisib can be administered with or without food. Coadministration of
inavolisib with proton-pump inhibitors in Study GO39374 did not have a clinically relevant
effect on the exposure of inavolisib. Hence, no dose adjustment of inavolisib is needed when it
is co-administered with acid-reducing agents.
