Dropouts and/or Discontinuations Due to Adverse Effects

Data:

Tislelizumab was associated with a lower incidence of adverse events (19.2%) that resulted in
permanent discontinuation of study treatment compared with the ICC Arm (26.7%) in Study 302.
A general trend of higher event rate of adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation was
observed across multiple preferred terms in the ICC Arm, without clustering in any specific
group. Adverse events leading to dropout/treatment discontinuation by region and by baseline
PD-L1 expression status showed similar trends across all subgroups and between arms for each
subgroup as those seen in the overall population.

The Applicant’s Position:
Fewer adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation were observed in patients in the

tislelizumab arm compared with patients in the ICC arm.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees that 19% and 27% of patients permanently discontinued tislelizumab and ICC,
respectively, due to AEs. As with SAEs, the events leading to treatment discontinuation are
consistent with morbidity associated with ESCC itself as well as treatment toxicity (e.g.
pneumonitis for the tislelizumab arm and diarrhea and fatigue for the ICC arm).

The AEs with incidence >= 2% that led to permanent discontinuation of tislelizumab were:
e hemorrhage (2.4%)
pneumonitis (2.4%)
e pneumonia (2%)

The AEs with incidence >= 2% that led to permanent discontinuation of ICC were:
e hemorrhage (2.9%)
@ pneumonia (2.1%)
e diarrhea (2.1%)
e fatigue (2.1%)
