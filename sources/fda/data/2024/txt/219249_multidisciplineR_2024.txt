Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
The review team recommends granting regular approval for inavolisib, in combination with
palbociclib and fulvestrant, for the treatment of adults with endocrine-resistant, PIK3CAmutated, HR-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, as detected
by an FDA-approved test, following recurrence on or after completing adjuvant endocrine
therapy in accordance with 21 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) 314.126(a)(b).
The basis for recommendation for regular approval of inavolisib is the efficacy and safety results
obtained from INAVO120, an ongoing, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind,
multicenter trial in 325 patients with endocrine resistant, PIK3CA-mutated, HR-positive, HER2negative (defined as IHC 0 or 1+, or IHC 2+/ISH-), advanced or metastatic breast cancer,
following recurrence on or within 12 months of adjuvant endocrine therapy and who have not
received prior systemic therapy for locally advanced or metastatic disease. Patients were
randomized 1:1 to receive either inavolisib 9 mg or placebo orally once daily, in combination
with palbociclib 125 mg orally once daily for 21 consecutive days followed by 7 days off
treatment, and fulvestrant 500 mg administered intramuscularly on Cycle 1, Days 1 and 15, and
then on Day 1 of every 28-day cycle. The primary efficacy endpoint was PFS as assessed by
investigator. Key secondary endpoints included OS and ORR. DOR was a secondary endpoint.
Presence of a PIK3CA mutation was determined through testing of blood plasma-derived ctDNA
with a next-generation sequencing (NGS) assay performed at a designated central laboratory, or
through local testing of ctDNA or tumor tissue with an appropriately validated NGS or PCR
assay. Randomization was stratified by presence of visceral disease (yes or no), endocrine
resistance (primary or secondary), and geographic region (North America/Western Europe,
Asia, other). Primary endocrine resistance in the trial was defined as relapse while on the first 2
years of adjuvant endocrine therapy (ET) and secondary endocrine resistance was defined as
relapse while on adjuvant ET after at least 2 years or relapse within 12 months of completing
adjuvant ET.
INAVO120 demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in the
primary endpoint of PFS based on investigator assessment. The median PFS was 15 months
(95% CI 11.3, 20.5) vs. 7.3 months (95% CI: 5.6, 9.3) favoring inavolisib + palbociclib +
fulvestrant (HR 0.43, 95% CI: 0.32, 0.59; p-value <0.0001). PFS by blinded independent central
review (BICR) was prospectively planned and was supportive of the primary endpoint. At the
planned interim analysis for OS, a total of 97 events had occurred (63% of planned OS events)
and did not reach statistical significance based on the interim analysis stopping boundary.
Interim OS results showed a HR of 0.64 (95% CI: 0.43, 0.97) with median OS not estimable (95%
CI: 27.3, NE) on the inavolisib + palbociclib + fulvestrant arm and 31.1 months (95% CI: 22.3, NE)
on the placebo + palbociclib + fulvestrant arm. Thus, there appears to be no detriment in OS at
this time. The final analysis of OS is expected in the second quarter of 2025 and a postmarketing
commitment (PMC) has been issued for the final OS.

Endocrine therapy is the mainstay for HR-positive breast cancer. However, resistance may
occur to endocrine therapy and a major challenge to treating HR-positive breast cancer is
overcoming endocrine resistance. Activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway may lead to endocrine
resistance. Endocrine resistant breast cancer is not uniformly defined but generally refers to
cancer that grows in the presence of endocrine therapy (ET) or soon after completing ET.
Endocrine resistant breast cancer is considered to be a negative prognostic factor as these
patients tend to have worse survival outcomes compared to patients with endocrine sensitive
breast cancer.

Analysis of Current Treatment Options

The Applicant’s Position:
Treatment Options in HR-positive, HER2-negative LA/mBC
Endocrine therapy (ET) remains the cornerstone of treatment for patients with HR+ breast
cancer. The current treatment paradigm for advanced HR+, HER2─ breast cancer is sequential
endocrine-based therapies to control the disease and delay chemotherapy in the majority of
cases. For patients considered endocrine-resistant/refractory or with visceral crisis (imminent
organ failure), chemotherapy is recommended (NCCN 2024).
The combination of a cyclin dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitor with either fulvestrant or
an aromatase inhibitor (AI) is considered the standard of care first- and/or second-line
treatment for LA/mBC; endocrine monotherapy may be considered appropriate for frail
patients with comorbidities (ESMO 2023; NCCN 2024). Endocrine-based therapy is
recommended for both post- and pre-/peri-menopausal patients, though pre-/peri-menopausal
women are recommended to have ovarian function suppression or ablation. In men, the
treatment approach is similar to that of women (ESMO 2023; NCCN 2024; Vasconcelos de
Matos et al. 2023).
In patients who relapse after ET plus a CDK4/6 inhibitor, several treatment options are
available, which are tailored depending on several factors including clinical (e.g. disease
burden), molecular (e.g. presence of somatic PIK3CA, AKT1, PTEN, or ESR1 mutations), response
to prior agents (treatment resistance), patient preference, and treatment availability. As such,
the treatment options range from endocrine monotherapy (e.g. elacestrant for ESR1 mutated
tumors) to combinations with targeted agents, like alpelisib plus fulvestrant (for PIK3CAmutated tumors), everolimus plus ET, and capivasertib plus fulvestrant (for PIK3CA/AKT/PTEN
altered tumors), and in some cases, second-line CDK4/6 inhibitor plus an endocrine agent
(ESMO 2023; NCCN 2024).

adjuvant therapy.
Treatment Options in PIK3CA-mutated, HR-positive, HER2-negative LA/mBC
Currently, patients with HR+, HER2─ LA/mBC whose tumors harbor a PIK3CA mutation can be
treated with any standard-of-care therapy for HR+, HER2─ LA/mBC since these therapies have
been approved without a specific biomarker requirement. However, as none of these
treatment options target the PI3K pathway directly, patients with PIK3CA-mutated tumors may
derive less benefit compared to patients whose tumors do not harbor a PIK3CA mutation
(Fillbrunn et al. 2022). This has been observed with both endocrine-based (Fillbrunn et al. 2022)
and chemotherapy-based options (Mosele et al. 2020).
Because PIK3CA mutations are a known predictive biomarker of response to PI3Kα AKT
inhibitors (André et al. 2019; Turner et al. 2023), there are additional treatment options for
patients with PIK3CA-mutated tumors (André et al. 2019; Turner et al. 2023). Alpelisib (PI3Kα
inhibitor) plus fulvestrant is a preferred second/later line option for patients with PIK3CAmutated, HR+, HER2─ LA/mBC (André et al. 2019; Rugo et al. 2020). The challenging safety and
tolerability of alpelisib has affected its adoption in clinical practice and is a key reason it is not
recommended as a first-line treatment option (ESMO 2023; NCCN 2024). In addition,
everolimus (an mTOR inhibitor) in combination with exemestane is recommended as
second/later line option, regardless of PI3KCA mutation status.
Recently, capivasertib (AKT inhibitor) plus fulvestrant was approved for patients with HR+,
HER2─ LA/mBC that harbors a PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alteration following recurrence or
progression on an endocrine-based regimen and is a preferred second/later -line option (NCCN
2024).

5 Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
Executive Summary
Inavolisib (ITOVEBI), also known as GDC-0077, RO7113755, and G02967907, is an inhibitor of
phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) with inhibitory activity predominantly against PI3Kα,
encoded by the PIK3CA gene, and belongs to the pharmacological class of kinase inhibitors.
PI3K is a signal transducer downstream of cell surface receptors and regulator of cell
metabolism and survival. The PI3K signaling pathway has been reported to be dysregulated in
hormone receptor (HR)-positive breast cancer, often due to activating PIK3CA mutations. In this
NDA submission, the Applicant submitted nonclinical pharmacology (primary, secondary,
safety), ADME/PK, and toxicology (general, developmental, genetic) data to support the
approval of inavolisib in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant for the treatment of
adults with endocrine-resistant, PIK3CA-mutated, HR-positive, human epidermal growth-factor
receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, as detected by an
FDA-approved test, following recurrence on or after completing adjuvant endocrine therapy.
The proposed recommended dosage and administration of inavolisib is 9 mg taken orally once
daily.
In an in vitro biochemical assay, inavolisib inhibited PI3Kα with higher potency compared to the
other PI3K isoforms including PI3Kβ, PI3Kδ, and PI3Kγ with inhibitory constant (Ki) values of
0.038 nM, 101.7 nM, 12.8 nM, and 21.8 nM, respectively. The steady-state Cmax for inavolisib is
69 ng/mL (169 nM or ~107 nM unbound) at the recommended dose of 9 mg/day. In a kinase
panel of 224 kinases, inavolisib only inhibited 3 kinases (defined as > 80% inhibition at 1 µM) –
PI3Kα, PI3Kγ, and PI3KC2β, (96.4%, 96.2%, and 81.7% inhibition at 1 µM, respectively).
Inavolisib treatment of a human breast cancer cell line (HCC1954) reported to contain the
hotspot H1047R PIK3CA alteration, led to proteasome-dependent depletion of the PI3K
catalytic subunit alpha isoform (p110α) protein but not in a human breast cancer cell line
(HDQ-P1) reported to be PIK3CA wild type. Similarly, inavolisib treatment resulted in greater
increases in apoptosis, as measured by cleaved PARP protein, and decreases in downstream
signaling, as measured by pAKTS473, in the PIK3CA-mutated breast cancer cell line (HCC1954)
than in the PIK3CA-WT breast cancer cell line (HDQ-P1). Inavolisib treatment of the MCF-7
breast cancer cell line (estrogen receptor [ER]-positive, HER2-negative, and PIK3CAE545Kmutated) resulted in reduced in vitro cell viability alone and a synergistic effect in combination
with fulvestrant or palbociclib. Inavolisib showed anti-tumor effects in xenograft mouse models
of human breast cancer using KPL-4, HCC1954X1, and MCF7 cell lines. KPL-4 and HCC1954X1
are reported to be ER-negative/HER2-positive and to harbor a hotspot alteration in the p110α
catalytic domain (H1047R), resulting in elevated levels of pAKT and constitutive PI3K pathway

Primary pharmacology
The Applicant’s Position: Inavolisib is a highly selective oral small molecule inhibitor of the
catalytic subunit alpha isoform protein of PI3K (p110α; encoded by the PIK3CA gene), with subnanomolar potency. In addition, inavolisib promotes the degradation of mutated p110α. With
its dual mechanism of action, inavolisib inhibits the activity of downstream PI3K pathway

combination with standard-of-care therapies for breast cancer, including fulvestrant or
palbociclib (CDK4/6 inhibitor). Together, these nonclinical data for inavolisib support
combination therapy use as a treatment for patients with PIK3CA-mutated, HR+, HER2─
LA/mBC.

Pivotal Study WO41554 (INAVO120) and Supportive Study GO39374
Trial Design
8.1.1.1. Pivotal Study: WO41554 (INAVO120), Phase III
The Applicant’s Description: Study WO41554 is a Phase III, randomized, double-blind, placebocontrolled, multi-center, global study designed to compare the efficacy, as measured by
investigator-assessed PFS, and the safety of the triplet combination of inavolisib with
palbociclib and fulvestrant (Inavo+Palbo+Fulv) vs. placebo with palbociclib and fulvestrant
(Pbo+Palbo+Fulv) in patients with PIK3CA-mutated, HR+, HER2─ LA/mBC whose disease
progressed during treatment or within 12 months of completing adjuvant ET and who had not
received prior systemic therapy for locally advanced or metastatic disease.
Patient biomarker eligibility (i.e., positive for a study-eligible PIK3CA mutation) was determined
through testing of blood plasma-derived circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) with a next-generation
sequencing (NGS) assay performed at the Sponsor-designated central laboratory, or through
local testing of ctDNA or tumor tissue with an appropriately-validated NGS or polymerase chain
reaction (PCR) assay.
Eligible patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to one of two treatment arms:
Inavo+Palbo+Fulv or Pbo+Palbo+Fulv. Inavolisib/placebo was administered at 9 mg PO QD on
Days 1-28 of each 28-day cycle; palbociclib was administered at 125 mg PO QD on Days 1-21 of
each 28-day cycle; fulvestrant was administered at 500 mg by intramuscular injection on Days 1
and 15 of Cycle 1 and then on Day 1 of each subsequent 28-day cycle, or approximately every
4 weeks. Patients were stratified by: visceral disease (yes or no), endocrine resistance (primary
or secondary according to European Society for Medical Oncology Advanced Breast Cancer 4
(ESMO ABC4) guidelines and geographic region (North America/Western Europe, Asia, Other).
Patients received study treatment until unequivocal disease progression as determined by the
Investigator, unacceptable toxicity, patient withdrawal of consent, loss to follow-up, death, or
study termination.
After randomization, tumor assessments were performed to assess for response every 8 weeks
(±7 days) during the first 2 years of study treatment and every 12 weeks (±7 days) thereafter.
Primary efficacy analyses were based on the tumor assessments performed by the local
radiologists or Investigators. Response was assessed on the basis of physical examinations,

and the investigator’s assessment. Interruption, but not reduction, was permitted for

The Applicant’s Position:
Pivotal Study WO41554: Inavolisib in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant in patients
with PIK3CA-mutated, HR+, HER2─ LA/mBC demonstrated manageable safety and tolerability
profile as shown by the high median relative dose intensity of inavolisib, the comparable
median relative dose intensities of palbociclib and fulvestrant in both arms, and low treatment
discontinuation rate of the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv combination due to AEs. The safety profile of the
Inavo+Palbo+Fulv combination regimen was consistent with the known safety profile of each
individual study drug component and the underlying disease. No new safety findings were
identified. The addition of inavolisib did not contribute to increased treatment burden in
patients, as indicated by the majority of patients in both arms reporting levels of selected
symptoms from the PRO-CTCAE and overall bother from treatment side effects at moderate
levels or less. A summary of key AE-related safety results are presented in Table 36.

The overall safety profile of inavolisib in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant in Study
WO41554 was comparable with the overall safety population (Arms A-F) and Arms B, E and F of
Study GO39374, strengthening the characterization of the inavolisib safety profile across
inavolisib-containing treatment regimens including triplet regimens with palbociclib and
fulvestrant or with palbociclib and letrozole and supporting the tolerability and AE management
of inavolisib. Data from both studies were consistent with the anticipated safety profile of
inavolisib and the respective safety profiles of palbociclib, fulvestrant, and letrozole. No new or
unexpected safety findings were identified in either of the studies.
In both Studies WO41554 and GO39374, the proportion of patients who experienced AEs
leading to withdrawal of any study drug was overall low indicating manageability of AEs and
good tolerability of inavolisib in combination with palbociclib and an ET.
Hyperglycemia is a reversible on-target toxicity with early onset associated with drugs that
target the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway. In Study WO41554, the majority of patients who
experienced a selected AE of hyperglycemia in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm had Grade 1 or Grade
2 events; all events were non-serious, and manageable with oral anti-hyperglycemic agents and
dose reductions (2.5%) or interruptions (27.8%) in all patients including pre-diabetic patients
enrolled in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm. Grade 3 hyperglycemia events were reported in a low
proportion of patients in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm and no Grade 4 or Grade 5 hyperglycemia
events were reported in either arm. Median time to first onset of hyperglycemia was 7 days.
The majority of events of hyperglycemia in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm were reversible with only
2 patients (1.2%) discontinuing from the study due to AEs of hyperglycemia and Type 2 diabetes
mellitus. The overall incidences of hyperglycemia AEs in the Inavo+Palbo+Fulv arm of
Study WO41554 (58.6%) was lower than that seen in the overall safety population (66.8%) or in
Arm E or Arm F individually (70.0% each) of Study GO39374 and could be due to differences in
NCI-CTCAE grading criteria, more restrictive hyperglycemia inclusion criteria and an improved
manageability of hyperglycemia in Study WO41554. No cases of diabetic ketoacidosis or
hyperosmotic hyperglycemic nonketotic states were reported across both studies thereby
indicating that hyperglycemia was well-managed per the protocol-defined guidance irrespective
of underlying risk factors.
The majority of diarrhea AEs across both studies were Grade 1-2 with no Grade 5 events and a
total of 3 SAEs (2 in Study WO41554 and 1 in Study GO39374). Dose reduction, interruption and
withdrawal of any study drug due to diarrhea were not frequent and supported the
manageability of diarrhea AEs.
Across both studies, most rash events were Grade 1-2 with one Grade 3 event, no Grade 4 or 5
events and a total of 2 SAEs (both in Study GO39374). No patient discontinued any study drug
due to rash across both studies. A low proportion of patients required dose interruptions or
dose reductions due to rash AEs.

Circulating metabolites were minimal with the majority (99.5% of drug-related material in
plasma) being parent inavolisib in the human mass balance study. A mean of 48% of the dose
(40% as the parent drug and 8% as metabolites) was recovered in urine, and 48% (10% as the

metabolites in feces and urine accounts for approximately 46% of the administered dose.
Therefore, a >20% contribution of hepatic elimination cannot be ruled out and patients with
hepatic impairment may have increased exposure to inavolisib. The Applicant provided data to
support dosage recommendations for patients with mild hepatic impairment but did not provide
sufficient data to support dosage recommendations for patients with moderate or severe renal
impairment. If patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment have increased inavolisib

severe toxicity including hyperglycemia, stomatitis/mucosal inflammation, diarrhea, and other

on sensitive CYP3A substrates. Induction of CYP3A by inavolisib may result in reduced exposure of
sensitive CYP3A substrates which could reduce effectiveness of concomitantly administered
CYP3A substrates.
The Applicant did not evaluate the induction potential of inavolisib on CYP2C isoforms. If the
induction potential of inavolisib on CYP3A is confirmed, an additional study to evaluate the
induction potential of inavolisib on CYP2Cs in an in vitro setting and/or in a clinical trial may be
needed.
In addition, if inavolisib is at least a weak inducer of CYP3A in vivo, there is the potential for
drug-drug interaction between inavolisib and combined oral contraceptives (COC), which are
known substrates of CYP3A, which could reduce the effectiveness of the COC and increase the
risk of unintended pregnancy. If the induction potential of inavolisib on CYP3A is confirmed, an
additional study to evaluate the DDI potential between inavolisib and COC in a clinical trial may
be needed.

2. Can PBPK analyses be used to estimate the effects of inavolisib on the CYP3A
substrate midazolam?
The potential induction effect of inavolisib on the sensitive CYP3A substrate midazolam
following oral administration of inavolisib 9-mg QD cannot be ruled out for the reasons
discussed below.
In vitro, inavolisib is a time-dependent inhibitor and inducer of CYP3A. Following oral
administration of single and multiple doses of inavolisib, inavolisib AUC increased
approximately proportional to the dose. The clinical DDI study of inavolisib with midazolam in
patients with cancer has not been conducted. The available clinical PK data are insufficient to
differentiate the CYP3A inhibition or induction of inavolisib, thus the CYP3A inhibition and
induction parameters of inavolisib cannot be independently verified. In addition, the ability of
PBPK modeling to accurately predict complex DDI has not been demonstrated. A study
conducted by the Innovation and Quality (IQ) Induction Working Group showed that, for
compounds that were both in vitro time-dependent inhibitors and inducers of CYP3A,
prediction of the DDI trended toward an overprediction of the inactivation or a net effect of no
interaction (PMID: 23995268). The inhibition or induction effects of this type of compound
should be evaluated separately.
