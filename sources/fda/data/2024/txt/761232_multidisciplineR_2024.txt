TEVIMBRA (tislelizumab)
Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA)
Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
Tislelizumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds to programmed-death receptor 1 (PD-1) and blocks its interactions with both its ligands. This
releases the PD-1/PD-L1-mediated inhibition of the immune response, including activation of the anti-tumor immune response. There is
extensive clinical experience with immune checkpoint inhibitors, which are approved by the FDA for multiple indications, either alone or in
combination with other drugs.
The safety and effectiveness of tislelizumab for the treatment of patients with metastatic or locally advanced unresectable esophageal squamous
cell carcinoma (ESCC) with disease progression after prior chemotherapy therapy is demonstrated by the results of a single multicenter,
international, open-label, randomized trial, RATIONALE-302 (NCT03430843). The study evaluated tislelizumab as a single agent versus
chemotherapy (investigator’s choice between irinotecan, paclitaxel, or docetaxel). Patients were eligible if they had histologically confirmed
predominant squamous cell histology, irrespective of PD-L1 status, and ECOG PS of 0 or 1. Prior chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant/adjuvant
setting was considered prior therapy if patients had a recurrence-free period of less than 6 months. Patients with prior therapy with PD-1 or PDL1 monoclonal antibodies were not eligible for the trial. Tumor cell PD-L1 was determined by a central lab using the Ventana SP263 assay
(vCPS).
Patients were randomized (1:1) to receive
 Tislelizumab 200 mg intravenously (IV) every 3 weeks (Q3W)

2. Therapeutic Context
2.1. Analysis of Condition
The Applicant’s Position:
Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the dominant histological subtype
(approximately 90%) of esophageal cancer (EC) worldwide (Abnet et al 2018). Regions of high
incidence include Eastern to Central Asia, along the Rift Valley in East Africa, and into South
Africa. ESCC is more common in the elderly (aged ≥ 60 years) and has a mean male to female
ratio of 3:1 to 4:1 (Lagergren et al 2017). Epidemiologic factors associated with higher ESCC
prevalence include prolonged consumption of tobacco and alcohol, poor nutrition and diet, and
low socioeconomic status that can complicate its treatment (Uhlenhopp et al 2020; Tran et al
2005; Engel et al 2003). Certain histopathologic and anatomic features of ESCC (e.g., high-grade
dysplasia, early submucosal invasion, location in mid-to-low esophagus) can render the ESCC
difficult to detect, prone to rapid lymph node infiltration and distant metastases, and challenging
to treat (Gibson et al 2021).
The prevalence of ESCC has decreased in Western regions due to changes in lifestyle and
improvements in general health. In North America, ESCC has become less prevalent than
esophageal adenocarcinoma, which is twice as common and has a different etiology (e.g.,
Barrett's esophagus, obesity, reflux), management, and prognosis. Despite this decrease in
prevalence, EC remains the 10th leading cause of cancer-related death in the United States (US)
overall, and the 7th among males (Patel 2018; Siegel 2020; American Cancer Society 2021).
Diagnosis of ESCC is often made after symptoms appear later in the course of the disease. Onethird of US patients present with ESCC that has already spread to regional lymph nodes, and
39% have distant metastases at the time of diagnosis (SEER 2021). The 5-year survival for
localized disease is 47.1% but drops to 25.2% for regional disease and 4.9% for patients with
distant metastases.
Advanced (metastatic, non-resectable, or recurrent) ESCC is aggressive with frequent recurrence
and limited survival. Lymphatic spread of ESCC to internal jugular, cervical, supraclavicular,
mediastinal, and celiac nodes is common. Metastases to lung, liver, and distant lymph nodes are
most common, but metastases to the adrenal gland, pleura, bone, peritoneum, kidney, and heart
also occur (Verstegen et al 2020). Patients with advanced ESCC experience several debilitating
symptoms including dysphagia, cough, tracheo-esophageal fistulas, anemia, weight loss, and
aspiration pneumonia that complicate care, diminish underlying health, and worsen quality of
life. Advanced ESCC is a rapidly progressing and symptomatic disease with poor clinical
outcomes.

6.3. Comprehensive Clinical Pharmacology Review
6.3.1.

General Pharmacology and Pharmacokinetic Characteristics

Data:
An overview of the clinical pharmacokinetics and immunogenicity is provided below.
Absorption: Tislelizumab is administered intravenously. Maximum serum concentrations are
observed immediately after the end of the infusion.
Distribution: Consistent with the volume of distribution of endogenous IgG antibodies,
tislelizumab has low volume of distribution (6.42 L).
Metabolism: The metabolic pathway for tislelizumab is expected to be non-specific degradation
to peptides and amino acids by lysosome through fluid-phase or target-mediated endocytosis.
Excretion: Clearance of tislelizumab is 0.153 L/day and does not appear to be time varying. The
t½ was estimated to be approximately 23.8 days.
Clinical Pharmacokinetics: A population PK (popPK) analysis was conducted to evaluate the
PK of tislelizumab and to identify factors contributing to between-patient variability in PK using
pooled PK data from 2596 patients enrolled in 12 clinical studies (Studies 001, 102, 203, 204,
205, 206, 208, 209, 302, 303, 304 and 307).
Tislelizumab exhibited a dose proportional increase in exposure over the entire dose range/dose
regimens tested (0.5 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg Q2W or Q3W, including the 200 mg Q3W flat dose).
The PK of tislelizumab was best characterized using a 3 compartmental model with linear
clearance mechanism. No time-varying clearance was observed in tislelizumab PK. The typical
estimates of CL, central volume of distribution (Vc), and peripheral volumes 2 and 3 (V2 and V3,
respectively), were 0.153 L/day, 3.05 L, 1.27 L, and 2.10 L, respectively, with interindividual
variability in CL (26.3%), Vc (16.7%), V2 (74.7%), and V3 (99.9%). The terminal half-life (t½)
was estimated to be approximately 23.8 days. Steady state is expected to be reached in
approximately 12 weeks. The accumulation ratios are estimated to be 2.14 and 2.49 for area
under the serum concentration-time curve at steady state (AUCss) and minimum concentration at
steady-state (Cmin,ss), respectively.
PopPK analysis demonstrated that race, baseline alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate
aminotransferase (AST), bilirubin, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), estimated glomerular filtration
rate (eGFR) and ECOG PS score did not have a statistically significant influence on tislelizumab
PK. Baseline body weight, tumor size of solid tumors, albumin level, immunogenicity, sex, age,
and tumor type were found to be statistically significant covariates on the PK of tislelizumab.
However, none of them are considered to result in clinically relevant changes in exposure of
tislelizumab because the CL and Vc derived based on the extremes (10th and 90th percentiles) of
covariate distribution were still within ±20% of the typical estimates. As the impact of these
covariates on tislelizumab exposure metrics is relatively small compared to the overall variability
of exposure in the population, dose adjustment is not warranted based on these covariates. This is

study.

Efficacy Analyses
The primary and key secondary endpoints were tested sequentially at a 1-sided alpha of 0.025.
No statistical inference was planned for the other efficacy and safety analyses; the p-values
presented for these analyses were calculated for descriptive purpose only.
Primary Efficacy Endpoint
The primary efficacy endpoint for this study was overall survival in the ITT Analysis Set,
defined as the time from the date of randomization to the date of death from any cause. In the
absence of death on or before data cutoff date, overall survival was censored either at the date
that the patient was last known to be alive or the date of data cutoff, whichever occurred earlier.
Overall survival was compared between the Tislelizumab Arm and the ICC Arm. The null and
alternative hypotheses were set as follows:
H0: overall survival in the Tislelizumab Arm ≤ overall survival in the ICC Arm
Ha: overall survival in the Tislelizumab Arm > overall survival in the ICC Arm
The primary analysis of overall survival was to be performed in the ITT Analysis Set once the
targeted number of deaths (approximately 400) was reached. The p-value was obtained from 1sided log-rank test stratified by selected stratification factors at randomization: ECOG PS score
(0 versus 1) and ICC option (paclitaxel versus docetaxel versus irinotecan). The region as the
third stratification factor was not included in the analysis due to the potential sparse number of
events, in particular, with the small number of patients enrolled from Japan.
The median overall survival with 95% confidence interval (CI), if estimable, was constructed
using a generalized Brookmeyer and Crowley method. The cumulative probability of overall
survival at 6 and 12 months, if estimable, was calculated and presented with 2-sided 95% CI by
using Greenwood’s formula.
The treatment effect was estimated by fitting a Cox regression model to the overall survival
times including treatment arm as a covariate and was stratified by ICC option and ECOG PS
score. From this model, the hazard ratio (HR) of overall survival was estimated and presented
with a 2-sided 95% CI.
Secondary Efficacy Endpoints


Overall Survival (OS) in patients with PD-L1 vCPS ≥ 10%: The same statistical methods
described in the primary efficacy analysis were applied for the analysis of this key
secondary efficacy endpoint.



Objective Response Rate (ORR): ORR per RECIST v1.1 was tested using CochranMantel-Haenszel (CMH) test adjusting for ECOG PS score, and ICC option in the ITT
analysis set and PD-L1 positive analysis set. The common odds ratio for ORR adjusted
for strata and its two-sided 95% CIs will be calculated. ORR and difference in ORR as
well as Clopper-Pearson 95% CI were calculated.

Therapies Excluded During Tislelizumab Treatment
The following medications were prohibited or restricted at the time of screening and during the
administration of tislelizumab:


Immunosuppressive agents (except to treat a drug-related adverse event)


Systemic corticosteroids > 10 mg daily (prednisone or equivalent), except to treat or
control a drug-related adverse event or for short-term use as prophylactic treatment

Live vaccines ≤ 28 days before the first dose of tislelizumab and 60 days after the last
dose of tislelizumab

Herbal remedies with immune-stimulating properties (eg, mistletoe extract) or that were
known to potentially interfere with liver or other major organ functions (eg, hypericin). Patients
were required to notify the investigator of all herbal remedies used during the study.
Therapies Excluded During Investigator-Chosen Chemotherapy Treatment
 Caution was exercised for patients receiving paclitaxel concomitantly with substrates,
inhibitors, or inducers of CYP2C8 or CYP3A4. The metabolism of paclitaxel is catalyzed by
cytochrome P450 isoenzymes CYP2C8 and CYP3A4. Potential interactions between
paclitaxel, a substrate of CYP3A4, and protease inhibitors, which were substrates and/or
inhibitors of CYP3A4, were not evaluated in clinical studies.
 Patients receiving docetaxel were required to avoid concomitant use of strong inhibitors
of CYP3A4; however, if strong inhibitors of CYP3A4 could not be avoided, the docetaxel
dose was reduced per the product label.
 Strong CYP3A4 inducers were not allowed to be administered with irinotecan unless
there were no therapeutic alternatives. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and UGT1A1 inhibitors or
both CYP3A4 and UGT1A1 inhibitors were not allowed when patients were treated with
irinotecan unless there were no therapeutic alternatives.
Therapies Excluded During All Study Treatment
Concurrent antineoplastic therapy (eg, chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, immunotherapy, or
standard or investigational agents [including Chinese herbal medicine and Chinese patent
medicines] for the treatment of cancer) and extensive radiation therapy (except for local,
palliative radiotherapy) were prohibited or restricted at the time of screening and during the
administration of study treatment. Palliative (limited-field) radiation therapy is permitted, but
only for pain control or prophylaxis of bone fracture to sites of bone disease present at baseline.
During the study, surgical procedures required during study participation were reported as
treatments for adverse events and patients were required to avoid abuse of alcohol and other
drugs.

Adverse Events
In Study 302, the most frequently reported adverse events (ie, ≥ 5% patient incidence) in the
Tislelizumab Arm consisted of various preferred terms, including anemia, weight decreased,
cough, pyrexia, decreased appetite, and constipation (Table 35). These preferred terms (PTs)
were not typically associated with immune-mediated adverse events, and the incidences of these
events were generally similar between the Tislelizumab Arm and the ICC Arm. Adverse events
with a ≥ 5% higher incidence reported for the Tislelizumab Arm compared with the ICC Arm
were cough, transaminase elevation, and hypothyroidism, consistent with an immune-mediated
association.
The most frequently reported adverse events (≥ 15% patient incidence) in the ICC Arm were
predominantly related to hematologic, constitutional, and gastrointestinal events, and these
events were also the types of adverse events reported with a higher incidence for ICC compared
with tislelizumab. The events were generally consistent with the safety profile of paclitaxel,
docetaxel, and irinotecan in the ICC Arm.
In exposure-adjusted analyses, tislelizumab was associated with a substantially lower rate of
adverse events of any grade compared with ICC (171.8 per 100 person-months versus 442.3 per
100 person-months).
In the All Doses All Indications population, adverse events of any grade were consistent overall
with those in Study 302 Tislelizumab Arm (Table 35).

≥ Grade 3 Adverse Events
In Study 302, the incidence of ≥ Grade 3 adverse events was lower in the Tislelizumab Arm
(46.3%) compared with the ICC Arm (67.9%) (Table 37). The most frequently reported ≥ Grade
3 adverse events in the Tislelizumab Arm were dysphagia, anemia, and hyponatremia. These

these events in the Tislelizumab Arm were either similar or lower than the ICC Arm, except for

investigators as related to the tumor.
The most frequently reported ≥ Grade 3 adverse events in the ICC Arm were predominantly
hematologic events, and these ≥ Grade 3 adverse events also occurred at higher incidence in the

events were associated with dose modification and, to a lesser extent, study drug discontinuation.
These findings were consistent with known adverse events of ≥ Grade 3 of paclitaxel, docetaxel,
and irinotecan in the ICC Arm.

in the Study 302 Tislelizumab Arm, except for a higher dysphagia in Study 302 compared with
the All Doses All Indications population (6.3% versus 1.4%). The higher rate of dysphagia in
