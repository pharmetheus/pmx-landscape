Tisotumab vedotin exposure and safety relationships were evaluated in a safety population
comprising subjects treated at the 2.0 mg/kg dose. Logistic regression analyses suggested that
tisotumab vedotin exposure (cycle 1 AUC and Cmax) was a predictor of the probability of
treatment-related TEAEs leading to dose interruptions; tisotumab vedotin Cycle 1 AUC was a

treatment-related grade 3 or higher AEs. Tisotumab vedotin Cycle 1 AUC, Cycle 1 Cmax and

the GCT1015-04 trial, where a comprehensive OMP was used, tisotumab vedotin exposure

ocular AEs. Logistic regression analyses suggested that MMAE Cycle 1 AUC was a predictor of
the probability of treatment-related SAEs and all SAEs. Logistic regression analyses suggested
that neither tisotumab vedotin or MMAE exposures were predictors of the probability of grade
2 or higher bleeding AEs or grade 2 or higher peripheral neuropathy. Although logistic
regression analyses revealed relationships between tisotumab vedotin and MMAE exposures
and reported safety outcomes, treatment with 2.0 mg/kg tisotumab vedotin was associated
with a manageable safety profile across the entire range of concentrations in subjects with solid
tumors.

The Applicant's Position:

Tisotumab vedotin administered by IV infusion at 2.0 mg/kg, up to a maximum dose of 200 mg
1Q3W demonstrates a favorable benefit-risk profile for patients with recurrent or metastatic
cervical cancer.

The FDA’s Assessment:

In general, the FDA agrees that the proposed dosing regimen of 2.0 mg/kg Q3W IV, up toa
maximum dose of 200 mg in patients 2 100 kg, is acceptable for the general patient population
in the recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer indication. The proposed dosing regimen is
supported by efficacy data in the pivotal Study 04, and the exposure-response relationships of
tisotumab vedotin with safety and efficacy at this dosage.
