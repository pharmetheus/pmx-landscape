Risk and risk
management

No serious safety signals were identified in this review.

There were no deaths in the controlled clinical trials.

« There were two deaths in the long-term safety study, related to a group A
streptococcus infection and homicide, respectively. Neither was assessed as being
causally related to atogepant.

« There were no serious adverse events that were clearly associated with the use of
atogepant. The most common adverse reactions in the controlled clinical efficacy
trials in atogepant treated patients, occcuring at greater than 2%, were constipation
(6%), nausea (5 to 9%), fatigue/somnolence (4 to 6%), and decreased appetite (1 to
2%).

° Hepatic risk was assessed as a safety signal of special interest due to previous
experience for earlier oral CGRP receptor antagonists. Overall, the rate of
transaminase elevations over 3X ULN were similar between atogepant treatment
arms and placebo (1.0% versus 1.8%). There were cases of serum transaminase
elevations (alanine aminotransferase 7 to 10X upper limit of normal [ULN] and
aspartate aminotransferase 3 to 7X ULN) that were associated with atogepant use
in the controlled clinical trials; however, all cases were asymptomatic and resolved
after discontinuation of atogepant. Routine monitoring of liver enzymes is not
recommended.

« Atogepant-treated patients experienced weight loss at a greater frequency than
patients treated with placebo. The proportion of patients with a weight decrease
greater than or equal to 7% at any point was 2.8% for placebo, 3.8% for atogepant
10 mg, 3.2% for atogepant 30 mg, and 4.9% for atogepant 60 mg QD.
