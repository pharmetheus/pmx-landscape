BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {Biologics License Application (BLA) 761208}
{tisotumab vedotin}

Overall, tisotumab vedotin administered at 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W (up to 200 mg for body weight
2100 kg), demonstrates robust activity in patients with cervical cancer. This dosing regimen was
selected based on the benefit/risk ratio observed in the Phase 1 clinical trial GEN701 where a
range of doses was tested. While activity was observed at lower dose levels, 2.0 mg/kg was
associated with greater antitumor activity compared to lower doses, with a manageable safety
profile.

The Applicant's Position:
The recommended clinical dose of tisotumab vedotin is 2.0 mg/kg administered by IV infusion

1Q3W. Tisotumab vedotin at 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W had a manageable safety profile and provided
clinical benefit to subjects with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees that the proposed 2 mg/kg Q3W IV with a cap of 200 mg for patients 2100 kg is
acceptable for the general population. However, FDA notes that benefit-risk balance at doses
lower than the proposed 2 mg/kg Q3W was not adequately characterized due to limited sample
sizes in the lower dose cohorts. In Study GEN701, n=3 patients were evaluated at all dose levels
(0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2, 1.5, 1.8 and 2 mg/kg) other than 2.2 mg/kg where n=6 patients were
evaluated. Of note, in the dose escalation phase of Study GEN 701, only one patient responded
at the 1.2 mg/kg dose level and there were no responders at the proposed 2 mg/kg dose.
Despite limited evaluation of the benefit-risk profile of dosages lower than 2 mg/kg, further
evaluation of alternative dosing regimens to mitigate the high incidence of ocular toxicities at
the proposed dose was not recommended, as lower exposures may lead to lower efficacy
based on limited exposure-response relationship assessment at only the 2 mg/kg Q3W dosage.
Further lowering of efficacy is undesirable given the currently observed ORR and DoR, and the
late-line setting.

6.2.2.2 Therapeutic Individualization

Data:
Tisotumab vedotin has been administered based on subject body weight, with the exception of
subjects 2100 kg for which a dose cap (200 mg) is in place.

Impact of intrinsic factors on tisotumab vedotin and MMAE PK was assessed in the population
PK model. The final model included body weight, baseline serum albumin, and sex as covariates
for tisotumab vedotin PK and age, sex, body weight, cancer type, albumin, estimated
glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) status, hepatic
impairment and baseline tumor burden as covariates for MMAE PK. The model-predicted cycle
1 average serum concentration (Cave) values were evaluated for these covariates following

2.0 mg/kg tisotumab vedotin (up to 200 mg for body weight 2100 kg). Comparison of exposures
