The safety profile of tisotumab vedotin generally reflects the TF target as well as the MMAE payload. Fatal adverse reactions occurred in 4% of
patients and included septic shock and pneumonitis. Serious adverse reactions occurred in 43% of patients and included ileus (6%), hemorrhage
(5%), pneumonia (4%), sepsis (3%), constipation (3%), pyrexia (3%), and peripheral neuropathy (3%). The most common adverse reactions
(230%) in GCT1015-04 included fatigue, nausea, peripheral neuropathy, alopecia, epistaxis, conjunctival adverse reactions, and hemorrhage.
Adverse reactions leading to dose discontinuations occurred in 13% of patients and adverse reactions leading to dose reductions occurred in
23% of patients.

Ocular toxicity is an important safety signal associated with tisotumab vedotin and affected 55% of patients enrolled to GCT1015-04, including
6% of patients with ulcerative keratitis, including one patient with a corneal ulcer that perforated and required corneal transplantation. There is
a Boxed Warning for ocular toxicity in the tisotumab vedotin USPI to alert providers to the risk of patients developing severe ocular toxicity
including corneal ulcers. Patients and providers should adhere to the premedication and required eye care plan outlined in the tisotumab
vedotin USPI, and patients should be promptly referred to an eye care professional for any new or worsening ocular symptoms.

Other important safety signals associated with tisotumab vedotin include peripheral neuropathy, hemorrhage, pneumonitis, and embryo-fetal
toxicity, which are listed under Warnings and Precautions in the tisotumab vedotin USPI.

Patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer have limited therapeutic options following first-line treatment with a chemotherapy
doublet with bevacizumab. Available therapies in this treatment setting include single agent chemotherapies with ORRs ranging from 5-21%
and median DORs ranging from 2-4 months. These data are from small trials conducted prior to establishment of a chemotherapy doublet with
bevacizumab as standard-of-care first-line treatment for patients in the U.S. Given that all patients who received tisotumab vedotin on
GCT1015-04 received first-line doublet chemotherapy and 69% of patients had prior bevacizumab as per U.S. standard of care, the review team
considers that the efficacy of tisotumab vedotin demonstrates an improvement compared to available therapies, and these results are
