In vivo studies were conducted to characterize the pharmacodynamic (PD) activity of
efgartigimod (i.e., reduction in plasma IgG levels) and the potential for efficacy in animal
models of MG. The expected reduction in plasma IgG was observed across studies in all species
tested. Specificity was demonstrated in multiple studies in which levels of plasma IgM or IgA
were not affected after dosing with efgartigimod.

When administered IV to cynomolgus monkeys at a dose of 20 mg/kg (single dose or repeated
dosing every 4 days), IgG levels were reduced approximately 55% after both single and repeated
dosing. With repeated dosing, the reduction in IgG was similar to that observed after a single
dose but was of longer duration. In a study comparing the PD of efgartigimod among species,
dose-related reductions in IgG were observed in mouse (up to 66% at 2-100 mg/kg), rat (up to
48% at 2-100 mg/kg), and monkey (up to 55% at 0.2-200 mg/kg).
