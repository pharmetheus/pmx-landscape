5.0Assessment: While the development of two CGRP antagonists, telcagepant and
MK-3207, stopped due to hepatotoxicity, we see little evidence of DILI in this NDA
for Atogepant. Two other in-class agents (rimegepant and ubrogepant) were
approved without significant liver injury concerns. These later generation agents may
carry a lower DILI risk. However, the two approvals were recent (2019 and 2020).

In vitro, in silico, and animal studies in this NDA did not raise significant liver injury
concerns. Three animal models (mouse, rat and monkey) dosing Atogepant for 3, 6
and 9 months respectively did not show histopathologic liver injury. Using in vitro
data, quantitative systems toxicology studies (in silico) predicted less injury risk
compared to telcagepant and MK-3207. The in silico and in vitro modeling predicted
an increased risk of mild ALT elevation beginning at twice the highest Atogepant
dosing used in the registration trials.

Avoiding the use of Atogepant in patients with severe hepatic impairment may be
prudent because the in-silico data suggest a dose response for DILI. Hepatic
impairment studies showed a 38% increase in systemic exposure in severe hepatic
impairment. Also, patients with severe impairment have less tolerance of DILI.

Registration trial data for this NDA did not suggest significant liver injury risk.
Treatment duration ranged from 12 to 52 weeks and dosing from 10 mg daily to 60
mg twice daily. Total number of patients exposed was large at over 1300. There
were no obvious imbalances in elevation of ALT or AST elevations between placebo
and treatment arms. There were no cases meeting Hy’s Law criteria. Case level
analyses found just 2 patients with probable DILI due to Atogepant and 4 possible
cases. No cases had abnormal bilirubin levels. The 4 possible cases had competing
etiologies to explain the liver enzyme elevations. Both patients with probable DILI
had short latencies, were asymptomatic and had quick resolution in enzyme
elevations after drug stop. The patient who died with prior elevations in ALT and
AST, had a myopathy causing these elevations, not DILI. These elevations were
resolving when she died of sepsis.

Therefore, we did not find a liver injury risk that should prevent approval, but the risk
is not zero. While the registration trials were large, wider use in the market can
uncover a rare risk of significant DILI not captured in these trials. We do not
recommend routine monitoring of liver enzymes, but we suggest mentioning
potential for ALT or AST elevation in the label. It may be prudent to avoid use in
patients with severe hepatic impairment.

5.1 Recommendations
a. Routine blood tests for liver enzyme monitoring is not necessary.

b. Consider avoiding use of Atogepant in patients with severe hepatic
impairment.

c. Consider the wording below for labeling:
