6.3.2.4 Are there clinically relevant food-drug or drug-drug interactions,
and what is the appropriate management strategy?

No, based on available data from another ADC that contains MMAE, brentuximab vedotin,
there is a low potential for clinically relevant interactions between tisotumab vedotin and
concomitant medications. Tisotumab vedotin is intended for administration via IV infusion;
therefore, food-drug interactions are not expected.

Data:

Coadministration of CYP3A4 Substrates with Tisotumab Vedotin

In a clinical DDI study with another MMAE-containing ADC, brentuximab vedotin (1.8 mg/kg),
circulating levels of free MMAE (at levels similar to those observed following tisotumab vedotin
administration) did not affect the PK of midazolam, a CYP3A4 substrate (Han 2013). Data
suggest that circulating concentrations of free MMAE that result from tisotumab vedotin are
not likely to cause clinically significant changes in the PK of drugs metabolized by CYP3A4.
