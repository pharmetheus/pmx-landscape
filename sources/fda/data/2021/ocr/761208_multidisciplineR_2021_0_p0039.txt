The FDA’s Assessment:

The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position. However, in the in vivo skin bleeding
time assessment in monkeys (Study# ©®514850; reviewed in more detail under Section 5.5.1)
bleeding time increased in a dose dependent manner by 13%, 65% and 93% at 1, 10, and 100
mg/kg, respectively, 1 hour after the infusion compared to the saline dosed Day 1
measurement, corresponding to approximately 0.6, 5, and 55-fold the exposure at the
recommended dose based on Cmax. Additionally, minimal hemorrhage and myocardial
degeneration in the heart and hemorrhage in the urinary bladder of one female was noted at
necropsy.

Data (presented by the FDA):

Study# © 1015-086: Tisotumab inhibited TF:FVIla induced FXa generation by 19% at the
highest concentration of 0.9 g/mL. Tisotumab led to delayed clotting by 2.5-fold at the highest
concentration of 10 pg/mL; however, it did not inhibit clot formation.

Study# © 64005: In the standard PT assay, neither tisotumab or tisotumab vedotin up to 20
pg/mL had an effect on prothrombin time in human or monkey pooled plasma (single
experiment). In the diluted (x3000) PT assay, a 1.4-fold increase in PT was seen with both
tisotumab and tisotumab vedotin at 20 pg/mL compared to the control in human plasma; the
variation between replicates was greater than those seen in the standard PT assay. In monkey
plasma, there appears to be a trend towards increased PT; however, the variation between
replicates in the control sample were too large to reliably compare to the treatment groups. In
the 13-week repeat-dose monkey toxicology study, a 10-20% increase in PT at an exposure
(Cmax) >8-fold higher than in this assay (100 g/mL) was observed; no change in PT was
observed with tisotumab vedotin near the highest dose (100 g/mL) tested in this assay. Per
the Applicant, the diluted assay was run for the first time and was not optimized; therefore, the
results may not accurately reflect those that may be obtained had the assay been optimized.

Study# HxTFADC-N-Study-Report-0361: Tisotumab increased time to clot and clot formation
time in a dose dependent manner up to 2.6-fold and 1.6-fold, respectively, compared to control
in human whole blood (single donor) at 20 pg/mL in the rotational thermoelectrometry assay;
the maximal clot formation was not affected by tisotumab. Tisotumab delayed thrombin
generation in plasma; the lag time increased by 15-fold, and thrombin activity decreased by 20-
fold at 20 ug/mL compared to control in the calibrated automated thrombography (CAT) assay.
