In the GCT1015-04 study, all subjects received the current, comprehensive OMP. This OMP.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA disagrees with portions of the Applicant’s statements. FDA conducted an independent
analysis of ocular toxicity focusing on the GCT1015-04 and Pool 2 safety populations. FDA’s
search strategy for ocular toxicity relied on identifying preferred terms which connotated ocular
toxicity and this strategy produced results similar to the Applicant’s for overall frequency of
ocular toxicity in GCT1015-04 and in Pool 2, including the frequencies of Grade 1 and Grade 2
adverse reactions.

The most common ocular toxicities in Pool 2 were: conjunctival adverse reactions (40%), dry
eye (28%), and corneal adverse reactions (21%). The frequencies of these events were similar in
the GCT1015-04 population. Differences in frequencies reported by the FDA and the Applicant
were due to grouping of preferred terms. FDA’s groupings of preferred terms are listed at the
end of this section. Ocular toxicity led to discontinuation of tisotumab vedotin in 6% of patients
in the Pool 2 population.

FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s description of Grade 3 and Grade 4 ocular toxicity in the
GCT1015-04 and Pool 2 safety populations. With respect to Grade 3 ocular toxicity, in addition
to the n=3 patients with grade 3 ulcerative keratitis © in GCT1015-04 and n=1
additional patient with grade 3 conjunctivitis in Pool 2 reported by the Applicant, there was n=1
patient who had grade 3 ulcerative keratitis © in GCT1015-04 in the 90-day DSUR. There
were also n=2 patients who developed corneal ulcers (grade 3 keratitis) >30 days after the last
dose of tisotumab vedotin ©® in GCT1015-04. Although these toxicities fall outside the
treatment-emergent window, FDA considered these events in the overall safety assessment of

tisotumab vedotin.

worsening to grade 4 keratitis. She experienced rupturing of her corneal ulcer requiring corneal
transplantation.
