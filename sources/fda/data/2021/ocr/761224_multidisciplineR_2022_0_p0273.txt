5.1.5 Quantitative Measurement Properties

To assess the quantitative measurement properties of the ASD, the applicant used data from a
multicenter, prospective, observational study, in which there were 276 asthma participants (250
adults and 26 adolescents; mean age [SD] = 42.9 [16.4]; 70% female [n=192]; 70% White
[n=195]; mean asthma duration [SD] = 23.3 [16.8] years) who completed the ASD twice daily
for 28 days. Participants also completed the first 6 items of the ACQ, AQLQ12+, and the Work
Productivity and Activity Impairment Questionnaire: Asthma (WPAI-Asthma; subjects aged 16
and older only) at each study visit, and the Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC) at Visit
2 and Visit 3.

Data were analyzed as follows:
e Item pool analyses (means, standard deviation, floor effect, ceiling effect, percentage of
missing, and inter-item correlation) were performed on baseline values to identify poorly
performing items as candidates for deletion.

e Factor and Rasch analyses were conducted to further support content validity, explore
dimensionality, and test for measurement gaps.

e The ASD’s measurement properties were analyzed, assessing internal consistency and
test-retest reliability, construct and known-groups validity, and ability to detect change.

Results from these analyses showed:

e No notable ceiling effects (maximum response >25%) were obtained for the ASD items
at Visit 1; however, there was a large daily floor effect (minimum response >25%) for
these items ranging from 39.3% to 67.7% of the sample. Weekly floor effect (defined as
selecting the “No Symptom” responses for 7 consecutive days) from Day 2 to Day 8 for
each item ranged from 23.1% to 44.9%.

e The one-dimensional model fitted to the ASD items for the confirmatory factor analysis
had the following fit statistics: confirmatory fit index (CFI) = 0.952, root mean square
error of approximation (RMSEA) = 0.179, and weighted root mean square residual
(WRMR) = 1.415.

No items were identified as mis-fitting the Rasch model as indicated by no significant
chi-square results (p >0.05); in addition, the items displayed fit residuals within the +2.5
range (indicating optimal fit), except for one item which exhibited a fit residual of 2.641.
