NDA 215206
Qulipta (atogepant)

II. Interdisciplinary Assessment

3. Introduction

Atogepant is an orally-administered calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist.
CGRP is widely distributed in the central and peripheral nervous systems. Evidence suggests that
CGRP plays an important role in migraine attacks, as CGRP levels have been demonstrated to
increase during an acute migraine attack, and infusions of CGRP have been demonstrated to
induce migraine-like headaches in patients with a diagnosis of migraine (Goadsby and Edvinsson
1993; Lassen et al. 2002).

Several agents that inhibit the CGRP pathway have been approved for the treatment of migraine
in adults. Four monoclonal antibodies (erenumab, galcanezumab, fremanezumab, and
eptinezumab) that target CGRP (the peptide), or its receptor, have been approved for the
preventive treatment of migraine in adults (comprising both chronic migraine [CM] and episodic
migraine [EM]). These drugs are administered either subcutaneously or intravenously. Two
small molecules that are orally administered have also been approved for the acute treatment of
migraine in adults (ubrogepant and rimegepant) and, most recently, rimegepant was also
approved for the preventive treatment of EM in adults.

The approach to the evaluation of efficacy in development programs for the preventive treatment
of migraine is well established and includes a comparison between treatment and placebo in the
reduction in the mean monthly migraine days and mean monthly headache days in a double-blind
treatment period from baseline. Several additional efficacy endpoints are also generally
evaluated (e.g., the number of patients with at least a 50% reduction in mean monthly migraine
days and the use of monthly acute migraine treatments). Development programs for the
preventive treatment of migraine have also sought to evaluate the impact of migraine on patients’
daily functioning through the use of existing and novel patient-reported outcome measures.

CGRP has been described in the literature as a microvascular vasodilator that has a role in
compensatory vasodilatation in the setting of ischemia.
