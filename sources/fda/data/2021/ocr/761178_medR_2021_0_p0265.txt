Over the next several days, the subject displayed increased agitation, irritability, confusion, and
visual hallucinations. The subject was started on pregabalin and risperidone and the delirium,
unrest, and irritability gradually improved. The subject remained disoriented to time and place.

He was discharged on Study Day 238 with the final diagnosis of behavioral disorder and
delirium due to dementia associated with delayed onset Alzheimer’s disease. The event of
delirium remained unresolved. Study treatment was permanently discontinued as a result of
the event.

Reviewer comment: Confounding factors in this case exist as the subject may have had
worsening of his Alzheimer’s disease that contributed to the delirium and had as past medical
history symptoms of hallucinations (although none had required hospitalization previously). A
role for aducanumab in the event of delirium cannot be ruled out as the symptoms started
within a month of being diagnosed with symptomatic ARIA-E. Although repeat MRI scan showed
resolution of ARIA-E, it is possible that the event of ARIA-E contributed to worsening delirium.

Cardiac arrest
Six SAEs with the preferred term of cardiac arrest were reported in six aducanumab-treated
subjects in Studies 103, 301, and 302.

One case reported events of seizure, cardiac arrest, and symptomatic ARIA. | note a likely role
for aducanumab in the event of seizure in a subject with symptomatic ARIA-E and summarize
the narrative here. A role for aducanumab in the event of cardiac arrest cannot be established

due to the confounding event of seizure which preceded cardiac arrest.
