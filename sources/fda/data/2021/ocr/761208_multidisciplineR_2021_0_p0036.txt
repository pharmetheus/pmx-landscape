5.3 Pharmacology

Primary pharmacology

Original studies were submitted to support the mechanism of action of tisotumab vedotin and
the unconjugated TF-directed antibody tisotumab. Key studies that support the label are
summarized below.

Binding Affinity

Study# © 1015-083: Binding of tisotumab and tisotumab vedotin to TF was evaluated using
surface plasmon resonance, ELISA, and flow cytometry assays. Binding to TF via surface
plasmon resonance demonstrated a KD of 3.15 nM for tisotumab. Avidity studies using DNA
shuffling between human and murine TF resulted in loss of tisotumab binding to TF when the
human sequence encoding amino acids 42 to 84 was replaced by the murine sequence;
tisotumab did not bind mouse TF. Tisotumab and tisotumab vedotin bound to the recombinant
extracellular domain of TF in a dose dependent manner with similar ECso values of 2.5 nM (373
ng/mL) and 3.1 nM (469 ng/mL), respectively. Additionally, membrane binding to TF expressing
(>300,000 TF molecules/cell) A431 epidermoid carcinoma, BXPC3 pancreatic adenocarcinoma,
and MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells resulted in ECso values for tisotumab in the range of 0.19
nM (30 ng/mL) to 1.01 nM (150 ng/mL). As seen with binding to recombinant extracellular
domain of TF, membrane binding of tisotumab and tisotumab vedotin to pancreatic
adenocarcinoma cell line HPAF-II (>350,000 TF molecules/cell) resulted in similar ECso values of
2.08 nM (313 ng/mL) and 2.30 nM (346 ng/mL), respectively, suggesting that conjugation of the
TF specific antibody does not affect the binding affinity.

Study# © 1015-088: Human TF shares 94.1%, 98.6%, 75.8%, 57.3%, and 56.4% amino acid
sequence homology with TF from cynomolgus monkey, chimpanzee, dog, rat, and mouse,
respectively. Cross-reactivity of tisotumab was evaluated using immunohistochemistry, flow
cytometry, and ELISA assays. Tisotumab showed cross reactivity with cynomolgus TF by
immunohistochemistry, but not with dog, pig or rabbit TF as evaluated using normal kidney
tissues where TF is expressed in human renal corpuscles under normal physiological conditions;
however, the conservation of TF expression in renal corpuscles across the different species was
not confirmed. In a flow cytometry assay, tisotumab bound human embryonic kidney HEK293F
cells expressing exogenous TF from human, chimpanzee, and cynomolgus monkey with similar
ECso values of 8.3 ng/mL, 6.8 ng/mL, and 6 ng/mL, respectively, with approximately 100%
binding achieved at <100 ng/mL. Tisotumab did not bind to HEK293F cells expressing
exogenous mouse, rat, rabbit, pig, or dog TF. Additionally, tisotumab did not cross-react with
recombinant mouse TF in an ELISA assay.
