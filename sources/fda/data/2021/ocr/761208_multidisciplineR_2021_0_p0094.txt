Statistical Analysis Plan and Amendments

The Applicant's Description:
The current version of the statistical analysis plan (SAP), version 7.0, was finalized on 17 Jun

2019, prior to the database lock. There were no changes to the analysis of the primary endpoint
(confirmed ORR per IRC). The trial was designed such that by applying a one-sided exact
binomial test at the 0.025 significance level, the planned sample size of 100 subjects provided
280% power to reject an ORR of 11%, the combined ORR for available second-line therapies, or
less for true ORRs in the range 21% - 25%, representing clinically relevant improvements over
available second-line treatments.

Key analysis populations defined include:
¢ The Full Analysis Set (FAS) and Safety Set, which included all subjects who received at
least one dose of tisotumab vedotin. Since the FAS and the Safety set are identical, all
safety analyses used the term FAS as the analysis population.

Primary analysis on confirmed ORR was performed on the FAS so that any treated subjects with
missing information regarding response to treatment were counted as non-responders. ORR
including unconfirmed responders was also analyzed, and confirmed ORR was analyzed by
subgroup using a forest plot. Further, confirmed ORR as well as confirmed and unconfirmed
ORR, as assessed by the investigator, were analyzed and summarized in the same way.

DOR (months) was listed for all subjects in the FAS with a confirmed best overall response of CR
or PR and presented graphically as well as summarized using survival analysis methods.
Six-month DOR rate (ie, percentage of subjects with DOR 26 months) and 95% Cl were
summarized using Kaplan-Meier estimates. TTR was summarized in the FAS population
restricted to subjects with a confirmed response using descriptive statistics. PFS and OS were
analyzed in the FAS population and presented graphically as well as summarized using Kaplan
Meier curves and Kaplan-Meier estimates of the median time, the first and third quartiles,
along with approximate 95% confidence interval of the median.
