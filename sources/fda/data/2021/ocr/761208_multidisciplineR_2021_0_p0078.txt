Primary evidence of clinical benefit in the proposed indication at the dosage of 2.0 mg/kg Q3W
IV, up to a maximum dose of 200 mg in patients > 100 kg, is derived from in Study 04 in 101
patients with cervical cancer. In Study 04, the observed ORR by IRC (primary endpoint) was
23.8% (95% Cl: 15.9- 33.3 with a median DOR was 8.3 months (95% Cl: 4.2 months, NR) by IRC.
While a relatively high incidence of Grade > 3 TEAEs (60.4%) and all Grade ocular toxicities
(54.5%) was observed at the proposed dosage in Study 04, the mean (+ SD) relative dose
intensity remained high at 91 (£13) %. In addition, the median time to first dose modification
due to a TEAE was 3.1 months. This is of relevance as the median time to response in Study 04
was 1.4 months (range, 1.1 to 5.1 months) and the majority of responders (19/24 patients with
CR or PR), had responses early i.e. at the first post-baseline scan, within the first two months of
treatment. In addition, the majority of dose discontinuations were due to progressive disease,
with 13/101 (12.9%) of patients requiring dose discontinuation due to TEAEs.

Additionally, as stated in section 6.3.2.1, the Applicant’s exposure-response analyses support
the proposed dose. Exposure-efficacy analyses indicated that the probability of achieving an
objective response increased with increasing ADC exposure Exposure-safety analyses indicated
that increasing tisotumab vedotin exposure at the proposed dosage led to increased probability
of treatment-related (TR) TEAEs leading to dose modifications, TR Grade >3 AEs, TR SAEs and
grade > 2 ocular TEAEs.

toxic but also less efficacious. As part of the review, FDA also considered whether alternative
dosing approaches such as fractionated dosing or a pre-specified dose reduction after the initial
cycles might help mitigate the high incidence of ocular toxicities observed at the proposed
dosage. FDA concluded that evaluating lower doses or alternative regimens may have utility to
