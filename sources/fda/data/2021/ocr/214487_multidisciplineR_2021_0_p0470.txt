ADME

Absorption

Oral bioavailability of CCX168 was found to range from 55-104% in SD rats when
formulated in a vehicle comprised of PEG- ©@® (Study

PC0365_168). Bioavailability studies were not conducted in cynomolgus monkeys.

Distribution

Study PC0632_168:

Protein binding of CCX168 and CCX168-M1 in plasma from CD-1 mice, SD rats,
rabbits, hamsters, dogs, and humans was determined using commercially available
plasma samples. CCX168 and the M1 metabolite were tested at 2.5, 10, and 50 1M
plasma concentrations and subjected to equilibrium dialysis for 4 hours at 37°C. The
mean % bound values for CCX168 and M1 were >99.9% in plasma from all species
tested.

Metabolism

Study PC0623_168:

In vitro metabolism studies were conducted with liver microsomes from humans and
several nonclinical species. Briefly, 1 1M PH]CCX168 was incubated with 1 mg/ml
liver microsomes from various species at 37°C for 4 hours. M1, the major human
metabolite formed in vivo (Figure 3, Table 2), is also formed to a comparable extent by
monkey, rabbit, hamster, and rat liver microsomes (Table 5).
