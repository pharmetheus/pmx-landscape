© Overall, the safety profile of anifrolumab IV assessed at a dose of 300
mg IV every 4 weeks in the adult SLE population that participated in
studies 1013, 04 and 05 appeared to be similar to placebo.

Adverse events were reported in 87% of patients receiving
anifrolumab 300 mg and 79% of patients receiving placebo.

The most commonly reported adverse events (25%) during
anifrolumab treatment, irrespective of causality, were
nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory infection, urinary tract infection,
bronchitis, infusion-related reaction, headache, herpes zoster, back
pain, sinusitis, and cough.

The proportion of patients with serious adverse events was 12% for
anifrolumab 300 mg and 17% for placebo.

The proportion of patients who discontinued treatment due to
adverse events was 4.1% for anifrolumab 300 mg and 5.2% for
placebo.

which to conclude the initial safety of
anifrolumab 300 mg IV every 4 weeks in
subjects with SLE who are receiving standard
therapy. Review of the safety database
demonstrated that anifrolumab-treated

hypersensitivity reactions including
