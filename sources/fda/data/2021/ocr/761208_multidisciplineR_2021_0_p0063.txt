BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {Biologics License Application (BLA) 761208}
{tisotumab vedotin}

The clinical pharmacology review focused on the appropriateness of the proposed dosing
regimen for the general patient population and dosing recommendations for patients with
hepatic impairment (HI).

Dosages ranging from 0.3-2.2 mg/kg Q3W were investigated in the dose escalation study
GEN701; 2.0 mg/kg Q3W IV was selected as the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) and
recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D). The 2 mg/kg Q3W with a dose cap of 200 mg in patients >
100 kg dosing regimen was evaluated in the phase 2, single-arm, open label study GCT1015-04
(Study 04). The observed ORR was 23.8% (95% Cl: 15.9- 33.3%) with a DoR of 8.3 months (95%
Cl: 4.2 months, NR). Exposure-safety analyses identified a relationship between tisotumab
vedotin exposure at the 2 mg/kg dose level and safety findings including the probability of
treatment-related (TR) TEAEs leading to dose modifications, TR Grade 23 AEs, TR SAEs and
grade > 2 ocular TEAEs. Exposure-efficacy analysis at the 2 mg/kg dose level indicated that over
the range of exposures achieved at this dose level, lower exposure was correlated with lower
ORR. Despite limited evaluation of the benefit-risk profile of dosages lower than 2 mg/kg,
further evaluation of alternative dosing regimens to mitigate the high incidence of ocular
toxicities at the proposed dose was not recommended, as lower exposures may lead to lower
efficacy, based on exposure-response relationships at the 2 mg/kg dose level. As further
lowering of efficacy is undesirable given the currently observed ORR and DoR, and considering
the late-line setting, the proposed dose was assessed as acceptable for the proposed recurrent
or metastatic cervical cancer indication.

Population PK analysis indicated that patients with mild HI had higher MMAE exposure (37%
higher average concentration over a dose interval i.e. Cave) compared to patients with normal
function. However, no dose modification is necessary because this increase in MMAE exposure
was not associated with a substantial decrease in tolerability of tisotumab vedotin in patients
with mild HI. As unconjugated MMAE exposures are expected to increase in moderate and
severe hepatic impairment and the proposed dosage of tisotumab vedotin was identified as the
MTD, patients with moderate or severe HI should avoid treatment with tisotumab vedotin.
