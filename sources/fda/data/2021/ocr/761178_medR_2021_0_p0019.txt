The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in CDR-
SB. CDR-SB is an integrated scale that adequately and
meaningfully assesses both daily function and cognition in
early Alzheimer’s disease.

Key secondary endpoints include the MMSE (a widely used
performance-based assessment of cognitive ability), ADAS-
Cog 13 (a cognitive assessment consisting of clinical ratings
and cognitive tasks), and ADCS-ADL-MCI (feedback from
caregivers regarding activities of daily living).

Each of the clinical endpoints captures distinct information
regarding the patient’s disease status.

placebo, resulted in a statistically significant reduction in the change

from baseline on CDR-SB by -0.39, or -22%, over 78 weeks.

Statistically significant treatment effects in favor of high-
dose aducanumab were observed for all three secondary
clinical endpoints: 0.6 (-18%) for MMSE, -1.4 (-27%) for
ADAS-Cog 13, and 1.7 (-40%) for ADCS-ADL-MCI.

Favorable results were observed for the primary and
secondary endpoints across 79 of 80 subgroups of interest.
Results were robust to sensitivity analyses.

Favorable trends were also observed for low-dose
aducanumab for all endpoints, suggesting the presence of a
dose-response relationship.

The breadth of the treatment effect observed
across several instruments assessing cognition,
function, and behavior are meaningful in this
early Alzheimer’s disease population which
does not currently have available treatment
options to slow or delay disease progression.
The effectiveness has not been studied in an
earlier disease population, but there is a
potential for a more meaningful benefit in this
population, given the possible irreversibility of
pathophysiological deterioration responsible
for clinical findings.
