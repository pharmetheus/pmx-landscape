{tisotumab vedotin}

Benefit-Risk Assessment (BRA)

Cervical cancer is the fourth most common cancer in women worldwide. In the U.S., there will be an estimated 14,480 new cases and 4,290
deaths from cervical cancer in 2021. Almost all cases of cervical cancer are associated with HPV infection. The incidence of cervical cancer in the
U.S. has fallen over the past few decades due to the implementation of screening with Pap tests. However, once diagnosed, cervical cancer is a
serious and life-threatening condition with an estimated 5-year survival of 66%. If detected at an early stage, cervical cancer is potentially
curable. Recurrent cervical cancer that is not amenable to local intervention (surgery and/or radiation) or metastatic cervical cancer is
incurable, and treatment is palliative.

For early-stage disease, patients will typically receive surgery or radiation treatment with chemotherapy. For recurrent or metastatic disease,
patients in the US will typically receive first-line systemic treatment with a chemotherapy doublet with bevacizumab. For second and later-line
therapy, treatment options are limited and include single agent chemotherapies such as docetaxel, gemcitabine, ifosfamide, irinotecan, and
others, with modest ORRs of 5%-21% and limited median DORs of 2-4 months. An additional challenge with these treatment options is that
their efficacy following first-line treatment with a chemotherapy doublet with bevacizumab is unknown. Patients with tumors that express PD-
L1 (CPS>1) may receive pembrolizumab which is under an accelerated approval and thus not considered available therapy, based on an ORR of
14% and a median DOR not reached.

The efficacy and safety assessment for tisotumab vedotin was primarily based on results from GCT1015-04, a single arm, international trial
which enrolled 101 patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer. Patients had received <2 prior lines of treatment in the recurrent or
metastatic setting, including a chemotherapy doublet, with bevacizumab, if eligible to receive bevacizumab. Patients received tisotumab
vedotin 2 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Tumor assessments occurred every 6 weeks for
the first 30 weeks, and every 12 weeks thereafter. The primary endpoint was confirmed ORR assessed by IRC according to RECIST v1.1. DOR
was a secondary endpoint.
