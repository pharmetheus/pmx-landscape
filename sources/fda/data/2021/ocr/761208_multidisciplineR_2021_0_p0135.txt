The Applicant's Position:
TEAEs leading to death were infrequent. Most TEAEs leading to death were considered not

related to tisotumab vedotin therapy. The single case of a drug-related AE leading to death was
confounded by comorbidities and underlying disease.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA performed an independent analysis of all patient deaths due to TEAEs and selected other
patient deaths that occurred in GCT1015-04. In GCT1015-04, there were 58 patients who died,
including 8 deaths on study treatment and 50 deaths post-treatment. Of the 8 deaths which
occurred on study treatment, 4 were due to progression of disease, and 4 were due to an
adverse event. Of the 50 deaths which occurred post-treatment, 48 were due to progression of
disease, 1 was due to euthanasia due to progression of disease, and 1 was due to an adverse
event. FDA reviewed narratives for all 4 deaths due to adverse events which occurred on study
treatment. FDA attributes these 4 deaths from adverse events to septic shock related to
tisotumab vedotin, pneumonitis potentially related to tisotumab vedotin, sudden death of
uncertain cause, and multisystem organ failure related to ileus. These 4 deaths are included in
Section 6.1 of product labeling. The narratives describing the deaths are summarized below:
Patient ID: GCT1015-04- © Septic shock
This 64 yo woman had metastatic SCC of the cervix involving liver, lung, and pelvic and extra-
pelvic lymph nodes at study entry. She received the first cycle of tisotumab vedotin. On study
day 10, she was admitted to the ICU with neutropenic sepsis and received antibiotics and
antifungal treatment. CT showed signs of colitis and she underwent colonoscopy on Day 12 and
Day 16. Pathologic samples from each colonoscopy confirmed diagnosis of colitis. On study day
17, patient developed septic shock, and on day 20 she died. FDA agrees with the investigator
and Sponsor that cause of death was septic shock related to tisotumab vedotin.
