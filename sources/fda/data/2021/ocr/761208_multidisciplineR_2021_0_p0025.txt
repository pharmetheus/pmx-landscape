BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {Biologics License Application (BLA) 761208}
{tisotumab vedotin}

The Applicant's Position:

Patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer have poor survival and no standard of care
treatment options in the second line and beyond. With the exception of KEYNOTE-158, results
from trials of currently-used therapies in the second line and beyond were conducted prior to
the adoption of chemotherapy doublet plus bevacizumab as the standard of care first-line
therapy. The trial results do not reflect the current US population being treated after first-line
progression and thus it is difficult to extrapolate the data to current second-line treatment
outcomes for patients. In addition, these trials generally had small sample sizes, often included
unconfirmed response rates in the ORR calculation, and were not independently assessed by an
IRC. These trials also do not have consistent eligibility criteria for first-line therapy: some trials
allowed single agent chemotherapy to be used as the standard of care in the first line therapy,
some included adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapy as well as chemotherapy plus radiation as
prior lines of therapy before the utilization of single-agent chemotherapy, and one excluded
patients subjects who that have received taxanes in the first line. Too few patients derive
clinical benefit from these therapies, including pembrolizumab. Significant medical need
remains for patients whose disease does not respond to or progress after first-line therapy.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees with the Applicant that current standard-of-care first-line treatment for patients in
the U.S. with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer is a chemotherapy doublet with
bevacizumab.

In the Applicant’s table of available therapies, FDA did not consider the following therapies to
be available therapies for patients enrolled to GCT1015-04:

e FDA does not consider single agent bevacizumab as an available 2L therapy for patients
enrolled to GCT1015-04 as most patients enrolled to this trial had already received
bevacizumab in combination with chemotherapy in the first line metastatic setting.

e@ FDAdoes not consider pembrolizumab as an available 2L therapy for patients with
recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer, as this product is under an accelerated approval
and clinical benefit has not yet been confirmed.

e FDA does not consider fluorouracil or mitomycin+cisplatin as available 2L therapies, as
the studies evaluating those products only enrolled patients in the 1L
metastatic/recurrent setting, not in the 2L setting or beyond.

e FDAdid not consider nab-paclitaxel as an available therapy for patients enrolled to
GCT1015-04, as the nab-paclitaxel trial excluded patients who had received prior
paclitaxel. All patients enrolled to GCT1015-04 had received prior paclitaxel.
