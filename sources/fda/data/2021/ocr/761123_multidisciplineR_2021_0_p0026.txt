Aspirin, corticosteroids, hydroxychloroquine and belimumab are FDA-
approved for treatment of SLE

Other therapies currently used in SLE management are used off-label

The toxicities associated with both the
approved drugs for SLE as well as off-label
treatments commonly used to treat SLE
contribute to the long-term morbidity and
mortality observed in patients with SLE.

Only one targeted therapy —belimumab--has
been approved for SLE in the last 60 years.?? It
targets one pathway and is therefore not
effective in all patients or for all SLE disease
manifestations. 13

There is a significant unmet medical need for
safe and efficacious treatment for adults with
SLE.

© Studies CD-IA-MEDI-546-1013 (1013), D3461000004 (04), and

D3461C00005 (05) were multicenter, international, randomized,
double-blind, placebo-controlled studies that evaluated the efficacy
and safety over 52 weeks of 300 mg IV anifrolumab every 4 weeks
versus placebo every 4 weeks in 925 adult subjects with active SLE
receiving standard therapy.

Primary endpoint was the week 52 BICLA response rate comparing
the anifrolumab IV group versus placebo group in phase 3 study 04.

Primary endpoint in phase 2 study 1013 was week 24 SRI-4 plus OCS

tapering. Primary endpoint in phase 3 study 05 was week 52 SRI-4.

The Applicant has submitted substantial
evidence meeting the evidentiary standard for
the clinical effectiveness of anifrolumab 300
mg IV every 4 weeks in subjects with SLE who
are receiving standard therapy based on one
adequate and well-controlled phase 3 study
and confirmatory evidence from two
additional studies. These studies enrolled a
population of subjects who were generally
