Available therapies in this treatment setting include single agent chemotherapies with ORRs
ranging from 5-21% and median DORs ranging from 2-4 months. One important caveat in
interpreting these ORRs/DORs is that these data are from small trials conducted prior to

ORR and DOR) in the current era of second-line or later treatment for cervical cancer following
first-line treatment with a chemotherapy doublet and bevacizumab is unknown. However, the
review team expects that the efficacy of several of the available single agent chemotherapies
would be less in the current treatment landscape, after bevacizumab-containing doublet
chemotherapy.
As a result, the review team concludes that tisotumab vedotin represents a new treatment
option for patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer who have received ≤2 prior lines
of systemic treatment in the metastatic setting, including doublet chemotherapy, with
bevacizumab if eligible. This patient population has a significant unmet medical need for better
therapies.
FDA review team recommends granting accelerated approval to tisotumab vedotin for the
following indication:
Tivdax is a tissue factor-directed antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate indicated for the
treatment of adult patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer with disease
progression on or after chemotherapy.
This indication is approved under accelerated approval based on tumor response rate and
durability of response. Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon
verification and description of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

2 Therapeutic Context
2.1

Analysis of Condition

The Applicant’s Position:
According to National Cancer Institute (NCI) estimates, more than 291,000 patients are living
with invasive cervical cancer in the US, and more than 4,290 women in the United States (US)
were estimated to have died from the disease last year (National Cancer Institute (NCI) 2020).
An estimated 16% of patients with cervical cancer are first diagnosed with metastatic disease
(National Cancer Institute (NCI) 2020). For patients diagnosed in stages I through III, the
chances of developing recurrent or metastatic disease after first-line treatment increase with
disease stage. Almost half of patients with stage I cervical cancer will see their disease progress
to become recurrent or metastatic, while for patients with stage III cervical cancer this figure is
nearly 65% (Kantar Health 2020). While uncommon, due to immunization and screening,
cervical cancer remains the fourth most common and fourth deadliest cancer in women
globally with a 5 year survival rate in the US of 17% (Bray 2018)(National Cancer Institute (NCI)
2020). No curative therapy is available for these patients.
The FDA’s Assessment:
FDA clarifies that no curative therapy is available for patients with metastatic cervical cancer or
for patients with recurrent cervical cancer not amenable to local treatment. Other patients may
have curative treatment options. Otherwise, FDA agrees with the Applicant’s description of
cervical cancer.

2.2

Analysis of Current Treatment Options

First-Line Treatment
For the vast majority of patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer who are not
surgical candidates, palliative chemotherapy represents the only treatment option.
In the first-line, treatment of recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer is paclitaxel + cisplatin or
paclitaxel + topotecan combined with bevacizumab (if eligible). Current data indicate that
approximately half of all US patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer receive
bevacizumab as part of a first-line treatment regimen (Kantar Health).
Treatment in the Second Line and Beyond
Patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer that has progressed on first-line therapy
have limited treatment options, and no standard-of-care therapy has been identified in the
second-line setting.
Current National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines for second-line treatment
of recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer include pembrolizumab as a category 2A therapy
option supported by a contemporary single-arm trial, and a host of chemotherapy agents as
category 2B options supported by historical data developed before the adoption of

(DOR) was not reached with median 10.2 months of follow up, and responses were seen in 14%

5 Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
5.1

Executive Summary

The FDA’s Assessment:
Tisotumab vedotin (Tivdak) belongs to the pharmacological class of tissue factor (TF)-directed
antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate, composed of a TF specific human IgG1κ antibody
linked to the microtubule disrupting agent monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE) via a proteasecleavable maleimidocaproyl-valyl-citrullinyl-p-aminobenzyloxycarbonyl (mc-val-cit-PABC) linker.
The average drug to antibody ratio is approximately 4. Tisotumab vedotin is administered by
intravenous infusion once every 3 weeks and is proposed for the treatment of adult patients
with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer with disease progression on or after chemotherapy
at a recommended dose of 2 mg/kg. The nonclinical development program for tisotumab
vedotin and the cytotoxic payload, MMAE, consisted of studies in mice, rats, and monkeys to
evaluate the primary and safety pharmacology, general and developmental toxicology, and
genotoxicity. During the nonclinical development, tisotumab vedotin was referred to as IgG11015-011-vcMMAE or HuMax-TF-ADC (IgG1-1015-011 or HuMax-TF for the unconjugated
antibody, tisotumab) and MMAE as SGD-1010.
Human tissue factor is a 43-47 kDa plasma membrane glycoprotein, consisting of an
extracellular domain (aa 1-218), a hydrophobic transmembrane domain (aa 220-242), and a
cytoplasmic domain (aa 243-263). Tissue factor is the main physiological initiator of the blood
coagulation cascade. The extracellular domain binds to serine protease factor VII (FVII) to
activate coagulation. The TF:FVIIa complex initiates the extrinsic pathway of blood coagulation
by producing activated FX (FXa) and FIX (FIXa) resulting in production of thrombin and clot
formation composed of insoluble fibrin and activated platelets. In addition to its role as a procoagulant activator, TF plays a role in both vasculogenesis in the developing animal and
angiogenesis in normal and malignant adult tissues.

The Applicant’s Description:
Basic Study Design
This is a single arm, international, multicenter trial of tisotumab vedotin in subjects with
recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer. Eligible subjects had experienced disease progression
during or after treatment with a chemotherapy doublet in combination with bevacizumab (if
eligible to receive bevacizumab) and had received no more than 2 prior systemic treatment
regimens for their recurrent or metastatic disease. Subjects were treated with IV tisotumab
vedotin 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W. Imaging was obtained every 6 weeks for the first 30 weeks and then
every 12 weeks thereafter, continued until the subject experiences IRC verified disease
progression, began a new anti-cancer therapy, withdrew from the trial, or had died, whichever
came first. For subjects who achieved a response, a repeat scan was obtained no earlier than
4 weeks (28 days) after the first scan to confirm the response. Survival status was assessed
every 60 days (±7 days), beginning from the day of last IMP dose, or more frequently around
the time of a database lock. The study schema is shown in Figure 7.

The Applicant’s Position:

in GCT1015-04. In earlier trials (GEN701, GEN702), dose reductions in response to observed
AESIs were added to protocols as amendments. As such, dose reductions were not used as
extensively to manage AESIs, particularly ocular events, in the populations for the pooled

measures in the GCT1015-04 trial. Safety-related dose modification criteria in the GCT1015-04
trial may have contributed to a higher percentage of dose interruptions (46.5%) among these
subjects relative to subjects in Pool 1 (15.8%), which may have allowed them to remain on
treatment longer with fewer discontinuations due to TEAEs.

Protocol-defined ocular mitigation plan (OMP) measures intended to limit the risk of ocular AEs
with tisotumab vedotin administration have evolved over the course of the clinical
development program. The initial version of a protocol-defined OMP for tisotumab vedotin was
introduced in the GEN701 and GEN702 trials in June 2016, with additional measures added in
October 2016, December 2016, July 2017, and March 2018 (see July 2019 FDA Information
(b) (6)
(b) (4)

). Thus, ocular safety data from trials
that enrolled subjects before or during the time period of OMP refinement may reflect ongoing
changes. The integrated safety pooled analysis groups include subjects from GEN701 and
GEN702 who were treated during this period of OMP refinement. The GCT1015-04 trial began
enrollment in June 2018. The current and most comprehensive protocol-defined OMP in
GCT1015-04 was implemented from initiation onward and all subjects treated in GCT1015-04
received this current OMP. It included prophylactic measures (vasoconstrictor eye drops and
cold packs during infusion as well as steroid and lubricating eye drops throughout each
treatment cycle), symptom-induced ophthalmological exams, and dose modifications based on
low grade ocular events.
Ocular TEAEs in subjects by OMP status at Cycle 1 (per the defined measures in the GCT1015-04
protocol) are summarized in Table 35. Overall, results showed that when the current and most
comprehensive OMP was implemented as per the GCT1015-04 protocol, subjects had a
reduced and consistent incidence of ocular TEAEs (range 54.5% to 58.5% across groups)
compared to subjects who received earlier or variable implementation of the OMP (75.0% in
Pool 2 and 72.6% in Pool 3). The reduction in ocular TEAEs seen with the current OMP appeared
primarily driven by the difference in reports of conjunctivitis (reported in 30.3% of subjects with
GCT1015-04 protocol-defined OMP at Cycle 1 versus 53.6% of subjects without in Pool 3) (ISS
Table 10.3.12). Corneal disorders were consistent across all groups and irrespective of the OMP
(range 18.8% to 21.1%) with the exception of Pool 3 wherein they were reported in 3.6% of
subjects who had not received the current OMP measures at Cycle 1. Preferred terms under
corneal disorders included keratitis, punctate keratitis, ulcerative keratitis, and vital dye
staining cornea present. In addition, a small numerical increase in dry eye was observed (range
24.5% to 26.8%) in subjects who received the OMP as per the GCT1015-04 protocol versus
those subjects who did not (12.5% in Pool 2 and 21.4% in Pool 3) (ISS Table 10.3.12).

Bleeding Events

Data:
Tisotumab vedotin is an ADC-targeting tissue factor, which is the molecular initiator of the
coagulation cascade after vascular injury in normal physiology. For this reason, bleeding was
identified as an AESI for tisotumab vedotin. Pelvic bleeding is a well-known complication of
cervical cancer because of local tumor growth in vaginal and pelvic regions. In addition, as
irradiated areas of the body are more prone to bleeding, prior pelvic radiotherapy, may
increase the risk of bleeding. Bleeding events have been commonly reported in trials of
therapies for cervical cancer. Patients with a history of bleeding conditions or severe bleeding
events were typically excluded from trials of tisotumab vedotin.
The safety analysis of bleeding AEs in subjects treated with 2.0 mg/kg tisotumab vedotin 1Q3W
focused on SMQs detailed in the ISS.

8.2.10 Integrated Assessment of Safety
Data:
The integrated safety profile of tisotumab vedotin in this application is based upon a total of
272 subjects with malignant tumors, primarily cervical cancer. The pivotal trial GCT1015-04
consists of 101 subjects with previously treated, recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer. All
subjects received the recommended dose of 2.0 mg/kg of tisotumab vedotin 1Q3W. Overall
exposure is adequate to support the characterization of the safety profile of tisotumab vedotin.
At 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W most AEs were manageable with either supportive or preventative care or
dose modifications. The overall characteristics of the population studied in GCT1015-04 are
representative of, and generalizable to, subjects with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer
who have received at least one prior systemic therapy. AESIs for tisotumab vedotin are ocular
AEs, epistaxis, and peripheral neuropathy. Of those, ocular AEs and peripheral neuropathy have
been classified as important AESIs. Most ocular events were grade 1 or 2, were transient, and
achieved resolution. The protocol-defined OMP measures are effective. With these measures,
most events are low grade and resolve or improve quickly. The measures also reduced
frequency of ocular events and led to fewer treatment discontinuations. Peripheral neuropathy
AESIs were mostly grade 1 and 2 and were manageable with dose modifications and standard
supportive care. Major bleeding events were typically associated with tumor location, were

The full models had the following structure: logit(ORR) ~ Intercept + Exposure + AGE + WT + SUMDIAM +
ALB + LDH + TFE + factor(RENAL2) + factor(RENAL3) + factor(ECOG)+ factor(SQUAM) + factor(RADI2) +
factor(BEVA), where SQUAM is 1 for patients with squamous cancer, RADI2 is 1 for patients with prior
radiation therapy, BEVA is 1 for patients with prior bevacizumab therapy.
The relationships between exposure and TTE were first characterized using base Cox proportional
hazard (CPH) models that describe the marginal effect of exposure on survival without consideration of
covariates. Linear dependence of log(hazard) on exposure was tested. A significance level of α = 0.05
was used for model selection and for evaluation of the exposure coefficients of the selected models. The
covariates were added linearly in the log hazard scale as follows:
log(hazard)~ Exposure +AGE + WT + SUMDIAM + ALB + LDH +TFE+ factor(RENAL2) + factor(RENAL3) +
factor(ECOG) + factor(SQUAM) + factor(RADI2) + factor(BEVA).
Covariate analysis was not conducted for DOR as DOR analysis data included only 24 patients, not
sufficient for a meaningful covariate analysis. The main exposure efficacy analyses results are
summarized in Table 53. For all significant exposure-efficacy relationships, the relationship with MMAE
exposure was opposite to the relationship with ADC exposure even though there were no strong
correlations between ADC exposure measures and MMAE exposure measures. Note that the ER
relationships have not been fully characterized given the data included in ER analyses were from studies
at one dose level.

ADC Cycle 1 Cmax remained statistically significant.
