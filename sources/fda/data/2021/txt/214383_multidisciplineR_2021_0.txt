PEPAXTO (melphalan flufenamide)

1 Executive Summary
1.1.

Product Introduction

Melphalan flufenamide is a peptide conjugated alkylating drug. Similar to other nitrogen
mustard drugs, crosslinking of DNA is involved in the antitumor activity of melphalan
flufenamide.
The recommended dose of melphalan flufenamide is 40 mg intravenously over 30 minutes on
day 1 of each 28-day treatment cycle in combination with dexamethasone.
Melphalan flufenamide is administered in combination with dexamethasone until disease
progression or until unacceptable toxicity.

1.2.

Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness

The review team recommends accelerated approval of melphalan flufenamide for the following
indication:
In combination with dexamethasone for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed
or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least 4 prior lines of therapy and
whose disease is refractory to at least one proteasome inhibitor, one
immunomodulatory agent and one CD38-directed monoclonal antibody.
The recommendation for accelerated approval is based on the overall response rate (ORR) and
duration of response (DOR) in the subpopulation of 97 patients in trial op-106 who received at
least 4 prior lines of therapies and were refractory to a proteasome inhibitor (PI),
immunomodulatory drug (IMiD) and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody. The overall response
rate(ORR) per investigator in this patient population was 23.7% [95% Confidence interval (CI)
:15.7, 33.4]. The median duration of response (DOR), was 4.2 months [95% CI :3.2, 7.6].
Additional information from confirmatory trials is needed to verify the benefit of melphalan
flufenamide in patients with multiple myeloma.
Trial OP-106 is a single arm, open label, Phase 2 multicenter trial that enrolled patients with
relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) and who received at least 2 lines of prior
therapy including an IMiD and a PI. A total of 157 patients accepting a central venous catheter
and with estimated creatinine clearance by Cockcroft-Gault formula ≥45mL/min were enrolled.
Patients with primary refractory disease (i.e. never responded with at least minimal response to
any prior treatment) were excluded. The primary endpoint was ORR, as assessed by the
Investigator. The ORR was 26.1% (95% CI: 18.44%, 34.89%) in the Applicant defined triple-class
refractory subpopulation.

The safety evaluation was based on the safety population (n=157) of the pivotal trial, OP-106.
The median duration of treatment was 15.7 weeks (range, 4.1-99.1 weeks). The median
number of treatment cycles was 3 ( range, 1-17). A total of 7.6% of patients died within 30 days
of last dose, 2.5% of these patients died within 30 days of the last dose of melphalan
flufenamide. Approximately 50% of the patient population had at least 1 serious adverse event
(SAE). The SAEs that occurred in more than 5% of the overall population were febrile
neutropenia, pneumonia, and respiratory tract infections. Melphalan flufenamide was
permanently discontinued in approximately 22% of patients. Thrombocytopenia led to drug
withdrawal in 10.3%. Thrombocytopenia was the most common TEAE that led to a dose
reduction in 14% of patients. The most common treatment emergent adverse events (TEAEs)
that occurred in ≥20% of the overall population include anemia, thrombocytopenia,
neutropenia, fatigue, nausea, leukopenia, diarrhea, pyrexia and respiratory tract infection.
Based on an observed increased risk of mortality in non-clinical studies at dosages higher than
the recommended dosage, the USPI will include a limitation of use statement that melphalan
flufenamide is not indicated and is not recommended for use as a conditioning regimen for
transplant outside of controlled clinical trials.
The recommended indication in contrast to the Applicant’s proposed indication is restricted to
patients with RRMM who have received at least 4 prior lines of therapy and refractory to a PI,
IMiD and an anti CD38 monoclonal antibody. The FDAs considered the prior treatment and
lines of therapy of the patient population enrolled on Study OP-106 and efficacy data in the
proposed triple class refractory patient population in the context of available therapies.
Selinexor has regular approval for the treatment of adult patients with RRMM who have
received 4 prior therapies and refractory to 2 PI, 2IMiDs and one CD38 monoclonal antibody; a
more refractory patient population. The ORR for selinexor in this refractory patient population
was 25.3 (95% CI 16.4, 36.0) and the DOR was 3.8 months (95% CI: 2.3, not estimable). The
efficacy of melphalan flufenamide in patients with RRMM treated with 4 prior lines of therapy
and refractory to at least one PI, one IMid and a CD-38 directed monoclonal antibody is at least
comparable to available therapies for this population. Melphalan flufenamide offers a different
safety profile compared to available therapies for the indicated patient population. The benefit‐
risk ratio for melphalan flufenamide supports accelerated approval in a more heavily pretreated
population; patients who have received at least 4 prior lines of therapy and whose disease is
refractory to at least one PI, one IMiD and one CD-38 directed monoclonal antibody than the
proposed line agnostic triple class refractory patient population.
There are residual uncertainties regarding the magnitude of treatment effect of melphalan
flufenamide. The contribution of melphalan flufenamide to the treatment efficacy in the
combination cannot be adequately ascertained in the absence of a comparator arm. The single
agent activity with melphalan flufenamide evaluated in one study OP-12-M1 was 7.7%.
However, the patient population enrolled in OP-12-1 were less refractory than the population
in the pivotal trial and the recommended patient population. There are several limitations to
assessing the activity of single agent dexamethasone from historical trials.

1.3.

Benefit-Risk Assessment

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
Melphalan flufenamide is an alkylating drug. Due to its lipophilicity, melphalan flufenamide is passively distributed into cells and thereafter
enzymatically hydrolyzed to melphalan. Crosslinking of DNA is involved in the antitumor activity of melphalan flufenamide.
Efficacy of melphalan flufenamide in combination with dexamethasone was evaluated in Trial OP-106 a single arm, open label, Phase 2
multicenter trial that enrolled 157 patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) who received at least 2 lines of prior therapy
including an IMiD and a PI. A total of 157 patients accepting a central venous catheter and with estimated creatinine clearance by CockcroftGault formula ≥45mL/min were enrolled. Patients with primary refractory disease (i.e. never responded with at least minimal response to any
prior treatment) were excluded. Patients received melphalan flufenamide 40 mg administered intravenously over 30 minutes through a central
line on day 1 of each 28 day cycle until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Dexamethasone was administered 40 mg orally or
intravenously on days 1, 8, 15 and 22 of each cycle. For patients 75 years of age or older, the dexamethasone dose was reduced to 20 mg.
The recommendation for approval is based on a subpopulation of 97 patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma in trial OP-106 who
were treated with 4 or more lines of therapy and whose disease was refractory to at least 1 proteasome inhibitor, 1 immunomodulatory drug
and 1 CD38 directed monoclonal antibody. The median age of the 97 patients was 65 years (range 35 to 86), 87% were white and 6% were
Black or African American.
Efficacy: Efficacy was established based on overall response rate and duration of response assessed according to the International Myeloma
Working Group criteria by investigators. The overall response rate in the 97 patients was 23.7% (95% confidence interval: 15.7, 33.4). A total of
9.3% of patients achieved a very good partial response and 14.4% achieved a partial response. There were no complete or stringent complete
responses. The median duration of response was 4.2 months (95% Confidence interval: 3.2, 7.6).
Safety: The safety population of trial OP-106 (n=157) served as the primary safety population. Melphalan flufenamide demonstrated an
acceptable safety profile for the patient population. Important safety risks include thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, anemia, infections,
secondary malignancies, and embryo-fetal toxicity. There was an increased risk of mortality at dosages higher than the recommended dosage
in animal studies and limited efficacy or safety data at doses higher than the recommended 40 mg dose in humans. The USPI should include a
limitation of use that melphalan flufenamide is not recommended for use as a conditioning regimen for transplant outside of a controlled
clinical trial. Serious adverse reactions occurred in 40% of patients who received melphalan flufenamide in combination with dexamethasone.

2 Therapeutic Context
2.1. Analysis of Condition

Multiple myeloma is a plasma cell neoplasm characterized by proliferation of a neoplastic
plasma cell which results in the production of a monoclonal immunoglobulin. Multiple myeloma
can present with lytic bone disease, renal disease, anemia, and hypercalcemia. The prognosis of
multiple myeloma is heterogenous. The five-year overall survival can range from 40% to 82%
depending on the stage of newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (Palumbo, 2015). Patients with
relapsed disease have a worse prognosis. A multicenter trial in patients who have received 3
prior lines of therapy and are refractory to both an IMiD and a PI demonstrated a median
overall survival of 13 months (Kumar, 2017). Patients who are refractory to the three commonly
used classes of drugs, proteasome inhibitor, immunomodulatory agent and CD 38 monoclonal
antibodies are reported to have poor outcomes (Gandhi, 2019).

5 Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
5.1.

Executive Summary

Melphalan flufenamide (Pepaxto), also referred to as J1 or melflufen, is a dipeptide comprised
of the amino acid derivatives of the alkylating drug melphalan and para-fluoro-L-phenylalanine.
Melphalan flufenamide is lipophilic, relative to melphalan, but both contain identical bis-(2chloroethyl) amino alkylating moieties. The high lipophilicity of melphalan flufenamide results
in enhanced cellular uptake. Melphalan flufenamide is subject to intracellular cleavage by
esterases to desethyl-melphalan flufenamide, and subsequently or directly by aminopeptidases
to melphalan. The enhanced cellular uptake of melphalan flufenamide results in higher
intracellular concentrations of alkylator, consistent with the greater cytotoxicity and antitumor
activity observed with melphalan flufenamide relative to melphalan under the conditions
tested.
The conversion of melphalan flufenamide to the de-esterified metabolite desethyl-melphalan
flufenamide and melphalan was evaluated in biochemical assays, in cell-based pharmacology
studies, and in an ex vivo study. In a biochemical assay, purified aminopeptidase N hydrolyzed

Pharmacology

Primary pharmacology
Melphalan flufenamide is converted by intracellular enzymes into desethyl-melphalan
flufenamide and the alkylating drug melphalan. Melphalan flufenamide and melphalan both
contain bis-(2-chloroethyl) amino alkylating moieties, which are expected to bind and crosslink
DNA. The ability of melphalan flufenamide to cause DNA damage was demonstrated in multiple
myeloma cell lines. Melphalan flufenamide induced concentration-dependent phosphorylation
of the DNA damage marker protein H2AX (γ-H2AX), and treatment for as little as two hours
caused irreversible DNA damage as determined by acridine orange staining. Melphalan
flufenamide caused greater DNA damage than an equimolar concentration of melphalan.
The conversion of melphalan flufenamide into melphalan was demonstrated in a biochemical
assay with purified peptidase. In the presence of purified aminopeptidase N (0.125 U/mL),
melphalan flufenamide (10 µM) was converted into melphalan with a half-life of ~2.5 minutes.
In contrast, conversion of melphalan flufenamide to melphalan was not observed in the
absence of the enzyme, and the non-enzymatic hydrolysis of melphalan flufenamide to release
the free alkylating moiety occurred with a half-life of ~41 minutes. Aminopeptidase N also
catalyzed the conversion of the metabolite desethyl-melphalan flufenamide into melphalan,
but the rate of conversion of desethyl-melphalan flufenamide was approximately 50-times
slower than that of melphalan flufenamide. Studies demonstrating the conversion of melphalan
flufenamide into desethyl-melphalan flufenamide with purified esterases were not submitted.
The intracellular metabolism of melphalan flufenamide was evaluated in a multiple myeloma
cell line. RPMI 8226 cells were incubated with melphalan flufenamide (0.5, 1, 5 µM) or
melphalan (10, 100 µM) under physiologic conditions. Samples were withdrawn at prespecified
time points and cell lysates were prepared and frozen for later analysis by mass spectrometry.
Cellular uptake of melphalan flufenamide was observed at the first time point, corresponding to
5 minutes of incubation (see Figure 1). Melphalan flufenamide could be quantified after up to
30 minutes of incubation at 1 μM, and after up to 15 minutes or 60 minutes of incubation at 0.5
μM and 5 μM, respectively (data not shown). Levels of melphalan and desethyl-melphalan
flufenamide were always greater than the levels of melphalan flufenamide at corresponding
time points. Exposure (AUC0-last) of melphalan and desethyl-melphalan flufenamide was 27-fold
and 6-fold higher than that of melphalan flufenamide, respectively. The AUC0-last obtained with
100 μM of melphalan (414 nmol*min/mL) was similar to the AUC0-last of melphalan obtained
with 1 μM of melphalan flufenamide (368 nmol*min/mL), consistent with the relativelyVersion date: April 2, 2018

The ex vivo metabolism of melphalan flufenamide and melphalan was evaluated in whole blood
and plasma samples from humans and dogs. Melphalan flufenamide and melphalan (37-38 µM)
were spiked into whole blood and plasma samples and incubated under physiologic conditions.

and separated into blood cell and plasma fractions and frozen, while plasma samples were
directly frozen. Samples were analyzed by mass spectrometry. When melphalan flufenamide
was added to human whole blood, melphalan flufenamide penetrated into the blood cells and
was metabolized primarily into melphalan (see Figure 2). After a temporal delay melphalan was
also found in the plasma compartment of whole blood; however, when melphalan flufenamide
was directly added to human plasma no significant metabolism was observed (see Figure 3).

intracellularly metabolized, and metabolites are transported back into the plasma in a delayed
manner. Similar trends in metabolism were observed with whole blood and plasma samples
from dogs (data not shown). Contrariwise, melphalan flufenamide was rapidly de-esterified to
desethyl-melphalan flufenamide in rat plasma (data not shown), which was attributed to higher
plasma esterase activity in rodents.

Safety Pharmacology
Melphalan flufenamide was evaluated in a GLP-compliant combined cardiovascular and
respiratory safety pharmacology study in rats. Telemetered rats were administered a single

were measured by plethysmography. Melphalan flufenamide treatment was associated with
significant respiratory changes at the end of the infusion including increases in respiratory rate,
expiration time, minute volume, and peak inspiratory and expiratory flow, and a decrease in
inspiration time. The only cardiovascular finding was an increase in heart rate. All findings were
transient and generally normalized within 10 minutes of the end of infusion. ECGs were also
collected as part of the repeat-dose toxicology study in dogs and in the single-dose comparative
toxicology study in dogs; ECG findings were limited to increase in heart rate in the single-dose
comparative toxicology study in dogs.
Melphalan flufenamide was evaluated in a non-GLP-compliant in vitro human ether à-go-go
related gene (hERG) screening assay; the hERG ion current IC50 was 1.6-3.1 μM. A confirmatory
assay was not conducted.
Central nervous system (CNS) safety pharmacology endpoints were incorporated into the
repeat-dose toxicology study in rats. The high dose level of melphalan flufenamide was
associated with slowed body movements and reduced locomotor activity.

A non-GLP single-dose comparative toxicology study was conducted in dogs. Male dogs
(n=3/group) were administered melphalan flufenamide (17.5 mg/kg) or an equimolar dose of
melphalan (10 mg/kg) by 30-minute intravenous infusion and observed for 4 days. In
anticipation of high toxicity, dogs were pretreated with metoclopramide and dexamethasone.
During or immediately after the infusion, both test articles were associated with redness,
swelling, irritation, agitation, vocalization, and vomiting, and melphalan flufenamide was also
associated with tremors. On Days 2-3 the dogs administered melphalan flufenamide were
found dead or sacrificed in moribund condition. The dogs administered melphalan
demonstrated signs of GI toxicity (loose dark stools, liquid feces sometimes with blood, and
emesis) but survived to study termination on Day 4. The main target organs in all animals were
the hemolymphopoietic system and GI tract, the latter of which were associated with mucosal
changes. Melphalan exposure (AUC0-inf) was similar in animals administered melphalan
flufenamide or melphalan. Due to the small number of animals per group, a definitive
assessment of the relative toxicity could not be established.

Other Toxicology Studies

No formal genotoxicity studies were conducted with melphalan flufenamide. Melphalan

two hours caused irreversible DNA damage as determined by acridine orange staining.
Melphalan flufenamide caused greater DNA damage than an equimolar concentration of

6 Clinical Pharmacology
6.1. Executive Summary

Melphalan flufenamide is an alkylating agent proposed for the treatment of adult patients with
multiple myeloma (MM) whose disease is refractory to at least one proteasome inhibitor, one
immunomodulatory agent, and one anti-CD-38 monoclonal antibody. Melphalan flufenamide is
distributed into cells where it is metabolized to melphalan, the active metabolite. The proposed
dosing regimen of melphalan flufenamide is 40 mg via intravenous (IV) infusion over 30 minutes
on Day 1 of every 28-day treatment cycle.
The clinical pharmacology review focused on the appropriateness of the proposed dosing
regimen and dose recommendations for patients with renal impairment. An efficacy subgroup
analysis for ORR as well as multivariate logistic regression using data from Study OP-106
supported the effectiveness of melphalan flufenamide in the general patient population. There
is a trend of increased incidence of Grade ≥ 3 thrombocytopenia and neutropenia with
increasing melphalan exposure; however, there are appropriate dosage modifications in the
event of these adverse reactions. No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with renal
impairment (RI) (CLcr 45 to 89 mL/min) based on a safety subgroup analysis by different renal
function groups.

6.2.1.

Pharmacology and Clinical Pharmacokinetics

Melphalan flufenamide is an alkylating agent of the nitrogen mustard bischloroethylamine type
and cross-linking of DNA is involved in the antitumor activity of melphalan flufenamide. Due to
its lipophilicity, melphalan flufenamide is distributed into cells where it is metabolized to the
active metabolite, melphalan. In cellular assays, melphalan flufenamide inhibited proliferation
and induced apoptosis of hematopoietic and solid tumor cells.

minutes after the end of melphalan flufenamide infusion. Plasma concentrations of the
metabolite desethyl-melphalan flufenamide remain very low (approximate 1% of melphalan
exposure) during and after melphalan flufenamide administration. Following melphalan

Renal Impairment
The elimination pathway of melphalan flufenamide and contribution of renal excretion to
clearance of melphalan following melphalan flufenamide administration have not been
characterized. Linear multiple regression analysis using combined data from Studies OP-103,
OP-104 and OP-107 demonstrated that AUC0-inf and elimination half-life of melphalan increased

mL/min/1.73 m2 for a patient with a BSA of 1.8 m2. However, it is unknown what the clinical
impact is given that there is no exposure-response analysis for safety available in the current
submission.

Food-Drug Interactions
There are no relevant food-drug interactions, as melphalan flufenamide is administered
through IV infusion.
Drug-Drug Interactions
Clinical drug-drug interaction studies have not been performed with melphalan flufenamide.
Melphalan flufenamide is rapidly distributed into cells where it is metabolized to melphalan. It
rapidly disappears in plasma after the end of infusion with a mean elimination half-life of about
2 minutes and there is no late reappearance of measurable melphalan flufenamide
concentrations in plasma after the end of infusion. Therefore, drug-drug interactions are highly
unlikely for melphalan flufenamide.
Melphalan is eliminated from plasma primarily by spontaneous hydrolysis to nonalkylating
metabolites (monohydroxy-melphalan and dihydroxy-melphalan). Therefore, drug-drug
interactions with melphalan caused by inhibition or induction of drug metabolic enzymes are
unlikely.

7.2. Review Strategy

Trial OP-106 was the pivotal trial supporting efficacy of melphalan flufenamide in combination
with dexamethasone in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. Efficacy
evaluation was conducted on the triple class refractory (TCR) population of trial OP-106 as
defined by the Applicant. The protocol defined TCR as a subpopulation of patients who were
refractory or intolerant to at least 1 IMiD, 1 PI, and 1 anti-CD38 mAb. A patient was defined as
having refractory MM if their disease was nonresponsive (i.e., there was failure to achieve a MR
or development of disease progression while on therapy) while on salvage therapy or
progressed within 60 days of the last therapy. A patient was defined as intolerant to a specific
therapeutic drug class if the patient discontinued the drug due to toxicity and did not receive a
drug in the same therapeutic drug class again before entering the current study.
The FDA efficacy analysis for the primary endpoint of ORR and secondary endpoint DOR
focused on the population of patients who had received 4 prior lines of therapy and whose
disease is refractory to one proteasome inhibitor, one immunomodulatory agent and at least
one CD 38 monoclonal antibody (N=97). Patients who were intolerant but not refractory were
excluded. The safety evaluation focused on the entire safety population (n=157) of trial OP-106.

8 Statistical and Clinical and Evaluation
8.1. Review of Relevant Individual Trials Used to Support Efficacy

8.1.1.
OP-106: A Single Arm, Open-Label, Phase 2 Study of Melphalan
flufenamide in Combination with Dexamethasone in Patients with Relapsed
Refractory Multiple Myeloma who are Refractory to Pomalidomide and/or
Daratumumab
Trial Design
OP-106 is a single-arm, open-label, phase 2 trial in patients with RRMM who were treated with
at least 2 prior lines of therapy including an IMiD and a PI. Patients were also required to be
refractory to pomalidomide and/or an anti-CD-38 antibody. Refractory was defined as patients
who relapse while on therapy or within 60 days of last dose.
Patients were treated on a schedule of 28 days. Melphalan flufenamide was given 40 mg on day
1 and dexamethasone 40 mg on days 1, 8, 15 and 22. For patients ≥ 75 years of age the
dexamethasone was reduced to 20 mg using the same schedule. Patients were to continue
treatment until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or it was determined it was not in
the patient’s interest to continue.
The primary objective was to assess ORR defined as patients with ≥ partial response including
sCR, CR, VGPR (very good partial response) and PR (partial response) assessed by investigators
according to IMWG Uniform Response Criteria. The key secondary endpoint was DOR. Other
secondary endpoints included PFS, OS and clinical benefit rate.
Inclusion criteria (summarized)
• 18 years of age or older
• Diagnosis of multiple myeloma with disease progression in need of treatment
• Measurable disease defined as monoclonal protein ≥ 0.5 g/dL by SPEP (serum protein
electrophoresis), ≥ 200 mg/24 hour monoclonal protein in the urine determined by a 24
hour UPEP (urine protein electrophoresis) or serum immunoglobulin free light chain ≥
10 mg/dL and abnormal immunoglobulin kappa to lambda free light chain ratio
• A minimum of 2 prior lines of therapy, including an IMiD and a PI. Refractory to
pomalidomide and/or an anti-CD 38 monoclonal antibody. Refractory was defined as
patients who relapsed while on therapy or within 60 days of last dose of pomalidomide
and/or anti CD 38 antibody

Sample Size
The sample size for the primary analysis was chosen to rule out a minimal threshold level for
ORR of at least 15%. A total number of approximately 150 patients are planned to be enrolled
to achieve 130 patients evaluable for efficacy. Assuming an observed ORR=30%, the exact 95%
confidence interval will be (22.3%, 38.7%) for a sample size of 130.
Statistical Reviewer’s comments:
• The Sponsor proposed that ruling out a minimal threshold of ORR of at least 15%
would represent a clinically relevant treatment effect. The rationale was based on the
assumption that there is no available therapy for the Applicant proposed TCR patient
population and in this patient population ORR>15% represents meaningful clinical
benefit in this heavily pre-treated population. However, it is important to note that
the accelerated approval for selinexor “ for treatment of adult patients who have
received at least four prior therapies and whose disease is refractory to at least two
proteasome inhibitors, at least two immunomodulatory agents, and an anti-CD38
monoclonal antibody was converted to regular approval in 2020. Selinexor would be
considered available therapy for the applicant proposed TCR patient population.
• The Applicant defined a subpopulation of 119 patients identified as triple class
refractory population. Assuming the threshold ORR=15%, 2-sided α=0.05, a sample
size of 119 patients would allow a 2-sided binomial exact test to detect an ORR of ≥
30% against the threshold ORR of 15% with 97% power.

8.1.4 Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness
The efficacy of melphalan flufenamide in patients with RRMM is based on Trial OP-106, a single
arm, open label, Phase 2 multicenter trial that enrolled 157 patients with relapsed or refractory
multiple myeloma (RRMM) and received at least 2 lines of prior therapy including an IMiD and a
PI. A total of 157 patients accepting a central venous catheter and with estimated creatinine
clearance by Cockcroft-Gault formula ≥45mL/min were enrolled. Patients with primary
refractory disease (i.e. never responded with at least minimal response to any prior treatment)
were excluded. The primary endpoint was ORR, as assessed by the Investigator. The Applicant’s
primary analysis was based on the triple class refractory patient subpopulation as defined in
trial OP-106. The FDAs efficacy evaluation focused on the 97 patients who have received 4 or
more lines and therapy and are refractory to at least one PI, one IMiD and one CD38 directed
monoclonal antibody. In this population, the primary endpoint of ORR based on investigator
assessment was 23.7 % [95% CI (15.7, 33.4)] and the median duration of response was 4.2
months [95% CI (3.2, 7.6)].
Efficacy for the indicated population: FDAs restricted indication is based on the efficacy data
considering the patient population enrolled in Study OP-106 and the benefit risk profile in the
context of available therapies. Although, the triple class refractory patient population as
defined by the Applicant had received a median of 5 lines of therapy, only few patients had
received 2 prior lines (n=2) and 3 prior lines (n=15). Additionally, there was uncertainty about
the magnitude of effect of ORR of 26.1% (95% CI:18.4, 34.9) in the context of available therapy.
Selinexor has regular approval for the treatment of adult patients with RRMM who have
received 4 prior therapies and refractory to 2 PI, 2 IMiDs and one CD38 monoclonal antibody, a
more refractory patient population. The ORR for selinexor in this refractory patient population
was 25.3 (95% CI: 16.4, 36.0). The efficacy of melphalan flufenamide in the patient population
patients with RRMM treated with 4 prior lines of therapy and refractory to at least one PI, one
IMid and a CD-38 directed monoclonal antibody is at least comparable to available therapies for
this population.
Isolation of Contribution of Effect: OP-106 trial evaluated the combination of melphalan
flufenamide and dexamethasone in a single arm trial. The single arm trial does not isolate the
treatment effect of melphalan flufenamide from dexamethasone in this combination. The
sponsor submitted trial O-12-M1, an early phase trial which included a cohort of patients who
received single-agent melphalan flufenamide. The patients also received lower dose

trial.

Integrated Assessment of Safety

The safety evaluation focused on the safety population of the pivotal trial, OP-106 (n=157). The
safety population was defined as any patient who received at least one dose of melphalan
flufenamide or dexamethasone. The median weeks of treatment for the overall safety
population was 16.7 weeks with a range of 4.1-99.1 weeks. A total of 12 patients (7.6%) died
within 30 days of last dose of treatment in the overall safety population. A total of 4 of these
patients died within 30 days of last dose of melphalan flufenamide.
For the total population, 49.0% had at least 1 SAE. The majority of SAEs occurred in the System
Organ Classes of Infections and Infestations and Blood and lymphatic system disorders.
A total of 21.7% of patients in the overall safety population had treatment withdrawn due to an
adverse event. Thrombocytopenia led to drug withdrawal in 10.8% of patients. A total of 97
patients (61.8%) of the overall safety population had melphalan flufenamide dose held as a
result of an adverse event. Thrombocytopenia led to a hold in the dose of melphalan
flufenamide in 42.7% of patients and neutropenia in 28.7% of patients. A total of 26.8% of the
patient population had a dose reduction of melphalan flufenamide due to an adverse event.
Thrombocyotpenia was the most common TEAE that led to a dose reduction.
All patients had at least 1 TEAE. The common TEAE that occurred in >20% of the overall
population include anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, fatigue, nausea, leukopenia,
diarrhea, pyrexia and respiratory tract infection. TEAE ≥ grade 3 that occurred in more than
20% of the overall population include anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia and thrombocytopenia.
Laboratory abnormalities which changed in >50% of patients overall include absolute
neutrophil count decrease, hemoglobin decrease, leukocyte count decrease, lymphocyte count
decrease, platelet count decrease and creatinine increase. Grade 3 and 4 laboratory studies
that occurred in >50% of patients were all hematologic laboratory studies including absolute
neutrophil count decrease, leukocyte decrease, lymphocyte decrease, and platelet count
decrease.
Myelosuppression and infections were both determined to be important safety risks and are
addressed in the Warnings and Precautions section of the Prescribing Information. Additional
important safety risks with melphalan flufenamide treatment include increased risk for second
primary malignancies.

Reviewer Comment: No new safety signals were noted based on the integrated safety
assessment. Infections are an important toxicity of melphalan flufenamide, an alkylating
agent. Both SAEs and fatal AEs were reported with the highest frequency in the infections
SOC. The risk for infections with melphalan flufenamide treatment should be included in the
Warnings and Precautions section of the USPI.

8.3 Statistical Issues
There are no major statistical issues identified from this submission. The following caveats may
need to be taken into account in the final decision:
The study treatment was a combination of melphalan flufenamide and dexamethasone. The
single arm trial design does not isolate the treatment effect of melphalan flufenamide .
Evidence of efficacy for single agent melphalan flufenamide was limited. Trial O-12-M1 was a
Phase I/IIa trial that evaluated 40mg melphalan flufenamide as a single-agent in multiple
myeloma patients. One out of 13 patients achieved partial response, which gave an ORR of
7.7%. The Applicant submitted literature data on single agent dexamethasone, however, there
are limitations with assessing the activity of dexamethasone from literature.

8.4 Conclusions and Recommendations
The efficacy of melphalan flufenamide in combination with dexamethasone in the
recommended patient population was based on the observed ORR and duration of response in
trial OP-106 in a subpopulation of 97 patients who received at least 4 prior lines of therapy and
refractory to one PI, one IMiD and at least one CD 38 directed monoclonal antibody. The ORR
by investigator for the recommended patient population was 23.7% (95% CI: 15.7%, 33.4%).
ORR was supported by the median duration of response of 4.2 months 95% CI (3.2, 7.6). No
statistical inferential testing was performed since this was a single arm study.

Myelosuppression and infections are primary toxicities associated with melphalan flufenamide
treatment. The common TEAE that occurred in >20% of the overall population include anemia,
thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, fatigue, nausea, leukopenia, diarrhea, pyrexia and respiratory
tract infection. TEAE ≥ grade 3 that occurred in more than 20% of the overall population
include anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia and thrombocytopenia. Grade 3 and 4 laboratory
studies that occurred in >50% of patients were all hematologic laboratory studies including
absolute neutrophil count decrease, leukocyte decrease, lymphocyte decrease, and platelet
count decrease.
In conclusion, the combination of melphalan flufenamide and dexamethasone offers patients
treated with 4 or more lines of therapy and who are refractory to a PI, IMiD and CD 38 directed
monoclonal antibody a treatment option with a different safety profile to current available
therapies. The clinical and statistical review team recommends accelerated approval for
melphalan flufenamide in combination with dexamethasone for the treatment of adult patients
with relapsed or refractory MM who have received at least 4 prior lines of therapies and whose
disease is refractory to one PI, one IMiD and at least one CD8 directed antibody. A PMR will be
issued to verify and describe the clinical benefit of melphalan flufenamide in a randomized
clinical trial in patients with RRMM.

Data source
Data were sourced from three ongoing melphalan flufenamide studies OP-103 (Phase 3
randomized, open-label confirmatory study), OP-104 (Phase1/2 combination treatment) and
OP-107 (renal impairment PK study) in RRMM patients. Melphalan flufenamide (the parent drug)

analysis was not performed by the applicant for this NDA submission.

The Applicant’s NCA derivation of individual AUC parameters for melphalan may be

Summary and conclusion
Prior report of melphalan Pop PK characterization and the present Applicant’s exploratory
analysis indicated an inverse relationship between body size/ renal function and melphalan
systemic exposure when melphalan flufenamide was given as a flat dose. The covariate effect
appeared to be more prominent for body size (BSA) compared to renal function (CLcr).
Using BSA or CLcr as proxy for exposure, the subgroup and regression analyses of efficacy and
safety indicated no appreciable effect of mild to moderate RI on ORR and safety profile. In
contrast, smaller BSA was associated with increased incidences of Grade 3/4 neutropenia,
thrombocytopenia and dose modifications following the proposed fixed dose regimen of 40 mg.
While the observed correlation was presumably mediated through the effect of body size on
melphalan exposure, the underlying relationship remains to be confirmed by the additional
exposure-response analysis to better inform the potential of dose optimization in patient
subpopulation.
