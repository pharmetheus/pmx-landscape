Conclusion

The Applicant’s proposed 6-point improvement threshold in the 0-100 scaled SF-36 v2.0 Vitality
domain score did not represent a clinically meaningful within-patient improvement. Statistically
significant results were reported on the key secondary endpoint of change from baseline to Week
28 in the 0-100 scaled SF-36 v2.0 Vitality domain-score in ASCEND-NHQ. However, FDA
determined that these results did not substantiate a clinically meaningful improvement of
daprodustat compared to placebo due to the minimal magnitude of changes observed at the item-
and domain-level (using raw and transformed score scales), and modest to small between arm
differences in response rates. In addition, the Hb target of 11 to 12 g/dL studied in ASCEND-
NHQ is higher than the Hb target used in ASCEND-ND and ASCEND-D (i.e., higher than the
target Hb used to establish the efficacy and safety of daprodustat for the approved use), further
calling into question the relevance of the findings in ASCEND-NHQ.

6.1. Efficacy Summary

After conducting a thorough evaluation of the ASCEND program, we agree with the Applicant
that non-inferiority to an ESA comparator, based on hemoglobin response, was demonstrated in
the DD, incident dialysis (ID), and NDD populations, and the DD population using three times a
week dosing. We agree superiority was achieved against placebo for Hb in ASCEND-NHQ, but
we do not agree a clinically important benefit was demonstrated on patient reported outcomes
(See Section 6.3.2).
