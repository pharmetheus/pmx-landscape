NDA 216951
Jesduvrog (daprodustat)

A juvenile toxicity study was conducted in rats with dosing beginning on post-natal day 4
(generally approximates a full-term human neonate) to evaluate the pharmacological
consequence of daprodustat increasing RBC mass during development. The study showed dose-
telated decreases in body weight/body weight gain in both sexes of young rats and delayed
attainment of developmental landmarks in females (e.g., increased the mean age of vaginal
patency and increased the estrous cycle length). Minimal decreases in femur length were also
noted, which correlated with decreased body weight/body weight gain. Decreased body weight
gain and developmental delay is reasonably explained by the impact of excess red blood cell
mass in the young, developing non-anemic rat. While the toxicity profile in juvenile animals was
qualitatively similar to that observed in adults, younger animals appeared more sensitive than
adults (the NOAEL in juvenile rats was below the clinical dose based on body surface area).
These data suggest that the use of daprodustat in pediatric patients warrants caution.

As discussed above, the plasma protein binding of daprodustat is extremely high (approaching
100%) in rat and rabbit, which precluded an accurate estimation of pharmacologically active
(free) drug in rat and rabbit plasma for comparison to human free drug levels. Exposure margins
(see Table 68) for labeling purposes were therefore derived using body surface area
extrapolation. Supporting the use of body surface area extrapolation, this method predicts a 3-
fold animal:human safety margin for gastric erosions/ulcers, an imbalance of which occurred in
clinical trials. In contrast, the use of the maximum plasma concentration (Cy,4x) or the area under
the concentration-time curve (AUC) to determine the animal:human exposure multiple appears
to grossly overpredict clinical safety (e.g., the use of unadjusted AUC produces a safety margin
for gastric erosions/ulcers as high as 845-fold).

Overall, the nonclinical data support chronic clinical use of daprodustat for the treatment of
anemia, but studies consistently demonstrate a narrow therapeutic index with the potential for
severe consequences related to hematocrit overshoot. No other outstanding nonclinical safety
issues of significant concern were observed at clinically relevant exposures in the nonclinical
toxicology program. Therefore, the nonclinical pharmacology/toxicology data support approval
of this NDA.
