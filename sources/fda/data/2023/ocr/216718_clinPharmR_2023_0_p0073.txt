The MOXie trial evaluated the clinical effects of omaveloxolone in Friedreich's Ataxia patients
over 12 weeks at dose levels of 2.5-300 mg/day [Lynch ef a/. 2018]. A small number of patients
displayed increased ALT (12%) or AST (12%) levels which were not related with other indicators
of liver injury such as increased direct bilirubin, decreased albumin, or changes in total protein
[Lynch et a/. 2018]. In another report on the MOXie trial, the increased serum ALT and AST levels
in treated patients were maximal within the first 12 weeks of treatment and declining to baseline
values within 4 weeks following the end of drug administration [Lynch et a/. 2021]. Of the treated
patients, 15 (29%) had maximum ALT elevations >3x ULN of which 80% had normal ALT levels
within 4 weeks after drug termination [Lynch ef a/. 2021]. The increases in ALT and AST were
not related to increased total bilirubin. None of the patients met the criteria for Hy’s law and had
no other symptoms indicative of liver injury. In fact, the treated patients had a small, but
statistically significant, reduction in total bilirubin in relation to baseline and compared to placebo
