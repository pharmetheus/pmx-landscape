TEAEs that occurred in at least 2% of subjects treated with etrasimod and more frequently than
placebo are described below.

e Headache: Headache was the most common TEAE reported by subjects during APD334-
301. There was an imbalance in the proportion of subjects who received etrasimod that
reported headache (9%) compared to placebo (5%). The majority of headache AEs were
mild or moderate. One headache was an SAE (preferred term “migraine”) and is
described in greater detail in Section 8.2.4.2. No AEs of headache led to study
discontinuation. An imbalance was also observed in the pooled data from APD334-302
and APD334-003 (refer to Section 8.2.5.2). The mechanism by which etrasimod may
increase the risk of headache is unknown; however, headache is an adverse reaction
observed in several other S1P receptor modulators, including Zeposia (ozanimod)*,
which is approved for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC. Therefore,
headache will be included in the Adverse Reactions section of the label.

e Elevated liver tests: A greater proportion of subjects who received etrasimod had an AE
of elevated liver tests (6%) compared to placebo (5%). The majority of elevated liver test
AEs were mild or moderate in severity. One AE was considered an SAE and is described
in detail in Section 8.2.4.2. Two subjects were discontinued from study treatment due to
an AE of elevated liver tests (one subject with ALT increased to 8.7 x ULN and one
subject with blood alkaline phosphatase increased to 1.2 x ULN); refer to Section 8.2.4.3
for full details of the AEs. An imbalance was also observed in the pooled data from
APD334-302 and APD334-003 (refer to Section 8.2.5.2).
