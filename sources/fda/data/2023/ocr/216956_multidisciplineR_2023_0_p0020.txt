NDA/BLA Multi-Disciplinary Review and Evaluation NDA 216956
Velsipity (etrasimod)

Dimension

Evidence and Uncertainties

Conclusions and Reasons

@ The overall goal in the treatment and management of UC is to reduce
signs and symptoms of active disease, decrease the underlying
mucosal inflammation, and prevent short-term (e.g., severe bleeding,
bowel perforation) and long-term complications (e.g., colon cancer).
The choice of therapy is guided by the disease severity, extent of
disease, and presence of other manifestations (i.e., extraintestinal
complications, malabsorption).

¢ Therapeutic options for treatment include 5-ASA products (e.g.,
mesalamine), corticosteroids, antibiotics, immunomodulators (e.g.,
AZA, 6-MP, MTX), biologic therapies (e.g., TNFa blockers, anti-integrin
receptor blockers), S1P receptor modulators, and JAK inhibitors.
Corticosteroids are not recommended for long-term use given the
toxicities associated with chronic steroid use. While these
medications are widely used in clinical practice, not all are FDA
approved for the treatment of UC. There remains a need for novel
therapies as not all patients will respond or have continued response
to any given treatment.

Etrasimod is a S1P receptor modulator with
high affinity to S1P receptors 1, 4, and 5,
proposed for the treatment of moderately
to severely active UC. Another S1P.
receptor modulator (that binds with high
affinity to S1P receptors 1 and 5),
ozanimod (ZEPOSIA), is approved for the
same indication.

Additional therapies are needed to treat
moderately to severely active UC, as many
patients do not respond or lose response
over time to the currently available
therapies. Etrasimod represents another
oral, daily treatment option for patients.

Efficacy of etrasimod was demonstrated in one 52-week, randomized,
double-blinded, placebo-controlled “treat-through” trial (APD334-
301) and one 12-week, randomized, double-blinded, placebo-
controlled induction trial (APD334-302).

e In both trials, subjects with moderately to severely active UC were
enrolled. A total of 408 adult subjects with a baseline modified Mayo
Score (mMS) of 5 to 9, including an endoscopic subscore (ES) of at
least 2, were enrolled in APD334-301, of which 121 had prior
biologic/JAK inhibitor exposure. A total of 333 adult subjects with the
same definition of moderately to severely active UC were enrolled in
APD334-302, of which 112 had prior biologic/JAK inhibitor exposure.

The two trials, APD334-301 and APD334-
302, provided evidence for the
effectiveness of etrasimod compared to
placebo for achieving clinical remission in
subjects with moderately to severely active
UC.
