Carcinogenicity

Key Findings:

In a 2-year carcinogenicity study in mice, male and female animals were orally
administered etrasimod at 2, 6, or 20 mg/kg/day. Etrasimod dose-dependently and
significantly increased the mortality rate in high dose males and females compared to
vehicle controls. Drug-related non-neoplastic lesions were observed in the lungs
(minimal to marked alveolar histiocytosis, minimal to moderate alveolar eosinophilic
accumulation, minimal to mild multifocal fibrosis, minimal to mild osseous metaplasia,
and minimal fibrin deposition) in all treated groups and in the thymus (minimally to
moderately increased lymphocytes in the medulla) in the mid and high dose groups.
Additionally, angiomatous hyperplasia was observed in various tissues (bone marrow,
epididymides, heart, liver, subcutis, femorotibial joint) in high dose males and in the
uterus of mid and high dose females with increased incidence compared to control
animals. Etrasimod-related neoplastic lesions included a dose-dependent increase in
the incidence of hemangiosarcomas (multicentric neoplasm) and combined
hemangiomas/hemangiosarcomas in both male and female mice compared to control
animals.

In a 2-year carcinogenicity study in rats, male and female animals were orally
administered etrasimod at 2, 6, or 20 mg/kg/day. Etrasimod did not significantly affect
the mortality rate. Drug-related non-neoplastic lesions were observed in the lymphoid
tissues (increased medullary lymphocytes in the thymus; decreased marginal zone
lymphocytes and increased incidence of extramedullary hematopoiesis in the spleen;
sinus dilation, sinus histiocytosis and/or sinus erythrocytosis/erythrophagocytosis in the
mandibular, mesenteric, iliac, renal and/or mediastinal lymph nodes) and brain (minimal
to mild focal thalamic mineralization) of males and females at all doses as well as in the
liver (basophilic, clear, and/or eosinophilic foci of cellular alteration and minimal to mild
bile duct hyperplasia) of all treated females and males in the mid and high dose groups.
No statistically significant increases in tumor incidences occurred in this study.
