Effect of ADA on ISR AEs

Because of the high rate of ADA in subjects treated with somatrogon (refer to Section 7.7.3), the
question was also raised whether the somatrogon-induced ISRs are associated with
immunogenicity. To explore this, the review team conducted additional analyses of the incidence
of somatrogon-induced AEs of ISR by antidrug antibody status. The results of these analyses are
briefly summarized below.

There was a slightly higher incidence of AEs of ISR in the ADA-positive somatrogon subgroup
compared to the negative ADA subgroup during the main period of Trial CP-4-006: in 40 out of
the 84 subjects (48%) who were ADA-positive and in 8 of the 25 subjects (32%) who were
ADA-negative, respectively (Table 38). Similarly, in the OLE period, among subjects who were
originally randomized to somatrogon, more subjects with positive ADA experienced AEs of
injection site reactions compared to subjects with negative ADA. This observation does not
confirm a relationship between ADA status and ISR.

All AEs of ISR were nonserious, mild or moderate, transient, and self-limiting.

Conclusion

The imbalance observed in the rates of AEs of injection site reactions between somatrogon and
Genotropin groups in Trial CP-4-006 (43% versus 25%) may be related to the Applicant’s
overall strategy of reporting ISR. Not all ISR recorded in the study were labeled as AEs- only
those that met certain criteria. In addition, ISRs were reported weekly and in the Genotropin
group (daily injection), only the ISR that had the highest severity during a week of reporting
were reported as AEs; the less severe ISR in the same week would not be reported as an AE. In
contrast, for the one, weekly somatrogon injection, any ISR that met prespecified criteria were
