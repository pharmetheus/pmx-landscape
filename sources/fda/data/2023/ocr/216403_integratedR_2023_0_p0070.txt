The subject was a 27-year-old white male with IgAN. At baseline, his ALT was 28 U/L, AST
was 71 U/L, ALP was 65 IU/L, and TB was “normal.” On a he started sparsentan.
On © (Day 258), his ALT was 253 U/L, AST 77 U/L, ALP 104 U/L, and TB

0.6 mg/dL. His sparsentan was continued; however, on y (Day 266), his ALT and
AST peaked at 277 U/L and 89 U/L respectively, and his sparsentan was temporarily
discontinued. Thereafter, his liver enzymes fell back to normal range. His sparsentan was

restarted on “ (Day 343). Shortly after, his liver enzymes began to increase again, and
sparsentan was permanently discontinued on y (Day 376) (Figure 11). No other

liver evaluation tests were performed.

DHN Assessment

This case is thought to be probably due to sparsentan because of the reoccurrence of elevated
TAs after restarting sparsentan.
