Conclusions and Reasons

CRBSIs are an important cause of morbidity and mortality, with
particular relevance to patients with kidney failure who require
vascular access for HD, sometimes for prolonged periods spanning
months or years.

A significant number of patients with kidney failure use temporary or
permanent CVCs as their primary vascular access for HD. In these
patients, CRBSIs can occur an average of 1-2 times per year, making
prevention of CRBSIs an important challenge. In addition to the
morbidity and mortality directly tied to the resultant infections, CRBSIs
also result in difficulties with maintaining vascular access needed for
HD.

CRBSls are serious and potentially life-
threatening infections causing significant
morbidity and mortality and are particularly
relevant to patients with kidney failure who
require temporary or permanent CVCs as
vascular access for HD.

There are currently no FDA-approved antimicrobial or antiseptic CLSs
for preventing or reducing the incidence of CRBSI.

Several antimicrobial and antiseptic solutions are used off-label as CLSs
to reduce the risk of CRBSIs in susceptible populations. Examples of
antimicrobial products that have been used as CLSs alone or in
combination for CRBSI prophylaxis include vancomycin, gentamicin,
ciprofloxacin, minocycline, amikacin, cefazolin, cefotaxime, and
ceftazidime. Other products used as CLSs include alcohol, taurolidine,
and trisodium citrate.

CDC guidelines for the prevention of CRBSIs recommend the use of
prophylactic antimicrobial lock solutions in patients with long term
catheters who have a history of multiple CRBSls.

As there are no FDA-approved antimicrobial or
antiseptic CLSs for preventing or reducing the
incidence of CRBSls, there is an unmet medical
need for safe and effective therapies. s.
