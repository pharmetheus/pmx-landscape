Bridge between to-
be-marketed and
clinical trial/study
formulations

The tablet formulation used in the pivotal Phase 3 trials is different from the final to-be-marketed tablet formulation. The Applicant
conducted a relative bioavailability study (Study 213022 Part B) in healthy adults to establish a PK bridge between the clinical trial
formulation and the to-be-marketed formulation. The 90% confidence interval for the least square means for the PK parameters (Cmax
and AUC) are within the 80-125% bioequivalence range for the 1 mg, 2 mg, 4 mg, 6 mg, and 8 mg tablet strengths.

Distribution
Volume of 14.3L
distribution
Plasma protein The binding of daprodustat to plasma protein is high (99.4%). Binding was also high to human serum albumin (>99.3%), while low to
binding alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (<20%). In general, there was no evidence of concentration-dependent protein binding of daprodustat over the

concentration range evaluated (0.2, 1, 10 and 50 g/mL).

Drug as substrate
of transporters

e Daprodustat is a substrate of human breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP) in vitro but not a substrate of human drug transporters
P-glycoprotein (P-gp), OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OATP2B1 OCT1, OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, MATE1, or MATE2-K. Concomitant
administration with BCRP inhibitors is not expected to have a clinically significant effect on daprodustat PK in vivo.

e All 6 predominant circulating metabolites showed some degree of active uptake activity in transporter-overexpressed cells
(OATP1B1, OATP1B3), but did not exhibit active uptake in human hepatocytes.

e The 6 metabolites are not in vitro substrates of the renal transporters OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, MATE1, or MATE2-K, with the exceptions
of M2, M3, and M13, which are weak substrates for OAT3, and M2, which is a weak substrate for OAT1.
