Literature Review

FDA reviewed the Applicant’s summary report of literature review (Appendix 16 of the SF-36
PRO Dossier) and noted much of the cited literature is based on approaches that are not aligned
with FDA guidance (e.g., use of distribution-based methods, between-group differences). Based
on FDA Guidance for Industry Patient-Reported Outcome Measures: Use in Medical Product
Development to Support Labeling Claims, sponsors should use anchor-based methods* to
directly incorporate patients’ perspective to help interpret the clinical meaningfulness of PRO
endpoints. Other approaches, such as distribution-based approaches using an effect size or a
standard deviation, or model-based approaches, are inappropriate as a primary method to
determine clinical meaningfulness as they do not directly take into account the patient

perspective. FDA concluded that the Applicant’s literature review is not adequate a

Anchor-Based Analyses

In response to the FDA’s recommendation in the IND phase, the Applicant conducted anchor-
based analyses using data from Studies ASCEND-ND and ASCEND-ID and derived a
meaningful within-patient change threshold range of 6- to 21-point improvement (see Figure 25)
based on 4 patient-reported anchor scales: PGI-S scale, Patient Global Impression of Change
(PGI-C) scale, and items 1 and 2 from the CKD-AQ. The Applicant used the PGI-S and the PGI-
C scales as the primary anchors and CKD-AQ items 1 and 2 as supportive anchors in the
quantitative anchor-based analyses. Refer to Table 227 in Section 16.1 for a summary of the
anchor scales.
