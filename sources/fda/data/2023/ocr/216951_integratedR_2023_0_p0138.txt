Jesduvrog (daprodustat)

Our non-inferiority conclusion was based on the prospectively defined and agreed upon primary
endpoint, statistical test, and NI margin. The primary endpoint, change in Hb value between
baseline and mean of values during Weeks 28 to 52, represents the key measurement used to
clinically define anemia, averaged over a time frame sufficient to capture steady state. In
constructing the NI margin of -0.75 g/dL, we considered Hb changes that would result in a
clinically meaningful difference to an individual patient, proportion of comparator (i.e., ESAs)
efficacy preserved by the margin, measurement variability, and precedent from prior trials (i.e.,
darbepoetin, peginesatide, and methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta). Non-inferiority was
defined as being achieved if the two-sided lower 95% confidence limit for the mean treatment
difference (daprodustat minus comparator) was greater than the pre-specified NI margin, as is
customary. Although some ASCEND studies were open label, bias due to knowledge of
treatment assignment was minimized by evidence of appropriate titration in the two treatment
arms and the central laboratory-Hb objective endpoint.

We found the conclusion of noninferiority to be robust to multiple sensitivity analyses which
assessed the impact of important model assumptions, such as accounting for rescue therapy,
endpoint missingness mechanisms (such as missing at random, or missing not at random), choice
of analysis time window, and estimand strategy. We also generally found little heterogeneity
between the subgroups studied, showing daprodustat was effective regardless of age, sex, race,
body weight, dialysis type, CKD stage, prior ESA use, and Baseline Hb (Refer to Section 16).

Regarding ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND

We placed particular emphasis on the efficacy results for ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND, since
it is well known hemoglobin can causally affect cardiovascular safety. In ASCEND-ND and
ASCEND-D, the observed Hb versus time profile confirms that the titration algorithms resulted
in similar Hb vs. time trajectories for daprodustat and the ESA comparator (Section 16). We are
reassured that mean-level plots fell in the middle of the Hb target range for both arms during the
EP. This provides assurance that the comparators were titrated and subsequently performed
acceptably. In addition, the comparators performed reasonably similarly with other programs
with regards to Hb vs. time. The within-subject variability in Hb data was also similar between
arms. The proportion of participants who received transfusions during the evaluation period was
generally similar between the daprodustat and ESA groups, as would be expected given the
similarity of the achieved Hb levels (Section 6.3.1). Results from the key secondary analyses of
the on-treatment monthly IV iron dose (mg/month) from Day 1 to Week 52 in ASCEND-D (not
assessed in ASCEND-ND, due to infrequent indication for IV iron, but assessed in ASCEND-TD
and ASCEND-ID) showed little or no difference between daprodustat and ESA in the mean IV
iron dose. These supporting data further support the non-inferiority conclusion of the efficacy of
daprodustat on Hb.

Disposition and conduct during ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND was not of concer. For each
study, the treatment arms were balanced in relation to baseline demographics and clinical
characteristics, with appropriate representation across ages, genders, and races/ethnicities. As
mentioned above, we did not identify a heterogeneous efficacy response in a subgroup that
warranted further study or concern. Importantly, follow-up was remarkably complete (>95% of
potential follow-up time was accounted for).
