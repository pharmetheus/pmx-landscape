Regarding ASCEND-ID, ASCEND-TD, and ASCEND-NHQ

ASCEND-ID established non-inferiority of daprodustat for efficacy (mean change from baseline
in Hb over evaluation period of Weeks 28 to 52) to darbepoetin in the ID population. As the
study was not designed to demonstrate general safety or cardiovascular outcomes, |

it is unclear if the risks in this population
will be similar to the NDD population © or the DD population.
Further discussion regarding cardiovascular safety in the ID population is in Section 6.2.4 and
Section 7.7.6.

In the ASCEND-TD, analysis of mean change in Hb between baseline over the evaluation period
of Weeks 28 to 52 demonstrated that daprodustat TIW was non-inferior to epoetin alfa. The
study was not designed to demonstrate general safety or cardiovascular outcomes. A detailed
discussion of the CV safety and extrapolation of CV safety for the TIW dosing regimen in the
DD population is in Section 7.7.5.

ASCEND-NHQ established superiority for the primary efficacy endpoint of mean change in Hb
between baseline over the evaluation period of Weeks 24 to 28 compared to placebo. It is
important to note that the evaluation period in this trial was shorter than the other trials and the
comparator arm was placebo. In addition, the target hemoglobin level was higher (11 to 12 g/dL)
compared to 10 to 11 g/dL for all other trials in the ASCEND program. The principal secondary
endpoint was a patient reported outcome (PRO), the mean change in SF-26 Vitality Domain
between baseline and Week 28. We did not agree with the Applicant’s conclusions regarding
demonstrated benefit on the PRO. This is a key efficacy review issue discussed under Section
6.3.2.

Lastly, for each ASCEND study, we reviewed the protocols, the statistical analysis plan, study
report, and study data (Study Data Tabulation Model and Analysis Dataset Model). The quality
of the original data submission was adequate. In general, the reviewers independently
corroborated the Applicant’s analyses using the submitted datasets.

continued need for rescue therapy. Most patients with CKD receiving hemodialysis require ESAs
