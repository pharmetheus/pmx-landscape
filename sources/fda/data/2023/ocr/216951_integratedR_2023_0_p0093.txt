NDA 216951
Jesduvrog (daprodustat)

Primary Efficacy Analysis

The primary analysis of post-randomization mean change in Hb between baseline and over the
EP (Weeks 28 to 52) for the ITT population was analyzed using an ANCOVA model adjusted
for treatment, baseline Hb and region.

Sensitivity Analyses

e An alternative “while on-treatment” estimand using only evaluable Hb data. Missing Hb
data were not imputed.
PP population analysis based on the evaluable Hb analysis.
A tipping point analysis using all available Hb values (on and off treatment) as a
sensitivity analysis for the primary estimand. Missing post-baseline Hb values were
imputed.

e A tipping point analysis using only evaluable Hb values. Missing Hb data were not
imputed.

e An alternative EP analysis from Week 28 to 36 using all available Hb values (on and off
treatment). Missing post-baseline Hb values were imputed.

e Analternative EP analysis from Week 28 to 36 using only evaluable Hb values. Missing
Hb data were not imputed.

Secondary Efficacy Analyses

An ANCOVA model was used to compare the difference in average monthly IV iron dose per
participant between arms, adjusting for treatment, baseline monthly IV iron dose and region.

A least square mean estimates and two-sided 95% CI for the treatment difference are provided,
together with the one-sided superiority test p-value.

The secondary analysis of Hb change from baseline to Week 52 Hb was tested for noninferiority
using the -0.75 g/dL margin used in the primary analysis. A MMRM approach was used with an
unstructured covariance matrix to compare the difference in means between arms. The model
was fitted to Hb data collected post-baseline up to Week 52, excluding values collected during
the SP (Day 1 to Week 28). The model included factors for treatment, time, region, baseline Hb
and the baseline Hb by time and treatment by time interaction terms.

For the Hb responder analysis, responders were participants with a mean Hb during the EP that
fell within the Hb analysis range of 10 to 11.5 g/dL. A CMH chi-squared test, adjusting for
treatment and region, was used to compare the number and % of responders between the
treatment groups.

For the analysis of time to stopping study treatment due to meeting rescue criteria, a Cox
proportional hazards model, adjusting for treatment and region, was used to generate the hazard
ratio and associated 95% CI for the treatment group comparison. A one-sided superiority p-value
was generated using a Wald test. The incidence rate per 100 person-years (PY) and associated
95% CI for each treatment group, as well as the absolute rate difference per 100 PY and
associated 95% CI were also provided.
