7.6.1.1.2. On-Study vs. On-Treatment Analyses

To assess risks, two approaches were considered: an on-study analysis and OT analysis. An on-
study analysis considers a subject at risk of experiencing an event from the time of
randomization until trial discontinuation, which includes events that occur while a subject is on
treatment and off treatment (for those subjects who prematurely discontinued treatment but
remained in the trial). An OT analysis considers a subject at risk of experiencing an event from
the time of randomization to the time of treatment discontinuation plus a pre-defined fixed period
of time thereafter (e.g., 28 days). Refer to Section 7.7.1 for discussion of the pros and cons of the
two approaches in assessing risks.

In the development program for daprodustat, an on-study analysis approach was the primary
analysis, and an OT analysis approach was a supportive analysis. Three variations of the OT
analysis accounting for differing treatment dosing intervals, resulting in different ascertainment
windows, were considered (see Section 7.7.1 and Table 72).

In ASCEND-ND, the prespecified primary on-study analysis approach captured events from
randomization to study completion/withdrawal (mean follow-up duration 1.9 years). Treatment
discontinuation was similar between treatment arms (daprodustat 38%, darbepoetin alfa 38%), as
was study withdrawal (daprodustat 3%, darbepoetin alfa 3%) and use of rescue therapies
(daprodustat 2%, darbepoetin alfa 3%).

The prespecified primary analysis of MACE was on-study (HR 1.03; 95% CI 0.89, 1.19; see
Section 7.6.2.1.1). The prespecified supportive OT analysis (OT+28) resulted in a higher
incidence of MACE in the daprodustat arm compared to the control arm and an HR estimate (HR
1.40; 95% CI 1.17, 1.68) that was greater than the on-study analysis estimate (Table 72).
