7. Safety (Risk and Risk Management)

7.1. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on
Nonclinical Data

The safety of daprodustat and major human metabolites was evaluated in a comprehensive
nonclinical program. Toxicological characterization was performed in good laboratory practice-
compliant toxicology studies in mice, rats, dogs, and monkeys, in reproductive toxicology
studies in rats and rabbits, and in a carcinogenicity assessment that included data from lifetime
carcinogenicity studies in mice and rats. Monkeys were considered the most relevant nonclinical
species for general toxicological evaluation based on the similarity of the daprodustat metabolite
profile to humans and higher bioavailability compared to other species.

Daprodustat binding was shown to be specific for HIF-prolyl hydroxylases in primary
pharmacology and secondary pharmacology (evaluations of potential targets not associated with
the desired therapeutic effect) assessments. No significant risks were identified during in vitro
and in vivo cardiovascular, respiratory, or neurologic safety pharmacology assessments.
Daprodustat is highly protein-bound in rats and rabbits (~100%), humans (>99%), monkeys and
dogs (~98%), and mice (>99%). Because only the unbound fraction of drug is “active”, the high
level of protein binding complicated the determination of appropriate exposure margins between
nonclinical species and humans. Ultimately, exposure extrapolation based on body surface area
(mg/m?) was deemed the most appropriate method to inform clinical risk.

The IND enabling nonclinical toxicology studies produced effects consistent with the
exaggerated primary pharmacology of daprodustat (polycythemia) and secondary impacts on
hemodynamics (systemic thrombosis, hemorrhage, congestion, and necrosis in lung, kidney,
heart, and other organs). Thus, the clinical safety of daprodustat was predicted to be largely a
function of its primary pharmacology and impacts on blood viscosity.

In a 6-month toxicology study in non-anemic rats, adverse findings associated with excess
hematocrit (e.g., thrombosis, hemorrhage, congestion, and necrosis occurred in various organs,
along with splenomegaly, extramedullary hematopoiesis, and platelet reductions) at the highest
dose of 10 mg/kg/day (4 times the clinical dose based on body surface area) in both sexes. In
males, these effects caused mortality and moribundity leading to early termination of all high-
dose male animals, which occurred coincident with total blood hematocrit levels as high as 75%
at Week 13 (versus total blood hematocrit levels of 52% in untreated controls), whereas females
tolerated similar effects on hematocrit. Daprodustat-related gastric hemorrhage, erosions and/or
ulcerations also occurred due to downstream complications related to increased blood viscosity
at the highest dose. In the 6-month rat study, the no-observed-adverse-effect-level (NOAEL) was
