7.3. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Identified
Through Postmarket Experience

Daprodustat was approved in Japan on June 29, 2020, for the treatment of anemia due to CKD.
The Applicant estimates ~11000 PY of exposure has been obtained in Japan up until the cutoff
date of June 2021. The Applicant claims there are no new safety concerns with daprodustat
based on the available post-marketing data to date.

7.4. FDA Approach to the Safety Review

The safety review of daprodustat was divided into two populations of patients with CKD: those
who were not dependent on chronic dialysis, and those who were dependent on chronic dialysis.
We viewed the data this way because the patient populations are distinct with regards to
cardiovascular physiology, severity of anemia, and patient management.

Of the five phase 3 clinical studies submitted to support this application (2 in NDD, 3 in DD), we
focused our safety review on ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND. ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND
were large, randomized, active controlled trials designed to assess noninferiority for MACE (as a
coprimary endpoint). The sample size and study duration required to adequately power for
MACE also fulfilled safety database size recommendations in ICH E1. The remaining phase 3
studies, ASCEND-NHQ, ASCEND-TD, and ASCEND-ID were designed to assess specific
efficacy objectives (e.g., specific patient populations, dosing, or outcomes). These remaining
studies had a limited size and duration, and importantly, unique design elements that limit their
utility to formally integrate with the CVOT. Therefore, we concluded that pooling these smaller,
disparate studies with the CVOTs was not useful.

ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND were open-label (sponsor-blind) trials: patients and investigators
were aware of treatment assignment, which may have influenced the reporting for adverse event
occurrence — particularly for those who were randomized to a treatment that was similar to what
they were treated with prior to the study. This reporting bias is likely attenuated for the serious
adjudicated outcomes and objective laboratory evaluations. Although we did not identify any
obvious signs of reporting bias, we placed particular emphasis on events adjudicated by a
blinded committee (see below).
