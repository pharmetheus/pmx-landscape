The primary safety objective of ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND was to assess NI of daprodustat
to ESA in first occurrence of MACE. MACE was a composite endpoint consisting of all-cause
mortality (ACM), nonfatal MI and nonfatal stroke. A pre-defined NI margin of 1.25 for the HR
(daprodustat versus ESA) was adopted following discussions between the FDA and Applicant
during the IND. All-cause mortality, rather than cardiovascular mortality, was included in the
composite since ACM represents a clinically important and highly prevalent competing risk in
the dialysis and non-dialysis dependent CKD populations. In addition, studies in anemia of CKD
with similar designs and objectives (i.e., TREAT, CHOIR, EMERALD, PEARL) used all-cause
mortality.

Analyses of individual CV events (ACM, ML stroke, thromboembolic events, and
hospitalization for HF) were conducted as additional secondary endpoints to provide a
comprehensive evaluation of CV safety data obtained in the trials. The FDA considered
additional exploratory time-to-event endpoints of CV MACE (composite of CV mortality,
nonfatal MI, and nonfatal stroke) and VAT," a type of TEE, to be clinically meaningful; these
results are presented below. The secondary endpoints and these additional safety endpoints were
not controlled for multiple comparisons.

The Applicant used an external Clinical Events Committee (Duke Clinical Research Institute)
group to conduct blinded adjudication of all investigator reported events suspected to meet the
definition of MACE as well as other adjudicated CV events (including all-cause mortality,
thromboembolic events, and hospitalization for HF), as well as select CKD progression events
for the ASCEND-ND study. FDA did not review narratives for the adjudicated events or
adjudication quality.

During our review, we identified investigator reported serious acute kidney injury (AKI) and
gastrointestinal erosions/bleeding as potentially significant harms.

Analysis of MACE and CV Endpoints

The primary population for the analyses of MACE and CV endpoints is the ITT population,
defined as all randomized subjects analyzed according to the treatment to which subjects were
randomized.
