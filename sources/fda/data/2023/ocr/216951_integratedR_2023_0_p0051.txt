Jesduvroq (daprodustat)

5. Pharmacologic Activity, Pharmacokinetics,
and Clinical Pharmacology

5.1. Nonclinical Assessment of Potential
Effectiveness

Daprodustat is a small molecule inhibitor of HIF prolyl-4-hydroxylases (PH1, 2, and 3). Hypoxic
conditions or inhibition of PHs leads to increased accumulation of unhydroxylated HIFa
subunits. These unhydroxylated HIFa subunits travel into the nucleus and form dimers with
HIFf subunits. These dimers promote transcription of HIF-responsive genes that stimulate
erythropoietic processes for the treatment of anemia in a similar manner to those resulting from
hypoxia.

Daprodustat reversibly binds HIF-PH1, 2, and 3 (Kj of 1.7-4.4nM) and selectively inhibits all
three PH isoforms (half maximal inhibitory concentration [ICs9] of 3.3-8.9nM), in contrast to
ICsos with close homologues collagen prolyl hydroxylase (CP4H) and factor inhibiting HIF
(FIN), which were in the 1M range.

Daprodustat was evaluated in cell-based assays, which confirmed that inhibition of PH isoforms
by daprodustat stabilizes dimers of unhydroxylated HIFa with HIFB subunits and induces
erythropoiesis by stimulating the downstream cascade normally induced by hypoxia.
Specifically, PH inhibition resulted in decreased hepcidin mRNA expression, leading to
increased utilization of iron (increased hemoglobin production), and increased transcription of
HIF-responsive genes, including EPO, heme oxygenase-1, phosphoglycerate kinase-1 (PGK1),
and VEGF, leading to stimulation of erythropoietic processes. Likewise, in vivo, in wild-type
