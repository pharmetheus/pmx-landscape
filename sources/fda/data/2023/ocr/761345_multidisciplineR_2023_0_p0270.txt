The main model interactions in the central and BM compartments are outlined below. Briefly,
in the central compartment, the key interactions described by the model involve binding of
BsAb to sBCMA receptors in circulation and to circulatory T cell CD3 receptors. The synthesis of
sBCMA in the central compartment is assumed to be proportional to total tumor burden.
Besides binding drug, T cells can transit between the central and the BM compartments. To
retain T cell steady state dynamics in circulation, a zero-order proliferation term, proportional
to baseline T cell density and clearance rate, was incorporated in the T cell model equations.
Serum FLC and M-protein dynamics are described by a balance of synthesis rates (following the
same form as those for sBCMA, i.e., proportional to total tumor burden) and degradation rates.
In the BM compartment (tumor site of action) where MM cells are produced and proliferate, a

BsAb can bind: i) myeloma cell BCMA receptors, ii) T cell CD3 receptors, and iii) shed sBCMA.
