Reproductive Toxicology

Key Findings:

Pregnant Sprague Dawley rats were administered vehicle or etrasimod at 0.4, 2, or 4
mg/kg/day from GD 6 through LD 20. There were no adverse effects on maternal
clinical signs, or body weights at any dose of etrasimod, although decreased feed
consumption was observed in all treated groups during gestation.

One female at 4 mg/kg/day was prematurely terminated on GD 22 due to severe
adverse Clinical signs during delivery. A significant increase in post-implantation loss
was observed at 4 mg/kg/day and significantly increased gestation length was observed
at 2 and 4 mg/kg/day compared to controls. A significant increase in the number of
females with stillborn pups at 2 and 4 mg/kg/day and increased number of stillborn pups
per litter at 2 mg/kg/day compared to controls were also observed. The viability index
was decreased at 2 and 4 mg/kg/day while the lactation index was decreased at 4
mg/kg/day compared to controls.

Etrasimod-related clinical signs in F1 offspring included cold to touch, suspected
dehydration (based on skin turgor), discolored skin, skin pallor, swelling, and thin
appearance at 4 mg/kg/day and no milk band in the stomach at 2 and 4 mg/kg/day.
Decreased maternal feed consumption during lactation corresponded to decreased pup
body weights and body weight gain during the preweaning period at 2 and 4 mg/kg/day.
