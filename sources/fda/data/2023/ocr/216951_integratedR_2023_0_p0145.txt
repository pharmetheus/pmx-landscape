The safety population was defined as all subjects in the randomized population who received at
least one dose of study drug and analysis was based on the actual treatment received. The ITT
population was defined as all randomized subjects based on planned randomized treatment
assignment. In ASCEND-D and ASCEND-ND, there was no meaningful difference between the
ITT and safety populations since the number of subjects included in each arm differs by 2 to 5
patients. Nonetheless, we have attempted to maintain consistency throughout our analyses.

The general safety assessment considered subjects to be at risk for the total duration of follow-up
regardless of being on or off treatment. FDA generally relied on the on-study (i.e., regardless of
treatment discontinuation) analyses to ascertain estimates of treatment differences for all
endpoints (e.g., AEs, vitals, laboratory assessments). Through the course of our review, we
identified limitations in the prespecified on-treatment definitions® related to disparate dosing
frequency of assigned treatment (reference 7.6.1.1.2). We have generally found that the study
design, and particularly the disposition of study patients, supports a robust and interpretable
estimate of treatment difference from the ASCEND CVOTs.

AEs were reported by verbatim term and coded and categorized using Medical Dictionary for
Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) version 24.0. To assess the reliability and quality of the data,
the clinical reviewer compared the coding of verbatim reported adverse event terms, the
MedDRA lowest coded level term, and preferred term in the safety datasets. No major data
quality, translation (i-e., verbatim to preferred) or integrity issues were identified that would
preclude performing a safety review for this NDA. In addition to adverse event analyses grouped
by MedDRA preferred terms, we used FDA Medical Query (FMQ) groupings. FMQs are a
standardized approach to group preferred terms of a similar concept; as such, FMQs consolidate
a medical condition with scattered preferred terms and are more likely to identify any signal of
safety issues. The review team used FMQ version 2.0. Extensive documentation is available
publicly on the Federal Register.?

Cumulative incidence proportions estimate of within arm risk were based on incident outcomes.
Treatment arm comparisons and confidence intervals were based on marginal, unadjusted,
absolute risk differences. We acknowledge that exposure adjusted analyses would be more easily
interpretable given the varying duration of follow up for participants in the event driven CVOTs.
Nonetheless, the exploratory analyses were conducted to identify any potential safety signals
which would then be subject to more rigorous analyses such as time-to-event analyses (e.g.,
Kaplan-Meier methods). Accordingly, safety signals identified in Section 7.6 which we felt
merited potential concern were analyzed appropriately and discussed in detail in Section 7.7.
