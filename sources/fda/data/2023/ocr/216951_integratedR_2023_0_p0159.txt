Jesduvrog (daprodustat)

Organ Class is noteworthy (RD [95% CI]: 2.1 [0.0 - 4.2]). The MedDRA PTs
contributing to most of the SOC treatment difference are ‘Cardiac failure, congestive’,
‘Cardiac failure’, ‘Myocardial infarction’, and ‘Cardiac arrest’. The first two PTs
(‘Cardiac failure, congestive’, and ‘Cardiac failure’) and second two PTs (‘Myocardial
infarction’, ‘Cardiac arrest’) may represent the same medical concept, but the clinical
presentation may differ slightly or may represent heterogeneous reporting by the
investigator. The treatment difference in FMQs, which counts these similar terms
together, was 1.3% (-0.1 — 2.8) for heart failure, and 0.2 to 0.5% for myocardial
infarction/ischemia/acute coronary syndrome.

e Thrombosis: There is a treatment difference not favoring daprodustat for the FMQ of
thrombosis (RD of 0.9%). The treatment difference seems to be driven by arterial events,
a nFMQ of arterial thrombosis (RD 1.0) is nested within the thrombosis nFMQ and
includes MI and CVA (but excludes venous thromboembolism). Similar signals were
seen for nFMQs of myocardial infarction (RD: 0.4) and myocardial ischemia (RD: 0.5)
and acute coronary syndrome (RD: 0.2). Importantly these cover similar concepts in the
continuum of acute coronary syndrome and are correlated (i.e., these queries include
overlapping PTs) and thus not directly additive. There was a treatment difference not
favoring daprodustat in the Stroke/TIA nFMQ (RD: 1.4) which seems notable, given the
small proportion of ESA treated subjects with an event reported (3.3% in daprodustat and
1.9% in ESA).

e Renal outcomes: The treatment difference not favoring daprodustat in proportion of
subjects who were reported to have a SAE in the MedDRA Renal System Organ Class is
noteworthy (RD [95%CI]: 3.1 [0.8 - 5.4]). The PTs contributing mostly to this difference
are AKI, CKD, and azotemia. Additionally, a treatment difference not favoring
daprodustat in the proportion of subjects who were reported to have a SAE nFMQ for
AKI (RD 1.5) emerged.

e Gastrointestinal bleeding: There was slight imbalance in the PT for serious
gastrointestinal hemorrhage (RD0.4%), and the Gastrointestinal SOC (0.9% RD) not
favoring daprodustat. Additionally, the RD based on the FMQ for serious hemorrhage
(RD 0.7) was consistent with the PT analysis. In isolation (i-e., only considering the
readout from this analysis) one might reasonably suspect this treatment difference may be
due to chance alone. However, gastrointestinal erosions were a prespecified adverse
event of special interest (AESI) and were seen in the nonclinical program.

The safety signals for thrombosis and the cardiovascular organ class cover similar or overlapping
concepts. Additionally, the safety issues covered by these medical concepts were prespecified
and adjudicated as part of a primary composite endpoint (MACE) or as secondary endpoints
(HHF, CVA, MI, TEE) and are major safety review issues (Sections 7.6.1.1, 7.6.1.2, and
7.6.1.8). Nonetheless, the investigator-reported TESAEs provide further support for safety
findings observed in primary safety and key secondary safety CV endpoints (see discussion in
AESI section).

We considered the treatment differences not favoring daprodustat for gastrointestinal bleeding
and AKI major safety review issues (see Section 7.7). There were no other concerning safety
signals for SAEs based on PTs, narrow FMQs and SOCs occurring at an incidence of <1% (data
not shown).
