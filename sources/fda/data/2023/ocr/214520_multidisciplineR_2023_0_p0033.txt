NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation — NDA 214520 Defencath (taurolidine 1.35% and heparin 5,000 units/5 mL (1,000

Units/mL))

Dimension

Reference ID: 4752984

Evidence and Uncertainties

Conclusions and Reasons

The efficacy of Defencath was demonstrated in a single, randomized,
controlled Phase 3 trial, LOCK-IT-100, in a population of patients with
kidney failure receiving chronic HD through a CVC. An interim analysis
showed that Defencath met the primary endpoint of time to CRBSI
with 6 cases of CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs in the Defencath arm and 22 in
the heparin arm (p=0.0034), resulting in a 71% reduction in incidence
of CAC-adjudicated CRBSI. Analysis at the completion of the trial also
showed reduction in CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs in the Defencath arm
with statistical significance in favor of Defencath. Consistent results
were seen in multiple sensitivity analyses.

In the Phase 3 trial, Defencath was found to be
superior to the heparin control with a 71%
reduction in the incidence of CRBSI in patients
with kidney failure receiving chronic HD through
aCvc.

The safety finding of concern in LOCK-IT-100 was loss of catheter
patency with a higher incidence in the Defencath arm (16%) compared
to the heparin arm (12%). Thrombolytic therapy such as tPA usage was
also higher among subjects in the Defencath arm (12%) than the
heparin arm (8%). The overall number of catheter removals was
greater in the Defencath arm (59%) compared to the heparin arm
(57%). The main driver of the observed difference in catheter removal
rates between the two arms was the higher number of patients with
AV graft/fistula maturation (a positive reason for catheter removal) in
the Defencath arm (39%) compared to in the heparin arm (32%). The
remainder of the ARs occurring in LOCK-IT-100 were consistent with
prior knowledge of the safety of taurolidine, heparin, and the citrate
excipient. No serious risks that would necessitate specific risk
management strategies were identified.

The risk of loss of catheter patency will be
discussed in the labeling for Defencath. No
serious risks that warrant specific risk
management strategies were identified. The ARs
will be communicated via labeling and
monitored via routine pharmacovigilance.
