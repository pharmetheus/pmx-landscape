TEAEs that occurred in at least 2% of subjects treated with etrasimod and more frequently than
placebo are described below.

e@ Headache: Headache was the most common TEAE reported by subjects during APD334-
302 and APD334-003. A greater proportion of subjects who received etrasimod (6%)
reported a TEAE of headache compared to placebo (4%). The majority of headache
TEAEs were mild or moderate in severity. One subject reported a severe TEAE of
headache, which was considered an SAE (preferred term “migraine”), which is described
in detail in Section 8.2.5.2. No headache TEAEs led to study discontinuation. An
imbalance was also observed in APD334-301 (refer to Section 8.2.5.5). The mechanism
by which etrasimod may increase the risk of headache is unknown; however, headache
is an adverse reaction observed in several other S1P receptor modulators, including
Zeposia (ozanimod), which is approved for the treatment of moderately to severely
active UC. Therefore, headache will be included in the Adverse Reactions section of the
label.
