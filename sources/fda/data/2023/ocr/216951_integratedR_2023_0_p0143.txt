NDA 216951
Jesduvrog (daprodustat)

7.2. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on
Drug Class or Other Drug-Specific Factors

Daprodustat is a small molecule inhibitor of HIF-PH enzymes. HIF-PHIs are a new class of
drugs that increase hemoglobin by inducing endogenous EPO production (rather than exogenous
administration as per the ESAs). Currently, the standard of care for the treatment of anemia due
to CKD is ESA, which include Epogen/Procrit (epoetin alfa), Aranesp (darbepoetin alfa),
Mircera (pegylated epoetin beta) and Retacrit (epoetin alfa-epbx). ESAs are approved for the
treatment of anemia due to CKD, including patients on dialysis and not on dialysis. Given the
similar end effect of increased EPO, as a common mechanism of action between daprodustat and
ESAs, safety concems observed with ESAs were considered potential safety concems in our
safety evaluation of daprodustat.

Briefly, current ESA labeling includes a boxed waming for increased risk of death, thrombosis,
and thromboembolism (such as myocardial infarction stroke, venous thromboembolism, and
thrombosis of vascular access) and congestive heart failure. Although these risks were observed
in randomized controlled trials with randomized arms targeting higher hemoglobin levels using
ESAs (ie., >11 g/dL), a hemoglobin target level or dosing strategy that avoids the risks of ESAs
has not been established. As a result, the ESA labeling recommends using the lowest ESA dose
sufficient to reduce the need for red blood cell transfusions, and specifically wars against
exceeding 11 g/dL of Hb. Additionally, the labeling for ESAs includes the following safety
concerns in the warnings and precautions:

e Hypertension, worsening or new onset, with a contraindication in patients with
uncontrolled hypertension
Increased risk for seizures in patients with CKD
Serious allergic reactions, including anaphylactic reactions, angioedema, bronchospasm,
skin rash and urticaria

¢ Severe cutaneous reactions, including blistering and skin exfoliation

Vadadustat and roxadustat are also HIF-PH inhibitors under development and are approved in
other countries.’ These molecules share a mechanism of action, targeted indication and patient
population with daprodustat. The drug development programs have been broadly similar. In July
2021, the Cardiovascular and Renal Drugs Advisory Committee considered the roxadustat NDA
for the treatment of anemia due to CKD in patients on dialysis (DD) and not on dialysis (NDD).
The Committee did not support approval for either population and recommended that additional
trials be conducted to determine a dose and dose strategy with a better benefit-risk profile. The
FDA issued a complete response letter (CRL) for roxadustat for both NDD and DD populations,
citing concems for thrombosis and thromboembolism that were echoed by the members of the
advisory comunittee. Vadadustat was also issued a CRL due similar safety concerns, as well as
concerns for drug-induced liver injury. Generally, clinical trial results have disfavored HIF-PHIs
versus active control or ESAs for MACE (which includes MI, CVA, and all-cause mortality).
