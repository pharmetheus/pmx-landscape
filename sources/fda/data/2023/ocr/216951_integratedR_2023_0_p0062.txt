For 90% power to test the co-primary non-inferiority Hb hypothesis, with a two-sided 95% CI,
pre-specified margin of -0.75 g/dL and a two-sample t-test, a total of 86 evaluable participants
per treatment group was required. The planned study size far exceeded this requirement and
provided more than 99% power for non-inferiority and a high level of precision to estimate the
treatment effect (two-sided 95% CI half width of 0.128 g/dL, assuming 30% of participants
would be non-evaluable for efficacy). The largest (most negative) difference between arms that
would meet the statistical criterion for non-inferiority would be -0.622 g/dL. If the two-sided
95% CI for the treatment difference is completely negative (i-e., lies fully within the range -0.75
to < 0 g/dL) non-inferiority would still be concluded.

For all four of the active-controlled trials, the pre-specified margin for efficacy of -0.75 g/dL was
determined based on Hb changes that would result in a clinically meaningful difference to a
patient, the percentage of the rhEPO efficacy preserved by the margin and consideration of
changes that could be due to variability and precedent for margins used in past trials for
comparative Hb efficacy assessments (Aranesp, Omontys and Mircera).

Interim Analysis

There were no prospectively defined interim analyses planned to stop the study early for benefit.
While the planned futility analysis described below had a small impact on reducing study-wise
Type I error rate, there were no plans to adjust the alpha level used for the final analysis.

The Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC) evaluated the co-primary MACE
endpoint to assess for futility of achieving non-inferiority at study completion. Pre-specified
guidelines governing the decision to continue or stop the study considered signals for harm, the
predictive probability of achieving at least non-inferiority at trial end and the risk of incorrectly
stopping for futility.

Missing Data Handling for Efficacy Endpoint

For the analysis of the co-primary efficacy endpoint, a multiple imputation approach was used to
handle missing Hb values. Both intermittent missing and monotone missing scheduled Hb data
were imputed based on the missing at random (MAR) assumption. However, it is important to
examine the sensitivity of inferences to departures from the MAR assumption for the monotone
missing data. A tipping point analysis was performed as a sensitivity analysis for the
assumptions of missing data, that is, to evaluate missing not at random (MNAR) in which the
missing observations depend on unobserved values.

Analysis for Primary Efficacy Endpoint

The primary population for efficacy analyses was the intent-to-treat (ITT) population. The
primary efficacy evaluation was the effect of daprodustat relative to rhEPO on the change in Hb
from Baseline to the average of all Hb values in the EP, regardless of adherence to treatment
including interruptions and discontinuation, the use of non-randomized ESA medication for any
reason including rescue therapy, or the use of blood transfusions, in participants on dialysis
currently treated with an ESA with anemia secondary to CKD and assuming participants did not
die before the end of the EP.
